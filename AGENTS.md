## Scientific editorial contract
- The purpose is deeply understanding science (observation → evidence → mechanism → mathematical reasoning → biological implications → exceptions → original explained practice), in accessible, connected academic English.
- Never use high HTML character count, many tabular question rows or a node-number quota to substitute for serious narrative theory. Audit visible *non-table* theory prose and compare with substantial published chapters.
- Fully verify equations, units, quantum states, isotope/electron counts, data interpretation, original diagrams and citations before publication.
- No shallow or unfinished material on main. Preserve quality-sensitive work on a feature branch when incomplete.
- Read `quality_hold` in the progress checkpoint: do the required repair before moving forward if feasible; a published node can still need substantive educational improvement.
- Keep previously verified pages/design intact, don't duplicate menu or sitemap entries, use GitHub conditional non-force updates and second HEAD/file checks before publication.
- Do not call a site live unless GitHub Pages success/live availability was independently verified.

## Student-facing publication and editorial voice
- Treat every visible syllabus title, description, topic heading, learning node and navigation element as finished, publication-ready educational copy. Publish only useful student-facing material; do not expose internal planning, implementation status, scheduling, AI assistance, developer notes or procedural commentary in the rendered site.
- Never insert placeholder or filler disclaimers such as "topics will be added", "not yet planned", "coming soon", "generated by AI", "under development" or similar workflow messages into the visible curriculum or any unit. If content or a link is not ready, omit it from the student-facing menu rather than fill space with operational language.
- Write coherent, grammatical, natural academic English with connected explanations, precise terminology and consistent punctuation. Avoid robotic templates, artificial enthusiasm, chopped phrases, broken words, repetitive formulaic intros, meta commentary, and anything that reads like a chat response rather than a thoughtfully edited textbook.
- Preserve faithfully sourced syllabus scope and established terminology; do not introduce superficial renamings or omit official syllabus concepts merely to simplify copy. Cite genuine scientific and official sources where appropriate.
- Keep all working status and quality-gate instructions in repository documentation, not on published pages. Before each push, scan the diff and rendered student-facing text for placeholder/status language, typography defects, duplicate entries, unverified assertions and accessibility regressions. A finished menu can omit unpublished topics without disclaimers.

## Strict syllabus scope control: CSIR-NET Life Sciences & State SET
- **Audience and boundary:** Teach content that falls within the currently verified official CSIR-UGC NET Life Sciences / merged CSIR-UGC-DBT Life Sciences & Biotechnology syllabus, or within the relevant State SET Life Sciences syllabus. A chapter must never drift into an independent off-syllabus subject just because a related fact is interesting.
- **Syllabus mapping before prose:** Map every planned chapter and major microtopic to its actual official unit, subunit and topic line; mark genuine prerequisite knowledge explicitly, and reject or relocate concepts that have no defensible syllabus connection. Do not invent syllabus headings or claim an Academy node count is official.
- **Depth versus breadth:** Deliver full conceptual, mechanistic, experimental, mathematical and exam-reasoning depth **within** mapped syllabus topics. Rich teaching is encouraged; tangential expansion, unrelated advanced research, generic trivia and unnecessary professional/clinical elaboration are not.
- **Cross-unit discipline:** Brief explanations of prerequisites or applications are allowed solely to clarify the current mechanism; reserve substantive content for the syllabus unit where it belongs. Avoid duplication and ensure the roadmap remains chronological and coherent.
- **NET and SET alignment:** Verify the current NET syllabus and applicable State SET syllabus against authoritative official sources. Where boundaries or classification differ, explicitly reconcile them in an **internal** roadmap; teach the shared examinable concept once in the logical location. Distinguish core official requirements from optional explanatory examples without exposing editorial status labels to students.
- **Prepublication scope gate:** Independently inspect each section, heading, illustration, example, MCQ and practice task: What exact in-syllabus learning objective does this teach? Remove or tighten anything without a clear answer. Confirm all official syllabus points for the selected subunit are substantively covered across the assigned node sequence, not merely named in titles.
- **Unit 2.A implementation:** The finalized ten reading pages `2.A.01` to `2.A.10` form **Subunit 2.A only**, with coherent in-page microtopics; they do not complete Unit 02's separate subunits 2.B–2.E. Follow `curriculum/subunit-2a-cell-wall-membrane-node-map.md` and its official-syllabus mapping.

## Academic depth ceiling: NET/SET, not PhD or specialist research
- **Audience and maximum depth:** The Academy teaches to the conceptual and problem-solving level appropriate for the official CSIR-NET Life Sciences / Life Sciences & Biotechnology and State SET Life Sciences syllabi, approximately postgraduate entrance/eligibility examination preparation. Do **not** develop PhD-level, specialist research-level, or independent frontier-research instruction.
- **In-syllabus mastery, not PhD specialization:** Explain core concepts and genuinely exam-relevant mechanisms, classical supporting experiments, standard methods, derivations and quantitative problems clearly and thoroughly; do not introduce advanced specialist experimental protocols, cutting-edge debates, highly technical research methods, niche pathways, unpublished hypotheses, elaborate primary-literature reviews or research-only mathematical treatment.
- **Evidence is not research-level instruction:** Briefly describe landmark or canonical experiments where essential to explain a syllabus concept or a NET/SET-style question. Do not turn these into doctoral laboratory training or research methodology tutorials.
- **Strict microtopic screening:** Every microtopic, example, diagram, method and question must have a defensible connection to an official NET or State SET learning objective and must be no harder or broader than required for strong exam understanding. Omit, simplify, or relocate any PhD-only tangent instead of padding content.
- **Quality standard remains high:** Accessible, progressive, rigorous postgraduate-level explanations are mandatory. Never use this ceiling as a reason to publish shallow, incomplete, oversimplified or unsupported chapters.
- **Scope-sensitive roadmaps:** Existing chapter outlines and examples are provisional with respect to this ceiling; before writing each reading page, remove or narrow any research-level tangent that may have appeared in planning. Apply this rule to every unit and to both manual and scheduled publishing.
