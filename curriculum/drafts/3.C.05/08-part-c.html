Part C practice — quantitative and experimental reasoning

All datasets and experimental scenarios in these questions are hypothetical teaching examples unless a historical source is explicitly identified.

31. Numerical

A hypothetical RNA receives 500 complexes at its first AUG. The first captures 40%, and the second captures 70% of those remaining. Assume no dissociation or reinitiation. How many initiate at each site and at neither?

  1. 200, 350, −50
  2. 200, 210, 90
  3. 300, 140, 60
  4. 200, 150, 150
Answer and explanation

B. 200, 210, 90

First: 500 × 0.40 = 200. Remaining 300. Second: 300 × 0.70 = 210. Neither: 90. The second percentage is conditional on reaching the second site, not on the initial 500.

32. Numerical

Of 240 complexes at an upstream AUG, 25% initiate there. Of the bypassing complexes, 80% initiate at the downstream AUG. What percentage of all successful initiations occurs downstream?

  1. 60%
  2. 80%
  3. 70.6%
  4. 75%
Answer and explanation

C. 70.6%

Upstream = 60, downstream = 180 × 0.8 = 144. Successful total = 204. Downstream share = 144/204 × 100 ≈ 70.6%; 60% is the share of all arrivals.

33. Numerical

A mutant changes recruitment from 4 to 2 complexes per minute per mRNA and conditional start recognition from 0.50 to 0.90. Survival to the site remains 0.75. What is the mutant-to-control initiation-rate ratio?

  1. 0.90
  2. 1.80
  3. 0.50
  4. 1.125
Answer and explanation

A. 0.90

Control = 4 × 0.75 × 0.50 = 1.5. Mutant = 2 × 0.75 × 0.90 = 1.35. Ratio = 0.90. Recognition improves, but recruitment falls.

34. Numerical

In a stipulated initiation cycle, charging consumes one ATP to AMP, eIF2 and eIF5B each hydrolyse one GTP, and RNA remodelling uses four ATP to ADP. How many nucleotide triphosphate molecules and high-energy phosphate equivalents are used?

  1. 7 molecules, 8 equivalents
  2. 8 molecules, 8 equivalents
  3. 6 molecules, 7 equivalents
  4. 7 molecules, 7 equivalents
Answer and explanation

A. 7 molecules, 8 equivalents

There are 1 + 2 + 4 = 7 molecules. Charging costs two equivalents, so the equivalent total is 2 + 2 + 4 = 8. This is an assumed remodelling cost, not a universal stoichiometry.

35. Numerical

AUG positions are 31, 46 and 62 (positions of A on the same 5′→3′ RNA). Which pair shares a reading frame?

  1. 31 and 46 only
  2. 31 and 62 only
  3. 46 and 62 only
  4. All three
Answer and explanation

A. 31 and 46 only

46 − 31 = 15, divisible by three. 62 − 31 = 31 and 62 − 46 = 16, neither divisible by three. Frame sharing does not itself prove that no stop intervenes.

36. Numerical

At two in-frame AUGs at positions 16 and 43, both ORFs share a stop beginning at position 106, with no intervening stops. What are the predicted primary chain lengths?

  1. 30 and 21 residues
  2. 31 and 22 residues
  3. 90 and 63 residues
  4. 29 and 20 residues
Answer and explanation

A. 30 and 21 residues

The number of sense codons is (106 − 16)/3 = 30 or (106 − 43)/3 = 21. The stop is not translated. The upstream product has nine additional residues.

37. Numerical

In a hypothetical cap/tail experiment the protein synthesis rates per equal amount of RNA are neither=5, cap only=15, tail only=10, both=25 units. What are the additive and multiplicative expectations for both?

  1. 25 and 25
  2. 20 and 30
  3. 30 and 20
  4. 15 and 10
Answer and explanation

B. 20 and 30

Additive: 5 + (15−5) + (10−5) = 20. Multiplicative: 5 × (15/5) × (10/5) = 30. The observed 25 exceeds additive but not multiplicative expectation.

38. Numerical

A hypothetical message has a start A at nucleotide 80 and a characteristic toeprint 16 nt downstream of it. If start selection shifts 30 nt downstream and the toeprint offset remains the same, what are the old and new mapped positions?

  1. 80 and 110
  2. 96 and 126
  3. 64 and 94
  4. 96 and 110
Answer and explanation

B. 96 and 126

The first obstruction maps to 80 + 16 = 96. The second start is 110, so its obstruction maps to 126. The toeprint is displaced from the P-site start.

39. Experimental interpretation

Capped and uncapped versions of a reporter initially have equal RNA amounts. After 30 minutes the capped RNA retains 90% and the uncapped RNA retains 10%. Protein accumulation is six-fold higher from the capped RNA. Which claim is supported?

  1. The cap increases initiation exactly six-fold
  2. The difference combines possible RNA survival and initiation effects; measure intact RNA over time and synthesis rate
  3. The uncapped RNA cannot ever recruit a ribosome
  4. The cap changed the reporter protein sequence
Answer and explanation

B. The difference combines possible RNA survival and initiation effects; measure intact RNA over time and synthesis rate

RNA abundance differs during the measurement, so the accumulated product cannot isolate initiation efficiency. Equal starting concentrations are not enough. An instantaneous or time-resolved synthesis measure normalized to intact RNA is more diagnostic.

40. Experimental interpretation

A reconstituted system lacking eIF5B produces a start-dependent toeprint and abundant 48S complexes, but almost no 80S particles or peptide. Adding wild-type eIF5B restores both. Where is the defect most directly localized?

  1. Initial cap methylation
  2. Initiator tRNA aminoacylation
  3. Productive large-subunit joining
  4. Stop-codon recognition
Answer and explanation

C. Productive large-subunit joining

The start is already occupied and the defect is rescued by the joining factor. Upstream cap and charging steps must have supplied the observed 48S complex in this system; termination is downstream of peptide formation.

41. Experimental interpretation

A GTP-hydrolysis-defective eIF5B variant allows abundant 80S assembly but remains ribosome-associated, with low first-dipeptide synthesis. Which interpretation fits best?

  1. No 40S particles exist
  2. Final factor release or maturation is impaired despite physical joining
  3. The cap became an amino acid
  4. The mutation necessarily changes the stop codon
Answer and explanation

B. Final factor release or maturation is impaired despite physical joining

The assembled particles are not automatically elongation competent. Persistent factor binding supports a late initiation defect. An eIF5B joining-only defect would instead tend to prevent formation of the 80S particle.

42. Experimental interpretation

A mutant eIF4G binds eIF4E and eIF4A normally, but cannot associate with eIF3 in a defined mammalian recruitment assay. Cap occupancy is preserved; 43S recruitment falls. What is the best interpretation?

  1. Cap binding itself is necessarily abolished
  2. The defect is consistent with loss of a recruitment bridge
  3. eIF4A must be an elongation factor
  4. The 60S peptide-transfer centre is defective
Answer and explanation

B. The defect is consistent with loss of a recruitment bridge

The preserved cap binding and ATPase partners distinguish scaffold connectivity from loss of individual activities. The observed reduction precedes start selection and does not identify a catalytic defect of the large subunit.

43. Experimental interpretation

A structured and unstructured leader share an identical strong start. Reducing eIF4A activity lowers output from the structured leader much more strongly at equal RNA abundance. What additional control most directly tests whether secondary structure contributes?

  1. Replace the reporter protein with a longer one only
  2. Disrupt base pairing in the structured leader without changing the start, and compare a compensatory stem-restoring mutant
  3. Remove the stop from both constructs
  4. Measure genomic DNA concentration only
Answer and explanation

B. Disrupt base pairing in the structured leader without changing the start, and compare a compensatory stem-restoring mutant

Disruption and compensatory restoration separate a structure-dependent effect from the exact sequence of one mutant. Protein length and DNA abundance do not isolate leader structure.

44. Experimental interpretation

An AUG→AUC change eliminates the first ribosome-dependent toeprint and produces a new toeprint near a downstream AUG. The new protein is shorter. Which condition is necessary to conclude that it is an N-terminally truncated version of the same protein?

  1. The downstream AUG is in frame with the original and shares the downstream coding region
  2. The two AUGs must have identical cap structures
  3. The new AUG must use fMet
  4. The downstream triplet must be a termination codon
Answer and explanation

A. The downstream AUG is in frame with the original and shares the downstream coding region

A shorter product can be encoded in a different frame, so length alone is insufficient. Check the frame and sequence or a common C-terminal epitope. The cap is shared by the message.

45. Experimental interpretation

Two reporter messages have the same AUG. A −3 purine-to-pyrimidine change reduces start-site occupancy; RNA concentration and protein stability are matched. What is the most parsimonious interpretation?

  1. The nucleotide changes the initiating amino acid
  2. The altered local context reduces commitment to AUG
  3. It deletes the cap chemically
  4. It creates a ribosomal RNA
Answer and explanation

B. The altered local context reduces commitment to AUG

The −3 base is outside the candidate AUG, so the anticodon match is retained. The positional readout and controls support altered start selection rather than protein instability.

46. Experimental interpretation

A +4 G→U mutation lowers reporter enzyme activity, but immunoblotting shows the same amount of newly synthesized reporter protein. What inference is strongest?

  1. Initiation is definitely ten-fold lower
  2. The coding change may alter enzyme activity without lowering initiation; verify sequence and initiation occupancy
  3. eIF4E has no function
  4. The −3 nucleotide is necessarily mutated
Answer and explanation

B. The coding change may alter enzyme activity without lowering initiation; verify sequence and initiation occupancy

The +4 base starts the second sense codon. The same protein amount but reduced activity suggests a protein property may explain the signal. Reporter activity alone is insufficient evidence of initiation.

47. Experimental interpretation

A cell extract has normal total eIF2 but little eIF2-GTP and poor ternary-complex formation. Functional eIF2B restores both. Which step is supported as limiting?

  1. GDP-to-GTP exchange on eIF2
  2. AUG anticodon synthesis by the ribosome
  3. Covalent closure of the mRNA
  4. Peptide termination
Answer and explanation

A. GDP-to-GTP exchange on eIF2

Restoration by the exchange factor and measured nucleotide state identify the active-factor supply. Normal total eIF2 does not imply normal GTP-bound eIF2.

48. Experimental interpretation

A mutation weakening eIF1-dependent start discrimination increases initiation at an upstream near-cognate triplet while reducing main-AUG product, with unchanged total mRNA. Which explanation is most consistent?

  1. The early site captures more scanning complexes
  2. The mutation eliminates every initiating complex
  3. The 60S is no longer required
  4. The RNA polymerase must transcribe backwards
Answer and explanation

A. The early site captures more scanning complexes

A fidelity change can redirect initiation rather than simply reducing total recruitment. More upstream commitment can reduce the flux reaching the main AUG.

49. Experimental interpretation

A bicistronic DNA reporter gives high second-reporter activity after insertion of a candidate IRES. RNA analysis detects a shorter transcript carrying only the second reporter. What should be concluded?

  1. Internal initiation is proven
  2. An unintended monocistronic RNA offers an alternative explanation; test intact bicistronic RNA and transcription/splicing controls
  3. The first reporter cannot have been translated
  4. The IRES must be an enzyme
Answer and explanation

B. An unintended monocistronic RNA offers an alternative explanation; test intact bicistronic RNA and transcription/splicing controls

A shortened RNA can recruit at its own end. The original result cannot distinguish that mechanism from true internal entry. RNA integrity and promoter/splicing controls are necessary.

50. Experimental interpretation

A cap-binding perturbation lowers the first reporter of a bicistronic RNA while the second remains active. Both coding regions are detected on full-length RNA. Which is the most careful conclusion?

  1. The second product is relatively resistant to this perturbation; test reinitiation and internal entry before assigning a mechanism
  2. All eukaryotic translation is cap independent
  3. The second reporter must be transcribed independently
  4. No ribosomal factors are needed
Answer and explanation

A. The second product is relatively resistant to this perturbation; test reinitiation and internal entry before assigning a mechanism

Resistance to a particular cap perturbation is evidence about dependence, not definitive proof of the route. Full-length RNA detection helps but does not alone exclude all alternative origins or reinitiation.

51. Analytical reasoning

A short uORF precedes a main AUG. A mutation abolishes the uORF start; main product rises. Which model is compatible without invoking changed transcription?

  1. More scanning complexes bypass the former competing start and reach the main AUG
  2. The mutant necessarily prevents every initiation event
  3. The upstream ORF is now translated twice
  4. The main AUG no longer has a reading frame
Answer and explanation

A. More scanning complexes bypass the former competing start and reach the main AUG

The mutation removes a competing recognition site. If RNA abundance is unchanged, altered initiation flux is a plausible explanation. Reinitiation could modify the quantitative response in the original RNA.

52. Analytical reasoning

A cell is exposed to an intervention that selectively stops new initiation while leaving elongation unchanged. After enough time for existing ribosomes to finish, which result is expected?

  1. Existing ribosomes accumulate forever on every RNA
  2. Ribosome occupancy and new peptide synthesis generally decline as existing ribosomes run off
  3. Every message is immediately destroyed
  4. The ribosomal small subunit becomes bacterial
Answer and explanation

B. Ribosome occupancy and new peptide synthesis generally decline as existing ribosomes run off

No new entrants replace the completing ribosomes. A transient output can remain immediately after intervention, but occupancy and synthesis decline after runoff; the model assumes RNA stability is unchanged.

53. Experimental interpretation

A toeprint near AUG appears in both the complete reaction and the no-ribosome RNA control, at comparable intensity. Which next step is most justified?

  1. Declare a 48S complex at that position
  2. Test whether RNA structure or reverse transcriptase pausing explains the band and seek a ribosome-dependent signal
  3. Count the band as a peptide
  4. Assume the 60S has joined
Answer and explanation

B. Test whether RNA structure or reverse transcriptase pausing explains the band and seek a ribosome-dependent signal

A structure-dependent stop could explain both bands. A ribosome-dependent additional signal and suitable controls are needed to infer a bound complex; a band alone does not establish its composition.

54. Analytical reasoning

An upstream in-frame AUG produces a longer protein containing a targeting sequence. A context mutation favours a downstream AUG and protein shifts from an organelle to the cytosol. Which mechanistic connection best explains the change?

  1. The mutation forces the mature ribosome into the organelle
  2. The shorter primary product may lack the amino-terminal targeting information
  3. The downstream AUG must encode a stop
  4. PABP attaches a targeting peptide to the cap
Answer and explanation

B. The shorter primary product may lack the amino-terminal targeting information

Alternative initiation changes the amino-terminal coding segment. Loss of a targeting signal can alter localization without changing transport machinery itself. Test the new product's amino terminus and restore the signal to strengthen causality.

55. Experimental interpretation

A purified unstructured RNA supports scanning in a defined assay lacking eIF4A and added ATP-dependent remodelling factors, whereas a matched structured RNA fails. Which conclusion is valid?

  1. eIF4A is unnecessary in every cell
  2. The defined unstructured substrate can reduce the requirement for ATP-dependent RNA remodelling in this assay
  3. All cellular leaders are unstructured
  4. eIF4E necessarily performs peptide-bond catalysis
Answer and explanation

B. The defined unstructured substrate can reduce the requirement for ATP-dependent RNA remodelling in this assay

The comparison establishes conditional sufficiency for the tested system. Cellular messages and accessory interactions vary. It is not a general claim of in vivo dispensability.
