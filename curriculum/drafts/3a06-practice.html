15. Part B — Concepts and assertion–reason

Original questions, not previous-year papers. Attempt each before opening the explanation.

B1. Which property identifies an autonomous replicon?

  1. It is always circular
  2. It has a functional system for initiating its own DNA copying
  3. It encodes all enzymes it uses
  4. It must encode an essential cellular gene
Answer and reasoning

Answer: B. An autonomous replication unit has its own initiation system. Circularity, complete biochemical independence and essential gene content are not defining properties.

B2. Which component generally acts in cis?

  1. Diffusible Rep protein
  2. Host helicase
  3. Required local origin sequence
  4. DNA polymerase
Answer and reasoning

Answer: C. The origin is needed on the molecule that will initiate. The other choices are proteins that can act on suitable substrates.

B3. In canonical ColE1 initiation, what directly supplies the precursor of the DNA primer?

  1. RNA I
  2. RNA II
  3. A nick in both DNA strands
  4. A terminal protein
Answer and reasoning

Answer: B. RNA II forms the productive RNA–DNA hybrid and is processed. RNA I inhibits this route; terminal protein priming and double-strand cleavage are different mechanisms.

B4. Which activity is most directly associated with negative control in ColE1?

  1. RNA I interfering with RNA II
  2. DNA ligase sealing daughter DNA
  3. Pol I extending a primer
  4. Helicase unwinding a fork
Answer and reasoning

Answer: A. RNA I intercepts the primer precursor. The other activities support copying or completion rather than the characteristic negative feedback.

B5. What distinguishes a theta intermediate from the simplest rolling-circle intermediate?

  1. All theta products are single stranded
  2. Theta requires synthesis in the 3′→5′ direction
  3. A duplicated duplex arc rather than an extended displaced tail
  4. Theta is restricted to linear chromosomes
Answer and reasoning

Answer: C. Theta intermediates contain a growing duplicated duplex region. Rolling circles have displaced-strand tails. Both synthesize DNA 5′→3′ and theta is familiar in circular replicons.

B6. Which description of unidirectional theta is correct?

  1. It lacks all replication junctions
  2. One active fork advances around the circle
  3. It must produce long ssDNA concatemers
  4. It requires a nick-based primer
Answer and reasoning

Answer: B. A theta bubble may have one progressing fork. A displaced tail or a nick-dependent start is not required.

B7. Which chemical group is extended after origin-specific nicking in a typical rolling-circle plasmid?

  1. Free 5′ phosphate
  2. Free 3′ hydroxyl
  3. A protein-bound 5′ phosphate as a conventional primer
  4. A free base without a sugar
Answer and reasoning

Answer: B. The nick supplies a 3′-OH for polymerase. The 5′ end is not the conventional extension site.

B8. What is the usual function of an sso in a rolling-circle plasmid?

  1. Initiation of complementary-strand synthesis on ssDNA
  2. Segregation of chromosome copies
  3. RNA I inhibition
  4. Packaging of a bacterial ribosome
Answer and reasoning

Answer: A. An sso acts on the single-stranded replication product. The other options concern different processes.

B9. What is a concatemer?

  1. A protein complex that binds the origin
  2. A molecule containing repeated genome-length units in series
  3. A single origin with no flanking DNA
  4. An obligatory double-strand break
Answer and reasoning

Answer: B. Concatemers contain repeated DNA sequence units. They are distinct from protein assemblies and do not require every repeat to have a free end.

B10. What does a partition system principally control?

  1. The 5′→3′ polarity of polymerization
  2. The cellular allocation of replicated molecules
  3. The base-pairing rules
  4. The length of all DNA primers
Answer and reasoning

Answer: B. Partition concerns inheritance through spatial allocation; it does not change polymerase chemistry or base pairing.

B11. What is the defining outcome of incompatibility?

  1. A second plasmid can never enter
  2. Two plasmids cannot replicate even separately
  3. Unstable joint maintenance despite individual maintenance
  4. The two plasmids must have identical coding genes
Answer and reasoning

Answer: C. Joint persistence fails under suitable conditions. Entry exclusion, inability to replicate alone and identical coding content are not the definition.

B12. Which is a classical episome example?

  1. A nonreplicating DNA fragment
  2. F factor capable of free and integrated states
  3. An RNA primer
  4. A free nucleotide
Answer and reasoning

Answer: B. The F factor can be maintained independently or integrated into the chromosome. The alternatives cannot illustrate both states.

B13. Which enzyme is central to mammalian mitochondrial DNA synthesis?

  1. DNA polymerase gamma
  2. Bacterial DNA polymerase III
  3. Nuclear RNA polymerase II
  4. Peptidyl transferase
Answer and reasoning

Answer: A. Pol γ is the major mitochondrial DNA polymerase; the other choices are bacterial or nuclear enzymes or a ribosomal activity.

B14. In the classical mitochondrial model, which parental strand templates new heavy-strand synthesis?

  1. Parental heavy strand
  2. Parental light strand
  3. Both parental strands in one polymerase active site
  4. A mature rRNA
Answer and reasoning

Answer: B. A new heavy strand is complementary to the parental light strand. The name OriH refers to the strand synthesized.

B15. What is 7S DNA in the mitochondrial D-loop?

  1. A complete extra mitochondrial genome
  2. A short DNA strand associated with local displacement
  3. A nuclear chromosome
  4. A mitochondrial ribosomal protein
Answer and reasoning

Answer: B. A short third DNA strand can displace a parental strand locally. It is not proof of a completed extra genome.

B16. Which statement about plant organellar DNA is most defensible?

  1. Circular maps prove exclusively circular molecules
  2. All plastids use only rolling-circle replication
  3. Circular, linear and branched DNA forms can occur
  4. No nuclear proteins are involved
Answer and reasoning

Answer: C. Physical DNA forms vary. A reference map does not establish molecular conformation, and organelles rely on nuclear gene products.

B17. Which sequence describes the representative lambda replication program?

  1. Linear virion DNA → circle → early theta → later concatemers
  2. RNA → protein → DNA without copying
  3. Linear DNA → no replication → packaging
  4. Single-stranded RNA → theta
Answer and reasoning

Answer: A. Lambda circularizes and commonly uses early theta replication followed by production of concatemeric DNA; the other alternatives change its genome type or omit replication.

B18. Which distinguishes phiX174 from a conventional dsDNA plasmid at entry?

  1. It has no genetic material
  2. It enters with ssDNA and establishes a duplex replicative form
  3. It requires nuclear telomerase
  4. It initially contains RNA
Answer and reasoning

Answer: B. The incoming ssDNA genome must acquire a complement. Neither telomerase nor an RNA genome describes phiX174.

B19. Which is an example of a protein-primed linear DNA system?

  1. ColE1
  2. Adenovirus
  3. Mammalian mitochondrial OriL
  4. Bacterial chromosomal oriC
Answer and reasoning

Answer: B. Adenovirus uses a terminal protein precursor in initiation. The other examples use nucleic-acid-associated priming routes.

B20. Which observation alone is least sufficient to prove complete replication?

  1. Detection of a short nascent origin-associated DNA strand
  2. Detection of two separated complete duplex products together with synthesis labeling
  3. Repeated autonomous maintenance after confirmed copying
  4. Sequence and topology verified for daughter copies
Answer and reasoning

Answer: A. Short products can represent abortive initiation. The other observations provide much stronger evidence of complete products or maintenance.

B21. A: A plasmid can depend on host polymerase yet constitute an autonomous replicon. R: Autonomy in this context refers to its initiation unit, not biochemical self-sufficiency.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R explains A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A
  3. A is true, R is false
  4. A is false, R is true
Answer and reasoning

Answer: A. Both are true; the reason explains the distinction.

B22. A: Two plasmids with different replication origins must always coexist stably. R: Shared partition determinants can cause incompatibility.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R explains A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A
  3. A is true, R is false
  4. A is false, R is true
Answer and reasoning

Answer: D. The assertion is false and the reason is true: independent origins are not a guarantee of independent segregation.

B23. A: A high-copy plasmid may be lost even if DNA synthesis occurs normally. R: Copies can be clustered or allocated unequally.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R explains A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A
  3. A is true, R is false
  4. A is false, R is true
Answer and reasoning

Answer: A. Both are true and clustering or unequal allocation directly explains loss despite synthesis.

B24. A: The conventional mammalian mitochondrial strand-displacement model is identical to rolling-circle replication. R: Both can contain displaced parental DNA.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R explains A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A
  3. A is true, R is false
  4. A is false, R is true
Answer and reasoning

Answer: D. The assertion is false. The reason is true, but shared displacement does not establish the same priming or product geometry.

B25. A: A marker retained after chromosome integration does not prove autonomous plasmid replication. R: Chromosomal forks can copy the integrated marker.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R explains A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A
  3. A is true, R is false
  4. A is false, R is true
Answer and reasoning

Answer: A. Both statements are true and the reason directly explains the assertion.

B26. A: A circular plasmid may use one or two progressing theta forks. R: All plasmid origins encode a dedicated Rep protein.

  1. Both A and R are true, and R explains A
  2. Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A
  3. A is true, R is false
  4. A is false, R is true
Answer and reasoning

Answer: C. The assertion is true and the reason is false; ColE1 is a counterexample to the universal claim.

16. Part C — Analytical and experimental reasoning

All numerical data and experimental cases below are hypothetical educational examples. Explain the assumptions before solving.

C1. A 15 kb circular replicon has two forks moving at 500 and 750 bp/min. Predict completion time and meeting positions.

Answer and reasoning

12 min; 6 kb and 9 kb from the origin on opposite arcs. T=15,000/(500+750)=12 min. The two distances sum to the genome length. Equal-distance convergence would require equal speeds.

C2. A 9 kb circle uses one active theta fork at 450 bp/min. A second model uses two equal forks at the same speed. Compare traversal times.

Answer and reasoning

20 min versus 10 min. One fork traverses all 9,000 bp. Two forks cover 900 bp/min together. Neither result includes initiation or product processing.

C3. A connected theta intermediate derived from a 10 kb circle contains 16 kb duplex-equivalent DNA. Find the copied fraction.

Answer and reasoning

60%. L(1+f)=16 kb, so f=16/10−1=0.6. It does not mean 1.6 physically independent plasmids.

C4. A continuous strand-displacement polymerase copies a hypothetical 4 kb circular template at 300 nt/min for 30 min. How many complete lengths and residual nucleotides?

Answer and reasoning

Two complete lengths plus 1,000 nt. Total synthesis=9,000 nt; divide by 4,000. These are nucleotides, not necessarily base pairs.

C5. A 7 kb duplex plasmid gives one additional complete duplex. Approximate number of new DNA nucleotide residues?

Answer and reasoning

14,000. Both parental strands are copied over 7,000 positions. A single new strand would supply only half this count.

C6. A culture has 20 genome equivalents per cell. Each copy yields an average 0.6 additional copies per generation, with equal partition in the mean. After three generations?

Answer and reasoning

10.24 equivalents per cell. The per-generation multiplier is (1+0.6)/2=0.8. 20×0.8³=10.24; it is a mean, not an individual count.

C7. For k(n)=0.8−0.025n per hour and growth rate 0.3 per hour, find the positive steady copy abundance and response at n=16.

Answer and reasoning

20; abundance increases at n=16. Set k=0.3: n=0.5/0.025=20. At 16, k=0.4, greater than dilution. This is a local model.

C8. A cell has six independent copies before division. Probability a specified daughter receives none? Probability either daughter is empty?

Answer and reasoning

1/64 and 1/32. The first probability is 2⁻⁶; the mutually exclusive empty-left and empty-right outcomes sum to 2⁻⁵.

C9. For random allocation of 16 copies, calculate mean and standard deviation of the number in a specified daughter.

Answer and reasoning

Mean 8, standard deviation 2. Binomial N=16, p=0.5: mean Np=8, variance Np(1−p)=4. Standard deviation is 2.

C10. Eight genome equivalents are either eight separate molecules or four linked pairs that segregate as units. Compare empty-daughter probabilities.

Answer and reasoning

1/256 versus 1/16; sixteenfold difference. The relevant N is the number of independent units, not sequence equivalents. The four particles produce a 16-fold higher modeled risk.

C11. Half of cells have one independent copy and half have five. What is the mean probability that a specified daughter lacks the plasmid?

Answer and reasoning

17/64, or 26.5625%. Average the conditional probabilities: (1/2)(1/2)+(1/2)(1/32)=17/64. Substitution of mean count 3 would incorrectly yield 1/8.

C12. A lineage loses a plasmid with probability 0.1 per generation. With no reacquisition and equal growth, what fraction remains after four generations?

Answer and reasoning

65.61%. Retention is 0.9⁴=0.6561. Population selection or changing loss rates would violate this simple model.

C13. A cell has three A copies and two B copies independently allocated. Chance a specified daughter receives both identities?

Answer and reasoning

21/32, or 65.625%. P(A present)=7/8, P(B present)=3/4; multiply under independence. The total number five alone is insufficient.

C14. A plasmid marker to chromosome reference ratio is 15. Cells average two reference copies. The plasmid marker is present twice per intact plasmid. Estimate genome equivalents.

Answer and reasoning

15 per cell. There are 15×2=30 marker copies per cell; divide by two markers per complete plasmid. Fragmentation or integration would complicate inference.

C15. One 3 Mb chromosome and 30 copies of a 10 kb plasmid are complete. What fraction of represented DNA mass is plasmid?

Answer and reasoning

About 9.09%. Plasmids contribute 300 kb out of 3,300 kb, assuming equal mass per bp. Molecule fraction is a different quantity.

C16. In an ideal quantitative amplification assay a marker threshold occurs four cycles earlier than the reference. With equal doubling efficiencies, estimate the starting ratio.

Answer and reasoning

16-fold. Each cycle corresponds to a factor of two. The ratio 2⁴ estimates sequence abundance, not physical plasmid number without further assumptions.

C17. In a hypothetical organellar strand-displacement model, the second origin is exposed after 9 kb at 450 nt/min. The first 15 kb strand takes the same speed; second-strand speed is 600 nt/min. Earliest completion time?

Answer and reasoning

45 min after initial start. Delay=20 min; first strand=33.33 min; second=25 min after its start, ending at 45. The later completion constrains duplex completion.

C18. A plastid single-copy locus is detected 80 times and an inverted-repeat locus 160 times. What is the simplest genome-equivalent interpretation?

Answer and reasoning

80 complete equivalents under the intact-genome assumption. The repeat marker is present twice per genome. Equal corrected estimates are consistent with intact molecules, but do not prove topology.

C19. A 180 kb hypothetical concatemer contains full 35 kb repeats from its starting boundary. Maximum complete units?

Answer and reasoning

Five with 5 kb residual. 180/35=5 remainder 5. Particle yield can be lower and requires appropriate processing and packaging.

C20. Mutant I is rescued by supplying an initiator gene in trans; mutant O is not. Initial entry and host growth match. Which assignment is supported?

Answer and reasoning

I is a trans-acting initiator defect; O is a required local origin defect. Rescue distinguishes diffusible activity from a cis sequence under the specified controls. It does not identify every participating host protein.

C21. A ColE1-like origin produces normal RNA II transcripts, but origin hybrids and nascent DNA are low. Name two distinct hypotheses and a discriminating observation.

Answer and reasoning

Defective RNA II hybrid formation or excessive RNA I inhibition; compare RNA II hybrid formation under matched inhibition and primer precursor interaction. Normal transcription excludes only a major transcription deficit. A processing defect is another alternative if hybrids form normally but usable primer is absent.

C22. A rolling-circle sample accumulates displaced circular ssDNA while duplex products decline. Cleavage and first-strand synthesis remain normal. Which stage is implicated?

Answer and reasoning

Second-strand initiation or completion. The first-strand steps occur; conversion of ssDNA to duplex is impaired. This is not evidence of a complete block at dso cleavage.

C23. Two distinct-origin plasmids individually persist, but when co-resident their total copy number remains normal and one identity is lost. Both use the same partition machinery. Which interpretation is favored?

Answer and reasoning

Partition incompatibility is plausible. Independent origins do not exclude shared segregation. Matched copy counts and daughter distributions would help distinguish it from residual replication cross-control.

C24. In cells carrying a neutral plasmid, DNA synthesis per retained cell is unchanged, but cells without plasmids expand faster. Explain a falling population retention fraction.

Answer and reasoning

Selection can change prevalence independently of molecular synthesis. Relative growth changes population composition. A retention fraction is not a direct measurement of initiation rate or segregation loss.

C25. A gel shows two bands from a DNA preparation. The two forms become the same length when interpreted as linear molecules. What is a plausible explanation?

Answer and reasoning

Different topological forms of equal sequence length. Open circular and supercoiled forms can differ in mobility. Band migration alone is not a reliable molecular length estimate.

C26. A mapped fragment produces a Y pattern on a replication-intermediate gel while another fragment produces a bubble pattern. What follows?

Answer and reasoning

The former is consistent with fork passage; the latter supports initiation inside that fragment. The Y pattern does not establish that the whole replicon has one fork. The analysis has fragment-level resolution.

C27. Statements: (I) All rolling-circle replication yields infectious virions. (II) A displaced strand may later become duplex. (III) Both theta and rolling-circle DNA synthesis proceed 5′→3′. (IV) All theta systems have two moving forks. Which are correct?

Answer and reasoning

II and III only. I confuses a replication geometry with viral assembly; IV excludes unidirectional theta incorrectly.

C28. Match A: RNA I; B: ParB; C: TWINKLE; D: terminal protein. Functions: 1 mitochondrial helicase; 2 antisense inhibition; 3 protein priming; 4 centromere-like site binding.

Answer and reasoning

A–2, B–4, C–1, D–3. Each pair identifies a distinct level of replicon maintenance. A primer inhibitor, partition protein, helicase and protein primer are not interchangeable.

C29. Statements: (I) A free F element can initiate its own maintenance replication. (II) Integrated F sequence can be copied by chromosomal forks. (III) An integrated marker proves an active separate plasmid. (IV) Integration and replication are identical processes. Which are correct?

Answer and reasoning

I and II only. III confuses sequence presence with autonomous form; IV confuses DNA rearrangement with DNA synthesis.

C30. A mitochondrial sample has normal nuclear DNA synthesis, reduced new mtDNA incorporation, unchanged initial mtDNA count and later mtDNA depletion. What inference is best supported and what is not established?

Answer and reasoning

Reduced mitochondrial synthesis is supported; a specific Pol γ mutation is not established. The time order fits reduced synthesis followed by depletion. Primer formation, helicase, polymerase or substrate limitations could explain it. Independent turnover and organelle-mass measurements would strengthen the conclusion.
