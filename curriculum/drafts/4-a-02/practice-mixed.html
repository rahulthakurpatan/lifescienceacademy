Mixed-format practice

41. Assertion–Reason — Assertion: A tyrosine-derived hormone can signal through a nuclear receptor. Reason: T3 acts predominantly through thyroid hormone receptors after cellular uptake.

  1. Both are true and the reason explains the assertion.
  2. Both are true, but the reason does not explain the assertion.
  3. Assertion true, reason false.
  4. Assertion false, reason true.
Answer and explanation

A. T3 is a tyrosine-derived iodothyronine and its canonical receptor is nuclear; the reason provides a direct example explaining the assertion. Epinephrine is a counterexample to treating all tyrosine-derived hormones alike.

42. Assertion–Reason — Assertion: A surface receptor can induce delayed transcriptional responses. Reason: Some surface receptor pathways activate intracellular transcription factors.

  1. Both are true and the reason explains the assertion.
  2. Both are true, but the reason does not explain the assertion.
  3. Assertion true, reason false.
  4. Assertion false, reason true.
Answer and explanation

A. JAK-associated cytokine receptors exemplify this causal relationship through STAT activation. The external ligand need not enter the nucleus to alter transcription.

43. Assertion–Reason — Assertion: A cell with normal hormone binding can have defective hormone signaling. Reason: The equilibrium dissociation constant alone measures all downstream catalytic and coupling functions.

  1. Both are true and the reason explains the assertion.
  2. Both are true, but the reason does not explain the assertion.
  3. Assertion true, reason false.
  4. Assertion false, reason true.
Answer and explanation

C. Recognition and downstream coupling are separable. Kd is an equilibrium binding parameter and cannot measure kinase activity, G-protein coupling or transcriptional competence.

44. Multiple statements — Select all correct statements: I. Insulin receptor beta subunits carry kinase domains. II. Growth hormone receptor has intrinsic receptor tyrosine kinase activity. III. NPR-A forms cGMP from GTP. IV. Ligand-gated channels directly synthesize ATP.

  1. I and III only
  2. I, II and III only
  3. II and IV only
  4. III and IV only
Answer and explanation

A. I and III are correct. Growth hormone receptor requires associated JAK, and ion channel gating changes permeability without direct ATP synthesis.

45. Multiple statements — Select the correct statements: I. The ligand-binding and DNA-binding domains of a nuclear receptor are distinct. II. All nuclear receptors must enter the nucleus only after ligand binding. III. TR/RXR can occupy an element before ligand binding. IV. Coregulator recruitment can change without a large change in DNA occupancy.

  1. I and II only
  2. II and III only
  3. I, II, III and IV
  4. I, III and IV only
Answer and explanation

D. II is false: some nuclear receptor complexes are already DNA-associated. I, III and IV are consistent with nuclear receptor architecture and transcriptional regulation.

46. Matching — Match (P) insulin receptor, (Q) growth hormone receptor, (R) NPR-A, (S) nicotinic acetylcholine receptor with (1) associated kinase, (2) ion pore, (3) intrinsic tyrosine kinase, (4) guanylyl cyclase.

  1. P–1, Q–3, R–4, S–2
  2. P–3, Q–1, R–2, S–4
  3. P–3, Q–1, R–4, S–2
  4. P–4, Q–2, R–1, S–3
Answer and explanation

C. The insulin receptor phosphorylates tyrosines, growth hormone receptor couples to JAK, NPR-A produces cGMP and nicotinic receptors directly gate a pore. The alternative pairings swap these class-defining functions.

47. Matching — Match (P) T3, (Q) ANP, (R) glucagon, (S) TGF-beta with (1) GPCR, (2) nuclear receptor, (3) receptor guanylyl cyclase, (4) receptor serine/threonine kinase.

  1. P–2, Q–3, R–1, S–4
  2. P–3, Q–2, R–4, S–1
  3. P–2, Q–1, R–3, S–4
  4. P–4, Q–3, R–2, S–1
Answer and explanation

A. Canonical T3 action is mediated by nuclear TR, ANP by NPR-A, glucagon by a GPCR and TGF-beta by intrinsic type I/II receptor kinases.

48. Numerical — In an unsaturated carrier model, total carrier is 1,900 nM, Kc = 100 nM and total hormone is 2 nM. Assume free intracellular ligand equals free extracellular ligand and receptor Kd = 0.2 nM. What is receptor fractional occupancy?

  1. Approximately 0.05
  2. Approximately 0.333
  3. Approximately 0.50
  4. Approximately 0.90
Answer and explanation

B. Free fraction = 1/(1+1900/100)=0.05, giving free ligand = 0.1 nM. Occupancy = 0.1/(0.2+0.1)=1/3. The free fraction is not the receptor occupancy. This is an explicitly simplified hypothetical model.

49. Experimental interpretation — Hypothetical reporter values (relative units): normal receptor + hormone = 8, mutant receptor + hormone = 1, normal receptor without hormone = 1, mutant receptor + downstream bypass = 7. Both receptors bind ligand equally. What is most justified?

  1. The mutant is defective between ligand recognition and the bypassed step, but the exact molecular lesion is unresolved.
  2. The mutant lacks all ligand binding.
  3. The downstream reporter is incapable of responding.
  4. Bypass restored every normal receptor function.
Answer and explanation

A. Binding is preserved, while bypass almost restores the measured reporter. These observations locate an upstream functional defect but cannot distinguish trafficking, coupling or catalysis unless additional measurements are supplied.

50. Integrative reasoning — A candidate hormone receptor is a single-pass membrane protein. Ligand raises intracellular cGMP. A mutation in its cytoplasmic catalytic site abolishes cGMP synthesis by the isolated domain without altering surface binding. Which interpretation best integrates topology and biochemical evidence?

  1. A conventional GPCR directly converts GTP into cGMP.
  2. A nuclear hormone receptor regulates cGMP only by new transcription.
  3. An ion channel necessarily produces cGMP as its first product.
  4. A receptor guanylyl cyclase directly couples extracellular recognition to intracellular nucleotide cyclization.
Answer and explanation

D. Single-pass topology alone is nonspecific, but direct catalytic conversion and mutation evidence support receptor cyclase activity. G-protein switching, transcription and ion permeation are different molecular processes.
