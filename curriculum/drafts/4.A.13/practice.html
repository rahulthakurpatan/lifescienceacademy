Part B — Conceptual questions

Original practice questions; these are not official previous-year questions.

1. What is the immediate quantity detected by a receptor in a classical extracellular autoinducer system?

  1. The absolute total number of bacteria regardless of volume
  2. The instantaneous division rate of every producer
  3. The locally accessible concentration of cognate signal
  4. The total mass of extracellular matrix
Answer and explanation

C. The locally accessible concentration of cognate signal — Receptors interact with available ligand. Density, volume and retention affect concentration, but none is counted directly.

2. Which molecular activity is associated with LuxI in the classical V. fischeri model?

  1. Synthesis of an acyl-homoserine lactone
  2. Binding the lux box as a transcriptional regulator
  3. Phosphorylation of a response-regulator aspartate
  4. Catalysis of photon emission
Answer and explanation

A. Synthesis of an acyl-homoserine lactone — LuxI generates the AHL. LuxR controls the promoter, AgrC exemplifies a kinase, and LuxA/LuxB form luciferase.

3. The lux box is best described as which entity?

  1. An extracellular peptide-binding domain
  2. A bacterial membrane transport channel
  3. The chemical lactone ring of AHL
  4. A cis-acting regulatory DNA element
Answer and explanation

D. A cis-acting regulatory DNA element — The lux box is a DNA site for regulation; it is neither a receptor nor the ligand.

4. What does inclusion of luxI in an induced lux operon accomplish?

  1. It makes signal synthesis independent of metabolic precursors
  2. It enables reinforcement of signal synthesis after receptor activation
  3. It eliminates signal degradation
  4. It causes LuxI to bind DNA instead of LuxR
Answer and explanation

B. It enables reinforcement of signal synthesis after receptor activation — The induced synthase can increase production; precursor availability and signal losses still matter.

5. Which combination describes the classical AHL structure?

  1. A linear DNA oligonucleotide with a fatty acid end
  2. A homoserine lactone linked to an acyl group through an amide
  3. A phosphorylated histidine linked to ribose
  4. A polysaccharide carrying an adenine base
Answer and explanation

B. A homoserine lactone linked to an acyl group through an amide — AHLs contain a cyclic ester and an N-acyl substituent; the other structures are not AHLs.

6. Which statement about short-chain 3-oxo-C6-HSL transport is appropriate?

  1. Every molecule must be exported by a universal ATP-driven AHL pump
  2. The signal is permanently confined to the producing cell
  3. It can move only when bound to LuxR
  4. Diffusive equilibration can contribute to membrane crossing
Answer and explanation

D. Diffusive equilibration can contribute to membrane crossing — The classical short-chain AHL can diffuse; transport mechanisms vary among AHLs.

7. Which enzyme activity directly produces bacterial light in the classical lux system?

  1. Luciferase formed by LuxA and LuxB
  2. LuxR binding to AHL
  3. LuxI synthesis of AHL
  4. LuxS production of DPD
Answer and explanation

A. Luciferase formed by LuxA and LuxB — LuxA/B participate in the light reaction; the remaining proteins perform signaling or metabolic roles.

8. Which is a necessary substrate of the bacterial luciferase reaction?

  1. Extracellular ATP as the direct photon-producing substrate
  2. A peptide autoinducer
  3. Molecular oxygen
  4. A phosphorylated response regulator
Answer and explanation

C. Molecular oxygen — Oxygen, reduced FMN and aldehyde support the bacterial reaction; ATP is not the direct photon-emitting substrate in this overall reaction.

9. In the S. aureus agr example, which gene encodes the peptide precursor?

  1. agrC
  2. agrA
  3. agrB
  4. agrD
Answer and explanation

D. agrD — AgrD is the precursor, AgrB participates in maturation/export, AgrC is the sensor, and AgrA regulates transcription.

10. Which agr protein detects extracellular cognate AIP through a membrane-associated sensor?

  1. AgrC
  2. AgrD
  3. AgrA
  4. RNAIII
Answer and explanation

A. AgrC — AgrC is the histidine kinase. AgrA is downstream, AgrD is precursor, and RNAIII is a regulatory RNA.

11. Which molecular event directly connects a typical activating peptide sensor kinase to its response regulator?

  1. AHL-dependent attachment of the sensor to DNA
  2. Transport of the entire receptor into the nucleus
  3. Phosphoryl transfer from histidine to aspartate
  4. Transfer of an amino acid from a charged tRNA
Answer and explanation

C. Phosphoryl transfer from histidine to aspartate — The two-component relay uses His-to-Asp phosphotransfer, not nuclear transport or translation.

12. Which statement about agr P2 and P3 is correct?

  1. P2 and P3 are different extracellular peptides
  2. P2 controls core agr genes and P3 drives RNAIII expression
  3. P2 encodes LuxI and P3 encodes LuxR
  4. Both are proteins that hydrolyze AHL
Answer and explanation

B. P2 controls core agr genes and P3 drives RNAIII expression — P2 and P3 are promoters, with distinct transcriptional outputs.

13. Which reaction is catalyzed by LuxS?

  1. Conversion of ATP to cAMP
  2. Acylation of LuxR with a fatty acid
  3. Conversion of S-ribosylhomocysteine to homocysteine and DPD
  4. Opening the lactone ring of AHL
Answer and explanation

C. Conversion of S-ribosylhomocysteine to homocysteine and DPD — LuxS functions in the Pfs–LuxS metabolic route; its DPD product gives rise to AI-2 forms.

14. What was established by comparing ligand-bound LuxP and LsrB structures?

  1. AI-2 is always a boron-containing peptide
  2. All bacteria contain the same intracellular AHL receptor
  3. LuxS is a membrane sensor kinase
  4. Distinct DPD-derived chemical forms can be recognized by different receptors
Answer and explanation

D. Distinct DPD-derived chemical forms can be recognized by different receptors — LuxP recognizes a borated form, whereas Salmonella LsrB recognizes a nonborated form.

15. What is the appropriate interpretation of finding luxS in a bacterial genome?

  1. It proves universal interspecies signaling
  2. It suggests capacity for a relevant metabolic reaction but does not establish AI-2 reception
  3. It proves the organism has a LuxI–LuxR circuit
  4. It shows that all its biofilm genes are induced by AI-2
Answer and explanation

B. It suggests capacity for a relevant metabolic reaction but does not establish AI-2 reception — Production and reception are different capabilities; metabolic function is an alternative explanation.

16. Which change increases first-order active signal loss without directly blocking signal synthesis?

  1. Hydrolysis by an AHL lactonase
  2. Deletion of the AHL receptor alone
  3. Increased reporter stability
  4. Increased production of LuxI
Answer and explanation

A. Hydrolysis by an AHL lactonase — Lactonase opens the lactone bond. Receptor loss may indirectly affect feedback, but is not direct signal hydrolysis.

17. What distinguishes a classic chemotaxis response from a classic LuxI–LuxR response?

  1. Chemotaxis uses no receptors
  2. Quorum sensing always requires direct cell contact
  3. Every quorum-sensing signal is a nutrient attractant
  4. Chemotaxis adjusts movement in response to changing chemical exposure; Lux signaling regulates expression in response to a population-associated cue
Answer and explanation

D. Chemotaxis adjusts movement in response to changing chemical exposure; Lux signaling regulates expression in response to a population-associated cue — Both use receptors, but their information and classic outputs differ.

18. Which statement about Gram-positive peptide signaling is accurate?

  1. Every peptide crosses the lipid membrane by free diffusion
  2. Membrane sensor kinases are common, but some peptides are imported and recognized internally
  3. All Gram-positive bacteria use AgrC specifically
  4. Peptides cannot mediate intercellular signaling
Answer and explanation

B. Membrane sensor kinases are common, but some peptides are imported and recognized internally — Different peptide circuits use different reception architectures; agr is one named example.

19. How should the relationship between quorum sensing and biofilms be described?

  1. It may affect attachment, matrix or dispersal depending on organism and conditions
  2. It invariably increases attached biomass
  3. It is required for every biofilm
  4. It always kills dispersed cells
Answer and explanation

A. It may affect attachment, matrix or dispersal depending on organism and conditions — The regulated output differs among systems; the agr dispersal example illustrates why the direction is not universal.

20. Which statement about a Hill exponent obtained from a reporter fit is correct?

  1. It always equals the number of ligands bound to a receptor
  2. It is the dissociation constant in nanomolar units
  3. It describes effective response steepness, not necessarily receptor-binding stoichiometry
  4. It must be exactly one in bacterial systems
Answer and explanation

C. It describes effective response steepness, not necessarily receptor-binding stoichiometry — The exponent is dimensionless and can reflect network properties as well as binding.

Part C — Analytical questions

Original practice questions; these are not official previous-year questions.

21. In the minimal Lux model, a nonpolar luxI mutant responds to externally supplied AHL. Which conclusion is strongest?

  1. The luxI deletion has been genetically repaired
  2. Production is bypassed while reception and reporter regulation remain functional
  3. LuxR is unnecessary for reporter expression
  4. The mutant now necessarily produces its own AHL at wild-type rate
Answer and explanation

B. Production is bypassed while reception and reporter regulation remain functional — The response tests downstream competence; it does not restore the deleted synthase gene.

22. A luxR mutant produces less extracellular AHL than wild type despite an intact luxI coding region. Which model explains this?

  1. LuxR is the enzyme that directly synthesizes all AHL
  2. AHL cannot chemically exist without bound LuxR
  3. Loss of receptor-dependent positive feedback reduces induced luxI expression
  4. The observation proves luxI has a second deletion
Answer and explanation

C. Loss of receptor-dependent positive feedback reduces induced luxI expression — A reception defect can secondarily reduce production by interrupting feedback.

23. A strain has normal quorum-responsive transcription but little natural light. Which comparison would best distinguish output failure from signaling failure?

  1. Compare luciferase capacity and relevant substrate availability with the promoter readout
  2. Measure only extracellular AHL once
  3. Repeat the same light assay without normalization
  4. Count luxR genes without measuring their function
Answer and explanation

A. Compare luciferase capacity and relevant substrate availability with the promoter readout — A normal regulatory readout with reduced light points toward the output layer; a relevant enzyme/metabolic measurement tests that possibility.

24. A conditioned medium increases a reporter, but it also changes pH. What is the most appropriate conclusion?

  1. A specific AHL has been identified solely by this observation
  2. Direct cell contact is required
  3. The reporter necessarily measures receptor affinity
  4. An extracellular influence is plausible; signal-specific evidence requires suitable matched controls
Answer and explanation

D. An extracellular influence is plausible; signal-specific evidence requires suitable matched controls — pH and other medium changes are confounders. A cell-free effect rules out obligatory donor contact in that comparison, not all alternative mechanisms.

25. Two cultures have equal viable-cell density and production rate; one loses signal four times as rapidly. Under constant-density steady-state assumptions, what follows?

  1. The faster-loss culture has one quarter of the steady signal
  2. The faster-loss culture has four times the signal
  3. Both have identical concentrations because density is equal
  4. The receptors must differ in affinity
Answer and explanation

A. The faster-loss culture has one quarter of the steady signal — A*=qρ/k. With numerator unchanged, increasing k fourfold reduces A* fourfold.

26. A culture has source 18 nM/h and k=0.3/h. Its starting signal is zero. What is the steady-state concentration?

  1. 5.4 nM
  2. 18 nM
  3. 60 nM
  4. 6 nM
Answer and explanation

C. 60 nM — Divide 18 nM/h by 0.3/h to obtain 60 nM. Multiplication is dimensionally wrong.

27. For A*=40 nM and k=0.5/h, at what approximate time from zero signal is A=20 nM?

  1. 0.50 h
  2. 2.77 h
  3. 20 h
  4. 1.39 h
Answer and explanation

D. 1.39 h — At half-steady-state exp(-kt)=0.5, so t=ln(2)/0.5=1.386 h. 2.77 h is the time to reach 30 nM.

28. A receptor follows independent-site occupancy with Kd=12 nM. At free A=36 nM, what fraction is bound?

  1. 0.25
  2. 0.75
  3. 0.33
  4. 3.0
Answer and explanation

B. 0.75 — θ=36/(12+36)=0.75. Occupancy is bounded by zero and one.

29. Two reporters have identical K=10 nM but h=1 and h=2. At A=5 nM, which pair is correct (h=1, h=2)?

  1. 1/2 and 1/2
  2. 2/3 and 4/5
  3. 1/3 and 1/3
  4. 1/3 and 1/5
Answer and explanation

D. 1/3 and 1/5 — The first gives 5/15, the second 25/(100+25). The midpoint would be identical at 10 nM.

30. A dose-response curve shifts rightward but retains the same maximum. What does this alone demonstrate?

  1. A proven increase in the receptor's molecular Kd
  2. Reduced effective sensitivity of the measured system
  3. Deletion of every downstream output enzyme
  4. A necessary decrease in maximal reporter capacity
Answer and explanation

B. Reduced effective sensitivity of the measured system — The shift requires more signal for equal output; affinity, accessibility and regulatory changes remain possible explanations.

31. A peptide precursor transcript is normal but mature extracellular signal is low. Which defect is most directly compatible?

  1. Increased occupancy of the lux box by LuxR
  2. An obligatory increase in mature peptide stability
  3. Failure of peptide processing or export
  4. An increase in signal-independent reporter brightness
Answer and explanation

C. Failure of peptide processing or export — Mature signal requires events after transcription, so normal precursor RNA cannot exclude impaired maturation or export.

32. In a simplified AgrC–AgrA pathway, the receptor lacks its kinase activity but the mature peptide is present. Which response is expected through this pathway?

  1. Reduced AgrA phosphorylation and reduced AgrA-dependent induction
  2. Normal phosphotransfer solely because ligand is present
  3. Direct peptide binding to the agr promoter
  4. Replacement of AgrC by the peptide itself
Answer and explanation

A. Reduced AgrA phosphorylation and reduced AgrA-dependent induction — Ligand recognition does not supply missing catalytic phosphotransfer. Other pathways are excluded by the stated model.

33. A luxS mutant shows an altered metabolic phenotype and an altered signal-responsive reporter. DPD restores the reporter but not metabolism. Which interpretation is best?

  1. Both defects must be caused only by lost communication
  2. LuxS cannot be involved in metabolism
  3. The reporter defect has a signal-dependent component while the metabolic defect is not corrected by extracellular signal
  4. DPD has repaired the chromosomal luxS deletion
Answer and explanation

C. The reporter defect has a signal-dependent component while the metabolic defect is not corrected by extracellular signal — Extracellular signal bypasses the signaling deficit but does not restore the missing intracellular metabolic reaction.

34. In the classical V. harveyi AI-2 branch with other inputs fixed, which change accompanies high ligand?

  1. Reduced relay-dependent LuxO phosphorylation
  2. Obligatory activation of LuxQ kinase output
  3. Direct AI-2 binding to V. fischeri LuxR
  4. Conversion of LuxO into an AHL synthase
Answer and explanation

A. Reduced relay-dependent LuxO phosphorylation — Ligand favors phosphatase over kinase activity in this branch; the direction is the key distinction from AgrC.

35. An uptake-responsive AI-2 gene becomes induced while extracellular AI-2 activity declines. Which explanation is compatible?

  1. The two observations are logically incompatible
  2. Increased uptake/processing can remove signal from the exterior
  3. The cells must have stopped all gene expression
  4. The signal must have become an AHL
Answer and explanation

B. Increased uptake/processing can remove signal from the exterior — An uptake feedback loop can increase response while depleting the extracellular pool; reduced synthesis is another possible cause.

36. A fixed-density culture has 80 nM steady signal before treatment. The treatment halves effective synthesis and doubles loss. What is the predicted new steady state?

  1. 40 nM
  2. 80 nM
  3. 160 nM
  4. 20 nM
Answer and explanation

D. 20 nM — A* is proportional to synthesis and inverse loss. Multiplying by 0.5/2 gives one quarter, or 20 nM.

37. A stable protein starts at 100 units and production stops. For γ=0.2/h, how much remains after five hours?

  1. About 81.9 units
  2. About 36.8 units
  3. Exactly 0 units
  4. About 63.2 units
Answer and explanation

B. About 36.8 units — P=100 exp(-1)=36.8. 63.2 is the amount lost rather than remaining.

38. A population with 25% producers has equal total density and per-producer output to an all-producer control. If feedback and uptake differences are excluded, what is the signal ratio?

  1. 1.00
  2. 4.00
  3. 0.50
  4. 0.25
Answer and explanation

D. 0.25 — The source term is fρq; with constant k, the steady-state ratio is the producer fraction.

39. A treatment reduces both a quorum-responsive reporter and an unrelated reporter, while growth also declines. What conclusion is warranted?

  1. A specific receptor antagonist has been proven
  2. The signal synthase is the only possible target
  3. Selective quorum inhibition is not established
  4. The treatment necessarily increases autoinducer production
Answer and explanation

C. Selective quorum inhibition is not established — General cellular or reporter effects remain plausible. Selectivity requires additional evidence.

40. The signal concentration falls rapidly but an output protein stays high for an hour. Which inference is valid?

  1. Slow turnover can explain persistent protein without persistent receptor activation
  2. Receptor activity must remain at its maximum
  3. Signal removal has failed by definition
  4. The pathway cannot have negative regulation
Answer and explanation

A. Slow turnover can explain persistent protein without persistent receptor activation — A stable downstream protein has its own dynamics. Its abundance is not an instantaneous receptor-activity measurement.

Mixed-format examination practice

Original practice questions; these are not official previous-year questions.

41. Assertion–Reason: A: Equal cell density does not ensure equal extracellular autoinducer concentration. R: Signal retention and degradation can differ between environments.

  1. Both true, R does not explain A
  2. A true, R false
  3. A false, R true
  4. Both true, R explains A
Answer and explanation

D. Both true, R explains A — The mass balance includes removal and effective volume, making density insufficient.

42. Assertion–Reason: A: In a minimal LuxI–LuxR system, exogenous AHL can rescue a nonpolar luxI mutant. R: LuxI is the receptor responsible for transcriptional activation.

  1. Both true, R explains A
  2. Both true, R does not explain A
  3. A true, R false
  4. A false, R true
Answer and explanation

C. A true, R false — LuxI is synthase; intact LuxR is the receptor. Supplying ligand bypasses synthesis.

43. Assertion–Reason: A: A steep reporter response proves three ligands bind per receptor when fitted h=3. R: Feedback and multistep regulation can alter effective steepness.

  1. A false, R true
  2. A true, R false
  3. Both true, R explains A
  4. Both false
Answer and explanation

A. A false, R true — An empirical Hill exponent cannot establish binding stoichiometry. Feedback is one possible source of steepness.

44. Statements: I. LuxI forms AHL. II. AgrD is a precursor. III. LuxS acts on S-ribosylhomocysteine. IV. Every AI-2 receiver uses LuxR as its direct receptor. Which are correct?

  1. I and IV only
  2. I, II and III only
  3. II, III and IV only
  4. All four
Answer and explanation

B. I, II and III only — I–III correctly identify production roles. IV ignores LuxP and LsrB examples.

45. Statements: I. An extracellular signal can bypass a production defect. II. A missing receptor is normally restored simply by adding ligand. III. A downstream enzyme defect may preserve promoter activation. IV. Light output always equals transcription. Which are correct under the minimal model?

  1. I and III only
  2. II and IV only
  3. I, II and III only
  4. III and IV only
Answer and explanation

A. I and III only — Ligand cannot replace receptor; light is also constrained by enzyme and substrate availability.

46. Statements: I. AgrC is a sensor kinase. II. LuxPQ in V. harveyi favors phosphatase activity at high AI-2. III. Every ligand-bound sensor kinase must increase downstream phosphorylation. Which are correct?

  1. I and III only
  2. II and III only
  3. All three
  4. I and II only
Answer and explanation

D. I and II only — The LuxPQ example directly contradicts III.

47. Matching: 1 LuxI; 2 LuxR (V. fischeri); 3 AgrA; 4 LuxS. Roles: p AHL synthesis; q ligand-responsive transcription regulator; r peptide pathway response regulator; s DPD production.

  1. 1-q, 2-p, 3-s, 4-r
  2. 1-p, 2-q, 3-r, 4-s
  3. 1-s, 2-r, 3-p, 4-q
  4. 1-r, 2-s, 3-q, 4-p
Answer and explanation

B. 1-p, 2-q, 3-r, 4-s — LuxI/LuxS generate different signals; LuxR and AgrA are distinct regulatory proteins.

48. Matching: 1 AgrD; 2 AgrB; 3 AgrC; 4 RNAIII. Roles: p peptide precursor; q precursor processing/export; r sensor kinase; s regulatory RNA.

  1. 1-r, 2-p, 3-q, 4-s
  2. 1-q, 2-r, 3-s, 4-p
  3. 1-p, 2-q, 3-r, 4-s
  4. 1-s, 2-q, 3-p, 4-r
Answer and explanation

C. 1-p, 2-q, 3-r, 4-s — The four components map to production, maturation, reception and downstream regulation respectively.

49. Numerical: In a well-mixed culture qρ=24 nM/h, k_d=0.2/h and Q/V=0.4/h. What is A*?

  1. 120 nM
  2. 60 nM
  3. 40 nM
  4. 14.4 nM
Answer and explanation

C. 40 nM — Effective k=0.2+0.4=0.6/h. A*=24/0.6=40 nM. The alternatives omit exchange or misuse multiplication.

50. Numerical: With D=200 µm²/s in a simple 3D diffusion model, estimate the time for RMS displacement 120 µm.

  1. 12 s
  2. 0.6 s
  3. 72 s
  4. 24 s
Answer and explanation

A. 12 s — t=L²/(6D)=14400/1200=12 s. This is a characteristic estimate, not a deterministic travel time.

51. Data interpretation (hypothetical): WT light=800, biomass=200; mutant light=600, biomass=100. Which statement is correct?

  1. WT has twice the normalized activity
  2. Both have equal normalized activity
  3. The mutant has twice the total light
  4. The mutant has 1.5 times the light per biomass unit of WT
Answer and explanation

D. The mutant has 1.5 times the light per biomass unit of WT — WT=4 light units/biomass; mutant=6; ratio=1.5. Total light and normalized activity answer different questions.

52. Integrative: A signal-responsive reporter is activated in a recipient that produces no signal; donor cells are physically separated by a barrier permeable to small molecules. What does this support?

  1. Recipient signal synthesis is essential for reception
  2. A diffusible extracellular cue can transmit information without recipient synthesis or direct contact
  3. The donor necessarily transfers its genome to the recipient
  4. The recipient's response proves that all bacteria use the same receptor
Answer and explanation

B. A diffusible extracellular cue can transmit information without recipient synthesis or direct contact — The barrier and production defect separate communication from contact and recipient synthesis; the result does not establish universal receptor identity.
