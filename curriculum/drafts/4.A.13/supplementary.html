Examination traps and misconceptions

The quorum is a fixed number of cells.
A threshold is usually defined by concentration or response; density contributes to signal production but volume and loss change exposure.
All dense populations are necessarily induced.
A receptor may be absent, the synthase inactive, the signal removed rapidly, or another required input missing.
All growth-phase-dependent transcription is quorum sensing.
Nutrients, pH, oxygen and stress also change during growth. Causal receptor-dependent evidence is needed.
LuxI binds the promoter and LuxR makes the ligand.
LuxI is the AHL synthase. LuxR recognizes the signal and influences transcription.
AHL is the light-emitting molecule.
The AHL regulates gene expression; luciferase catalyzes the actual light-producing reaction.
AHLs and AIPs are chemically equivalent.
AHLs are N-acyl lactones, whereas AIPs are processed peptides; their synthesis and transport differ.
Every Gram-positive peptide sensor is AgrC.
AgrC belongs to a named example; other systems use different sensors or intracellular receptors following import.
Every histidine kinase responds to ligand by raising downstream phosphorylation.
The LuxPQ AI-2 branch can favor phosphatase activity at high ligand; follow the named circuit.
AI-2 means one invariant molecular structure for every bacterium.
DPD gives rise to multiple interconverting forms; LuxP and LsrB recognize different derivatives.
Deleting luxS proves a signaling defect alone.
LuxS has a metabolic role. Use receptor-dependent readouts and signal supplementation to separate effects.
Kd always equals the reporter EC50.
Kd describes equilibrium binding in a defined model; a functional midpoint depends on the wider regulatory system.
A Hill exponent equals a ligand count.
Effective response steepness can reflect several regulatory events and feedback.
Positive feedback always produces bistability.
Bistability requires the appropriate nonlinear source–loss relationships and stability conditions.
Quorum sensing always increases biofilm biomass.
Some circuits favor dispersal or different developmental stages; the effect is organism-specific.
Quorum quenching necessarily kills bacteria.
Reducing active signal or recognition does not by itself imply loss of viability.

Revision, active recall and mastery

Essential definitions

Autoinducer
A bacterial signaling substance participating in a regulated response through specific detection.
Quorum sensing
Regulation through production and detection of extracellular population-associated chemical cues.
AHL
N-acyl homoserine lactone; a family of nonpeptide signaling compounds.
AIP
Autoinducing peptide; in agr, a processed signal derived from AgrD.
AI-2
A family of related DPD-derived molecules recognized by particular receptors.
Positive feedback
A response reinforces an upstream input or activity; the reinforced variable must be identified.
Quorum quenching
Interference with signal activity or recognition, which is not necessarily bactericidal.

Core mechanism sequence

Production → release or access → accumulation versus loss → receptor recognition → regulatory transduction → specific output. In the classical Lux system, the regulated operon includes luxI, connecting the output to increased synthesis.
