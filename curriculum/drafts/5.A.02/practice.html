Part B — Conceptual questions

Original NET/SET-style practice; not attributed to past examinations.

1. A prospective fate is best described as:

  1. Every possible outcome of a cell
  2. The typical future contribution under specified normal conditions
  3. Its current differentiated phenotype
  4. A permanent molecular determinant
Answer and explanation

B. Normal prospective fate concerns what descendants usually become. A describes potential, C describes current state and D confuses an outcome with a causal component.

2. A fate map is primarily organized by:

  1. The chromosomal positions of genes
  2. The ancestry of genes across species
  3. Spatial positions of embryonic cells at a defined stage
  4. The molecular mass of differentiation markers
Answer and explanation

C. The early embryo supplies the coordinate system. Gene mapping, phylogeny and protein mass do not identify spatial tissue contributions.

3. A lineage tree primarily depicts:

  1. Parent–descendant relationships
  2. The physical distance between mature cells
  3. The current concentration of a morphogen
  4. The total genome size
Answer and explanation

A. Branches represent division histories. Position, morphogen concentration and genome size need separate measurements.

4. Which statement about a single-cell clone is correct?

  1. All cells of a clone must remain adjacent
  2. All cells of a clone must differentiate alike
  3. Every fluorescent patch is a clone
  4. Its cells share a defined founder
Answer and explanation

D. A clone is defined by ancestry. Descendants may migrate or differentiate differently, and one patch may contain several separately marked founders.

5. A region labeled prospective neural tissue on an early fate map:

  1. Must already contain neurons
  2. Normally contributes to neural derivatives
  3. Cannot produce another fate after grafting
  4. Must be a single clone
Answer and explanation

B. The label summarizes future contribution. Neither current differentiation, restricted potential nor clonality follows from the map.

6. Vogt's classical work is especially associated with:

  1. Vital-dye mapping of amphibian embryos
  2. Nuclear transplantation in frogs
  3. DNA sequencing of nematodes
  4. Cre-mediated DNA recombination
Answer and explanation

A. Vogt marked living embryonic regions with dyes. Nuclear transfer, sequencing and recombinase systems are different approaches.

7. Conklin's classical ascidian yellow crescent is associated principally with:

  1. All larval sensory neurons
  2. The entire larval endoderm
  3. Primary tail muscle precursors
  4. Only adult germ cells
Answer and explanation

C. The classic association is with early primary muscle precursors. The other alternatives misassign the yellow cytoplasmic domain.

8. The yellow pigment in a natural marker establishes:

  1. The pigment must be the causal regulator
  2. The correlation of marked material with lineage history
  3. All descendants are irreversibly determined
  4. The nuclear genomes are different
Answer and explanation

B. A visible marker helps follow material. Causality, stable commitment and differences in DNA require independent evidence.

9. The early C. elegans founder that gives rise to intestine is:

  1. AB
  2. MS
  3. D
  4. E
Answer and explanation

D. E produces intestinal descendants. AB has diverse somatic outputs, MS contributes mesodermal derivatives and D contributes body-wall muscle.

10. In the canonical nematode early tree, E and MS are daughters of:

  1. EMS
  2. P2
  3. AB
  4. P4
Answer and explanation

A. EMS divides into MS and E. P2 produces C and P3; AB is a separate somatic branch; P4 gives rise to germline precursors.

11. P4 in C. elegans produces:

  1. AB and EMS
  2. MS and E
  3. Z2 and Z3
  4. C and D
Answer and explanation

C. The two primordial germ cells Z2 and Z3 arise from P4. The other pairs belong to earlier or distinct branches.

12. The major advantage of a quail–chick graft as a classical lineage method is:

  1. The two species have identical-looking nuclei
  2. Donor cells can be recognized by distinctive markers
  3. Grafts always develop autonomously
  4. No controls are required
Answer and explanation

B. Species-associated cellular features identify the donor. Identical appearance would defeat this purpose, and autonomous behavior and control-free interpretation are not guaranteed.

13. An orthotopic graft places tissue:

  1. In the equivalent anatomical location
  2. In a different region by definition
  3. Only into an older host
  4. Only into a neutral culture medium
Answer and explanation

A. Orthotopic refers to the equivalent site. Heterotopic refers to different sites, heterochronic to different stages, and culture isolation is not a host graft.

14. An inducible permanent genetic lineage reporter is intended to:

  1. Measure only current driver expression
  2. Suppress all cell divisions
  3. Mark cells within a defined activation window and their descendants
  4. Guarantee that every positive cell belongs to one clone
Answer and explanation

C. A pulse limits the marking interval and inherited recombination can preserve the record. Direct reporters measure expression, and clonal resolution needs sparse validated marking.

15. In an ideal Cre-loxP stop-cassette reporter, Cre activity:

  1. Destroys all reporter mRNA permanently
  2. Removes a flanked stop sequence when sites have suitable orientation
  3. Labels only cells that never divide
  4. Replaces the entire genome
Answer and explanation

B. Appropriately oriented sites permit excision. This allows reporter expression without replacing the genome or restricting marking to nondividing cells.

16. A direct expression reporter disappears as its promoter becomes inactive. This most directly indicates:

  1. The original lineage has died
  2. The reporter signal is no longer detectable
  3. All cells have changed lineage
  4. The embryo has become mosaic
Answer and explanation

B. The direct observation concerns detectability, not ancestry or survival. Protein turnover can explain signal loss without any loss of cells.

17. A tracer that passes into neighboring unrelated cells can produce:

  1. A false-positive lineage assignment
  2. A proof of determination
  3. A proof of genomic equivalence
  4. An increase in embryonic potency by definition
Answer and explanation

A. Label leakage can make unrelated cells look like descendants. The other conclusions do not follow from marker transfer.

18. The principal reason to include unlabeled control embryos is to:

  1. Make all embryos clonal
  2. Prove the tracer is a determinant
  3. Measure baseline development and background signal
  4. Increase the number of divisions in the marked clone
Answer and explanation

C. Unlabeled controls establish normal development and background. They do not manipulate clone ancestry, prove causality or increase proliferation.

19. A specified cell is classically identified by its behavior in:

  1. A suitable neutral environment
  2. Any hostile environment
  3. A mature adult organ only
  4. A DNA sequence alignment
Answer and explanation

A. Specification concerns retention of an expected fate in permissive neutral conditions. The alternatives do not test that property.

20. A normal fate map alone cannot demonstrate:

  1. The typical origin of a tissue
  2. Which marked region contributes to a later tissue
  3. That a cell is unable to adopt any alternative fate
  4. The normal relationship between early position and later derivative
Answer and explanation

C. The map can establish normal contributions. Excluding alternative developmental outcomes requires additional tests.

21. In the canonical C. elegans hermaphrodite somatic lineage, the adult contains approximately:

  1. 131 somatic nuclei
  2. 959 somatic nuclei
  3. 1,090 surviving germline nuclei
  4. Exactly 1,031 somatic nuclei
Answer and explanation

B. The canonical adult hermaphrodite has 959 somatic nuclei; 131 is the number of programmed deaths and 1,031 is the canonical adult male somatic-nucleus count.

22. Differential interference contrast microscopy is useful for:

  1. Following visible nuclei and cell divisions in living transparent embryos
  2. Proving a particular transcription factor is active without a molecular assay
  3. Automatically producing a complete adult fate map from one snapshot
  4. Editing genes
Answer and explanation

A. DIC provides optical contrast for live observation. It does not directly measure gene expression, infer missing history or edit DNA.

23. Which is an example of a heterochronic graft?

  1. Dorsal tissue moved to ventral side at the same stage
  2. A marked patch left undisturbed
  3. Tissue from an early donor placed into a later-stage host
  4. A single cell labeled with dye
Answer and explanation

C. Heterochronic means donor and recipient differ in developmental age. The first is heterotopic; the other two do not involve age-mismatched transplantation.

24. Programmed cell death should be represented in a lineage tree because:

  1. Every daughter cell must die
  2. A terminal missing cell proves no precursor ever existed
  3. Deaths increase clone size
  4. A cell can be generated normally but later removed
Answer and explanation

D. A death branch records a real developmental event. A missing endpoint can reflect death rather than failure to generate the cell; deaths reduce cell number.

25. In a highly migratory animal tissue, a single clone may:

  1. Occupy separated locations
  2. Never divide
  3. Be recognizable by location alone
  4. Contain cells with unrelated ancestors by definition
Answer and explanation

A. Descendants can migrate apart. Clones may divide, their identity requires ancestry evidence and unrelated founders are not one clone.

Part C — Analytical questions

26. A labeled region normally forms skin. After transfer into a suitable neural region, viable labeled descendants become neurons. Which interpretation is strongest?

  1. The fate map was false
  2. The donor was not irreversibly restricted to its normal skin outcome in this test
  3. All cells in the embryo are totipotent
  4. The neurons must be host cells
Answer and explanation

B. Donor labeling identifies their contribution in the alternative environment. Normal mapping can still be correct; one response does not establish totipotency, and donor markers argue against a purely host origin.

27. A marked founder undergoes four synchronized binary divisions without loss. How many terminal cells and individual mitoses are recorded?

  1. 4 cells, 4 mitoses
  2. 16 cells, 4 mitoses
  3. 16 cells, 15 mitoses
  4. 8 cells, 7 mitoses
Answer and explanation

C. Four rounds yield 2⁴ = 16 cells; every mitosis adds one cell, so 15 events occurred. Four is branch depth, and eight corresponds to three rounds.

28. One founder undergoes 18 individual mitoses and 4 deaths, with no fusion or immigration. How many intact living descendants remain?

  1. 15
  2. 14
  3. 19
  4. 23
Answer and explanation

A. 1 + 18 − 4 = 15. Fourteen omits the initial founder; nineteen ignores deaths; twenty-three incorrectly adds losses.

29. A nonrenewed dye begins at 800 integrated units per founder, with equal partition. The reliable threshold is 25 units. After which division is each cell first below threshold?

  1. 3
  2. 4
  3. 5
  4. 6
Answer and explanation

D. After five divisions the signal is 800/32 = 25; after six it is 12.5. The other options are at or above the threshold.

30. Twenty of 80 marked descendants enter a tissue containing 200 total cells. What are the marked-descendant fraction entering the tissue and the tissue's marked fraction, respectively?

  1. 10%, 25%
  2. 25%, 10%
  3. 20%, 80%
  4. 40%, 25%
Answer and explanation

B. 20/80 = 25%; 20/200 = 10%. Reversing denominators gives the first option; the remaining values use incorrect populations.

31. In a tissue transplant, a new neural structure consists entirely of host-marker-positive cells. Donor cells survive nearby. What does the lineage evidence support?

  1. Direct donor production of every neural cell
  2. No possible donor influence
  3. An indirect influence on host development is consistent with the result
  4. Donor cells must be pluripotent
Answer and explanation

C. Host identity excludes a purely donor-derived neural structure. Induction is plausible but needs appropriate controls; survival nearby does not establish donor potency.

32. An early region labeled in 12 embryos contributes to both muscle and skin. Initially 20 cells per embryo were marked. Which additional observation would establish realized dual fate from one founder?

  1. A larger tissue graft
  2. A brighter dye
  3. A map with two colors for regions
  4. Validated single-cell labeling followed by both differentiated descendants
Answer and explanation

D. A single marked founder producing both identities establishes clonal output. Broader marking, brightness or regional colors cannot exclude separate restricted founders.

33. Early and late donor grafts are placed into equivalent viable hosts. Early: 30 of 40 change fate; late: 8 of 40 change fate. Which statement is justified?

  1. Observed responsiveness decreases from 75% to 20%
  2. All late cells are determined in every environment
  3. The host definitely lost its signal with age
  4. Early donor cells are totipotent
Answer and explanation

A. The proportions are 30/40 and 8/40. Host stage is controlled, and the change supports donor-state differences; it does not establish absolute determination or totipotency.

34. A mutant has half as many marked muscle descendants as control. All remaining cells express muscle markers. Which measurement best distinguishes decreased proliferation from increased death?

  1. A larger endpoint image
  2. Time-resolved division and death counts
  3. The number of different colors in the fate map
  4. The DNA mass of a mature muscle cell alone
Answer and explanation

B. A lineage movie with event counts directly distinguishes production and loss. Endpoint appearance, color and DNA mass do not provide the necessary history.

35. A recombinase driver is active in early population A and later in unrelated population B. A constitutive permanent reporter labels mature cells from both. What is the primary concern?

  1. The reporter cannot be inherited
  2. Every positive cell is currently expressing the driver
  3. The mark cannot uniquely identify early population A
  4. The reporter makes all cells totipotent
Answer and explanation

C. Independent activation creates multiple origins. Permanent labels can persist after driver shutoff, so neither current expression nor potency follows.

36. A 50% efficient random lineage label marks 30 cells in a later organ. Under representative marking and equal behavior assumptions, estimate the originating population's total contribution.

  1. 15
  2. 30
  3. 45
  4. 60
Answer and explanation

D. 30/0.5 = 60 under the assumptions. Fifteen multiplies by efficiency; thirty ignores incompleteness; forty-five is not the appropriate correction.

37. A recombinase-negative reporter control shows bright fluorescence in cells of interest. What is the priority?

  1. Investigate background or reporter leak before assigning ancestry
  2. Claim the cells are a clone
  3. Assume the fluorescence proves neural induction
  4. Ignore the control if the experimental group is brighter
Answer and explanation

A. Unexpected labeling undermines specificity. The remaining choices make lineage or causal claims without establishing a valid marker.

38. Assertion: A highly reproducible lineage may depend on inductive interactions. Reason: Neighboring cells can provide reproducible cues at each developmental stage. Choose the relation.

  1. Both true; reason explains assertion
  2. Both true; reason does not explain assertion
  3. Assertion true, reason false
  4. Assertion false, reason true
Answer and explanation

A. Regular environmental signals can generate consistent lineage outcomes, so the reason explains the assertion. The other combinations misclassify the truth or connection.

39. Assertion: Dye dilution can generate false-negative fate-map data. Reason: A nonrenewed tracer can fall below detection as cells divide. Choose the relation.

  1. Assertion false, reason true
  2. Both true; reason explains assertion
  3. Both true; reason unrelated
  4. Both false
Answer and explanation

B. Dilution can reduce detectability despite survival, directly explaining false negatives. Neither statement is false or unrelated.

40. In a removal experiment, tissues T and U disappear. Ordinary tracing shows removed cells contributed only to T. A replacement signal restores U but not T. Which conclusion best fits?

  1. The removed cells directly supplied both tissues
  2. U cannot be affected by signals
  3. The removed population directly contributed to T and was required indirectly for U under the test
  4. The restoration proves all embryonic fates are fixed
Answer and explanation

C. Normal lineage and selective rescue separate contribution from signaling. The other choices contradict the tracing, rescue or scope of the observation.

41. Three marked founders yield 2, 20 and 80 cells. All 102 descendants are pooled. Which is the relevant independent sample size when comparing reproducibility among founders?

  1. 102
  2. 80
  3. 20
  4. 3
Answer and explanation

D. There are three independently marked founders, assuming they are independent experimental units. Descendants share ancestry and are not 102 independent founder replicates.

42. A permanent lineage mark labels 12 cells in an organ; 6 also express a mature neuronal marker. What is directly established if the marker is reliable?

  1. At least six traced descendants show the neuronal marker
  2. All organ neurons arose from the traced population
  3. The six remaining cells are definitely dead
  4. The initial founder was totipotent
Answer and explanation

A. Co-labeling identifies six traced neuronal-marker-positive cells. The total organ origin, identity of the other six and developmental potential are not established.

43. Which matching set is entirely correct?

  1. Vogt—nuclear transfer; Conklin—Cre; Sulston—vital dyes
  2. Conklin—ascidian lineage; Vogt—amphibian vital dyes; Sulston—nematode lineage
  3. Sulston—quail chimera; Vogt—ascidian pigment; Le Douarin—nematode lineage
  4. Le Douarin—Cre; Conklin—mammalian cloning; Vogt—DNA barcoding
Answer and explanation

B. The second set connects each investigator to their classical work. All other sets misassign at least one technique or organism.

44. A movie shows two differently labeled cells cross in position. Which rule preserves the lineage interpretation?

  1. Relabel each cell according to its new position
  2. Assume that cells which cross become sisters
  3. Follow the individual marks and division continuity
  4. Identify ancestry solely by mature tissue identity
Answer and explanation

C. Cell identity should be followed over time. Position and phenotype can change, and crossing does not generate a shared immediate parent.

45. A labeled precursor forms six cells in control. In mutant all six survive, but one differentiation marker is absent and no alternative markers are tested. Which conclusion is strongest?

  1. The lineage is completely absent
  2. A neural fate switch is proven
  3. The cells are certainly dead
  4. A differentiation or marker-expression defect is possible; fate switch is unproven
Answer and explanation

D. Survival and cell count exclude a simple loss of the lineage. A missing marker does not by itself identify an alternative phenotype or its cause.

46. At a graft endpoint 15 of 20 survivors adopt the host fate, out of 40 original attempts. What are the response among survivors and success among attempts?

  1. 75%, 37.5%
  2. 37.5%, 75%
  3. 50%, 75%
  4. 15%, 20%
Answer and explanation

A. 15/20 = 75%; 15/40 = 37.5%. The swapped and remaining figures use the wrong denominator.

47. A balanced clone contains eight terminal cells. A mutation inherited from just one first-generation daughter is present in all its descendants. Assuming equal divisions and no loss, how many terminal cells carry it?

  1. 2
  2. 4
  3. 7
  4. 8
Answer and explanation

B. The first-generation daughter contributes one of two equal branches, hence four of eight. Two is a later branch; seven counts division events; eight would require the mutation in the founder or both daughters.

48. A labeled early plant meristem sector expands, but some descendants adopt the identity of the adjacent layer after an altered division. What is the best interpretation?

  1. Plant fates are always unrelated to position
  2. A new layer identity proves the clone has changed ancestry
  3. Lineage and positional fate can differ despite limited cell migration
  4. Every plant cell is experimentally totipotent
Answer and explanation

C. Cell walls constrain movement, not responsiveness to position. A changed identity does not change ancestry, and no universal totipotency follows.

49. An early fate map shows two adjacent cells usually form the same tissue. A graft test changes the fate of only one. Which conclusion is supported?

  1. Their prospective fates must have been different
  2. Both are completely determined
  3. The map is invalid because it is not causal
  4. The cells can share normal fate but differ in response or commitment
Answer and explanation

D. Normal outcome and response to new context are distinct. The same normal map can accommodate different potencies or commitment states.

50. Two clones each start with one cell and end with 9 cells. Clone A shows 8 mitoses and no loss; clone B shows 12 mitoses. With no fusion or immigration, how many losses occurred in B?

  1. 4
  2. 3
  3. 8
  4. 12
Answer and explanation

A. 1 + 12 − L = 9 gives L = 4. The other options do not satisfy the cell-count relation.

51. A donor is transferred to a region thought to induce neural identity. Its descendants remain epidermal, but the host region fails to form neural tissue even in control. What should be concluded?

  1. The donor is absolutely determined
  2. The alternative environment was not validated; determination remains unresolved
  3. The donor is necessarily totipotent
  4. Neural induction was proven
Answer and explanation

B. The host competence/instructive condition is unverified, so lack of response cannot prove determination. The other conclusions are unsupported.

52. A mutation alters the expression of the promoter used to activate a permanent reporter before the labeling pulse. Endpoint labeling decreases. What is the most informative first check?

  1. Count only unlabeled mature cells
  2. Infer lineage loss immediately
  3. Measure initial driver activity and actual marking at the pulse
  4. Increase the number of later tissue types recorded
Answer and explanation

C. A change in initial labeling efficiency can mimic lineage loss. Endpoint counts alone cannot separate the effects.

53. A grafted donor marker is detected in a mature cell, but a separate assay shows donor–host cell fusion. What qualification is needed?

  1. Ignore fusion because fluorescence is permanent
  2. Declare that all nuclei in the cell descended from donor
  3. Conclude that no donor material was present
  4. The marker establishes donor contribution but not exclusive ancestry of the fused cell
Answer and explanation

D. Fusion combines cellular histories. The marker supports contribution but not exclusive lineage origin; the alternatives overstate or deny the evidence.
