Why a plant needs a steroid growth signal

A growing seedling must coordinate irreversible changes in the dimensions of cells with the changing supply of water and carbon. A cell cannot make itself permanently longer merely by accumulating water: its surrounding wall must accommodate expansion without losing mechanical strength. The plant must also decide which cells should elongate, which should divide, and which should acquire specialized functions. Brassinosteroids (BRs) contribute to these decisions by controlling the activity of regulatory proteins and, ultimately, the expression of genes in responding cells. They are not nutrients or structural building materials supplied in bulk; their importance lies in their capacity to change cellular behavior at low concentrations.

Brassinosteroids are steroidal phytohormones, meaning that their molecular skeleton is related to the sterols found in biological membranes. The four fused rings characteristic of sterol chemistry provide a starting framework for enzymatic modification. The hormone differs from the precursor in the arrangement of functional groups and in biological activity. A molecule can therefore be a good membrane constituent without being an effective BR ligand. This distinction is important because disrupting early sterol production can change both membrane properties and hormone synthesis. A phenotype in such a mutant cannot automatically be attributed entirely to a deficiency of BR.

A hormone response depends on the concentration of the bioactive molecule, the properties of its receptor, the state of intracellular regulators and the developmental competence of the tissue. The same concentration can produce different changes in two organs. A root whose elongation is limited by excess BR need not be inconsistent with a shoot whose elongation is promoted by BR. These organs have different cell types, endogenous hormone levels and constraints on growth. A useful starting question is therefore not whether a hormone universally promotes growth, but which process changes in a specified tissue when the effective signal changes.

From growth-promoting extracts to receptor genetics

The history of BR research illustrates how experimental evidence transforms an observation into a molecular explanation. Growth-promoting substances were found in pollen extracts, but the ability of an extract to increase growth does not by itself identify the active chemical. Extracts may contain many metabolites. Chemical purification followed by repeated biological assays can progressively connect a measurable response to a particular compound. In 1979, brassinolide was identified as a steroidal lactone from rapeseed pollen. Its structural identity provided a basis for testing purified material, comparing related molecules and studying biosynthetic routes.

The isolation of a growth-promoting compound did not immediately establish whether a plant depended on it during ordinary development. A stronger line of evidence came from genetic mutants. Arabidopsis plants with disrupted BR biosynthesis display characteristic growth defects, often including short hypocotyls and dwarf adult shoots. Restoration of growth by a downstream hormone can connect the defect to loss of the hormonal product. The experiment becomes more persuasive when it includes genetically normal plants, solvent controls and a range of concentrations that avoid nonspecific toxicity.

A landmark 1996 study identified the Arabidopsis bri1 mutant using a response to supplied BR. Normal seedlings showed root-growth inhibition under the assay conditions, whereas the mutant was relatively insensitive. The mutant also exhibited dwarfism and defects in reproductive and seedling development. This combination was particularly informative: adding a hormone to a plant that cannot adequately perceive it fails to correct the signaling problem. Subsequent genetic identification of BRI1 as a receptor-like kinase explained why steroid responsiveness could be lost even when hormone biosynthesis was not the primary defect.

A rescue test is an inference about a causal pathway rather than a simple label for a mutant. A plant might fail to respond because hormone delivery is ineffective, because the receptor is defective, or because a downstream regulator cannot relay the signal. The BRI1 conclusion required further molecular evidence rather than the phenotype alone. Conversely, recovery with exogenous hormone argues for an upstream limitation but does not identify a particular biosynthetic enzyme without additional information about intermediates and genotype.

Sterol origin and bioactive molecular forms

Campesterol is an important starting sterol for the production of BRs. Sterols are built from isoprenoid precursors, and their synthesis is connected to primary carbon metabolism. The BR-specific portion of metabolism modifies the sterol skeleton rather than constructing an entirely unrelated chemical framework. Several modifications introduce or alter hydroxyl groups and carbonyl-containing structures. These changes affect how the molecule is recognized by enzymes and receptors.

Castasterone and brassinolide are representative bioactive BRs. Brassinolide contains a lactone in its modified B ring. Castasterone is a ketone rather than a lactone at the corresponding position and can itself act as a biologically effective hormone. In some plant species castasterone is a major active end product, so the absence of a particular final conversion to brassinolide does not necessarily mean that the organism lacks BR signaling. The name brassinosteroid refers to the chemical and functional family; it is not synonymous with a single molecule.

A precursor-product relationship is also not necessarily a one-to-one relationship between enzyme and compound. An enzyme may accept multiple related intermediates, and different intermediates can converge on common products. For teaching purposes it is useful to arrange the major chemical changes into an ordered pathway, but a simplified sequence must not be mistaken for proof that all BR molecules pass through the same obligatory set of intermediates in all species.

DET2 catalyzes a steroid reduction, while several cytochrome P450 enzymes catalyze oxidative transformations. DWF4 (CYP90B1) is associated with hydroxylation at carbon 22. CPD (CYP90A1) participates in oxidation at carbon 3 in the established Arabidopsis pathway. ROT3 (CYP90C1) and CYP90D1 contribute to hydroxylation at carbon 23. BR6ox enzymes of the CYP85 family catalyze late C6 oxidation; Arabidopsis CYP85A2 can also catalyze conversion of castasterone to brassinolide. These are functional landmarks rather than a claim that every enzyme has a single substrate. Assigning DWF4 to C22 rather than to the final lactone-forming step is useful when interpreting the rescue of different metabolic defects.

An exam may compare an early BR biosynthetic mutant with a mutation that affects a late enzyme. Supplying an intermediate located upstream of the blocked reaction will generally not circumvent that block, provided there is no bypass pathway. Supplying an active downstream product can circumvent it if the perception system remains functional. This argument requires knowledge of pathway position and tissue access. It cannot be obtained simply by memorizing that a particular hormone promotes elongation.

Synthesis, inactivation and local movement

The concentration of active hormone at a receptor is a dynamic property. Production increases the supply of hormone; oxidation, conjugation and other inactivation routes reduce the active pool; movement can redistribute metabolites between neighboring regions. The appropriate balance is not necessarily the same in a root meristem and a mature leaf. When the amount of active hormone rises above the range that produces normal development, feedback and metabolic inactivation can limit the signal.

CYP734-family enzymes, including Arabidopsis BAS1, are examples of oxidative BR inactivation. Conjugation to sugar groups is another way to change the activity and chemical properties of a BR molecule. Glycosylation can reduce the amount immediately available to activate receptors. The biological fate of a conjugate depends on its chemistry and the enzymes present: formation of a conjugate does not prove that it serves as a readily mobilized storage pool. Avoid equating all BR conjugates with a universally reversible storage form.

Feedback links signaling to production. When the downstream BR transcriptional regulators become active, expression of certain biosynthetic genes, including DWF4 and CPD, can decline. This is a negative feedback loop: a stronger hormone response acts to limit further production of the signal. It does not imply that every target gene is repressed. The same regulatory proteins can stimulate a class of growth-associated genes while repressing genes needed for hormone production.

Many hormone pathways involve delivery of signals between distant organs, but BR homeostasis is especially associated with local production and action. Classical grafting evidence in several systems does not support substantial long-distance transport of BR as the general mechanism of regulation. Nevertheless, local does not mean strictly immobile. Evidence from Arabidopsis roots shows that BR pathway intermediates can move between neighboring cells through plasmodesmata, allowing biosynthetic reactions distributed among different cell layers to cooperate. The movement of an intermediate and the extracellular perception of active hormone are distinct mechanistic questions.

Grafting can test the hypothesis that a hormone made in one organ supplies a distant organ. A BR-deficient shoot grafted onto a normal rootstock is compared with grafted normal and deficient controls, and recovery of growth or hormone concentration is assessed. Failure of a normal rootstock to restore the shoot phenotype supports limited systemic supply under those conditions. It does not establish that no BR-related molecule ever crosses a cell boundary. The experimental distinction between movement across a few cells and movement between organs is essential.

BRI1: recognition at the plant cell surface

The steroid structure of BRs might lead a student to expect a classical intracellular steroid receptor similar to the nuclear receptors that regulate many animal responses to steroid hormones. This expectation is misleading. A common chemical ancestry does not dictate a common receptor architecture. In the canonical plant BR signaling system, the primary receptor BRI1 is a leucine-rich repeat receptor-like kinase located at the plasma membrane.

The extracellular region of BRI1 contains leucine-rich repeats and a specialized region that contributes to ligand binding. The receptor crosses the plasma membrane, and its kinase domain is on the cytoplasmic side. Ligand recognition changes the activity of the receptor complex, permitting a response in the cytoplasm without requiring the receptor itself to move into the nucleus. The membrane therefore separates the initial site of recognition from the intracellular machinery that relays the information.

BAK1, also known as SERK3, acts as a co-receptor, with related SERK family members able to contribute in appropriate contexts. Brassinolide binding to the receptor favors productive association with the co-receptor, and phosphorylation events involving their kinase domains promote signaling. BAK1 should not be treated as the only BR-specific recognition protein or as a protein dedicated exclusively to BR responses. Its participation in other plant signaling systems provides an example of how a limited set of signaling proteins can be reused in different receptor complexes.

BKI1 is a negative regulator associated with the inner side of the plasma membrane in the low-signal state. Activation of BRI1 promotes phosphorylation-dependent release of BKI1 from its inhibitory position, contributing to productive receptor signaling. BRI1-associated kinases and related cytoplasmic signaling proteins then connect receptor activation to downstream phosphatase activity. The receptor's extracellular position and cytoplasmic catalytic activity explain how it couples two physically separated compartments.

BIN2, phosphatases and transcriptional regulation

In a cell with weak BR signaling, the GSK3-like kinase BIN2 can phosphorylate the related transcription factors BES1 and BZR1. Phosphorylation is an enzymatic transfer of a phosphate group to a protein substrate, but its biological consequence depends on the target protein and the sites involved. For BES1 and BZR1, phosphorylation by BIN2 generally reduces productive BR-dependent transcriptional regulation. It can influence localization, DNA-binding capacity and stability. Phosphorylated factors can interact with 14-3-3 proteins, which contributes to their retention outside the nucleus.

After activation of BRI1 and its co-receptor, cytoplasmic kinases including BSKs and CDG1 connect the receptor to the BSU1-related phosphatase relay. A canonical model includes phosphatase-dependent inactivation of BIN2. In this context the phosphatase does not simply turn the entire pathway off; it restrains a kinase that normally inhibits positive signaling output. This arrangement is an example of a double negative. Removing an inhibitor increases the activity of the process that the inhibitor normally restrains.

Protein phosphatase 2A can directly dephosphorylate BZR1 and BES1. The balance of kinase and phosphatase activity shifts toward forms capable of regulating transcription in the nucleus. These transcription factors bind regulatory DNA sequences and cooperate with other transcriptional regulators. The molecular effect is not that a BR molecule physically stretches the wall. Rather, hormone perception changes cellular gene regulation, and downstream gene products contribute to the machinery that allows or restricts growth.

Consider a gain-of-function BIN2 variant that remains strongly active despite upstream signaling. Adding BR may activate BRI1 but will not necessarily lead to the expected accumulation of dephosphorylated BES1 or BZR1. The pathway has been interrupted downstream of ligand binding. In contrast, suppressing the inhibitory effect of BIN2 can permit downstream responses even if receptor signaling is weakened. Such genetic comparisons help establish the direction of information flow. Because several related proteins exist, phenotype severity and rescue can depend on the allele and the specific organ examined.

An immunoblot of BES1 may reveal different bands corresponding to different phosphorylation states. In a conventional gel, the less phosphorylated form often migrates faster. A relative increase in the faster form after hormone treatment is consistent with pathway activation, particularly if phosphatase treatment supports the assignment. Band mobility alone, however, is not definitive evidence of protein identity or exact phosphorylation site. Total protein loading, antibody specificity and untreated controls must be considered.

From transcription to expansion and tissue differentiation

Expansion of a plant cell is an irreversible increase in size, distinct from cell division. Water uptake supplies turgor pressure against the wall, and wall extensibility determines whether this force can produce lasting deformation. BR-dependent transcription influences growth-associated proteins and enzymes that alter wall properties, including selected expansins and wall-remodeling enzymes. The resulting changes in cell dimensions are coordinated with other signals that affect water status and developmental position.

A short hypocotyl does not reveal by itself whether cells are fewer, shorter or both. Measuring the number and dimensions of cells in equivalent regions is a way to separate these possibilities. A BR treatment that increases average cell length without changing cell number supports a contribution from cell expansion. A meristem phenotype may instead involve altered proliferation or altered transition from proliferation to differentiation. Organ growth is the combined outcome of these processes rather than a direct measure of receptor activity.

A classical response to BR in Arabidopsis involves altered elongation of the hypocotyl, the embryonic stem below the cotyledons. In darkness, a typical seedling develops a long hypocotyl with an apical hook and closed cotyledons as part of skotomorphogenesis. BR-deficient and BR-insensitive seedlings may exhibit features of light-grown development in darkness, showing that hormonal signaling contributes to the developmental program ordinarily controlled by environmental light. This finding does not mean BRI1 detects light. Light perception and BR perception are separate inputs that can converge on shared transcriptional outputs.

BRs also influence vascular development. Xylem differentiation includes formation of cells adapted to transport water and support plant tissues. BR signaling affects the balance between proliferating vascular precursors and differentiated cells in context with other positional signals. An increase in the fraction of differentiated cells may result from altered developmental timing rather than an increase in the total number of precursors. The relevant comparison is between matched regions and stages, with both cell identity and cell number recorded.

Reproductive growth also requires coordinated hormonal activity. Mutants impaired in BR signaling may show reduced fertility, including defects related to male reproductive development. A reduction in seed number should not be assigned to pollen function without testing other possibilities such as ovule development, fertilization or post-fertilization seed survival. Reciprocal crosses help distinguish contributions from male and female tissues. The lesson from the BR phenotypes is that regulation of cell development has consequences across the life cycle, not that the hormone acts exclusively in stem elongation.

Integration with auxin, gibberellins and environmental information

The regulatory pathways that govern growth do not function as independent switches. Auxin-responsive ARF factors, BR-responsive BES1 and BZR1 factors, and light-regulated PIF proteins influence overlapping classes of growth genes. Their activities help explain why the response to one hormone can change when another hormone or light condition changes. Auxin has a distinct perception pathway involving TIR1/AFB and Aux/IAA repressors. Convergence on transcriptional outputs does not make auxin a BR ligand or make BRI1 an auxin receptor.

Gibberellin signaling promotes degradation of DELLA growth repressors. DELLA proteins can restrict the activity of transcriptional regulators involved in growth, including BZR1-related output in appropriate contexts. GA and BR can consequently cooperate to promote elongation. A low level of GA signaling may limit growth even if BR signaling is active. Such an interaction can be distinguished from an effect on BR concentration by measuring hormone-responsive protein states and growth-associated transcription separately.

Environmental constraints change the value of cell expansion. Under water shortage, continued rapid expansion can be disadvantageous because the capacity to sustain turgor is reduced. Growth and defense signaling also interact, with effects depending on developmental state, stress type and signal strength. It is not scientifically sound to state that BR universally increases resistance or always suppresses immunity. BAK1 participation in several receptor systems and the downstream interaction between hormonal and defensive regulators provide opportunities for coordination, not a single universal rule about the direction of the response.

A factorial experiment can distinguish combined treatment effects from independent hormone responses. For example, seedlings can be grown with vehicle, BR, GA, and both hormones at selected concentrations. If the combined treatment gives more elongation than predicted by a stated additive model, that is evidence for an interaction on that measurement scale, not proof that one hormone directly binds the receptor of the other. Changes in hormone uptake, developmental timing and ceiling effects must be ruled out before assigning the interaction to a particular molecule.

Quantitative reasoning with an illustrative rescue assay

Suppose three seedling genotypes are measured after growth under identical illumination and temperature. The numbers in the accompanying table are hypothetical teaching values rather than results of a published experiment. A normal genotype has a mean hypocotyl length of 8 mm without treatment. A biosynthesis-deficient genotype has a mean of 3 mm and reaches 7 mm after an active BR treatment. A receptor-impaired genotype begins at 3 mm and remains at 3 mm after the same treatment.

The fraction of the original length deficit restored in the biosynthesis-deficient genotype is calculated by dividing its increase in length by the original difference from the untreated normal genotype. The increase is 7 minus 3, or 4 mm. The original deficit is 8 minus 3, or 5 mm. The fraction is therefore 4 divided by 5, or 0.80, corresponding to 80 percent. This is not a measurement of receptor occupancy or the fraction of all BR-regulated genes that recovered. It is a normalized measure of organ length relative to a chosen control.

An untreated wild-type reference is useful for the deficit calculation, but a hormone-treated wild-type control is also needed to establish how the same dose affects normal development. Measurements from independent biological replicates, not repeated readings of a single seedling alone, are needed to estimate biological variability. Without such variation the table illustrates a pattern but does not demonstrate statistical significance.

The most economical interpretation is that the biosynthesis-deficient genotype retains substantial ability to respond to active hormone, whereas the receptor-impaired genotype does not under these assay conditions. A single concentration cannot distinguish every possible sensitivity defect from complete insensitivity. A dose-response series can show a shifted effective concentration range, a reduced maximal response, or altered baseline growth. Additional molecular evidence is needed to identify the mutated gene.

Misconceptions and examination traps

  1. All steroids use nuclear receptors. BRI1 is a membrane receptor kinase. Chemical classification is confused with receptor architecture; locate receptor first.
  2. BRI1 transports BR into the nucleus. It initiates a membrane-to-cytoplasm signaling relay. Ligand and signal movement are confused; follow the protein domains.
  3. BIN2 is activated by BR to promote growth. The canonical relay inhibits BIN2. Kinase activity is mistaken for positive signaling; trace the two inhibitory edges.
  4. Every phosphorylation activates its target. BIN2 phosphorylation generally restrains BES1/BZR1. General enzyme activation rules cannot determine the sign of this specific modification.
  5. PP2A directly deactivates BRI1 in the core model. PP2A contributes to dephosphorylation of BZR1/BES1. Phosphatase names alone do not identify the substrate.
  6. BR deficiency and BR resistance are the same. Supply and responsiveness are separable. Compare exogenous ligand rescue and signaling readouts.
  7. DWF4 is a receptor. DWF4 is a BR biosynthetic P450. Names derived from phenotypes do not indicate biochemical function.
  8. Castasterone is only an inactive precursor. Castasterone has biological activity. Do not assume every pathway intermediate is inert.
  9. All BRs must circulate through the phloem. BR regulation is predominantly local, with demonstrated short-range intermediate movement. The behavior of one plant hormone cannot be generalized to all.
  10. No long-distance transport means no movement. Adjacent-cell transport can occur. Separate spatial scales in a grafting or tissue-specific rescue question.
  11. Conjugation always creates a recoverable storage form. Conjugate fates depend on chemical form and enzymes. Do not infer reversibility without evidence.
  12. BR universally stimulates root elongation. Root responses depend on concentration and context. Read dose curves rather than memorizing one direction.
  13. A long hypocotyl proves cell division increased. Cell expansion and division both affect organ length. Measure cell count and size independently.
  14. BAK1 only participates in BR responses. BAK1 is shared by additional receptor systems. Co-receptor identity does not determine ligand specificity alone.
  15. GA and BR share the same receptor. GA uses GID1 and BR uses BRI1-family receptors; outputs can converge. Separate upstream recognition from downstream integration.

Original examination practice

Part B: Concepts

B 1. Which sterol is a major precursor of brassinosteroids?

  1. Campesterol
  2. Cholesterol exclusively
  3. Glucose
  4. Indole-3-acetic acid
Answer and explanation

A. Campesterol — Campesterol supplies the sterol framework; IAA is an unrelated auxin.

B 2. Which enzyme catalyzes a representative C22 hydroxylation?

  1. BES1
  2. DWF4
  3. BIN2
  4. BRI1
Answer and explanation

B. DWF4 — DWF4 is CYP90B1, a biosynthetic P450; the other proteins function in signaling.

B 3. DET2 is best classified as a:

  1. LRR receptor kinase
  2. Protein phosphatase
  3. Steroid reductase
  4. Transcription factor
Answer and explanation

C. Steroid reductase — DET2 catalyzes a reductive transformation of a steroid intermediate.

B 4. Which compound is itself bioactive in BR signaling?

  1. Cellulose
  2. DELLA
  3. PIN1
  4. Castasterone
Answer and explanation

D. Castasterone — Castasterone can act as a hormone; DELLA and PIN1 are proteins.

B 5. Where does canonical BR perception begin?

  1. Plasma membrane
  2. Chloroplast thylakoid lumen
  3. Mitochondrial matrix
  4. Nucleolus
Answer and explanation

A. Plasma membrane — BRI1 recognizes extracellular ligand and has an intracellular kinase domain.

B 6. What is the role of BAK1 in canonical BR perception?

  1. Auxin efflux carrier
  2. Co-receptor kinase
  3. Steroid reductase
  4. DNA-binding transcription factor
Answer and explanation

B. Co-receptor kinase — BAK1/SERK3 participates in receptor complex activation.

B 7. BIN2 is a member of which kinase family?

  1. Histidine kinases
  2. Cyclin-dependent kinases
  3. GSK3-like kinases
  4. Receptor guanylyl cyclases
Answer and explanation

C. GSK3-like kinases — BIN2 is a GSK3-related serine/threonine kinase acting downstream of the receptor.

B 8. Which proteins are downstream BR-responsive transcriptional regulators?

  1. DWF4 and DET2
  2. BRI1 and BAK1
  3. AUX1 and PIN1
  4. BES1 and BZR1
Answer and explanation

D. BES1 and BZR1 — BES1 and BZR1 regulate transcription; the other pairs are enzymes, receptors or carriers.

B 9. What generally happens to BIN2 activity in the canonical high-BR signaling state?

  1. It decreases
  2. It invariably increases
  3. It becomes a receptor
  4. It catalyzes steroid synthesis
Answer and explanation

A. It decreases — Inhibition of BIN2 relieves inhibitory phosphorylation of downstream transcription factors.

B 10. Which phosphatase directly contributes to dephosphorylation of BZR1?

  1. GID1
  2. PP2A
  3. CYP85A2
  4. DWF4
Answer and explanation

B. PP2A — PP2A acts on phosphorylated BZR1; GID1 is a GA receptor.

B 11. What is a major function of CYP85-family enzymes in BR metabolism?

  1. DNA methylation
  2. DELLA degradation
  3. Late C6 oxidation
  4. Auxin conjugation
Answer and explanation

C. Late C6 oxidation — BR6ox enzymes generate late oxidized BR products.

B 12. BAS1 is associated with which process?

  1. BR receptor binding
  2. GA perception
  3. Starch breakdown
  4. BR inactivation
Answer and explanation

D. BR inactivation — BAS1 encodes a CYP734-family oxidative catabolic enzyme.

B 13. Which statement best describes endogenous BR movement?

  1. Predominantly local action with demonstrated short-range intermediate movement
  2. Universal long-distance phloem translocation
  3. No molecule ever crosses between cells
  4. Mandatory polar PIN transport
Answer and explanation

A. Predominantly local action with demonstrated short-range intermediate movement — Local production and short-range movement are compatible; systemic transport is not a universal requirement.

B 14. Which structure can support intercellular movement of BR intermediates in Arabidopsis roots?

  1. Thylakoid grana
  2. Plasmodesmata
  3. Animal gap junctions
  4. Nuclear pores between different cells
Answer and explanation

B. Plasmodesmata — Plasmodesmata link the cytoplasm of adjacent plant cells.

B 15. Which response is a classical phenotype of severe BR deficiency?

  1. Loss of all chloroplasts
  2. Permanent stomatal opening in all organs
  3. Dwarf stature
  4. Obligate nitrogen fixation
Answer and explanation

C. Dwarf stature — BR deficiency commonly reduces elongation and alters development.

B 16. Which plant structure is the hypocotyl?

  1. Root cap
  2. Anther wall
  3. Mature xylem vessel
  4. Embryonic stem below cotyledons
Answer and explanation

D. Embryonic stem below cotyledons — Hypocotyl length is a standard developmental response in seedling assays.

B 17. What is the most direct effect of expansin-mediated wall loosening?

  1. Greater wall extensibility
  2. Elimination of turgor requirement
  3. Immediate DNA replication
  4. Loss of receptor binding
Answer and explanation

A. Greater wall extensibility — Wall extensibility permits turgor-driven irreversible expansion.

B 18. Which hormonal pathway uses GID1 and DELLA?

  1. Ethylene perception
  2. Gibberellin
  3. Brassinosteroid perception
  4. Cytokinin
Answer and explanation

B. Gibberellin — GA-bound GID1 facilitates DELLA removal, interacting with BR output downstream.

B 19. What is negative feedback in BR biosynthesis?

  1. All target genes increase equally
  2. BRI1 binds its own promoter directly
  3. Active signaling restrains expression of selected synthesis genes
  4. BIN2 synthesizes more ligand
Answer and explanation

C. Active signaling restrains expression of selected synthesis genes — Feedback limits further hormone production; it is distinct from activation of growth targets.

B 20. Which process can change BR availability without a receptor mutation?

  1. Loss of BRI1 kinase catalytic activity
  2. Constitutively active BIN2
  3. Loss of BES1 DNA binding
  4. Glycosylation
Answer and explanation

D. Glycosylation — Metabolic conjugation can alter free hormone availability while signaling components remain intact.

Part C: Analytical reasoning

C 1. A dwarf mutant recovers nearly normal growth after supplied brassinolide. Which primary lesion is most consistent?

  1. Loss of a BR biosynthetic reaction
  2. Complete loss of BRI1 signaling
  3. Constitutive downstream repression in all cells
  4. Loss of all wall synthesis
Answer and explanation

A. Loss of a BR biosynthetic reaction — Active ligand bypasses an upstream synthetic defect but requires adequate receptor and downstream function.

C 2. Two mutants have equal short hypocotyls. A responds to BR, B does not; both absorb the treatment. What follows?

  1. Both lack active ligand only
  2. A and B probably differ in causal pathway position
  3. They must be alleles of DWF4
  4. B cannot make any BR
Answer and explanation

B. A and B probably differ in causal pathway position — The response separates a likely supply defect from a response defect; it does not identify a gene uniquely.

C 3. After BR treatment, BZR1 shifts to a faster immunoblot band. Which control best tests phosphorylation as the cause?

  1. Measuring leaf color
  2. Removing the molecular mass marker only
  3. Phosphatase treatment of extract
  4. Adding more gel stain
Answer and explanation

C. Phosphatase treatment of extract — Phosphatase-dependent disappearance of the slow band supports differential phosphorylation.

C 4. A BIN2 gain-of-function line shows little growth response to a BRI1 agonist despite receptor activation. Which prediction follows?

  1. Campesterol is absent by definition
  2. BAK1 cannot be expressed
  3. All GA receptors are degraded
  4. BES1/BZR1 remain more phosphorylated
Answer and explanation

D. BES1/BZR1 remain more phosphorylated — Persistent downstream kinase activity can maintain inhibitory phosphorylation after upstream activation.

C 5. A receptor-impaired line gains partial growth after introduction of an active BZR1 variant. What is the most appropriate inference?

  1. Downstream output can bypass part of the receptor defect
  2. BRI1 is downstream of BZR1 in every pathway
  3. The receptor is a biosynthetic enzyme
  4. The original line had no receptor mutation
Answer and explanation

A. Downstream output can bypass part of the receptor defect — A suppressor acting downstream can restore output despite reduced upstream signaling.

C 6. After activation of BZR1, DWF4 transcript decreases while a wall-remodeling transcript increases. Why is this coherent?

  1. The result proves RNA degradation only
  2. Distinct targets support feedback and growth output
  3. BZR1 must be absent
  4. All target genes must be induced
Answer and explanation

B. Distinct targets support feedback and growth output — A transcriptional regulator can promote or repress different targets depending on promoter and partners.

C 7. A tissue contains high BR but has low growth and highly phosphorylated BES1. What is the best explanation to investigate?

  1. BR directly replaces wall cellulose
  2. Increased photosynthesis necessarily explains all
  3. Impaired signal transmission
  4. Absolute absence of hormone
Answer and explanation

C. Impaired signal transmission — Ligand abundance does not establish response competence.

C 8. A wild-type rootstock fails to rescue a BR-deficient scion. What can be concluded?

  1. No BR intermediate can move one cell
  2. BRI1 is located in the rootstock only
  3. All phytohormones lack vascular transport
  4. Substantial long-distance supply is not demonstrated under these conditions
Answer and explanation

D. Substantial long-distance supply is not demonstrated under these conditions — Grafting tests organ-scale transfer, not every local movement route.

C 9. Restoring DWF4 in one root cell layer partially improves neighboring cells. What additional experiment tests local transfer?

  1. Restrict symplastic connectivity and compare rescue
  2. Measure only total shoot mass
  3. Remove all light from control and mutant differently
  4. Sequence an unrelated bacterial gene
Answer and explanation

A. Restrict symplastic connectivity and compare rescue — A controlled perturbation of cell connectivity tests whether neighboring rescue depends on local movement.

C 10. A root dose-response increases length at low BR and reduces it at high BR. Which model is consistent?

  1. The receptor necessarily changes species
  2. An optimal concentration range
  3. A universal linear stimulation
  4. BR is inactive at all concentrations
Answer and explanation

B. An optimal concentration range — A nonmonotonic response is compatible with organ-specific regulation; dose and baseline matter.

C 11. A treated hypocotyl has 20 cells averaging 0.35 mm; control has 20 averaging 0.25 mm. What supports expansion?

  1. Average length decreases 40%
  2. The result proves mitosis increased
  3. Mean length increases by 40% at unchanged cell count
  4. Cell number doubles
Answer and explanation

C. Mean length increases by 40% at unchanged cell count — The difference is 0.10/0.25 = 0.40; cell count is unchanged in the measured interval.

C 12. An experimental BR-deficient shoot is 4 mm, after hormone 7 mm, untreated normal 10 mm. What fraction of deficit is restored?

  1. 30%
  2. 70%
  3. 150%
  4. 50%
Answer and explanation

D. 50% — The increase is 3 mm and initial deficit 6 mm, giving 3/6.

C 13. In a factorial assay, vehicle 4 mm, BR 6 mm, GA 7 mm, combined 11 mm. What is the additive prediction?

  1. 9 mm
  2. 13 mm
  3. 11 mm
  4. 4 mm
Answer and explanation

A. 9 mm — Baseline plus individual gains gives 4+(6−4)+(7−4)=9 mm; observed combined value is higher by 2 mm.

C 14. A stabilized DELLA protein reduces growth despite active BZR1. Which mechanism is consistent?

  1. BZR1 cannot be a transcription factor
  2. DELLA can restrain transcriptional growth output
  3. DELLA must catalyze brassinolide synthesis
  4. GA always binds BRI1
Answer and explanation

B. DELLA can restrain transcriptional growth output — DELLA interaction with growth regulators can limit their function downstream of hormone perception.

C 15. A BR biosynthesis inhibitor reduces growth; exogenous BL restores it. Which control is essential?

  1. No untreated group
  2. Different ages for all genotypes
  3. Matched solvent-treated seedlings
  4. An unrelated animal tissue only
Answer and explanation

C. Matched solvent-treated seedlings — Vehicle and developmental matching reduce alternative explanations for treatment effects.

C 16. A receptor variant binds BL but lacks kinase activity. Which immediate defect is predicted?

  1. Complete inability of BL to exist
  2. Guaranteed increased downstream signaling
  3. Direct loss of cellulose chemistry
  4. Impaired intracellular relay despite ligand binding
Answer and explanation

D. Impaired intracellular relay despite ligand binding — Recognition and catalytic signal transmission are separable functions.

C 17. A mutation changes only BZR1 DNA binding while receptor activity and phosphorylation remain normal. What is expected?

  1. Selected transcriptional outputs can fail
  2. The mutation must prevent ligand synthesis
  3. The receptor must move to the nucleus
  4. All other hormone pathways stop
Answer and explanation

A. Selected transcriptional outputs can fail — Downstream DNA recognition is needed for a subset of BR-responsive genes.

C 18. A mutant exhibits male infertility. Reciprocal crosses show mutant pollen fails on normal flowers, while normal pollen succeeds on mutant flowers. What does this support?

  1. No effect on reproduction
  2. A male-side contribution to the defect
  3. A strictly female defect
  4. Universal failure of seed maturation
Answer and explanation

B. A male-side contribution to the defect — The directional cross isolates a male contribution, although further experiments identify the affected stage.

C 19. Following BR treatment, differentiated xylem cells rise from 20 to 30 while total vascular cells remain 100. What changes?

  1. Xylem fraction decreases
  2. The data alone establish direct receptor binding
  3. Differentiated fraction rises by 10 percentage points
  4. Total vascular cell number rises 50%
Answer and explanation

C. Differentiated fraction rises by 10 percentage points — Fractions are 20% and 30%; differentiation or timing changes are plausible, not proven direct mechanisms.

C 20. A BR-responsive promoter is activated by BZR1 plus PIF4 but weakly by either alone. Which interpretation is most defensible?

  1. PIF4 must be a steroid receptor
  2. BR binds phytochrome directly
  3. GA is the only input
  4. Cooperation at transcriptional output is consistent with the data
Answer and explanation

D. Cooperation at transcriptional output is consistent with the data — Combined activity can reflect promoter-level cooperation; direct physical interaction needs independent evidence.

Short-answer and SET reasoning

1. Explain why steroid chemical identity does not predict the receptor's cellular location.

Answer and explanation

The ligand is recognized by a plasma membrane receptor in the canonical BR system, whereas many animal steroid hormones use intracellular receptors. Chemistry alone cannot establish receptor architecture.

2. A student says BAK1 functions only in BR signaling. Correct the statement.

Answer and explanation

BAK1 is a SERK co-receptor used in additional signaling systems. Partner receptor identity and ligand context matter.

3. Why is high total BR not the same as high active BR?

Answer and explanation

Measurements may combine bioactive forms, metabolites and conjugates; only the available active forms with responsive receptors support signaling.

4. Why must an untreated genotype be included in a growth-rescue experiment?

Answer and explanation

Baseline growth allows the treatment-dependent increase to be distinguished from constitutive size differences.

5. What is the predicted effect of enhanced PP2A-dependent dephosphorylation, assuming other factors constant?

Answer and explanation

A shift toward less phosphorylated BZR1/BES1 can support BR transcriptional output; the actual effect depends on localization and target competence.

6. Why does repression of a biosynthetic gene after hormone addition support feedback?

Answer and explanation

The signal reduces the capacity to generate more of itself. It is a homeostatic response rather than global transcriptional inhibition.

7. How could a kinase-dead BRI1 retain ligand binding but fail to signal?

Answer and explanation

The extracellular binding region and cytoplasmic kinase perform distinct steps. A lesion limited to catalysis may preserve recognition without productive relay.

8. Why is a single concentration insufficient to prove complete hormone resistance?

Answer and explanation

A mutant might have altered sensitivity, saturation, uptake or maximal response; a concentration series distinguishes several alternatives.

9. Why is a decrease in leaf area not necessarily a reduction in cell elongation?

Answer and explanation

Leaf area depends on cell number, dimensions, shape and developmental timing. Cellular measurements are required.

10. Why can growth and defense responses to BR differ across environments?

Answer and explanation

Signal strength, tissue competence, resource availability and interaction with defense regulators change the physiological outcome.
