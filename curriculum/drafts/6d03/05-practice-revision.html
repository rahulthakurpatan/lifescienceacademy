16. Misconceptions and examination traps

1. Cytokinin always stimulates division

A tissue can respond by differentiating or delaying senescence. The name emphasizes discovery, not a universal effect. In a root-meristem question ask whether cell exit or cell entry changed.

2. All cytokinin comes from roots

Shoots also synthesize and activate cytokinin locally. Root-to-shoot delivery is memorable but does not exclude local LOG activity. Distinguish source and activation site.

3. Kinetin is the principal endogenous cytokinin

Kinetin was important in discovery and is used experimentally; natural iP and zeatin are key physiological forms. A question about discovery differs from one about endogenous ligand identity.

4. Cis and trans zeatin are the same ligand in every context

Geometry affects recognition and metabolism. Similar formulas cause confusion; identify which isomer and receptor are specified.

5. Every adenine derivative activates cytokinin receptors

The appropriate N6 side chain and molecular form matter. Adenine, nucleotides and active cytokinin are distinct; check the chemical state.

6. IPT and LOG are interchangeable

IPT introduces a side chain; LOG releases free base from monophosphate. Both increase potential supply but a precursor-versus-free-base rescue separates them.

7. All conjugation is irreversible breakdown

O-glucosides can in some contexts be hydrolyzed. The term inactive describes current receptor activity, not permanent chemical destruction.

8. CKX cleaves all cytokinin types equally

Substrate preferences differ; DHZ and many aromatics are relatively resistant. Compare the side-chain chemistry rather than treating all hormones as equivalent.

9. Xylem concentration equals delivery

Delivery is concentration times flow. Concentration is easy to observe, but flux determines quantity per time. Calculate both if flow changes.

10. ABCG14 is the only cytokinin transporter

It contributes to specific systemic distribution; several proteins and pathways influence local and systemic transport. A transport phenotype does not establish exclusivity.

11. AHK is a typical animal receptor tyrosine kinase

Canonical cytokinin perception uses hybrid His kinase machinery. Membrane localization alone does not identify the phosphorylation chemistry.

12. The hormone itself is transferred from AHK to ARR

The phosphoryl group, supplied by ATP, is transferred. Ligand binding triggers the relay. Label the arrows with the entity that actually moves.

13. Every AHP is a positive relay protein

AHP6 lacks the conserved histidine and can inhibit signaling. A similar name is not proof of biochemical function.

14. All ARR proteins activate cytokinin responses

Type-B ARRs commonly activate transcription; type-A ARRs commonly provide negative feedback. Class membership is essential to predict mutant output.

15. A reporter is a direct concentration sensor

TCSn and ARR promoter reporters record transcriptional output. Receptor abundance and feedback can uncouple fluorescence from concentration.

16. High cytokinin-to-auxin ratio guarantees shoots

This is a context-specific culture tendency. Absolute dose, competence and treatment history matter. Compare both concentrations and explant conditions.

17. Greener leaves necessarily have higher yield

Pigment retention does not establish carbon fixation or reproductive allocation. Use functional measures and actual yield evidence.

18. EdU labeling proves completed cell division

EdU records DNA synthesis, including possible endoreduplication. Use cell counts and mitotic/cytokinetic measures to assess completed proliferation.

17. Original examination practice

These original questions are designed for NET and SET preparation. They are not previous-year questions.

Part B: foundational and mechanistic understanding

1. Which structural description best identifies most natural isoprenoid cytokinins?

  1. N6-substituted adenines
  2. Carotenoid cleavage products
  3. Indole carboxylic acids
  4. Sterol derivatives
Answer and explanation

A — N6-substituted adenines. iP and zeatin have a modified adenine N6 side chain. ABA and strigolactones are carotenoid-derived, IAA is an indole auxin, and brassinosteroids are sterol-derived.

2. Which two molecules differ in geometry around a side-chain double bond?

  1. iP and iP ribotide
  2. cis-zeatin and trans-zeatin
  3. Zeatin and its O-glucoside
  4. Adenine and adenosine
Answer and explanation

B — cis-zeatin and trans-zeatin. The cis/trans pair has the same connectivity but different double-bond geometry. The other pairs differ in sugar, phosphate or chemical substitution.

3. What is the principal function of adenylate IPT?

  1. Degrade zeatin
  2. Transfer phosphate to ARR
  3. Add an isoprenoid group to an adenine nucleotide
  4. Remove phosphoribose from cytokinin ribotide
Answer and explanation

C — Add an isoprenoid group to an adenine nucleotide. IPT initiates de novo isoprenoid cytokinin synthesis. CKX cleaves susceptible side chains; AHP transfers phosphate; LOG activates monophosphate precursors.

4. Which enzyme converts iP-type nucleotides into tZ-type nucleotides?

  1. LOG
  2. CKX
  3. AHP
  4. CYP735A
Answer and explanation

D — CYP735A. CYP735A hydroxylates the side chain. LOG releases a free base, CKX degrades susceptible substrates and AHP transfers phosphate.

5. What is the immediate product of LOG activity on tZRMP?

  1. Free trans-zeatin
  2. Trans-zeatin O-glucoside
  3. Adenine
  4. Phosphorylated ARR
Answer and explanation

A — Free trans-zeatin. LOG removes the phosphoribose group from the cytokinin riboside monophosphate, producing the free cytokinin base. Glycosylation, CKX cleavage and phosphorelay are distinct processes.

6. Which statement best distinguishes a reversible O-glucoside from a CKX degradation product?

  1. Both are necessarily active ligands
  2. O-glucoside may release intact hormone on hydrolysis
  3. CKX creates an O-glucoside
  4. Neither undergoes enzymatic conversion
Answer and explanation

B — O-glucoside may release intact hormone on hydrolysis. Certain O-glucosides conserve the cytokinin scaffold and can be hydrolyzed; CKX destroys the susceptible side chain. Neither should be assumed to be an active ligand.

7. Which type of compound is often relatively resistant to typical CKX side-chain cleavage?

  1. Free iP
  2. Free trans-zeatin
  3. Dihydrozeatin
  4. Every cytokinin nucleotide equally
Answer and explanation

C — Dihydrozeatin. The saturated side chain of DHZ confers resistance to many CKX enzymes; iP and tZ have susceptible unsaturated side chains. Substrate preference is isoform-dependent.

8. Which vascular association is generally appropriate in model plants?

  1. tZ-type compounds travel only through phloem
  2. iP is synthesized exclusively in roots
  3. All cytokinins move only in xylem
  4. tZ-type root-to-shoot signals often travel in xylem
Answer and explanation

D — tZ-type root-to-shoot signals often travel in xylem. Xylem commonly carries root-derived tZ-related compounds; iP-related compounds are often associated with phloem. These are tendencies, not exclusive routes.

9. Which protein contributes importantly to root-to-shoot cytokinin distribution in Arabidopsis?

  1. ABCG14
  2. DELLA
  3. TIR1
  4. PHYB
Answer and explanation

A — ABCG14. ABCG14 contributes to vascular cytokinin transport. DELLA is a GA repressor, TIR1 an auxin receptor component and PHYB a photoreceptor.

10. Which proteins are canonical Arabidopsis cytokinin receptors?

  1. AUX1 and PIN1
  2. AHK2, AHK3 and AHK4
  3. ARR5 and ARR7
  4. GID1 and BRI1
Answer and explanation

B — AHK2, AHK3 and AHK4. AHK2/3/4 are hybrid histidine kinase receptors. AUX1/PIN participate in auxin transport; ARR5/7 are response regulators; GID1 and BRI1 recognize other hormones.

11. Which order describes the canonical cytokinin phosphorelay residues?

  1. Ser–Thr–Tyr–Ser
  2. Asp–Asp–His–His
  3. His–Asp–His–Asp
  4. Tyr–His–Ser–Asp
Answer and explanation

C — His–Asp–His–Asp. The AHK kinase His transfers to AHK receiver Asp, then AHP His, then ARR Asp. The other orders do not describe this relay.

12. Which function characterizes type-B ARRs?

  1. Cytokinin synthesis
  2. Cleavage of cytokinin side chains
  3. Vascular water conduction
  4. Transcriptional activation of cytokinin-responsive genes
Answer and explanation

D — Transcriptional activation of cytokinin-responsive genes. Type-B ARRs are response-regulator transcription factors; IPT and CKX act in metabolism, and xylem conduits carry water.

13. Which class commonly mediates negative feedback after cytokinin induction?

  1. Type-A ARRs
  2. Type-B ARRs as a class
  3. PIN proteins as cytokinin receptors
  4. LOG enzymes as phosphatases
Answer and explanation

A — Type-A ARRs. Many type-A ARRs are induced by cytokinin and restrain signaling. Type-B ARRs usually promote transcriptional output; PIN and LOG have distinct functions.

14. Why is AHP6 called a pseudo-phosphotransfer protein?

  1. It is a glycosyltransferase
  2. It lacks the canonical phosphoaccepting histidine
  3. It is an auxin receptor
  4. It contains no amino acids
Answer and explanation

B — It lacks the canonical phosphoaccepting histidine. AHP6 lacks the conserved His needed for the ordinary relay and can inhibit signaling. It is still a protein and is not an auxin receptor or metabolic glycosyltransferase.

15. Which protein family links cytokinin to G1/S regulation?

  1. Rubisco
  2. Aquaporins
  3. D-type cyclins, including CYCD3
  4. Light-harvesting chlorophyll proteins
Answer and explanation

C — D-type cyclins, including CYCD3. CYCD3 induction contributes to the control of cell cycle entry. The other proteins have metabolic, water transport or light-harvesting roles.

16. What is WUSCHEL's central role in the shoot apex?

  1. Oxidative degradation of cytokinin
  2. Nitrate reduction
  3. Xylem loading of tZ riboside
  4. Maintenance of shoot stem cell identity through organizing-center activity
Answer and explanation

D — Maintenance of shoot stem cell identity through organizing-center activity. WUS is a developmental transcription factor, not a cytokinin metabolic enzyme or transporter. It supports a stem cell niche.

17. Which gene is induced by ARR1 in the classic Arabidopsis root differentiation model?

  1. SHY2/IAA3
  2. RBCS
  3. GID1
  4. ACS
Answer and explanation

A — SHY2/IAA3. ARR1 promotes SHY2, which influences auxin response and PIN expression. The others encode unrelated photosynthetic or other hormone components.

18. In a competent classic tobacco culture, a relatively high cytokinin-to-auxin balance commonly favors what?

  1. Only root initiation
  2. Shoot formation
  3. An inevitable arrest of all proliferation
  4. No developmental change under any conditions
Answer and explanation

B — Shoot formation. A cytokinin-rich balance can promote shoots; auxin-rich conditions often favor roots. Absolute dose, tissue and history prevent universal predictions.

19. Which treatment commonly stimulates root cytokinin production in Arabidopsis?

  1. Complete absence of all energy sources
  2. Direct removal of all receptors
  3. Nitrate resupply
  4. Conversion of every cytokinin to adenine
Answer and explanation

C — Nitrate resupply. Nitrate can induce IPT-dependent synthesis, contributing to systemic nutrient communication. Receptor loss or complete energy deprivation does not represent this induction mechanism.

20. What does the senescence-associated SAG12::IPT strategy accomplish?

  1. Constitutive degradation of all chlorophyll
  2. Inhibition of IPT synthesis
  3. Direct enzymatic synthesis of chlorophyll by IPT
  4. Senescence-linked cytokinin production that can delay further senescence
Answer and explanation

D — Senescence-linked cytokinin production that can delay further senescence. The promoter responds to senescence and drives cytokinin biosynthesis; the resulting hormone can delay the process. IPT is not a chlorophyll enzyme.

Part C: experimental, quantitative and integrated reasoning

21. A mutant has high root tZ-related pools, low shoot pools and small shoots; direct tZ supplied to the shoot improves growth. Which defect is best supported?

  1. AHK receptors cannot bind tZ
  2. Root-to-shoot distribution is impaired
  3. All cytokinin synthesis is absent
  4. Shoot WUS is permanently absent
Answer and explanation

B — Root-to-shoot distribution is impaired. The source retains hormone while the destination is deficient, and shoot application bypasses delivery. Receptor failure or permanent downstream loss would make rescue less likely; high root pools contradict total biosynthetic absence.

22. A LOG-deficient tissue is supplied with free tZ or tZRMP separately. Only free tZ restores a reporter under the stated conditions. What is the best inference?

  1. LOG is needed for efficient precursor activation
  2. LOG is a DNA-binding ARR
  3. Free tZ must be converted into tZRMP before perception
  4. LOG is the xylem water channel
Answer and explanation

A — LOG is needed for efficient precursor activation. Free tZ bypasses the impaired activation step. A precursor requires conversion, so its weaker response supports the LOG model. The result does not identify LOG as receptor, transcription factor or channel.

23. In a simple steady-state model, production is 18 nM h⁻¹ and removal coefficient 0.6 h⁻¹. A treatment halves the removal coefficient. What steady-state value follows if input is unchanged?

  1. 15 nM
  2. 30 nM
  3. 60 nM
  4. 10.8 nM
Answer and explanation

C — 60 nM. The model gives C=S/k. Initially 18/0.6=30 nM; after treatment 18/0.3=60 nM. The other values confuse initial and final values or multiply rates.

24. Xylem concentration rises from 20 to 40 nM while flow falls from 0.5 to 0.125 mL h⁻¹. What happens to delivery?

  1. It doubles
  2. It is unchanged
  3. It increases fourfold
  4. It falls to half
Answer and explanation

D — It falls to half. Initial delivery is 20×0.0005=0.010 nmol/h; final is 40×0.000125=0.005 nmol/h. A rise in concentration does not imply a rise in flux.

25. A receptor binds ligand with hypothetical Kd=5 nM. At 15 nM free ligand, what is predicted occupancy?

  1. 75%
  2. 25%
  3. 50%
  4. 100%
Answer and explanation

A — 75%. Occupancy is 15/(5+15)=0.75. This simple binding model does not establish the percentage of maximal organ growth.

26. A type-A arr multiple mutant displays normal early reporter induction but prolonged high output after cytokinin. What is the best explanation?

  1. All IPT activity is absent
  2. Loss of feedback inhibition
  3. AHP proteins now transport nitrate
  4. Type-B ARR transcription factors are necessarily missing
Answer and explanation

B — Loss of feedback inhibition. The preserved early response suggests intact positive signaling; prolonged activity is consistent with weaker negative feedback. Loss of biosynthesis, phosphotransfer or type-B activity would not explain the pattern as directly.

27. An AHK mutant retains ligand binding and autophosphorylation but lacks the receiver-domain phosphoacceptor Asp. What should be impaired most directly?

  1. CKX cleavage of hormone
  2. Synthesis of DMAPP
  3. Transfer from receptor kinase through receiver domain to AHP
  4. The presence of all type-A genes in DNA
Answer and explanation

C — Transfer from receptor kinase through receiver domain to AHP. The receptor's Asp is a required intermediate in the canonical relay. Upstream binding and kinase activation can persist; the mutation does not delete genes or directly alter cytokinin synthesis.

28. An AHP protein contains a substitution at its conserved histidine and cannot receive phosphate. Which early downstream event is likely reduced?

  1. IPT side-chain transfer itself
  2. O-glucoside hydrolysis itself
  3. ATP production in all cells
  4. Phosphorylation of cytokinin-responsive ARRs through that AHP
Answer and explanation

D — Phosphorylation of cytokinin-responsive ARRs through that AHP. Loss of the AHP phosphoacceptor interrupts the transfer to ARRs. Other AHPs can compensate; the mutation does not directly block metabolic steps.

29. A shoot apex contains high free cytokinin but weak TCSn reporter output after receptor disruption. What is justified?

  1. High hormone concentration does not ensure high signaling output
  2. TCSn directly measures chemical cytokinin concentration
  3. CKX must be absent
  4. The reporter proves total cytokinin is low
Answer and explanation

A — High hormone concentration does not ensure high signaling output. A reporter measures transcriptional output downstream of perception. A receptor lesion can uncouple concentration from response; CKX status cannot be inferred from the reporter alone.

30. A mutant expressing constitutive CYCD3 shows proliferation without exogenous cytokinin in a culture assay but fails to form normal shoots. Which conclusion fits?

  1. CYCD3 must be the cytokinin receptor
  2. Cell-cycle entry and meristem organization are separable
  3. All cytokinin effects are mediated by CYCD3
  4. Cytokinin cannot affect cell division
Answer and explanation

B — Cell-cycle entry and meristem organization are separable. A downstream cyclin can bypass some requirements for cell cycle entry without restoring positional regulators such as WUS. The other options overstate or contradict the evidence.

31. ARR1 overexpression shrinks the root meristem in wild type but not in a shy2 loss-of-function background. What does this support?

  1. SHY2 acts upstream of all AHK receptors
  2. ARR1 has no developmental function
  3. SHY2 is required downstream for this ARR1-dependent phenotype
  4. SHY2 is a cytokinin transporter
Answer and explanation

C — SHY2 is required downstream for this ARR1-dependent phenotype. Loss of the proposed downstream mediator suppresses the upstream perturbation's phenotype. The experiment supports dependency for this response, not every ARR1 target.

32. A root meristem adds 15 net cells/h and loses 15 cells/h to differentiation. A cytokinin treatment changes only exit to 20 cells/h. What is the immediate change?

  1. +5 cells/h
  2. −20 cells/h
  3. 0 cells/h
  4. −5 cells/h
Answer and explanation

D — −5 cells/h. The population balance is production minus exit: 15−20=−5 cells/h. Reduced meristem size can reflect faster differentiation even without reduced division.

33. A cytokinin-treated leaf contains 30% more area but unchanged total epidermal cell count. What is most directly supported?

  1. Greater mean projected cell area
  2. Increased cell count
  3. All cells entered mitosis
  4. WUS is necessarily overexpressed
Answer and explanation

A — Greater mean projected cell area. Area divided by cell number estimates mean projected area under comparable sampling. No evidence establishes greater proliferation or the identity of a particular regulator.

34. Two media contain auxin:cytokinin at 1:1, but doses are 0.05 and 5 μM respectively. Which prediction is warranted?

  1. They must induce identical organ numbers
  2. Equal ratios do not ensure equal responses
  3. The high-dose medium must always produce roots
  4. The low-dose medium cannot support any signaling
Answer and explanation

B — Equal ratios do not ensure equal responses. Absolute ligand concentration affects occupancy, metabolism and toxicity; a ratio alone does not determine outcome. Tissue competence also matters.

35. Thirty of 40 explants regenerate under A, producing 90 shoots. Twenty of 40 regenerate under B, producing 100 shoots. Which comparison is correct?

  1. A has higher mean shoots per responding explant
  2. B has higher regeneration frequency
  3. A has higher regeneration frequency but B has more shoots per responding explant
  4. Both have equal regeneration frequency
Answer and explanation

C — A has higher regeneration frequency but B has more shoots per responding explant. A: 75% and 3 shoots/responding explant. B: 50% and 5 shoots/responding explant. Frequency and multiplicity measure different outcomes.

36. A CKX-overexpressing line responds strongly to a resistant aromatic cytokinin but weakly to a susceptible isoprenoid ligand. Which explanation fits best?

  1. The receptor is absent in both treatments
  2. The aromatic compound is necessarily an auxin
  3. CKX phosphorylates ARR directly
  4. Differential hormone degradation can alter effective dose
Answer and explanation

D — Differential hormone degradation can alter effective dose. CKX specificity changes ligand persistence; the strong response to one cytokinin suggests some signaling competence. Receptor preferences may also contribute, so measure active pools.

37. A nitrate-starved plant receives active cytokinin. A shoot reporter rises, but biomass does not increase substantially. What is the best interpretation?

  1. Signaling can be restored without replacing missing nitrogen resources
  2. Cytokinin is a nitrogen fertilizer at normal signaling dose
  3. The reporter is invalid solely because biomass does not increase
  4. All nitrate assimilation enzymes must be normal
Answer and explanation

A — Signaling can be restored without replacing missing nitrogen resources. The reporter demonstrates a regulatory output, not sufficient nitrogen substrate for new biomass. Long-term growth requires resources and additional processes.

38. Leaves retain 1.2 mg chlorophyll/g after treatment, compared with 0.4 in controls; both began at 2.0. What are the retention fractions?

  1. 30% versus 10%
  2. 60% versus 20%
  3. 120% versus 40%
  4. 80% versus 40%
Answer and explanation

B — 60% versus 20%. Divide endpoint by initial concentration: 1.2/2.0=60%, 0.4/2.0=20%. Pigment retention alone does not prove retained photosynthetic function.

39. If SAG12-driven IPT expression delays senescence, what feedback relation can follow?

  1. Senescence continually accelerates itself
  2. IPT becomes a cytokinin receptor
  3. Delayed senescence can reduce the initiating promoter activity
  4. The construct necessarily expresses at maximum in every root
Answer and explanation

C — Delayed senescence can reduce the initiating promoter activity. The promoter is linked to a developmental state; hormone production suppresses that state, potentially reducing promoter activity. It is not a constitutive expression system.

40. A wild-type rootstock rescues much of a mutant shoot phenotype, but cytokinin was not measured. Which claim is strongest?

  1. ABCG14 is conclusively the only causal gene
  2. A mobile cytokinin molecule is conclusively identified
  3. No root-derived signal is involved
  4. Rootstock function influences the shoot phenotype; the mobile signal's identity remains to be tested
Answer and explanation

D — Rootstock function influences the shoot phenotype; the mobile signal's identity remains to be tested. Grafting demonstrates a systemic influence but does not uniquely identify the compound or transporter. Water, nutrient and additional signaling differences must be considered.

41. A treatment doubles the EdU-labeled fraction without increasing completed cell divisions. Which interpretation remains possible?

  1. Increased DNA synthesis including endoreduplication or delayed progression
  2. Every labeled cell has completed cytokinesis
  3. AHP6 must be overexpressed
  4. DNA synthesis has stopped
Answer and explanation

A — Increased DNA synthesis including endoreduplication or delayed progression. EdU detects DNA replication, not completed division. Endoreduplication and phase-duration changes can raise labeling without adding daughter cells.

42. A treatment increases primary root length from 5 to 8 cm while lateral root number remains 20. How does lateral root density change?

  1. Increases from 2.5 to 4 per cm
  2. Decreases from 4 to 2.5 per cm
  3. Remains 4 per cm
  4. Increases to 20 per cm
Answer and explanation

B — Decreases from 4 to 2.5 per cm. Density equals number divided by length. 20/5=4 and 20/8=2.5. Absolute initiation count is unchanged, so density cannot establish fewer initiation events.

43. A tissue has 100 pmol total cytokinin with 5 pmol free base; another has 60 pmol total and 15 pmol free base. If volume, access and receptor properties match, which is more likely to have greater immediate ligand input?

  1. The first because total is greater
  2. Both must be identical
  3. The second because free base is greater
  4. Neither can contain an active ligand
Answer and explanation

C — The second because free base is greater. For the stated matching conditions, the second has three times the free-base amount. The total pool may include weakly active or inactive species.

44. A researcher measures only shoot cytokinin after nitrate treatment and finds a decrease. Which additional measurements best distinguish reduced production from altered delivery?

  1. Only chlorophyll color
  2. Only flower number
  3. Only root length
  4. Root and shoot cytokinin forms, sap flux and a tracer or genetic transport test
Answer and explanation

D — Root and shoot cytokinin forms, sap flux and a tracer or genetic transport test. The additional measures separate source pools from transport and target availability. Phenotypes alone cannot distinguish those mechanisms.

45. A type-B arr mutant has poor WUS induction. Forced WUS expression restores a shoot stem-cell marker but not leaf longevity. What is supported?

  1. WUS is downstream for the rescued meristem output, not all cytokinin responses
  2. WUS is the universal CKX enzyme
  3. WUS is necessary for every cytokinin action without exception
  4. Cytokinin signaling is unrelated to WUS
Answer and explanation

A — WUS is downstream for the rescued meristem output, not all cytokinin responses. Selective rescue is evidence of a downstream role in a specified developmental context. The failure to restore another response reinforces pathway branching.

Assertion–reason and SET-style applications

46. Assertion: cytokinin can reduce Arabidopsis root meristem size. Reason: cytokinin can promote differentiation through ARR1–SHY2 regulation.

  1. Both true; reason explains assertion
  2. Both true; reason unrelated
  3. Assertion true; reason false
  4. Assertion false; reason true
Answer and explanation

A — Both true; reason explains assertion. Faster exit from the dividing population can reduce meristem size. The stated mechanism explains the observation in the relevant context.

47. Assertion: a high total cytokinin pool always means a high free-base concentration. Reason: cytokinin can occur as ribotides and glycosylated forms.

  1. Both true; reason explains assertion
  2. Both true; reason unrelated
  3. Assertion true; reason false
  4. Assertion false; reason true
Answer and explanation

D — Assertion false; reason true. Total includes chemically diverse species, so the free fraction can vary independently. The reason is true and explains why the assertion fails.

48. A student wants to distinguish delayed pigment loss from maintenance of photosynthesis. Which comparison is best?

  1. Measure color only
  2. Measure pigment plus gas exchange and appropriate fluorescence parameters
  3. Measure root hair number only
  4. Measure total DNA only
Answer and explanation

B — Measure pigment plus gas exchange and appropriate fluorescence parameters. Combining pigment measurements with physiological photosynthesis measures is more informative than color alone. Root hairs and DNA do not test leaf function directly.

49. A culture is treated with cytokinin dissolved in a small amount of solvent. Which negative control is essential?

  1. A culture lacking all mineral nutrients
  2. A culture of unrelated species with different age
  3. Matched medium with the same solvent but no added cytokinin
  4. A culture without any water
Answer and explanation

C — Matched medium with the same solvent but no added cytokinin. The solvent-only control separates cytokinin action from the vehicle. Other groups introduce additional variables.

50. A leaf contains 0.4 g dry root mass per plant and 0.8 g shoot mass before treatment; afterward root and shoot masses are 0.6 and 1.8 g. What is the correct allocation interpretation?

  1. Root mass decreased
  2. Root-to-shoot ratio increased
  3. Shoot mass decreased
  4. Both increased, but root-to-shoot ratio fell from 0.5 to 0.33
Answer and explanation

D — Both increased, but root-to-shoot ratio fell from 0.5 to 0.33. The ratio falls because the shoot grows proportionally more. Absolute root growth does not contradict a lower relative allocation.
