Original CSIR-NET Part B practice

These original questions are designed for practice; they are not claimed to be previous-year questions.

1. Which property defines an adequate stimulus?

  1. The stimulus to which the receptor is specialised and relatively sensitive
  2. The only event capable of activating the axon
  3. The smallest action potential amplitude
  4. The stimulus producing consciousness in every trial
Answer and scientific explanation

A. The stimulus to which the receptor is specialised and relatively sensitive

Adequacy describes receptor specialisation. Electrical stimulation can bypass transduction, and consciousness depends on subsequent circuits. Spike amplitude is not a criterion of adequacy.

2. A cutaneous mechanoreceptor and an arterial stretch receptor share which classification?

  1. Exteroceptors
  2. Mechanoreceptors
  3. Photoreceptors
  4. Separate epithelial receptor cells
Answer and scientific explanation

B. Mechanoreceptors

Both detect mechanical deformation. Their locations and cellular organisations need not be the same; the arterial receptor is an interoceptor.

3. Which event is sensory transduction?

  1. Conduction of a spike along an axon
  2. Interpretation of an object in cortex
  3. A mechanical displacement changing a receptor's membrane conductance
  4. Contraction of a muscle
Answer and scientific explanation

C. A mechanical displacement changing a receptor's membrane conductance

Transduction converts stimulus information into a cellular electrical signal. Conduction, central interpretation and motor output are subsequent processes.

4. The graded voltage change produced by a sensory stimulus is generally termed:

  1. Receptor potential
  2. An obligatory propagated spike
  3. Refractory period
  4. Conduction velocity
Answer and scientific explanation

A. Receptor potential

The receptor potential can vary with stimulus strength and need not produce a spike. The other terms describe properties of propagated excitation.

5. In a mature mammalian cochlear inner hair cell, the graded response directly regulates:

  1. Myelin thickness
  2. Glutamate release onto afferent neurons
  3. Action-potential amplitude in the optic nerve
  4. Skeletal muscle contraction
Answer and scientific explanation

B. Glutamate release onto afferent neurons

Hair-cell voltage regulates calcium-dependent transmitter release at the afferent synapse. The propagated auditory impulses occur in the sensory neuron.

6. Which receptor response illustrates that transduction need not depolarise the receptor?

  1. Ordinary opening of a cation conductance at a negative voltage
  2. Light-induced hyperpolarisation of a vertebrate rod
  3. A sodium-dependent axonal spike
  4. Opening of a nonselective cation channel at −60 mV with E = 0
Answer and scientific explanation

B. Light-induced hyperpolarisation of a vertebrate rod

Light closes cyclic-nucleotide-gated channels in rods, reducing inward current. The other listed events tend to depolarise a negative membrane or describe a spike.

7. For the convention I = g(V − E), a channel with E = 0 mV at V = −50 mV carries:

  1. Net outward positive current
  2. No current at any conductance
  3. Net inward positive current if g is nonzero
  4. Only potassium efflux regardless of selectivity
Answer and scientific explanation

C. Net inward positive current if g is nonzero

V − E is negative, so the conventional outward current is negative. The channel's ionic selectivity cannot be inferred solely from the sign.

8. The intensity of a suprathreshold stimulus is commonly represented in one sensory afferent by changes in:

  1. Spike height in direct proportion to intensity
  2. DNA content
  3. Axon length within milliseconds
  4. Impulse rate and timing
Answer and scientific explanation

D. Impulse rate and timing

Regenerative impulses are broadly all-or-none at a stable recording location. Rate and timing can vary with stimulus strength; recruitment operates across afferents.

9. Which response is most characteristic of a rapidly adapting receptor?

  1. Strong response at onset, little maintained response during an unchanged step
  2. No response to a changing stimulus
  3. Necessarily slow conduction
  4. A constant maximal response to all intensities
Answer and scientific explanation

A. Strong response at onset, little maintained response during an unchanged step

Phasic receptors emphasise changes. Adaptation describes the time course of activity under stimulation, not propagation speed.

10. A slowly adapting receptor is expected to:

  1. Generate no graded response
  2. Maintain some activity during a sustained adequate stimulus
  3. Respond only after stimulus removal
  4. Always lack accessory structures
Answer and scientific explanation

B. Maintain some activity during a sustained adequate stimulus

Tonic responses preserve information about a persistent stimulus. They can still decline from an onset peak and can have specialised associated tissue.

11. The receptive field of a cutaneous afferent is best defined as:

  1. The diameter of its axon
  2. The region of its cell body in a ganglion
  3. The skin region in which stimulation changes its response under stated conditions
  4. The distance between spinal cord and cortex
Answer and scientific explanation

C. The skin region in which stimulation changes its response under stated conditions

The field is defined functionally by stimulus-response mapping. It is not a geometric measurement of the axon or ganglion.

12. Convergence of several nearby receptors onto a downstream neuron can:

  1. Increase detection of weak combined input while broadening that neuron's receptive field
  2. Make all inputs anatomically identical
  3. Eliminate the need for transduction
  4. Always improve spatial discrimination
Answer and scientific explanation

A. Increase detection of weak combined input while broadening that neuron's receptive field

Summation can aid detection, but shared output can sacrifice location information. The broader network may preserve other inputs in parallel.

13. The labelled-line principle primarily relates modality to:

  1. The size of individual spikes
  2. The anatomical and functional identity of the activated pathway
  3. The mass of the receptor cell
  4. The colour of the sensory nerve
Answer and scientific explanation

B. The anatomical and functional identity of the activated pathway

Pathway identity and connections give impulses sensory meaning. Spike amplitude and gross nerve appearance are not modality codes.

14. Lateral inhibition often improves:

  1. The amplitude of every action potential
  2. The physical intensity of the incoming light
  3. Contrast between neighbouring sensory representations
  4. The number of photoreceptors formed during each stimulus
Answer and scientific explanation

C. Contrast between neighbouring sensory representations

The circuit changes relative neural output to emphasise spatial differences; it does not modify the physical light or necessarily raise absolute firing.

15. Uniform illumination of an ON-centre, OFF-surround field generally produces less excitation than illumination restricted to the centre because:

  1. The surround opposes centre excitation
  2. The optic nerve conducts only during darkness
  3. The centre no longer contains receptors
  4. All inhibitory synapses disappear
Answer and scientific explanation

A. The surround opposes centre excitation

The antagonistic surround contributes an opposing effect when illuminated. This comparison assumes a suitable operating state and stimulus geometry.

16. Which measurement directly distinguishes an absolute detection threshold from a difference threshold?

  1. Axonal diameter
  2. Whether the task compares a stimulus with absence or compares two stimulus intensities
  3. The number of synapses in a nerve
  4. The peak voltage of a propagated spike
Answer and scientific explanation

B. Whether the task compares a stimulus with absence or compares two stimulus intensities

Detection concerns presence relative to a blank; discrimination concerns reliably distinguishing two stimuli. Neither is determined by spike amplitude alone.

17. The Weber fraction is expressed as:

  1. S/ΔS²
  2. ΔS in volts for all senses
  3. ΔS/S
  4. ln(0)
Answer and scientific explanation

C. ΔS/S

It is a dimensionless ratio of just-noticeable increment to reference intensity. Its approximate constancy is restricted to suitable operating ranges.

18. Which observation alone is insufficient to establish peripheral receptor adaptation?

  1. A decline in measured receptor current with controlled stimulation
  2. A reduction in generator potential despite maintained external force
  3. A smaller response to a natural probe with normal current-injection responses
  4. A subject ceasing to report noticing a background stimulus
Answer and scientific explanation

D. A subject ceasing to report noticing a background stimulus

A behavioural decline can reflect central attention or habituation. The other observations provide physiological evidence at specified stages, though their interpretation still requires controls.

19. The main significance of a sensory receptor's saturation is that:

  1. Increments in a very strong stimulus may produce little additional output
  2. The neuron always dies
  3. The stimulus is no longer physically present
  4. All other receptors must also saturate
Answer and scientific explanation

A. Increments in a very strong stimulus may produce little additional output

Saturation compresses the measurable response at high input. Other populations may still distinguish intensities.

20. Which statement best distinguishes detection from perception?

  1. Both are identical to action-potential propagation
  2. Peripheral sensory activity supplies evidence, while interpretation depends on neural processing
  3. Every receptor potential is a conscious experience
  4. Perception is always completed inside an accessory organ
Answer and scientific explanation

B. Peripheral sensory activity supplies evidence, while interpretation depends on neural processing

A receptor initiates signals, but central processing determines their interpretation. Physiological sensory activity may also drive unconscious reflexes.

Original CSIR-NET Part C practice

These original questions are designed for practice; they are not claimed to be previous-year questions.

1. A 3 nS stimulus-gated conductance has E = +10 mV. At V = −40 mV, what current is predicted by the ohmic model?

  1. +150 pA
  2. −150 pA
  3. −30 pA
  4. 0 pA
Answer and scientific explanation

B. −150 pA

V − E = −50 mV, and 3 nS × −50 mV = −150 pA. The negative outward-current convention means inward current; +150 reverses the sign.

2. A receptor has R = 150 MΩ and C = 80 pF in a passive approximation. What is τ?

  1. 1.2 ms
  2. 120 ms
  3. 12 ms
  4. 0.12 ms
Answer and scientific explanation

C. 12 ms

150 × 10⁶ × 80 × 10⁻¹² s = 0.012 s = 12 ms. This is a passive-model time constant, not an adaptation constant or conduction delay.

3. A graded terminal response of 18 mV reaches a trigger region with 50% attenuation. The trigger rests at −64 mV and threshold is −50 mV. What is predicted?

  1. Voltage reaches −46 mV and fires
  2. Voltage reaches −55 mV and stays subthreshold
  3. Voltage becomes −73 mV
  4. No transduction occurs
Answer and scientific explanation

B. Voltage reaches −55 mV and stays subthreshold

Only 9 mV reaches the trigger region: −64 + 9 = −55 mV. The stimulus was transduced but the model predicts no spike.

4. A receptor fires 24 spikes in 0.6 s at stimulus A and 24 spikes in 0.3 s at B. Which comparison is correct?

  1. A has twice B's rate
  2. Both have the same rate
  3. A = 40 Hz; B = 80 Hz
  4. B must be a 80 Hz physical vibration
Answer and scientific explanation

C. A = 40 Hz; B = 80 Hz

Rate is count divided by time: 24/0.6 = 40, 24/0.3 = 80. These values do not establish the physical frequency of the stimuli.

5. An idealised afferent has a 2.5 ms absolute refractory period. Which is the appropriate upper bound from that fact alone?

  1. 40 Hz
  2. 400 Hz
  3. 250 Hz
  4. 2500 Hz
Answer and scientific explanation

B. 400 Hz

1/0.0025 s = 400 s⁻¹. Actual sustainable rate can be lower because recovery and charging take additional time.

6. Four idealised receptors have thresholds 1, 4, 6 and 9 units. The first is saturated by 5 units. Increasing intensity from 5 to 8 units can nevertheless be encoded because:

  1. The saturated fibre must increase spike height
  2. The stimulus becomes a different modality
  3. All four fibres become active
  4. The third fibre is recruited and other nonsaturated responses can change
Answer and scientific explanation

D. The third fibre is recruited and other nonsaturated responses can change

At 5 units the first two are active; at 8 the third also crosses threshold. The fourth remains below threshold; saturation of one fibre does not saturate the population.

7. Two stimuli evoke counts (45, 15) and (15, 45) in two identified afferents. What analysis preserves the distinction?

  1. Sum the counts only
  2. Compare the ordered response pair
  3. Average spike amplitude only
  4. Discard the identity of each fibre
Answer and scientific explanation

B. Compare the ordered response pair

Both sums are 60, but their ordered patterns differ. Preserving fibre identity carries spatial or feature information unavailable in the sum.

8. In two one-second trials each afferent produces 12 spikes. One has tightly phase-aligned spike times and the other has dispersed phases. Which inference is supported?

  1. Their mean rates differ
  2. The physical inputs must be identical
  3. Their timing codes differ despite equal mean rate
  4. Phase locking requires one spike on every cycle
Answer and scientific explanation

C. Their timing codes differ despite equal mean rate

The total rate is the same, but timing relative to the stimulus differs. Phase locking is statistical alignment and does not require a spike in every cycle.

9. An adapted response obeys r = 10 + 60e^(−t/2) Hz, t in seconds. Estimate r after 2 s.

  1. 10 Hz
  2. 32.1 Hz
  3. 70 Hz
  4. 60 Hz
Answer and scientific explanation

B. 32.1 Hz

At one time constant the excess is 60/e ≈ 22.1 Hz, giving 32.1 Hz. The 10 Hz maintained component must be retained.

10. Two receptors receive identical verified step inputs. A fires strongly at onset then returns to baseline; B declines from 70 to 30 Hz and remains elevated. Which conclusion is best?

  1. A is phasic and B has a tonic component
  2. A has slower axonal conduction
  3. B cannot adapt
  4. The maintained stimulus must have disappeared for both
Answer and scientific explanation

A. A is phasic and B has a tonic component

A reports the transition preferentially, while B retains information about the maintained step. Both can adapt; neither time course specifies conduction speed.

11. Removing a receptor's capsule increases the duration of its generator potential without changing its response to current injection. Which hypothesis gains support?

  1. The capsule contributes mechanical filtering
  2. The capsule generates propagated spikes
  3. The capsule is necessarily the sole adaptation mechanism
  4. Axonal sodium channels are absent
Answer and scientific explanation

A. The capsule contributes mechanical filtering

Changing the accessory structure alters natural stimulus coupling while tested excitability remains intact. Residual adaptation may still arise from other processes.

12. A sustained stimulus evokes stable receptor current and stable local voltage, but spike frequency declines. Which mechanism is a plausible contributor?

  1. Failure to deliver any stimulus
  2. Loss of all receptor channels
  3. Spike-frequency adaptation at the excitable region
  4. Change of adequate stimulus from touch to light
Answer and scientific explanation

C. Spike-frequency adaptation at the excitable region

The measured transduction and local voltage persist, directing attention to downstream excitability, coupling or recording issues rather than complete loss of transduction.

13. A test receptor fires at 60 Hz when illuminated alone and 36 Hz when neighbouring units are illuminated with its own light held constant. Which design best tests whether suppression is lateral rather than ordinary adaptation?

  1. Use matched repeated test-alone and test-plus-neighbour trials at comparable elapsed times
  2. Increase the test light every trial
  3. Record only the final combined trial
  4. Change both illumination and electrode position simultaneously
Answer and scientific explanation

A. Use matched repeated test-alone and test-plus-neighbour trials at comparable elapsed times

Matched trials isolate the neighbour manipulation and control time-dependent adaptation. Varying several factors or omitting baseline prevents specific inference.

14. For yB = max(0, xB − 0.2(xA + xC)), drives are (30, 70, 50). What is yB?

  1. 70
  2. 54
  3. 86
  4. 14
Answer and scientific explanation

B. 54

The neighbouring sum is 80, its weighted inhibitory contribution is 16, and output is 70 − 16 = 54. Adding the inhibition instead reverses its sign.

15. In a uniform region each channel has drive 40 and two neighbours of 40. In the model with k = 0.25, what is the interior output?

  1. 0
  2. 40
  3. 20
  4. 60
Answer and scientific explanation

C. 20

40 − 0.25(40 + 40) = 20. Uniform input need not be completely cancelled; the model's weights determine the residual.

16. An ON-centre cell fires 45 Hz with centre-only light, 20 Hz at baseline and 25 Hz with centre plus surround. Which result is most consistent with the observations?

  1. The surround has net antagonistic influence
  2. The surround must directly produce an identical excitatory response
  3. The centre becomes anatomically absent
  4. The recording alone identifies every synapse in the retina
Answer and scientific explanation

A. The surround has net antagonistic influence

Adding surround light reduces excitation from 45 to 25 while the centre is stimulated. This supports functional antagonism, but does not specify the exact circuit.

17. A skin region with large convergent fields and another with small densely sampled fields have equal single-probe detection. Which prediction is most defensible?

  1. They necessarily have identical two-point thresholds
  2. The densely sampled region may discriminate smaller separations
  3. The larger-field region cannot detect any probe
  4. The densely sampled region must have lower spike amplitudes
Answer and scientific explanation

B. The densely sampled region may discriminate smaller separations

Detection and spatial resolution can dissociate. Smaller fields and maintained spatial organisation tend to improve discrimination, although central processing and probe geometry matter.

18. In a two-alternative forced-choice task, 50% correct occurs at chance, 75% at amplitude 4 and 90% at amplitude 7. With a 75% criterion, the threshold is:

  1. 0
  2. 7
  3. 4
  4. Not definable because 50% is chance
Answer and scientific explanation

C. 4

The specified criterion is reached at amplitude 4. It is a probabilistic estimate for this task, not a fixed boundary below which detection is impossible.

19. A yes/no task yields hits and false alarms of 70% and 10% before a change, and 85% and 30% afterward. Which interpretation is justified?

  1. Peripheral sensitivity necessarily improved
  2. The receptor potential necessarily doubled
  3. No decision criterion could account for this
  4. A more liberal response criterion is a plausible contributor
Answer and scientific explanation

D. A more liberal response criterion is a plausible contributor

Both positive reports rose, including false reports on blank trials. The pattern alone does not prove improved sensory sensitivity or a specific peripheral mechanism.

20. A receptor's natural-stimulus current is reduced at a fixed clamp voltage; its injected-current spike response is normal. Which conclusion is supported?

  1. A defect at or upstream of measured transduction is plausible; the tested spike generator can function
  2. All central processing is proven intact
  3. The exact gene mutation is identified
  4. The natural stimulus must be stronger
Answer and scientific explanation

A. A defect at or upstream of measured transduction is plausible; the tested spike generator can function

The two measurements narrow the likely location but do not distinguish mechanical coupling, channel availability or driving-force changes without further tests.

Original SET and integrated practice

These original questions are designed for practice; they are not claimed to be previous-year questions.

1. SET: Which pair is correctly matched?

  1. Photoreceptor — photon absorption
  2. Mechanoreceptor — only chemical ligand binding
  3. Interoceptor — necessarily outside the body
  4. Phasic receptor — necessarily slow axonal conduction
Answer and scientific explanation

A. Photoreceptor — photon absorption

Photoreceptors specialise in light detection. Mechanoreception, internal location and phasic adaptation are independent concepts.

2. SET: Which is an example of accessory mechanical filtering?

  1. Voltage regeneration in a myelinated axon
  2. Mechanical stress redistribution through a receptor capsule
  3. Transmitter binding in cerebral cortex
  4. A change in DNA sequence
Answer and scientific explanation

B. Mechanical stress redistribution through a receptor capsule

Capsule mechanics can modify the temporal pattern of deformation reaching a receptor ending, upstream of transduction.

3. Assertion–reason: Assertion: sensory impulse rate can increase without an increase in spike amplitude. Reason: the graded input can shorten the interval between regenerative impulses. Choose the correct relation.

  1. Both true; reason explains assertion
  2. Both true; reason does not explain assertion
  3. Assertion true; reason false
  4. Assertion false; reason true
Answer and scientific explanation

A. Both true; reason explains assertion

Stronger maintained depolarising drive can bring the excitable region to firing conditions more often; individual propagated impulses remain broadly all-or-none.

4. Assertion–reason: Assertion: rapidly adapting receptors are especially useful for detecting changes. Reason: they necessarily conduct impulses more slowly than tonic receptors.

  1. Both true; reason explains assertion
  2. Both true; reason does not explain assertion
  3. Assertion true; reason false
  4. Assertion false; reason true
Answer and scientific explanation

C. Assertion true; reason false

The assertion follows from the time course of the response. Adaptation is not conduction velocity, so the reason is false.

5. Numerical: Weber fraction is 0.04 at an intermediate reference intensity of 200 units. Under the same assumptions, the predicted JND is:

  1. 0.0002 units
  2. 5 units
  3. 8 units
  4. 50 units
Answer and scientific explanation

C. 8 units

ΔS = kS = 0.04 × 200 = 8 units. The prediction is valid only within the assumed Weber range.

6. Numerical: In the Fechner model P₂ − P₁ = a ln(S₂/S₁), what happens when both reference and comparison intensities are multiplied by the same positive factor?

  1. The predicted difference doubles
  2. The difference remains unchanged
  3. The difference becomes zero in every case
  4. The expression becomes undefined
Answer and scientific explanation

B. The difference remains unchanged

The common factor cancels from the ratio, preserving its logarithm. The conclusion concerns the idealised model, not every actual perceptual scale.

7. Numerical: A hypothetical response R = 90S/(3 + S) gives what output at S = 6?

  1. 30
  2. 45
  3. 60
  4. 90
Answer and scientific explanation

C. 60

R = 90 × 6/9 = 60 response units. The maximum 90 is approached asymptotically rather than reached at S = 6.

8. Experimental: A receptor's response to a probe is reduced after a conditioning stimulus but recovers with a longer interval. What control is especially informative?

  1. Repeat the probe without the conditioning stimulus at matched times
  2. Remove all measurement units
  3. Change the electrode on each trial
  4. Use a different receptor for every interval without replication
Answer and scientific explanation

A. Repeat the probe without the conditioning stimulus at matched times

Probe-alone controls help separate recovery from drift or repeated testing effects. Keeping other conditions comparable improves the inference.

9. Integrated: A mutation reduces transmitter release from a hair cell while leaving its graded response intact. What is a likely result?

  1. The hair cell cannot possibly transduce movement
  2. Afferent excitation may be reduced despite preserved receptor voltage
  3. The optic nerve necessarily fires faster
  4. Every somatosensory receptive field enlarges
Answer and scientific explanation

B. Afferent excitation may be reduced despite preserved receptor voltage

Voltage-to-release coupling is a distinct step, so normal voltage does not guarantee normal afferent transmission. The other effects are not implied.

10. Integrated: Two points on skin produce indistinguishable activity in every available peripheral channel. Which statement about lateral inhibition is correct?

  1. It guarantees perfect discrimination
  2. It can recover an arbitrarily exact distance from identical inputs
  3. It cannot reconstruct spatial differences absent from the available input
  4. It changes the physical separation of the probes
Answer and scientific explanation

C. It cannot reconstruct spatial differences absent from the available input

Inhibition can accentuate sampled differences but cannot uniquely distinguish inputs that are genuinely identical at every relevant channel.
