7.J.01 — Examination and mastery material

Part B: original questions

  1. Which property primarily distinguishes an exocrine secretion from a classical endocrine secretion?

    1. Release into a duct or epithelial lumen rather than delivery to distant targets by blood
    2. Whether the product contains amino acids
    3. Whether the source is a gland
    4. Whether secretion depends on calcium
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: A. Exocrine products are delivered to a surface or lumen, often through ducts. Both endocrine and exocrine products may be proteins, originate in glandular cells and use calcium-dependent secretion.

  2. A hypothalamic neuron releases a peptide into a blood vessel. Which description best identifies this route?

    1. Paracrine only
    2. Neuroendocrine
    3. Exocrine
    4. Direct gap-junction signalling
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: B. A neuron is the source and the vascular system is the delivery route. Paracrine signalling acts locally; exocrine secretion reaches a lumen or surface; gap junctions transfer small molecules directly between adjacent cells.

  3. Which pairing correctly describes a hormone class and representative member?

    1. Steroid — insulin
    2. Peptide — cortisol
    3. Amino-acid derived — epinephrine
    4. Catecholamine — thyroxine
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: C. Epinephrine is a tyrosine-derived catecholamine. Insulin is a peptide; cortisol is a cholesterol-derived steroid. Thyroxine is also tyrosine-derived but is an iodinated thyroid hormone, not a catecholamine.

  4. Which process is most directly required for mature peptide hormone formation from a preprohormone?

    1. Iodination of plasma albumin
    2. Conversion of cholesterol to pregnenolone
    3. DNA methylation of a carrier protein
    4. Removal of signal sequence and appropriate precursor processing
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: D. Signal peptide removal and subsequent proteolytic processing produce many mature peptide hormones. Iodination concerns thyroid synthesis; cholesterol conversion initiates steroidogenesis; DNA methylation is not cleavage of a peptide precursor.

  5. Which statement best characterizes most steroid-secreting endocrine cells?

    1. The mature steroids are commonly synthesized as needed rather than stored in large regulated peptide-like secretory granules
    2. Steroids are ribosomally translated directly
    3. Steroids are synthesized exclusively in the Golgi
    4. Steroids always circulate entirely free
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: A. Steroids generally arise from enzymatic conversion of cholesterol in mitochondrial and smooth ER pathways and leave cells after synthesis. They are not translated as proteins, not exclusively formed in the Golgi, and many circulate associated with carrier proteins.

  6. Thyroid hormones are an important exception to the usual properties of many amino-acid-derived hormones because they:

    1. are encoded as mature free hormones by messenger RNA
    2. are stored extracellularly as iodinated residues in thyroglobulin and circulate substantially protein-bound
    3. are stored exclusively in neuronal synaptic vesicles
    4. are always water-soluble and act only at cell-surface receptors
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: B. Thyroid follicles store iodinated thyroglobulin in colloid and circulating thyroid hormones are extensively protein-bound. Their formation involves modification of tyrosyl residues rather than direct translation of T3/T4; they are not exclusively synaptic or surface-receptor signals.

  7. Which item is a stimulus sensed directly by an endocrine cell in a humoral control arrangement?

    1. A secretory vesicle
    2. A nuclear receptor bound to a different target hormone
    3. A change in extracellular ion or nutrient concentration
    4. A duct opening into the intestine
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: C. A humoral stimulus is a change in the chemical composition of the extracellular environment, such as calcium or glucose. Vesicles are storage structures; receptors and ducts are not the specified regulated extracellular variable.

  8. What does a hormone's metabolic clearance rate express?

    1. The mass of hormone in the entire body
    2. The rate of hormone synthesis in molecules per minute
    3. The proportion bound to a carrier
    4. An effective volume of fluid cleared of hormone per unit time
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: D. Clearance has dimensions of volume/time, such as L/min. Multiplying it by concentration gives amount removed per time in a linear model. Mass, production and binding fractions are different quantities.

  9. What is the principal role of a conventional plasma transport protein for a lipophilic hormone?

    1. Reversible binding that affects circulation and availability
    2. Producing new hormone molecules after synthesis stops
    3. Directly replacing the hormone receptor in every target
    4. Irreversibly destroying all bound hormone
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: A. Reversible association partitions hormone between free and carrier-bound pools. A carrier is not a synthetic enzyme, not automatically a signalling receptor, and binding does not by definition destroy the ligand.

  10. A hormone undergoes first-order disappearance after secretion stops. Which quantity remains constant in equal time intervals?

    1. Absolute amount removed
    2. Fraction of the current amount removed
    3. Concentration removed in nM
    4. Number of molecules newly secreted
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: B. First-order disappearance removes the same fraction in equal time intervals, so absolute amount removed becomes smaller as concentration declines. Secretion has stopped in the specified model.

  11. In a classical negative-feedback endocrine axis, what is the effect of increasing the final hormone, other inputs held constant?

    1. Every upstream cell is destroyed
    2. All hormones cease permanently
    3. Upstream stimulatory drive tends to decrease
    4. Peripheral blood flow necessarily stops
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: C. Negative feedback opposes increased output by reducing upstream drive in the specified axis. It does not require tissue destruction, permanent cessation or a universal change in blood flow.

  12. Which example can exhibit positive feedback under a particular physiological condition?

    1. Carrier binding at equilibrium
    2. Linear metabolic clearance
    3. A constitutive exocrine duct
    4. Sustained estradiol exposure contributing to the preovulatory LH surge
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: D. The preovulatory axis can switch to positive estradiol feedback in the appropriate context. Binding equilibrium and clearance do not themselves describe that amplifying regulatory loop; an exocrine duct is a structural route.

  13. Which feature establishes that a cell is a potential direct target of a particular hormone?

    1. Expression of an appropriate functional receptor and access to ligand
    2. Proximity to the source alone
    3. Possession of a nucleus alone
    4. Exposure to blood alone
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: A. Specific recognition and accessibility are required for direct signalling. Nearness, a nucleus and exposure to blood cannot establish receptor competence; different cells in the same circulation respond differently.

  14. Which statement best distinguishes receptor binding affinity from physiological sensitivity?

    1. They are always identical
    2. Affinity concerns ligand binding; sensitivity describes the concentration-response relation
    3. Sensitivity measures only hormone clearance
    4. Affinity is the maximal response
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: B. Affinity is associated with the equilibrium or kinetics of ligand binding. Operational sensitivity may depend on affinity, receptor abundance, coupling and tissue physiology. Clearance and maximal response are distinct quantities.

  15. An approximately 24-hour endogenous pattern of hormone secretion is termed:

    1. ultradian by definition
    2. a permanent steady state
    3. circadian
    4. a positive-feedback loop by definition
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: C. Circadian rhythms have a period near one day and can be synchronized to environmental cues. Ultradian rhythms recur faster than daily, and neither steady state nor feedback sign defines circadian timing.

  16. Which term describes one hormone allowing a second hormone to exert its normal effect without necessarily having a large direct effect on the same variable?

    1. Exocytosis
    2. Constitutive secretion
    3. Antagonism
    4. Permissiveness
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: D. Permissiveness concerns conditioning of the target or its response capacity. Exocytosis and constitutive secretion describe release; antagonism denotes opposing effects on a specified variable.

  17. Which pair illustrates opposing hormonal effects on hepatic glucose output in common mammalian physiology?

    1. Insulin and glucagon
    2. Insulin and insulin
    3. Thyroglobulin and albumin
    4. Secretin and pancreatic duct
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: A. Insulin generally suppresses hepatic glucose output, whereas glucagon promotes it. Identical insulin molecules cannot illustrate distinct opposing hormonal actions; thyroglobulin and albumin are proteins with other roles; a duct is not a hormone.

  18. Which conclusion follows from detection of a hormone in tissue homogenate?

    1. It must be synthesized in every cell of the tissue
    2. It is present in the sampled tissue, but local synthesis and function need independent evidence
    3. It must have been secreted into blood at a constant rate
    4. All its receptors are occupied
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: B. Detection establishes presence within the assay's specificity. The molecule might have arrived from blood or been stored. Neither synthesis location, release kinetics nor receptor occupancy follows directly.

  19. In an endocrine-versus-neural comparison, which claim is scientifically defensible?

    1. Neural communication always lasts longer
    2. Every endocrine signal acts only after hours
    3. Speed alone does not uniquely distinguish the route of signalling
    4. Endocrine signals never originate in neurons
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: C. Endocrine signals can act rapidly, and neural changes can have prolonged consequences. The route and cellular source determine the communication classification; neurosecretory neurons can release hormones into blood.

  20. Which comparative statement about animal endocrine systems is accurate?

    1. Only mammals use hormones
    2. All animals possess anatomically identical pituitary glands
    3. Insect ecdysteroids are peptide neurotransmitters
    4. Hormonal regulation occurs in diverse animals, but specific glands and developmental responses differ
    Answer and reasoning

    Correct answer: D. Endocrine coordination is widespread in animals. Anatomical organization and molecular signals vary; insect ecdysteroids are steroid hormones, not peptide transmitters. Similar functional principles do not establish homologous glands.

Part C: additional original analytical questions

  1. In a two-level axis A stimulates B and B inhibits A. An intact upstream source produces abundant A despite persistently low B. Give two different mechanisms compatible with the observation and a test that distinguishes them.

    Answer and reasoning

    Failure of the B gland to synthesize or release B and failure of that gland to respond to A are both compatible. Compare B release after a controlled A challenge and assess gland viability or synthesis capacity under an independent stimulus. A low response to A alone does not uniquely distinguish receptor failure from loss of gland cells.

  2. A labelled precursor enters a peptide-producing cell. Label appears first in a large protein, later in a smaller mature product. A processing inhibitor prevents accumulation of the smaller product but not the larger one. What is supported?

    Answer and reasoning

    The temporal order and inhibitor response support precursor-to-product maturation. The result is consistent with proteolytic processing rather than independent direct translation of the smaller hormone; specificity and cell viability controls are necessary. Demonstrating release of the smaller product separately would address secretion.

  3. A steroidogenic cell exposed to a trophic signal releases more final steroid while cholesterol-derived intermediate pools change. A protein synthesis inhibitor added briefly does not prevent the early response. Does this prove transcription is never important?

    Answer and reasoning

    No. Existing enzymes and substrate transport can support a rapid response before newly translated protein is needed. Longer-term maintenance of steroidogenic capacity may depend on transcription and translation. The time scale of the inhibition defines the conclusion.

  4. In a tissue, free hormone and receptor number are constant while an enzyme that converts a circulating precursor into a more active ligand is inhibited. Predict a possible change in response and explain why plasma measurements may be insufficient.

    Answer and reasoning

    Local active ligand availability and receptor stimulation may decline even if precursor concentration and receptor abundance are unchanged. Tissue conversion is a determinant of effective exposure; measuring active hormone inside the tissue or rescuing with the active product would better constrain the mechanism.

  5. A study compares two groups using single samples taken at 07:00 and 19:00 respectively. A hormone is lower in the evening-sampled group. Identify a design limitation and propose a correction.

    Answer and reasoning

    Group and clock time are confounded. A circadian rhythm could explain the apparent difference without altered endocrine capacity. Sample both groups at the same phase and preferably characterize repeated measurements across the day, controlling relevant sleep, feeding and other timing conditions.

  6. In a simple regulated system, removal of circulating final hormone causes a rise in upstream secretion. Why does the same upstream rise not necessarily establish a primary upstream tumour?

    Answer and reasoning

    The rise may be a physiological compensation for loss of inhibitory feedback. Measuring the downstream hormone and examining responses to restoration of feedback helps distinguish appropriate compensation from autonomous production. Concentration of one signal does not locate the causal lesion.

  7. In two animal species, the same chemically identified hormone binds a target receptor, but a developmental response occurs only in one species at the tested stage. Is receptor binding evidence sufficient for equivalent biological function?

    Answer and reasoning

    No. Receptor binding establishes a recognition event, whereas developmental competence, downstream partners, timing and target tissues can differ. A stage-matched dose-response experiment and measurement of relevant downstream events would be needed to assess functional equivalence.

  8. A local endocrine tissue culture secretes a factor. Conditioned medium stimulates a neighbouring cell population. In an intact animal the factor is not detected in peripheral blood. Can the factor still have a physiological signalling role?

    Answer and reasoning

    Yes. It may operate through paracrine communication and be degraded or diluted before peripheral detection. Local release, appropriate receptor expression and loss-of-function or neutralization experiments can test its contribution. Culture evidence alone does not prove the same role in vivo.

Assertion–reason, numerical and conceptual practice

  1. Assertion: A hormone with a long plasma half-life always has a slow onset of action. Reason: Half-life describes disappearance of hormone rather than the initial signalling rate.

    Answer and reasoning

    Assertion false; reason true. Binding to a rapid signalling receptor can initiate action long before much hormone disappears. Onset also depends on delivery, receptor activation and downstream processes.

  2. Assertion: Endocrine specificity can exist despite wide vascular distribution. Reason: Cells differ in expression and functional coupling of hormone receptors.

    Answer and reasoning

    Both are true, and the reason explains the assertion. Vascular delivery does not require identical biological responses. Local transport and metabolism can add additional selectivity.

  3. Assertion: More carrier-bound hormone necessarily means more free hormone. Reason: Total hormone equals free plus bound hormone.

    Answer and reasoning

    Assertion false; reason true. Changing carrier abundance can alter bound and total pools while free concentration remains similar. A mass balance alone cannot specify the equilibrium distribution.

  4. A hormone concentration starts at 64 nM and decreases to 16 nM in 12 min after secretion stops. Estimate half-life under a first-order model.

    Answer and reasoning

    The concentration falls by a factor of four, representing two half-lives. Half-life = 12/2 = 6 min. The result assumes no further secretion and first-order disappearance.

  5. The measured concentration of a hormone doubles while its independently determined clearance remains 3 L/min and the system is at steady state. What happens to net secretion?

    Answer and reasoning

    Under the linear steady-state model S = CL × C, secretion doubles. Unlike a concentration-only inference, clearance is independently constrained here; both steady state and comparable concentration definitions matter.

  6. A sample contains 120 nM total hormone and 3 nM free hormone. Calculate bound concentration and percent free.

    Answer and reasoning

    Bound = 120 − 3 = 117 nM. Percent free = 100 × 3/120 = 2.5%. Carrier-bound fraction is therefore 97.5%.

  7. A hormone has Kd = 4 nM and free concentration = 12 nM at equilibrium. What is the fractional occupancy for independent identical receptor sites?

    Answer and reasoning

    Occupancy = 12/(4+12) = 0.75. This is 75% binding-site occupancy, not necessarily 75% physiological response.

  8. A secretion route involves a hormone-producing epithelial cell that acts back on the same cell. Another target nearby also responds. Classify the two routes.

    Answer and reasoning

    The self-directed effect is autocrine; the neighbouring-cell effect is paracrine. The same molecule can participate in both modes depending on the target.

  9. In an acid-stimulated intestinal preparation, secretion by a remote gland continues after interruption of a specific neural route. What alternative signalling hypothesis gains support?

    Answer and reasoning

    A transferable chemical signal provides a plausible alternative. A stronger demonstration uses transfer of active material and appropriate controls. Persistence after denervation does not exclude every neural contribution under other conditions.

  10. A physiological variable rises, a gland releases a factor, and the resulting target response pushes that variable downward. Is it necessary that the factor concentration itself return immediately to baseline for the loop to be negative?

    Answer and reasoning

    No. The sign is determined by the effect of the loop on the initial deviation. The factor may remain elevated temporarily due to sustained input or clearance; negative feedback does not require instantaneous restoration.

Common misconceptions and examination traps

Every hormone is produced by a classical ductless gland

Many tissues and neurosecretory cells release hormones; the communication route matters more than the presence of a dedicated gland.

Reasoning: A question may describe renal or intestinal cells rather than named glands. Identify the source and delivery route.

Neuroendocrine means neurotransmission

A neurosecretory cell delivers its product into circulation, unlike a conventional synaptic signal.

Reasoning: A neuronal source is often mistaken for a synaptic route. Identify where the chemical messenger is released.

Endocrine means slow

Hormonal response speed depends on delivery and target mechanisms; catecholamine actions can be rapid.

Reasoning: Do not classify signalling solely by onset time.

All amino-acid-derived hormones behave alike

Catecholamines and thyroid hormones differ strongly in storage, transport and typical receptor access.

Reasoning: A shared precursor does not imply shared physicochemical properties.

Peptide hormones are all secreted immediately when synthesized

Many are processed and stored before regulated exocytosis.

Reasoning: Distinguish transcription, precursor maturation, storage and release.

Steroid synthesis is protein translation

Steroids arise through enzymatic conversion of cholesterol; the enzymes themselves are proteins.

Reasoning: A translation-inhibition experiment may affect enzyme renewal without immediately abolishing all steroid synthesis.

Bound hormone is permanently unavailable

Reversible binding permits exchange; carrier-associated hormone may replenish the free pool.

Reasoning: Distinguish current free concentration from irreversible elimination.

High total hormone proves excessive free hormone

Binding protein abundance and affinity can change the distribution.

Reasoning: A paired free measurement is crucial when a carrier change is suspected.

Concentration equals secretion rate

Concentration depends on net input, distribution and removal.

Reasoning: An exam can hold input fixed and change clearance; check the units.

A longer half-life necessarily means stronger action

Half-life describes persistence; potency and response depend on the target.

Reasoning: Do not infer receptor efficacy from a disappearance curve.

A negative feedback loop always suppresses every hormone

Negative feedback opposes the initial deviation; upstream hormones can rise when a downstream hormone falls.

Reasoning: Trace the signs of the actual circuit rather than assume all levels change together.

Positive feedback is always pathological

Some regulated physiological transitions use temporarily amplifying loops, such as the preovulatory surge.

Reasoning: Ask how the loop terminates and in which physiological state it operates.

EC50 and Kd are identical

EC50 describes a measured response; Kd describes binding equilibrium in a specified model.

Reasoning: If receptor reserve or downstream amplification changes, the response curve can shift independently.

Two hormones together exceeding either alone prove synergy

Synergy requires comparison with a specified additive or other justified null model.

Reasoning: Calculate the predicted combined effect using a shared baseline before interpreting the difference.

A single normal concentration establishes a normal rhythm

Sparse measurements can miss pulses and circadian changes.

Reasoning: Look for sampling intervals and phase matching before concluding that secretion is normal.

  2. Compare endocrine, neuroendocrine, autocrine and paracrine routes using a source and a target.
  3. Trace a peptide hormone from precursor translation to regulated release.
  4. Contrast steroid production with thyroid colloid storage.
  5. Derive bound-hormone concentration for finite independent carrier sites.
  6. Explain why a rise in total hormone need not increase its free fraction.
  7. Derive steady-state concentration from a linear mass-balance model.
  8. Relate half-life to clearance and effective distribution volume.
  9. Explain how repeated pulses can change concentration peaks and troughs without altering mean input.
  10. Predict upstream and downstream concentrations after peripheral gland failure.
  11. Design a comparison that distinguishes permissiveness from an additive simultaneous effect.
  12. Explain why receptor occupancy and maximum physiological response may not track each other.

Mastery checklist

Explain the route of a signal, identify its chemical class and distinguish its source from the target. Predict the consequences of changes in synthesis, release, transport, clearance and receptor competence. Interpret concentration curves with explicit assumptions about secretion and sampling. Trace feedback arrows and evaluate hormonal interactions against appropriate comparison conditions.
