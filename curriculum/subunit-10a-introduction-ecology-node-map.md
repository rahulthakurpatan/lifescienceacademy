## Subunit contract

Explore the fundamental relationships between organisms and their environment, progressing from ecological organisation and environmental factors to habitat selection, ecological niches, adaptations and geographical patterns of life.

**Planned nodes:** 5. See [Unit 10 master map](unit-10-ecology-behavioural-biology-subunit-map.md) for the full syllabus and legacy crosswalk.

## Node contracts

### 10.A.01 — Foundations and Levels of Ecological Organisation

**Student-facing description:** Explore the history, scope and branches of ecology, connecting organisms, populations, communities, ecosystems, biomes and the biosphere through ecological investigation.

**Core scope:** Autecology and synecology; levels of organisation, spatial and temporal scales, emergent properties; observational, experimental and modelling approaches; field versus laboratory studies, hypotheses, replication, controls and confounding; links with evolution and physiology.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Classify ecological questions by scale; compare observational and experimental evidence and design a conceptual controlled ecological study.

**Progression:** Build on organismal diversity, adaptation and evolution from earlier units. Prepare 10.A.02.

### 10.A.02 — Abiotic and Biotic Environmental Factors

**Student-facing description:** Explain how light, temperature, water, climate, soil, salinity, nutrients and biological interactions limit organismal performance and distribution.

**Core scope:** Solar radiation, photoperiod, temperature, precipitation, humidity, water availability, edaphic factors, pH, salinity and nutrients; resource versus condition; biotic factors; Liebig's minimum and Shelford's tolerance laws, optimum and stress zones, interacting limiting factors, acclimation and species-specific tolerances.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret tolerance and response curves, identify limiting factors and predict effects of interacting environmental gradients.

**Progression:** Build on 10.A.01. Prepare 10.A.03.

### 10.A.03 — Habitat, Ecological Niche and Resource Utilisation

**Student-facing description:** Distinguish habitat from niche and compare Grinnellian, Eltonian and Hutchinsonian concepts, fundamental and realised niches, overlap, partitioning and character displacement.

**Core scope:** Habitat and microhabitat, niche dimensions and n-dimensional hypervolume, fundamental versus realised niche including facilitation caveats, niche breadth and overlap, resource partitioning in space/time/diet, character displacement and evidence requirements, ecological equivalents and niche conservatism; competitive mechanisms developed in 10.C.04.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret niche curves and resource-use data; compare alternative explanations for character displacement and calculate simple overlap measures.

**Progression:** Build on 10.A.02. Prepare 10.A.04.

### 10.A.04 — Ecological Adaptations and Environmental Responses

**Student-facing description:** Compare morphological, physiological and behavioural adaptations and phenotypic plasticity, using plants and animals in contrasting environments to explain adaptive trade-offs.

**Core scope:** Acclimation, acclimatization and heritable adaptation, reaction norms, costs and benefits, xerophytes, hydrophytes, halophytes, thermal and water-balance adaptations in animals, behavioural habitat use, representative adaptations to extreme environments; avoid adaptive storytelling without evidence; cross-link Units 6, 7 and 9.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret common-garden and reciprocal-transplant experiments, distinguish plasticity from inherited differentiation and compare adaptive trade-offs.

**Progression:** Build on 10.A.03. Prepare 10.A.05.

### 10.A.05 — Biogeography, Biomes and Species Distribution

**Student-facing description:** Relate major terrestrial biomes, diversity gradients, species–area relationships, island biogeography, dispersal and vicariance to geographical patterns of life and India's biogeographical zones.

**Core scope:** Climate and biome distribution, tundra, taiga, temperate/tropical forests, grasslands, savannas and deserts, elevational and latitudinal gradients; species–area equation S=cA^z, island colonisation/extinction equilibrium and distance/area effects, dispersal and vicariance, historical biogeography, Indian biogeographic zones with authoritative classification and examples; aquatic ecosystem functioning in 10.D.05.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate species–area predictions, read island immigration/extinction curves and compare alternative explanations of distribution patterns.

**Progression:** Build on 10.A.04. Bridge to subunit 10.B.
