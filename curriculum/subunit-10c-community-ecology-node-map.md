## Subunit contract

Examine the structure and diversity of ecological communities, progressing from community attributes and trophic networks to species interactions, coexistence, succession and ecological stability.

**Planned nodes:** 7. See [Unit 10 master map](unit-10-ecology-behavioural-biology-subunit-map.md) for the full syllabus and legacy crosswalk.

## Node contracts

### 10.C.01 — Community Organisation, Structure and Attributes

**Student-facing description:** Describe species composition, abundance, dominance, stratification, zonation, guilds, functional groups, ecotones, edge effects and community assembly.

**Core scope:** Community boundaries and continuum versus discrete views, relative abundance, frequency, dominance and importance values, vertical stratification and horizontal zonation, guilds and functional groups, ecotones, edge effects, dispersal and environmental filtering, biotic assembly and priority effects; compare scales.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate relative abundance and simple importance values; interpret transects and distinguish assembly processes from descriptive patterns.

**Progression:** Build on the preceding ecological subunits and relevant earlier units. Prepare 10.C.02.

### 10.C.02 — Species Diversity and Quantitative Community Analysis

**Student-facing description:** Measure richness, evenness, alpha, beta and gamma diversity using Shannon and Simpson indices, rank–abundance curves, species accumulation and similarity measures.

**Core scope:** Richness and evenness, Shannon H=-sum(p ln p), Simpson dominance and alternative transformations (state conventions), Pielou evenness, alpha/beta/gamma partitioning, Jaccard and Sørensen similarity, rank–abundance, sampling effort and rarefaction/accumulation concepts, functional diversity context; distinguish diversity from abundance.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compute and compare indices on sample communities, interpret rarefaction and rank–abundance curves and explain sampling-dependent conclusions.

**Progression:** Build on 10.C.01. Prepare 10.C.03.

### 10.C.03 — Food Webs, Trophic Structure and Keystone Species

**Student-facing description:** Connect grazing and detrital chains, trophic levels, food-web connectivity, omnivory, keystone and foundation species, ecosystem engineers and trophic cascades.

**Core scope:** Food-chain versus food-web representation, trophic levels and omnivory, connectance with denominator conventions, interaction strength, keystone versus dominant and foundation species, ecosystem engineering, top-down and bottom-up control, direct/indirect effects and trophic cascades, Paine removal and representative aquatic examples; energetics 10.D.02.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret food webs and removal experiments, calculate simple connectance and predict indirect effects with stated assumptions.

**Progression:** Build on 10.C.02. Prepare 10.C.04.

### 10.C.04 — Competition, Coexistence and Resource Partitioning

**Student-facing description:** Explain intra- and interspecific competition, competitive exclusion, exploitation and interference, Gause's experiments and Lotka–Volterra competition models.

**Core scope:** Competition for limiting resources, interference/exploitation, Gause's Paramecium evidence, competitive exclusion under defined conditions, competition coefficients, two-species Lotka–Volterra isoclines and outcomes (coexistence, exclusion, priority effects), stabilising niche differences and fitness differences, resource partitioning and character displacement links.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Solve competition equilibrium and isocline problems, interpret outcomes and assess evidence for coexistence mechanisms.

**Progression:** Build on 10.C.03. Prepare 10.C.05.

### 10.C.05 — Predation, Herbivory and Consumer–Resource Dynamics

**Student-facing description:** Compare carnivory, herbivory and predator–prey dynamics using Lotka–Volterra models, Holling functional responses, numerical responses and prey defences.

**Core scope:** Predation and herbivory, consumer/resource feedback, classical Lotka–Volterra assumptions and cycles, predator/prey nullclines, Holling types I–III and handling time, numerical response, prey switching, refuges and enrichment context, camouflage, aposematism, Batesian/Müllerian mimicry and plant defences, trophic links.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret predator–prey phase plots and functional response curves, predict effects of handling time and distinguish numerical from functional responses.

**Progression:** Build on 10.C.04. Prepare 10.C.06.

### 10.C.06 — Mutualism, Parasitism and Other Species Interactions

**Student-facing description:** Compare mutualism, commensalism, amensalism, facilitation and parasitism, using pollination, seed dispersal, symbiosis and interaction networks to explain context-dependent outcomes.

**Core scope:** Interaction signs and context dependence, obligate/facultative mutualism, symbiosis versus mutualism, plant–pollinator and seed dispersal, mycorrhizae and nitrogen-fixing associations, commensalism and amensalism, facilitation, host–parasite and parasitoid distinctions, transmission and virulence trade-offs conceptually, network specificity and coevolution links; disease mechanisms in Unit 9.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Classify interactions from fitness evidence, compare network diagrams and explain how environmental context can change interaction outcomes.

**Progression:** Build on 10.C.05. Prepare 10.C.07.

### 10.C.07 — Ecological Succession and Community Stability

**Student-facing description:** Trace primary and secondary succession, autogenic and allogenic changes, facilitation, tolerance and inhibition, climax concepts, disturbance, resistance and resilience.

**Core scope:** Primary/secondary succession, pioneer and seral stages, soil and habitat development, autogenic/allogenic drivers, Connell–Slatyer models, Clementsian climax and Gleasonian individualistic views, disturbance frequency/intensity, resistance, resilience and recovery time, alternative stable states and hysteresis, succession not necessarily deterministic or ending in one climax.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret chronosequences and disturbance/recovery curves, compare succession models and assess evidence for alternative stable states.

**Progression:** Build on 10.C.06. Bridge to subunit 10.D.
