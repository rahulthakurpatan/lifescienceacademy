## Subunit contract

Investigate ecosystem structure and functioning through biological productivity, energy transfer, decomposition, nutrient cycling, terrestrial and aquatic ecosystem processes, and the services ecosystems provide to society.

**Planned nodes:** 6. See [Unit 10 master map](unit-10-ecology-behavioural-biology-subunit-map.md) for the full syllabus and legacy crosswalk.

## Node contracts

### 10.D.01 — Ecosystem Structure, Function and Functional Organisation

**Student-facing description:** Distinguish ecosystem components, producers, consumers, decomposers, standing crop, standing state, biomass, compartments, boundaries and material exchange.

**Core scope:** Tansley ecosystem concept, system boundaries and openness, biotic/abiotic pools, autotrophs/heterotrophs/decomposers, standing crop/biomass versus standing state of nutrients, stocks versus fluxes and turnover, food-chain link and input/output mass balance, ecosystem functional organisation.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret compartment diagrams and calculate simple stock, flux and residence-time relationships.

**Progression:** Build on the preceding ecological subunits and relevant earlier units. Prepare 10.D.02.

### 10.D.02 — Ecological Productivity and Energy Flow

**Student-facing description:** Quantify primary and secondary production, ecosystem respiration, ecological efficiencies and Lindeman's trophic dynamics using productivity measurements and ecological pyramids.

**Core scope:** GPP, autotrophic respiration, NPP=GPP-Ra, heterotrophic respiration and NEP, units per area/time, biomass versus production, light/dark bottle and biomass/eddy covariance conceptual methods with caveats, secondary production, consumption/assimilation/production efficiency, trophic transfer, Lindeman and non-universal ten-percent heuristic, pyramids of number/biomass/energy.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate GPP/NPP/NEP and transfer efficiencies, interpret aquatic inverted biomass pyramids and distinguish energy flow from nutrient cycling.

**Progression:** Build on 10.D.01. Prepare 10.D.03.

### 10.D.03 — Decomposition, Carbon Cycling and Ecosystem Metabolism

**Student-facing description:** Connect detritus processing, fragmentation, leaching, catabolism, humification and mineralisation with carbon reservoirs, fluxes, sequestration and ecosystem budgets.

**Core scope:** Detrital pathways, decomposer organisms and detritivores, fragmentation/leaching and enzymatic catabolism, soil organic matter formation and mineralisation, litter quality, C:N ratio and climate, exponential decay M=M0 exp(-kt), litterbag evidence, photosynthesis/respiration, terrestrial/ocean/atmospheric/geological carbon pools, disturbance and sequestration, gross versus net fluxes.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate decomposition constants and carbon budgets, compare litter decay data and distinguish storage from sequestration rates.

**Progression:** Build on 10.D.02. Prepare 10.D.04.

### 10.D.04 — Nitrogen, Phosphorus and Mineral Cycling

**Student-facing description:** Trace nitrogen fixation, ammonification, nitrification, denitrification, anammox and phosphorus cycling, relating nutrient limitation and stoichiometry to ecosystem productivity.

**Core scope:** Nitrogen reservoirs and microbial transformations, assimilation, mineralisation and gaseous losses, phosphorus weathering, sorption, biological uptake, sedimentation and geological return, sulfur cycle as supporting context, nutrient immobilisation, N/P co-limitation, ecological stoichiometry and Redfield ratio as context-dependent benchmark, nutrient additions and eutrophication link; microbial details Unit 9.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace element budgets, interpret nutrient-addition experiments and identify limiting nutrients without universal ratio assumptions.

**Progression:** Build on 10.D.03. Prepare 10.D.05.

### 10.D.05 — Water Cycling and Comparative Ecosystems of India

**Student-facing description:** Explain precipitation, evapotranspiration, infiltration, runoff and watershed processes, comparing forests, grasslands, lakes, rivers, wetlands, estuaries, marine ecosystems and mangroves.

**Core scope:** Hydrological cycle and catchment balance, groundwater and surface flows, transpiration, runoff and infiltration, terrestrial forest/grassland productivity, lake zonation and stratification, rivers and longitudinal gradients, wetlands, estuaries, marine pelagic/benthic zones, mangroves and representative Indian ecosystem case studies; seasonal hydrology and climate connections.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Solve simple water budgets, compare ecosystem productivity and zonation, interpret hydrological and seasonal profiles.

**Progression:** Build on 10.D.04. Prepare 10.D.06.

### 10.D.06 — Ecosystem Services and Integrated Ecosystem Functioning

**Student-facing description:** Link biodiversity and ecosystem functions to provisioning, regulating, cultural and supporting services, including pollination, soil, water, climate regulation and sustainability.

**Core scope:** Ecosystem function versus service and benefit, Millennium Ecosystem Assessment categories and alternative modern frameworks, pollination, productivity, nutrient cycling, soil formation, water purification, flood regulation and carbon storage, cultural values, biodiversity–function relationships, redundancy and complementarity, valuation methods and trade-offs, avoid double counting and equating all values with money.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret biodiversity–function experiments and service trade-off scenarios, distinguish intermediate processes from final benefits and compare valuation assumptions.

**Progression:** Build on 10.D.05. Bridge to subunit 10.E.
