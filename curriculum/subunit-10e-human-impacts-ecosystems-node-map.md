## Subunit contract

Analyse how human activities modify ecological systems through habitat transformation, climate change, pollution and biological invasions, and evaluate approaches to environmental monitoring, mitigation and ecological restoration.

**Planned nodes:** 5. See [Unit 10 master map](unit-10-ecology-behavioural-biology-subunit-map.md) for the full syllabus and legacy crosswalk.

## Node contracts

### 10.E.01 — Land Use Change, Habitat Loss and Ecosystem Fragmentation

**Student-facing description:** Compare deforestation, agriculture, urbanisation, mining and other land-use and land-cover changes, tracing habitat loss, degradation, fragmentation and desertification.

**Core scope:** Land-use versus land-cover classification, conversion and intensification, habitat amount versus configuration, patch size/isolation and edge effects, soil degradation and desertification, urban and mining impacts, ecological footprint concept and limits, landscape connectivity and ecosystem consequences, Indian case-study selection with evidence.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret before/after land-cover maps and fragmentation metrics, distinguish habitat loss from fragmentation per se and assess ecological trade-offs.

**Progression:** Build on the preceding ecological subunits and relevant earlier units. Prepare 10.E.02.

### 10.E.02 — Climate Change and Global Ecological Responses

**Student-facing description:** Explain greenhouse forcing and anthropogenic warming, linking climate and ocean changes to range shifts, phenological mismatch, extreme events and ecosystem responses.

**Core scope:** Natural greenhouse effect versus enhanced anthropogenic forcing, greenhouse gases, radiative forcing, climate versus weather, warming and precipitation shifts, extremes and sea-level rise, ocean acidification, range shifts, phenological mismatch, coral bleaching and ecosystem feedbacks, vulnerability and adaptation, mitigation versus adaptation, evidence and uncertainty from authoritative climate assessments.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret temperature, pH and range-shift trends, distinguish attribution from single-event correlation and compare mitigation/adaptation scenarios.

**Progression:** Build on 10.E.01. Prepare 10.E.03.

### 10.E.03 — Environmental Pollution and Ecotoxicology

**Student-facing description:** Compare air, water and soil pollutants, acid rain, ozone depletion, eutrophication and ecotoxicological processes, using BOD, COD, dissolved oxygen and bioaccumulation evidence.

**Core scope:** Air pollutants and particulate matter, photochemical smog, acid deposition, stratospheric ozone depletion distinguished from climate warming, water/soil pollution, eutrophication, dissolved oxygen, BOD/COD and oxygen sag, metals, pesticides, persistent pollutants, microplastics, thermal/noise/radioactive pollution, dose–response, exposure, bioaccumulation versus biomagnification, toxicity evidence and ecological endpoints; no hazardous handling instructions.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret BOD/COD/DO data and trophic concentration patterns, compare dose–response curves and distinguish exposure from ecological effect.

**Progression:** Build on 10.E.02. Prepare 10.E.04.

### 10.E.04 — Biological Invasions and Invasive Species Ecology

**Student-facing description:** Trace introduction, establishment and spread of alien species, explaining propagule pressure, invasion hypotheses, ecological impacts and prevention through representative Indian examples.

**Core scope:** Native, non-native, naturalised and invasive distinctions, intentional/unintentional introduction, propagule pressure, invasion stages and lag phases, enemy release and biotic resistance hypotheses, disturbance, competition and trophic impacts, Lantana, Parthenium and water hyacinth examples, prevention, early detection and context-dependent management, avoid assuming all introduced species are invasive.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret invasion curves and comparative studies, assess causal evidence for impacts and prioritise prevention versus response in conceptual cases.

**Progression:** Build on 10.E.03. Prepare 10.E.05.

### 10.E.05 — Applied Ecology, Environmental Monitoring and Restoration

**Student-facing description:** Evaluate ecological indicators, biomonitoring, environmental impact assessment, remote sensing, GIS, pollution-control principles, bioremediation and ecological restoration.

**Core scope:** Indicator taxa and physical/chemical indicators, baseline and reference conditions, before–after/control–impact designs, environmental impact assessment concepts, remote sensing and GIS land-cover monitoring, pollution source reduction and control hierarchy, bioremediation/phytoremediation principles, restoration versus rehabilitation/reclamation, reference ecosystems, targets and adaptive management, monitoring success and uncertainty; applicable EIA rules checked at lesson publication.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret monitoring datasets, propose conceptual BACI designs, compare restoration outcomes against references and identify confounding factors.

**Progression:** Build on 10.E.04. Bridge to subunit 10.F.
