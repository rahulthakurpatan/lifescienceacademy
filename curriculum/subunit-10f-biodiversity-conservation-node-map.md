## Subunit contract

Understand the value, distribution and vulnerability of biological diversity, using conservation genetics, population viability analysis, protected areas, ex-situ conservation and community-based approaches to evaluate biodiversity protection.

**Planned nodes:** 7. See [Unit 10 master map](unit-10-ecology-behavioural-biology-subunit-map.md) for the full syllabus and legacy crosswalk.

## Node contracts

### 10.F.01 — Levels, Patterns and Importance of Biodiversity

**Student-facing description:** Compare genetic, species, ecosystem, functional and phylogenetic diversity, biodiversity values, hotspots, endemism, inventories and global and Indian diversity patterns.

**Core scope:** Levels and dimensions of biodiversity, richness versus endemism, spatial patterns and gradients, hotspot criteria and Indian hotspot representation, biological resources and intrinsic/instrumental/relational values, inventory methods and taxonomic completeness, monitoring and biodiversity documentation; diversity metrics cross-link 10.C.02; distinguish hotspots from any species-rich site.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret richness/endemism maps, compare biodiversity dimensions and prioritise sampling while recognising incomplete inventories.

**Progression:** Build on the preceding ecological subunits and relevant earlier units. Prepare 10.F.02.

### 10.F.02 — Biodiversity Threats and IUCN Extinction Risk Assessment

**Student-facing description:** Connect habitat loss, exploitation, invasions, pollution and climate change with extinction processes, coextinction, rarity and IUCN Red List categories and criteria A–E.

**Core scope:** Direct and indirect drivers, overharvesting, extinction debt, local versus global extinction, background versus mass extinction, coextinction, Red List categories EX, EW, CR, EN, VU, NT, LC, DD, NE and criteria A population reduction, B geographic range, C small declining population, D very small/restricted population, E quantitative analysis; verify current thresholds and guidance, distinguish threat status from legal protection and rarity.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret simplified hypothetical assessment evidence under stated criteria, distinguish data deficiency from low risk and compare interacting threats.

**Progression:** Build on 10.F.01. Prepare 10.F.03.

### 10.F.03 — Conservation Genetics and Small-Population Biology

**Student-facing description:** Explain effective population size, genetic erosion, drift, inbreeding depression, loss of heterozygosity, genetic rescue and outbreeding risks in small populations.

**Core scope:** Census size versus Ne, sex ratio and variance in reproductive success, fluctuating size and harmonic mean, heterozygosity loss under ideal drift, inbreeding and deleterious recessives, bottlenecks and genetic erosion, adaptive potential, gene flow, genetic rescue, outbreeding depression risks and evidence, distinction from genetic purging assumptions; Unit 8 genetic foundations.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate simple effective-size and heterozygosity-loss estimates, interpret pedigree/marker data and compare genetic management trade-offs.

**Progression:** Build on 10.F.02. Prepare 10.F.04.

### 10.F.04 — Population Viability Analysis and Extinction Risk Modelling

**Student-facing description:** Integrate demographic and environmental stochasticity, catastrophes, extinction vortices and minimum viable population concepts with population viability models and conservation decisions.

**Core scope:** Deterministic versus stochastic projection, demographic/environmental variability, catastrophes, density dependence and Allee effects, extinction vortex, minimum viable population as conditional on risk/time horizon, quasi-extinction thresholds, simulation concepts, model assumptions, sensitivity and uncertainty analysis, management scenarios and monitoring; no universal MVP number.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret extinction-probability curves and sensitivity outputs, compare management scenarios and identify model uncertainty and data requirements.

**Progression:** Build on 10.F.03. Prepare 10.F.05.

### 10.F.05 — In-situ Conservation and Protected Area Management

**Student-facing description:** Compare national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves, conservation and community reserves, corridors, reserve design and Indian conservation programmes.

**Core scope:** In-situ versus ex-situ principles, Indian protected-area categories and distinct biosphere-reserve framework, zoning and landscape approaches, SLOSS debate and context, area/shape/connectivity, corridors and stepping stones, ecological restoration, species and habitat management, Project Tiger and Project Elephant as case studies, monitoring, human dimensions and protected-area effectiveness; verify current legal categories and programme details at lesson stage.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare reserve designs using species–area and connectivity reasoning, interpret monitoring evidence and assess trade-offs across conservation objectives.

**Progression:** Build on 10.F.04. Prepare 10.F.06.

### 10.F.06 — Ex-situ Conservation and Species Recovery Strategies

**Student-facing description:** Connect botanical gardens, zoos, captive breeding, genetic resource banks, cryopreservation, reintroduction and translocation with integrated species recovery and monitoring.

**Core scope:** Ex-situ collections and limitations, seed/gene banks overview with detailed PGR in 10.G.02, zoo/botanical garden roles, captive population genetic management, reproductive conservation and cryopreservation principles, habitat readiness, reintroduction versus reinforcement and conservation translocation, assisted migration as context-dependent intervention, disease/genetic/ecological risks, post-release monitoring and adaptive management.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare recovery strategies, interpret captive population and post-release data, evaluate feasibility and risks without operational veterinary or relocation protocols.

**Progression:** Build on 10.F.05. Prepare 10.F.07.

### 10.F.07 — Community-Based Conservation and Global Conservation Governance

**Student-facing description:** Integrate local and Indigenous knowledge, sacred groves, participatory conservation and human–wildlife coexistence with international conventions and Indian conservation policy.

**Core scope:** Community-led and participatory approaches, rights and equitable benefit sharing, Indigenous/local ecological knowledge and consent, sacred groves, coexistence and conflict mitigation principles, CBD and Kunming–Montreal GBF, CITES, Ramsar, CMS, Indian Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 and national conservation frameworks, distinguish treaties, targets and enforceable laws; detailed Biological Diversity Act and PPV&FR in 10.G; verify current provisions and programme status at lesson publication.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare governance instruments and case-study outcomes, assess social/ecological trade-offs and distinguish treaty objectives from domestic implementation.

**Progression:** Build on 10.F.06. Bridge to subunit 10.G.
