## Subunit contract

Understand animal behaviour through its developmental, neural, ecological and evolutionary foundations, progressing from individual decision-making and communication to navigation, social organisation, sexual selection and parental investment.

**Planned nodes:** 7. See [Unit 10 master map](unit-10-ecology-behavioural-biology-subunit-map.md) for the full syllabus and legacy crosswalk.

## Node contracts

### 10.H.01 — Foundations, Development and Methods of Studying Animal Behaviour

**Student-facing description:** Distinguish proximate and ultimate explanations using Tinbergen's four questions, innate and learned behaviour, fixed action patterns, sign stimuli, ethograms and sampling methods.

**Core scope:** Ethology and behavioural ecology, causation, ontogeny, adaptive function and phylogeny, innate/learned interaction and developmental plasticity, fixed/modal action patterns, sign/releasing stimuli and supernormal stimuli, sensitive periods, ethograms, focal, scan and all-occurrence sampling, observation bias, experimental controls and ethical study design, classical Lorenz/Tinbergen examples with limitations.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Classify explanations by Tinbergen's questions, compare sampling methods, interpret controlled stimulus and developmental studies.

**Progression:** Apply earlier population, community and evolutionary principles to animal behaviour. Prepare 10.H.02.

### 10.H.02 — Neural Basis of Behaviour, Learning and Biological Rhythms

**Student-facing description:** Connect neural and endocrine regulation with habituation, sensitisation, conditioning, imprinting, cognition, memory, sleep, arousal, circadian and seasonal rhythms and domestication.

**Core scope:** Neural circuits and hormones as proximate mechanisms, habituation/sensitisation, classical/operant conditioning, imprinting and sensitive periods, learning, memory and cognition across species, sleep and arousal as comparative phenomena, endogenous clocks, entrainment, zeitgebers, free-running period, circadian and seasonal timing, photoperiod and domestication-associated behavioural changes, gene–environment interactions and evidence; detailed neurophysiology Unit 7.D/J.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret conditioning and habituation experiments, actograms and free-running rhythms, distinguish learning from fatigue and domestication from individual taming.

**Progression:** Build on 10.H.01. Prepare 10.H.03.

### 10.H.03 — Foraging Behaviour and Habitat Selection

**Student-facing description:** Apply optimal foraging, prey choice, handling/search times, marginal value theorem, patch selection, risk-sensitive decisions and ideal free distribution to behavioural trade-offs.

**Core scope:** Fitness proxies and model assumptions, energy gain per time, prey profitability and diet breadth, search/handling time, patch depletion and travel time, marginal value theorem, risk-sensitive foraging and predation risk, habitat selection, ideal free distribution and interference/territorial constraints, behavioural plasticity and constraints on optimisation.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate prey profitability, interpret patch gain curves and marginal value predictions, compare habitat distributions and evaluate deviations from optimal models.

**Progression:** Build on 10.H.02. Prepare 10.H.04.

### 10.H.04 — Animal Communication, Conflict and Territorial Behaviour

**Student-facing description:** Compare visual, acoustic, chemical, tactile and electrical signals, honesty and deception, bee dances, dominance, aggression, territories and evolutionary game theory.

**Core scope:** Signal versus cue, sender/receiver and sensory ecology, multimodal communication, pheromones, signal reliability, costs and indices, deception and receiver responses, honeybee waggle dance orientation/distance context, dominance hierarchies, aggression, ritualisation, home range versus territory, resource defence, game payoffs, ESS and hawk–dove mixed equilibrium assumptions, frequency dependence.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret signal and receiver experiments, bee dance diagrams and dominance data; calculate simple hawk–dove payoffs and distinguish ESS from maximum group benefit.

**Progression:** Build on 10.H.03. Prepare 10.H.05.

### 10.H.05 — Migration, Dispersal, Orientation and Navigation

**Student-facing description:** Compare seasonal migration, natal and breeding dispersal, taxis, kinesis, compass orientation, landmark use, homing and biological timing.

**Core scope:** Migration versus dispersal and routine movement, partial and differential migration, natal/breeding dispersal, costs and benefits, orientation versus navigation, taxis and kinesis, sun and star compasses, time compensation, magnetic cues and evidence, olfactory/landmark maps and homing, endogenous programmes and environmental cues, representative birds, fish and insects, ecological links to metapopulations.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret displacement and compass-shift experiments, compare movement tracks and distinguish directional orientation from true navigation.

**Progression:** Build on 10.H.04. Prepare 10.H.06.

### 10.H.06 — Social Behaviour, Cooperation and Evolution of Altruism

**Student-facing description:** Explain group living, cooperation, kin selection, inclusive fitness, Hamilton's rule, reciprocal altruism, multilevel selection, eusociality and social conflict.

**Core scope:** Costs/benefits of group living, cooperation, mutual benefit, altruism and selfishness defined by fitness effects, direct/indirect fitness, relatedness and Hamilton rb>c under assumptions, reciprocal altruism and repeated interactions, partner choice and enforcement, group/multilevel selection frameworks and relationship to kin selection, eusociality and reproductive division of labour, caste and conflicts, haplodiploidy not sufficient explanation.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate simple relatedness and Hamilton-rule cases, interpret cooperation games and distinguish mechanisms from observed helping behaviour.

**Progression:** Build on 10.H.05. Prepare 10.H.07.

### 10.H.07 — Sexual Selection, Mating Systems and Parental Investment

**Student-facing description:** Connect mate competition and choice, sexual dimorphism, mating systems, sperm competition, parental care, parental investment, parent–offspring conflict and reproductive success.

**Core scope:** Intrasexual/intersexual selection and overlapping roles, direct/indirect benefits, Fisherian runaway and handicap/signalling hypotheses with caveats, operational sex ratio, sexual dimorphism and conflict, genetic versus social mating systems (monogamy, polygyny, polyandry, promiscuity), sperm competition and cryptic choice, parental investment and care, brood parasitism context, parent–offspring conflict and lifetime reproductive success; avoid universal sex-role assumptions.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret mate-choice and reproductive-success data, compare mating-system predictions, analyse parental allocation trade-offs and distinguish hypotheses from demonstrated mechanisms.

**Progression:** Build on 10.H.06. Integrate Unit 10 and connect quantitative data analysis with Unit 11.
