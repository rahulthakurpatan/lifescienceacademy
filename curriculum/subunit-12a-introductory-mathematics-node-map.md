## Subunit contract

Develop mathematical foundations for biochemical engineering through calculus, differential equations, linear algebra and numerical solutions of biological and engineering problems.

**Planned nodes:** 6. See [Unit 12 master map](unit-12-biochemical-engineering-industrial-biotechnology-subunit-map.md) for full syllabus traceability.

## Node contracts

### 12.A.01 — Mathematical Foundations and Differential Calculus

**Student-facing description:** Functions, limits, continuity, exponential and logarithmic functions, differentiation, partial derivatives, chain rule, Taylor series, maxima, minima and biological rate calculations.

**Core scope:** Define domain, range, dimensional consistency, derivative as slope and rate, product/quotient/chain rules, partial derivatives and local approximation; optimisation under stated domain constraints.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Differentiate growth and saturation functions, locate extrema and interpret rate units.

**Progression:** Build from basic mathematics without assuming engineering training. Prepare 12.A.02.

### 12.A.02 — Integral Calculus and Engineering Applications

**Student-facing description:** Indefinite and definite integration, substitution, integration by parts, multiple integrals, accumulated quantities and applications in biological processes.

**Core scope:** Antiderivatives, fundamental theorem, integration bounds and constants, substitution and parts, area under rate curves, double integrals and spatial averaging; distinguish definite integral from average.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate accumulated production from a supplied rate function and compare analytical and graphical estimates.

**Progression:** Build on 12.A.01. Prepare 12.A.03.

### 12.A.03 — First-Order Ordinary Differential Equations

**Student-facing description:** Order and degree, initial conditions, separable and first-order linear equations, integrating factors, growth, decay and reaction kinetics models.

**Core scope:** Dependent/independent variables, initial values, existence assumptions, separable and linear ODEs, integrating factors, exponential growth/decay, first-order conversion and steady-state approach.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Solve simple initial-value problems and check solutions by substitution and limiting behaviour.

**Progression:** Build on 12.A.02. Prepare 12.A.04.

### 12.A.04 — Second and Higher-Order Differential Equations

**Student-facing description:** Homogeneous and non-homogeneous equations, characteristic equations, complementary and particular solutions, boundary conditions, Laplace transforms and oscillatory systems.

**Core scope:** Linear constant-coefficient equations, distinct/repeated/complex roots, homogeneous and forced solutions, initial versus boundary conditions, higher-order systems and Laplace transform basics; damped oscillations and biological modelling context.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Solve representative second-order equations, compare damping regimes and interpret characteristic roots.

**Progression:** Build on 12.A.03. Prepare 12.A.05.

### 12.A.05 — Linear Algebra and Matrix-Based Biological Analysis

**Student-facing description:** Vectors, matrices, determinants, rank, inverse, simultaneous equations, Gaussian elimination, eigenvalues, eigenvectors and systems of differential equations.

**Core scope:** Matrix dimensions and operations, determinants, singularity and rank, row operations, linear systems, eigenpairs, diagonalisation conditions, matrix representation of coupled ODEs and balance equations.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Solve small linear systems and eigenvalue problems; interpret stability of a simple linear model.

**Progression:** Build on 12.A.04. Prepare 12.A.06.

### 12.A.06 — Numerical Methods and Computational Problem Solving

**Student-facing description:** Numerical errors, convergence, bisection, Newton–Raphson, interpolation, numerical differentiation, trapezoidal and Simpson's rules, Euler and Runge–Kutta methods.

**Core scope:** Truncation and rounding errors, root bracketing and convergence conditions, interpolation versus extrapolation, finite differences, quadrature and step-size requirements, Euler and RK methods, stability and error checks.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Perform small hand-worked iterations, compare numerical estimates against analytical solutions and explain step-size effects.

**Progression:** Build on 12.A.05. Bridge to subunit 12.B.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only; no finished lessons, fabricated lesson URLs, sitemap edits or completion-counter changes.
- Original friendly academic English, BSc foundations through MSc/NET/SET depth. Introduce mathematics from first principles; use chronological connected explanations, diagrams, dimensional checks, derivations, worked numerical examples, conceptual Part B and analytical Part C questions with solutions.
- Keep approved 48 nodes and 5–8 distribution; preserve all explicit syllabus topics. Chapter workload is conceptual, not identical word count.
- Unit 1 owns core biochemistry, enzymes and bioenergetics; Unit 2 microbial physiology; Unit 3 gene expression; Units 6–7 physiological mechanisms; Unit 11 computational modelling; Unit 13 biotechnology applications; Unit 14 analytical methods, statistics and ethics. Unit 12 owns engineering balances, transport, process interpretation and industrial biotechnology.
- Educational descriptions and benign illustrative datasets only. Do not include actionable pathogen cultivation, operational bioreactor optimisation for organisms or harmful products, genetic engineering protocols, or hazardous process recipes. Distinguish conceptual classroom models from qualified plant design, validated procedures and current regulations.
