## Subunit contract

Establish physical and engineering principles for biological production through material and energy balances, substance properties, fluid motion, heat and mass transfer and transport equipment.

**Planned nodes:** 6. See [Unit 12 master map](unit-12-biochemical-engineering-industrial-biotechnology-subunit-map.md) for full syllabus traceability.

## Node contracts

### 12.B.01 — Material Properties, Engineering Units and Process Fundamentals

**Student-facing description:** SI units, dimensional analysis, density, specific gravity, viscosity, pressure, concentration, ideal and real gases, phase behaviour and heat capacity.

**Core scope:** Intensive/extensive properties, unit conversions, dimensional homogeneity, mass and molar concentrations, pressure reference, equation of state, gas mixtures and phase equilibria as context, viscosity and thermal properties.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Convert process quantities, check dimensional consistency and distinguish mass, mole and volume bases.

**Progression:** Build on relevant preceding engineering and biological foundations. Prepare 12.B.02.

### 12.B.02 — Material Balance and Process Stoichiometry

**Student-facing description:** Mass conservation, system boundaries, steady and unsteady-state balances, reacting and non-reacting systems, limiting reactants, conversion, yield, selectivity, recycle, bypass and purge.

**Core scope:** Choose basis and control volume, accumulation=input-output+generation-consumption, total/component/element balances, stoichiometry, reaction extent and conversion, recycle and purge concepts, independent equations.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Solve benign stream-balance examples and check mass closure and limiting reactants.

**Progression:** Build on 12.B.01. Prepare 12.B.03.

### 12.B.03 — Energy Balance and Thermal Process Calculations

**Student-facing description:** Internal energy, enthalpy, sensible and latent heat, heat of reaction, steady and unsteady-state energy balances and integrated mass–energy calculations.

**Core scope:** Closed and open control volumes, first-law sign conventions, enthalpy and flow work, heat/work and kinetic/potential energy where relevant, sensible and latent heat, reference states and reaction enthalpy, steady-state balances and transient accumulation.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate illustrative heating/cooling duties and verify signs, units and assumptions.

**Progression:** Build on 12.B.02. Prepare 12.B.04.

### 12.B.04 — Momentum Transfer and Fluid Mechanics

**Student-facing description:** Newton's law of viscosity, laminar and turbulent flow, Reynolds number, continuity, Bernoulli and Hagen–Poiseuille equations, pressure drop and frictional losses.

**Core scope:** Transport flux and gradients, Newtonian shear stress, fluid statics, continuity and incompressible flow, ideal Bernoulli assumptions and losses, laminar pipe flow, Reynolds regimes and friction-factor conventions, non-Newtonian preview.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare flow regimes and calculate textbook pressure-drop and velocity examples with validity checks.

**Progression:** Build on 12.B.03. Prepare 12.B.05.

### 12.B.05 — Heat Transfer and Thermal Engineering

**Student-facing description:** Conduction, convection, radiation, Fourier's law, Newton's law of cooling, thermal resistance, overall heat-transfer coefficients, heat exchangers and log mean temperature difference.

**Core scope:** Heat flux and thermal conductivity, convective coefficient, radiation basics, planar/cylindrical thermal resistance, overall U and area basis, co-/counter-current exchangers, LMTD and limiting cases, fouling context.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate heat flux, thermal resistance and illustrative exchanger duty; distinguish temperature difference from absolute temperature.

**Progression:** Build on 12.B.04. Prepare 12.B.06.

### 12.B.06 — Mass Transfer and Separation Equipment

**Student-facing description:** Molecular diffusion, Fick's laws, convective and interphase mass transfer, coefficients, Sherwood and Schmidt numbers, packed towers, plate columns and gas–liquid contactors.

**Core scope:** Concentration gradients and diffusivity, steady/nonsteady diffusion, film model, equilibrium and driving force, overall versus individual coefficients, dimensionless correlations and assumptions, equipment roles and conceptual contacting patterns.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret concentration profiles, calculate simple diffusion flux and compare equipment by separation principle.

**Progression:** Build on 12.B.05. Bridge to subunit 12.C.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only; no finished lessons, fabricated lesson URLs, sitemap edits or completion-counter changes.
- Original friendly academic English, BSc foundations through MSc/NET/SET depth. Introduce mathematics from first principles; use chronological connected explanations, diagrams, dimensional checks, derivations, worked numerical examples, conceptual Part B and analytical Part C questions with solutions.
- Keep approved 48 nodes and 5–8 distribution; preserve all explicit syllabus topics. Chapter workload is conceptual, not identical word count.
- Unit 1 owns core biochemistry, enzymes and bioenergetics; Unit 2 microbial physiology; Unit 3 gene expression; Units 6–7 physiological mechanisms; Unit 11 computational modelling; Unit 13 biotechnology applications; Unit 14 analytical methods, statistics and ethics. Unit 12 owns engineering balances, transport, process interpretation and industrial biotechnology.
- Educational descriptions and benign illustrative datasets only. Do not include actionable pathogen cultivation, operational bioreactor optimisation for organisms or harmful products, genetic engineering protocols, or hazardous process recipes. Distinguish conceptual classroom models from qualified plant design, validated procedures and current regulations.
