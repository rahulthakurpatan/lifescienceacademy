## Subunit contract

Extend classical thermodynamics to living open systems through irreversibility, forces, fluxes, entropy production, reaction coupling, biological energy conversion and oscillations.

**Planned nodes:** 6. See [Unit 12 master map](unit-12-biochemical-engineering-industrial-biotechnology-subunit-map.md) for full syllabus traceability.

## Node contracts

### 12.C.01 — Classical Thermodynamics and Biological Energy Principles

**Student-facing description:** First and second laws, internal energy, enthalpy, entropy, Gibbs and Helmholtz free energies, chemical potential, equilibrium and reversibility.

**Core scope:** State functions versus paths, first-law conventions, entropy and spontaneous change, free-energy criteria under specified constraints, chemical potential and reaction quotient, standard versus actual free energy; thermodynamic feasibility versus kinetics.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate simple free-energy changes and interpret feasibility under stated conditions.

**Progression:** Build on relevant preceding engineering and biological foundations. Prepare 12.C.02.

### 12.C.02 — Living Systems as Open Non-Equilibrium Systems

**Student-facing description:** Open, closed and isolated systems, exchange of matter and energy, equilibrium versus steady state, dissipative systems, irreversibility and limits of equilibrium descriptions.

**Core scope:** Control volume, energy and material throughput, dynamic steady states, dissipation and entropy exchange, living systems obey thermodynamic laws; classical equilibrium descriptions alone do not capture sustained fluxes.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare equilibrium and steady-state diagrams, explain how local organisation is compatible with total entropy production.

**Progression:** Build on 12.C.01. Prepare 12.C.03.

### 12.C.03 — Thermodynamic Forces, Fluxes and Entropy Production

**Student-facing description:** Chemical potential, temperature and concentration gradients, conjugate forces and fluxes, entropy balance, entropy production, constitutive equations and Onsager reciprocity.

**Core scope:** Local equilibrium assumption, flux-force pairs, entropy production as sum of products with consistent signs and units, linear near-equilibrium relations, phenomenological coefficients and reciprocity conditions; far-from-equilibrium limitations.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret force–flux matrices and calculate simple entropy-production terms under supplied conventions.

**Progression:** Build on 12.C.02. Prepare 12.C.04.

### 12.C.04 — Thermodynamics of Coupled Biochemical Reactions

**Student-facing description:** Exergonic and endergonic reactions, reaction affinity, ATP coupling, phosphorylation potential, chemical and transport coupling, coupling coefficients and efficiency.

**Core scope:** Reaction Gibbs energy and affinity sign conventions, additive reaction free energies, ATP hydrolysis under cellular conditions, coupled fluxes, transport coupling and stoichiometry, efficiency and dissipation; avoid assuming negative net free energy proves mechanism.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate net reaction free energy and compare coupled versus uncoupled reactions.

**Progression:** Build on 12.C.03. Prepare 12.C.05.

### 12.C.05 — Thermodynamic Analysis of Oxidative Phosphorylation

**Student-facing description:** Redox potentials, electrochemical gradients, proton motive force, membrane potential, chemiosmotic coupling, ATP synthesis, proton leakage and energy-conversion efficiency.

**Core scope:** Redox free energy, electron stoichiometry, chemical and electrical components of ion gradients with explicit orientation, proton motive force, ATP synthase coupling, variable energetic costs and leak; molecular pathways in Unit 1.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate electrochemical and redox energy in illustrative systems; compare energy budgets and coupling limits.

**Progression:** Build on 12.C.04. Prepare 12.C.06.

### 12.C.06 — Glycolytic Oscillations and Biological Clocks

**Student-facing description:** Nonlinear dynamics, positive and negative feedback, glycolytic oscillations, phosphofructokinase regulation, limit cycles, dissipative structures, circadian oscillations and entrainment.

**Core scope:** Feedback and delay, nonlinear rate laws, sustained versus damped oscillations, glycolytic feedback and PFK context, limit cycles and nonequilibrium driving, biological clock period/phase/amplitude, entrainment and energy dissipation; cross-link 6.E and 10.H.02.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Read oscillatory time-series and phase diagrams, compare steady states and limit cycles and distinguish rhythm from mechanism.

**Progression:** Build on 12.C.05. Bridge to subunit 12.D.
