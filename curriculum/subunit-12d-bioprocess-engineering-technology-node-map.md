## Subunit contract

Explain upstream bioprocess fundamentals through microbial growth, substrate use, reactor configurations, asepsis, balances, oxygen transfer, industrial fermentation and economic evaluation.

**Planned nodes:** 8. See [Unit 12 master map](unit-12-biochemical-engineering-industrial-biotechnology-subunit-map.md) for full syllabus traceability.

## Node contracts

### 12.D.01 — Microbial Growth and Industrial Culture Fundamentals

**Student-facing description:** Microbial growth phases, biomass estimation, specific growth rate, generation time, nutrients, industrial production organisms, media components, growth factors and growth-associated products.

**Core scope:** Growth and viability measures, optical density versus dry biomass and viable counts, lag/exponential/stationary/death phases, nutrient and environmental factors, industrial culture selection at conceptual level, primary/secondary and growth-associated products; Unit 2 growth foundations.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret growth curves and calculate growth rate and doubling time from supplied non-pathogenic educational data.

**Progression:** Build on relevant preceding engineering and biological foundations. Prepare 12.D.02.

### 12.D.02 — Microbial Growth Kinetics and Substrate Utilisation

**Student-facing description:** Monod kinetics, substrate saturation, inhibition, biomass and product yields, maintenance, Pirt and Luedeking–Piret models and substrate consumption.

**Core scope:** Specific growth and consumption rates, saturation kinetics and assumptions, substrate/product inhibition concepts, yield bases, maintenance and growth/non-growth-associated production, batch/fed-batch/continuous balance differences and volume dependence.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Fit or interpret supplied benign model curves, compare yield and maintenance effects and distinguish rate from concentration.

**Progression:** Build on 12.D.01. Prepare 12.D.03.

### 12.D.03 — Bioreactor Design and Operating Configurations

**Student-facing description:** Stirred-tank, airlift, bubble-column, packed and fluidised beds, batch, fed-batch, continuous, plug-flow and enzyme reactors, chemostats, turbidostats, dilution and washout.

**Core scope:** Conceptual reactor geometry and ideal mixing, batch/fed-batch volume and mass-balance structure, CSTR/PFR comparisons, dilution and steady-state relationships, washout assumptions, chemostat versus turbidostat, immobilised enzyme reactor overview; safe textbook models only.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare reactor types, solve idealised non-pathogenic or cell-free balance problems and interpret steady-state/washout concepts.

**Progression:** Build on 12.D.02. Prepare 12.D.04.

### 12.D.04 — Sterilisation and Aseptic Bioprocess Technology

**Student-facing description:** Thermal inactivation concepts, D-, Z- and F-values, batch and continuous sterilisation principles, media and air filtration, contamination control, aseptic design and CIP/SIP.

**Core scope:** Distinguish sterilisation, disinfection and asepsis, conceptual log-reduction kinetics and time-temperature measures, batch/continuous comparison, filtration principles, contamination monitoring and cleaning/sterilisation-in-place concepts; no organism-specific cycles, operating setpoints or validation instructions.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret generic inactivation plots and compare aseptic design principles without prescribing a sterilisation process.

**Progression:** Build on 12.D.03. Prepare 12.D.05.

### 12.D.05 — Material and Energy Balances in Microbial Processes

**Student-facing description:** Growth stoichiometry, elemental balances, respiratory quotient, oxygen demand, biomass and product yields, degree of reduction, metabolic heat and cooling requirements.

**Core scope:** Elemental and electron balance, biomass empirical formula as model, substrate/biomass/product basis, respiratory quotient and oxygen balance, degree of reduction, heat generation and removal concepts; distinguish theoretical versus measured yield.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Check balance closure and calculate illustrative benign stoichiometric yields and energy balances from supplied data.

**Progression:** Build on 12.D.04. Prepare 12.D.06.

### 12.D.06 — Oxygen Transfer, Aeration, Agitation and Fluid Rheology

**Student-facing description:** Dissolved oxygen, oxygen uptake and transfer, volumetric transfer coefficient, oxygen limitation, mixing, power and Newtonian/non-Newtonian broth rheology.

**Core scope:** Gas–liquid driving force and OTR/OUR concepts, volumetric kLa meaning, dissolved oxygen balance, oxygen limitation and measurement principles, agitator/aeration roles, mixing time, power and shear concepts, Newtonian, shear-thinning and other rheological behaviour; conceptual non-pathogenic examples, no organism-specific optimisation.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret supplied oxygen-balance and rheology graphs, distinguish transfer capacity from demand and compare engineering trade-offs.

**Progression:** Build on 12.D.05. Prepare 12.D.07.

### 12.D.07 — Industrial Fermentation and Microbial Product Technology

**Student-facing description:** Compare industrial production principles and process flow sheets for antibiotics, organic acids, alcohols, vitamins, enzymes, bioplastics and microbial steroid biotransformation.

**Core scope:** Primary/secondary products and product location, high-level block flows for representative industrial products, fermentation versus biotransformation, differences in recovery and quality constraints, safe food/industrial examples, historical antibiotic production overview; no production recipes, strain optimisation, or operational conditions.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare conceptual block diagrams and classify products by metabolism, location and recovery needs.

**Progression:** Build on 12.D.06. Prepare 12.D.08.

### 12.D.08 — Integrated Fermentation Processes and Process Economics

**Student-facing description:** Integrate upstream and downstream flow sheets, titre, yield, productivity, raw materials, utilities, capital and operating costs and industrial feasibility.

**Core scope:** Process boundary and product basis, flowsheet interpretation, titre versus yield versus volumetric productivity, material and utility cost categories, CAPEX/OPEX, annualisation and capacity utilisation, simplified cost per unit, sensitivity and uncertainty; no detailed production optimisation for hazardous products.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate cost and productivity from illustrative benign process data and compare economic trade-offs.

**Progression:** Build on 12.D.07. Bridge to subunit 12.E.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only; no finished lessons, fabricated lesson URLs, sitemap edits or completion-counter changes.
- Original friendly academic English, BSc foundations through MSc/NET/SET depth. Introduce mathematics from first principles; use chronological connected explanations, diagrams, dimensional checks, derivations, worked numerical examples, conceptual Part B and analytical Part C questions with solutions.
- Keep approved 48 nodes and 5–8 distribution; preserve all explicit syllabus topics. Chapter workload is conceptual, not identical word count.
- Unit 1 owns core biochemistry, enzymes and bioenergetics; Unit 2 microbial physiology; Unit 3 gene expression; Units 6–7 physiological mechanisms; Unit 11 computational modelling; Unit 13 biotechnology applications; Unit 14 analytical methods, statistics and ethics. Unit 12 owns engineering balances, transport, process interpretation and industrial biotechnology.
- Educational descriptions and benign illustrative datasets only. Do not include actionable pathogen cultivation, operational bioreactor optimisation for organisms or harmful products, genetic engineering protocols, or hazardous process recipes. Distinguish conceptual classroom models from qualified plant design, validated procedures and current regulations.
