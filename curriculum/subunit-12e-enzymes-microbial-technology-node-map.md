## Subunit contract

Explore industrial biocatalysis through enzyme properties, alternative reaction media, protein engineering, immobilisation, reactor kinetics and diffusion limitations.

**Planned nodes:** 6. See [Unit 12 master map](unit-12-biochemical-engineering-industrial-biotechnology-subunit-map.md) for full syllabus traceability.

## Node contracts

### 12.E.01 — Industrial Enzymes and Fundamentals of Biocatalysis

**Student-facing description:** Microbial enzymes, industrial biocatalysts, activity, specificity, stability, catalytic efficiency, cofactors, enzyme selection and industrial applications.

**Core scope:** Enzyme versus whole-cell catalysis, activity units and assay conditions, turnover and catalytic efficiency, stability versus activity, cofactors and regeneration concepts, industrial food, detergent and benign synthesis examples; fundamental kinetics Unit 1.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare activity and stability data and interpret biocatalyst selection trade-offs.

**Progression:** Build on relevant preceding engineering and biological foundations. Prepare 12.E.02.

### 12.E.02 — Enzymes in Organic Solvents and Ionic Liquids

**Student-facing description:** Non-aqueous enzymology, water activity, solvent polarity, organic solvent effects, ionic liquids, biphasic reactions, selectivity and limitations.

**Core scope:** Water activity versus water content, enzyme hydration, solvent effects on structure and partitioning, nonaqueous and biphasic concepts, ionic-liquid classes and variable compatibility, reaction selectivity, environmental and safety trade-offs; no hazardous solvent handling instructions.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret comparative activity/selectivity plots and identify confounding solvent properties.

**Progression:** Build on 12.E.01. Prepare 12.E.03.

### 12.E.03 — Enzyme Engineering and Protein Engineering Strategies

**Student-facing description:** Structure–function relationships, random mutagenesis, directed evolution, rational and semi-rational design, screening and computational approaches to activity, specificity and stability.

**Core scope:** Conceptual random versus rational approaches, genotype–phenotype link, library and screening logic at high level, structure-based hypotheses, directed evolution as iterative selection, stability/activity trade-offs and validation; benign educational enzyme examples only, no operational engineering or sequence designs.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare hypothetical benign enzyme variants, infer trade-offs and distinguish screening from selection and computational prediction from measurement.

**Progression:** Build on 12.E.02. Prepare 12.E.04.

### 12.E.04 — Immobilisation of Enzymes and Whole Cells

**Student-facing description:** Adsorption, covalent attachment, entrapment, encapsulation, cross-linking, carrier materials, whole-cell immobilisation, recovery, reuse and stability.

**Core scope:** Immobilisation classes and binding mechanisms, carrier properties, leakage and activity retention, enzyme versus whole-cell constraints, mass transfer and reusability, loading and retained activity definitions, conceptual examples.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare immobilisation methods using supplied retention and reuse data; calculate apparent recovery and activity retention.

**Progression:** Build on 12.E.03. Prepare 12.E.05.

### 12.E.05 — Immobilised Enzyme Kinetics and Reactor Engineering

**Student-facing description:** Free versus immobilised kinetics, apparent Michaelis–Menten parameters, batch and continuous reactors, packed/fluidised beds, residence time, conversion and deactivation.

**Core scope:** Apparent kinetic parameters and partitioning, ideal reactor balances for benign cell-free catalysts, residence time and conversion concepts, packed/fluidised bed differences, catalyst lifetime and deactivation, distinguish intrinsic kinetics from transport effects.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret conversion and activity curves and solve simple idealised cell-free reactor balances.

**Progression:** Build on 12.E.04. Prepare 12.E.06.

### 12.E.06 — Diffusional Resistance, Thiele Modulus and Effectiveness Factor

**Student-facing description:** External and internal mass-transfer resistance, diffusion in porous matrices, reaction–diffusion equations, Thiele modulus, effectiveness factor and concentration profiles.

**Core scope:** Film resistance and internal diffusion, effective diffusivity, reaction–diffusion assumptions, dimensionless Thiele modulus with geometry and kinetic-order dependence, effectiveness factor and limits, external/internal coupling, nonuniform profiles.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate ideal first-order cell-free effectiveness factors from supplied parameters and interpret limiting regimes.

**Progression:** Build on 12.E.05. Bridge to subunit 12.F.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only; no finished lessons, fabricated lesson URLs, sitemap edits or completion-counter changes.
- Original friendly academic English, BSc foundations through MSc/NET/SET depth. Introduce mathematics from first principles; use chronological connected explanations, diagrams, dimensional checks, derivations, worked numerical examples, conceptual Part B and analytical Part C questions with solutions.
- Keep approved 48 nodes and 5–8 distribution; preserve all explicit syllabus topics. Chapter workload is conceptual, not identical word count.
- Unit 1 owns core biochemistry, enzymes and bioenergetics; Unit 2 microbial physiology; Unit 3 gene expression; Units 6–7 physiological mechanisms; Unit 11 computational modelling; Unit 13 biotechnology applications; Unit 14 analytical methods, statistics and ethics. Unit 12 owns engineering balances, transport, process interpretation and industrial biotechnology.
- Educational descriptions and benign illustrative datasets only. Do not include actionable pathogen cultivation, operational bioreactor optimisation for organisms or harmful products, genetic engineering protocols, or hazardous process recipes. Distinguish conceptual classroom models from qualified plant design, validated procedures and current regulations.
