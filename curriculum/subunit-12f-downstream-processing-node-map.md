## Subunit contract

Understand biological product recovery from biomass separation and cellular disruption through isolation, purification, concentration, drying and integrated recovery evaluation.

**Planned nodes:** 6. See [Unit 12 master map](unit-12-biochemical-engineering-industrial-biotechnology-subunit-map.md) for full syllabus traceability.

## Node contracts

### 12.F.01 — Biomass Removal and Cell Disruption Technologies

**Student-facing description:** Product location, sedimentation, flocculation, filtration, centrifugation, mechanical and non-mechanical disruption, homogenisation and bead milling.

**Core scope:** Extracellular versus intracellular recovery, harvest and clarification, sedimentation and centrifugal principles, filtration and flocculation, conceptual disruption classes, debris removal, product stability and selection trade-offs; benign non-pathogenic or cell-free examples only.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare recovery routes for hypothetical product locations and interpret clarification or recovery data.

**Progression:** Build on relevant preceding engineering and biological foundations. Prepare 12.F.02.

### 12.F.02 — Product Isolation by Precipitation and Extraction

**Student-facing description:** Salting-in/out, ammonium sulfate, isoelectric and solvent precipitation, partition coefficients, liquid–liquid, aqueous two-phase and supercritical extraction.

**Core scope:** Solubility and ionic strength, pH and isoelectric point, salt/solvent precipitation principles, distribution coefficient and mass balance, single/multiple extraction concepts, aqueous two-phase partitioning, supercritical fluid principles, selectivity and product stability; no hazardous operating recipes.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate simple partition/recovery examples and compare isolation mechanisms.

**Progression:** Build on 12.F.01. Prepare 12.F.03.

### 12.F.03 — Membrane-Based Separation and Purification

**Student-facing description:** Microfiltration, ultrafiltration, nanofiltration, reverse osmosis, dialysis, diafiltration, molecular-weight cut-off, flux, rejection, concentration polarisation and fouling.

**Core scope:** Membrane separation classes, driving forces and selectivity, transmembrane pressure concept, flux and rejection definitions, MWCO limitations, concentration/diafiltration balances, fouling versus polarisation and product compatibility.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate simple membrane flux and rejection using supplied measurements and compare technologies.

**Progression:** Build on 12.F.02. Prepare 12.F.04.

### 12.F.04 — Adsorption and Chromatographic Purification

**Student-facing description:** Adsorption isotherms, ion exchange, affinity, size exclusion, hydrophobic interaction and reverse-phase chromatography, capacity, breakthrough and resolution.

**Core scope:** Surface binding and Langmuir/Freundlich models, stationary/mobile phases, charge/affinity/size/hydrophobic separation, capacity, breakthrough curves, retention and resolution, purity/yield trade-offs; detailed analytical techniques Unit 14.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret illustrative chromatograms and adsorption curves, calculate resolution and compare purification modes.

**Progression:** Build on 12.F.03. Prepare 12.F.05.

### 12.F.05 — Product Concentration, Crystallisation and Drying

**Student-facing description:** Evaporation, concentration, crystallisation, moisture removal, drying kinetics, spray, freeze and fluidised-bed drying, stability and formulation.

**Core scope:** Solvent removal and concentration balances, supersaturation and crystallisation concepts, wet/dry moisture bases, drying-rate stages, comparison of spray/freeze/fluidised-bed technologies, heat-sensitive product considerations, product formulation and quality attributes.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret moisture-loss and drying-rate curves and calculate simple concentration and moisture balances.

**Progression:** Build on 12.F.04. Prepare 12.F.06.

### 12.F.06 — Integrated Downstream Process Design and Recovery Analysis

**Student-facing description:** Select and sequence separation operations using flow sheets, mass balance, percentage recovery, purification fold, specific activity, cumulative yield, purity, quality and economics.

**Core scope:** Product attributes and unit-operation sequence, mass and activity balances, total versus specific activity, step and cumulative yield, purification fold and purity, cost and waste trade-offs, integrated conceptual flow sheets for benign products.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Complete a hypothetical purification table, compare alternative sequences and explain purity/recovery trade-offs.

**Progression:** Build on 12.F.05. Bridge to subunit 12.G.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only; no finished lessons, fabricated lesson URLs, sitemap edits or completion-counter changes.
- Original friendly academic English, BSc foundations through MSc/NET/SET depth. Introduce mathematics from first principles; use chronological connected explanations, diagrams, dimensional checks, derivations, worked numerical examples, conceptual Part B and analytical Part C questions with solutions.
- Keep approved 48 nodes and 5–8 distribution; preserve all explicit syllabus topics. Chapter workload is conceptual, not identical word count.
- Unit 1 owns core biochemistry, enzymes and bioenergetics; Unit 2 microbial physiology; Unit 3 gene expression; Units 6–7 physiological mechanisms; Unit 11 computational modelling; Unit 13 biotechnology applications; Unit 14 analytical methods, statistics and ethics. Unit 12 owns engineering balances, transport, process interpretation and industrial biotechnology.
- Educational descriptions and benign illustrative datasets only. Do not include actionable pathogen cultivation, operational bioreactor optimisation for organisms or harmful products, genetic engineering protocols, or hazardous process recipes. Distinguish conceptual classroom models from qualified plant design, validated procedures and current regulations.
