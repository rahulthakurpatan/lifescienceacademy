## Subunit contract

Integrate production requirements, equipment selection, flow sheets, scale-up and scale-down, and safe, economically sustainable plant operation.

**Planned nodes:** 5. See [Unit 12 master map](unit-12-biochemical-engineering-industrial-biotechnology-subunit-map.md) for full syllabus traceability.

## Node contracts

### 12.G.01 — Fundamentals of Bioprocess Plant Design and Process Flow Sheets

**Student-facing description:** Design basis, production capacity, process selection, block and process flow diagrams, piping and instrumentation concepts and integrated balances.

**Core scope:** Product and capacity requirements, design basis and constraints, block versus process flow diagrams and P&ID literacy, stream tables, mass/energy closure, utilities and waste boundaries, conceptual process alternatives for benign applications.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Read a simplified flow sheet, identify missing stream information and compare conceptual process alternatives.

**Progression:** Build on relevant preceding engineering and biological foundations. Prepare 12.G.02.

### 12.G.02 — Bioprocess Equipment Selection and Engineering Specifications

**Student-facing description:** Bioreactors, tanks, pumps, compressors, heat exchangers, filters, centrifuges, membranes, columns, dryers, materials, sizing and utilities.

**Core scope:** Functional equipment specifications, throughput and product compatibility, materials and corrosion context, containment and cleanability, conceptual sizing basis and utilities, equipment selection criteria, distinction between classroom sizing and qualified engineering design.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare equipment choices against illustrative benign duty and quality constraints; interpret specification sheets.

**Progression:** Build on 12.G.01. Prepare 12.G.03.

### 12.G.03 — Bioreactor Scale-Up and Scale-Down Principles

**Student-facing description:** Laboratory, pilot and industrial scales, geometric similarity, dimensionless numbers, power per volume, tip speed, mixing and oxygen transfer criteria, gradients and scale-down models.

**Core scope:** Geometric similarity and dimensionless groups, scale-dependent transport and surface/volume effects, competing mixing, power, shear, heat and oxygen constraints, conceptual scale-down representation of gradients, validation and uncertainty; no operational scale-up of organisms or hazardous processes.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare conceptual scaling criteria and identify incompatible assumptions using abstract or cell-free engineering examples.

**Progression:** Build on 12.G.02. Prepare 12.G.04.

### 12.G.04 — Scale-Up of Downstream Processing and Integrated Operations

**Student-facing description:** Industrial centrifugation and filtration, membrane area, column scale-up, residence time, drying, recovery, bottlenecks and upstream–downstream coordination.

**Core scope:** Throughput and separation duty, area/volume and residence-time concepts, capacity versus selectivity, filtration and membrane scaling concepts, centrifugation and chromatographic capacity, drying and quality constraints, scheduling and bottleneck analysis, safe product examples.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret throughput and recovery constraints and identify bottlenecks in a supplied conceptual process.

**Progression:** Build on 12.G.03. Prepare 12.G.05.

### 12.G.05 — Plant Layout, Process Control, Safety and Economic Evaluation

**Student-facing description:** Facility layout, utilities, sensors, process instrumentation, feedback and PID concepts, GMP, quality assurance, biosafety, waste management, investment, costs and feasibility.

**Core scope:** Functional zoning and flow separation, utilities, measurement and control loops, proportional/integral/derivative roles and conceptual response, QA versus QC and GMP principles, occupational/environmental safety and waste, CAPEX/OPEX and feasibility, applicable standards verified at lesson stage; no construction or operating instructions.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret a generic feedback diagram and compare cost/safety/quality criteria for hypothetical benign facilities.

**Progression:** Build on 12.G.04. Bridge to subunit 12.H.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only; no finished lessons, fabricated lesson URLs, sitemap edits or completion-counter changes.
- Original friendly academic English, BSc foundations through MSc/NET/SET depth. Introduce mathematics from first principles; use chronological connected explanations, diagrams, dimensional checks, derivations, worked numerical examples, conceptual Part B and analytical Part C questions with solutions.
- Keep approved 48 nodes and 5–8 distribution; preserve all explicit syllabus topics. Chapter workload is conceptual, not identical word count.
- Unit 1 owns core biochemistry, enzymes and bioenergetics; Unit 2 microbial physiology; Unit 3 gene expression; Units 6–7 physiological mechanisms; Unit 11 computational modelling; Unit 13 biotechnology applications; Unit 14 analytical methods, statistics and ethics. Unit 12 owns engineering balances, transport, process interpretation and industrial biotechnology.
- Educational descriptions and benign illustrative datasets only. Do not include actionable pathogen cultivation, operational bioreactor optimisation for organisms or harmful products, genetic engineering protocols, or hazardous process recipes. Distinguish conceptual classroom models from qualified plant design, validated procedures and current regulations.
