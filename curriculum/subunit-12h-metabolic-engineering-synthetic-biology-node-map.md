## Subunit contract

Understand cellular metabolism and designed biological systems through pathway regulation, flux analysis, synthetic circuits, industrial biosynthesis and responsible applications.

**Planned nodes:** 5. See [Unit 12 master map](unit-12-biochemical-engineering-industrial-biotechnology-subunit-map.md) for full syllabus traceability.

## Node contracts

### 12.H.01 — Foundations of Metabolic Engineering and Pathway Regulation

**Student-facing description:** Metabolic networks, pathway regulation, feedback inhibition, precursor availability, bottlenecks, pathway competition, resource allocation and engineering objectives.

**Core scope:** Network structure, pathway flux versus metabolite pool, feedback and regulation, competing resource demands, cofactor and precursor concepts, cellular burden, trade-offs and engineering goals in benign systems; distinguish regulation from design intervention.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret a conceptual network and identify possible explanations of flux changes without proposing operational genetic modifications.

**Progression:** Build on relevant preceding engineering and biological foundations. Prepare 12.H.02.

### 12.H.02 — Metabolic Flux Analysis and Pathway Optimisation

**Student-facing description:** Stoichiometric networks, isotope-based flux analysis, flux balance analysis, metabolic control analysis, constraints, theoretical yields and production trade-offs.

**Core scope:** Stoichiometric matrix and mass balance, flux measurements and isotope-tracing principles, FBA assumptions and objective dependence, metabolic control coefficients, theoretical yield and alternative solutions, uncertainty; computational foundations 11.G.04, abstract benign networks only.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Solve a small abstract mass-balance example, interpret flux maps and compare assumptions of kinetic versus constraint-based models.

**Progression:** Build on 12.H.01. Prepare 12.H.03.

### 12.H.03 — Metabolic Pathway Engineering and Industrial Cell Factories

**Student-facing description:** Pathway modification principles, metabolic redirection, expression regulation, cofactor balance, heterologous pathways, systems-level approaches and benign products such as organic acids, amino acids and bioplastics.

**Core scope:** High-level cell-factory concept and representative benign products, pathway competition, resource and cofactor balance, genetic stability and burden, conceptual design constraints and evaluation, trade-offs in titres/yields/productivity; no gene targets, sequences, engineering protocols or operational optimisation.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare published high-level benign product case studies, evaluate evidence and identify conceptual trade-offs.

**Progression:** Build on 12.H.02. Prepare 12.H.04.

### 12.H.04 — Synthetic Biology, Biological Parts and Genetic Circuit Design

**Student-facing description:** Biological parts, devices and systems, modularity, standardisation, orthogonality, promoters, regulatory elements, toggle switches, oscillatory circuits, biosensors and design–build–test–learn concepts.

**Core scope:** Abstract circuit representation, input/output, feedback, toggle and oscillator motifs, modularity and context dependence, biological versus electronic analogies, qualitative mathematical models, biosensor concepts, DBTL cycle and validation; no build instructions or sequence-level designs.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Read abstract circuit diagrams and qualitative response curves, explain feedback and limits of modularity.

**Progression:** Build on 12.H.03. Prepare 12.H.05.

### 12.H.05 — Integrated Synthetic Biology Applications and Responsible Biomanufacturing

**Student-facing description:** Synthetic metabolic pathways, cell-free systems, biological materials, sustainable manufacturing, validation, metabolic burden, genetic stability, containment, bioethics and biosafety.

**Core scope:** High-level benign applications, cell-free versus cellular platforms, biomaterials, sustainability and life-cycle boundaries, performance validation and genetic stability, containment concepts, dual-use awareness, ethics and governance; cross-link Unit 14.J. No operational genetic engineering instructions.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare benign platform case studies and assess evidence, sustainability boundaries and responsible innovation considerations.

**Progression:** Build on 12.H.04. Integrate Unit 12 and bridge to Unit 13 applications.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only; no finished lessons, fabricated lesson URLs, sitemap edits or completion-counter changes.
- Original friendly academic English, BSc foundations through MSc/NET/SET depth. Introduce mathematics from first principles; use chronological connected explanations, diagrams, dimensional checks, derivations, worked numerical examples, conceptual Part B and analytical Part C questions with solutions.
- Keep approved 48 nodes and 5–8 distribution; preserve all explicit syllabus topics. Chapter workload is conceptual, not identical word count.
- Unit 1 owns core biochemistry, enzymes and bioenergetics; Unit 2 microbial physiology; Unit 3 gene expression; Units 6–7 physiological mechanisms; Unit 11 computational modelling; Unit 13 biotechnology applications; Unit 14 analytical methods, statistics and ethics. Unit 12 owns engineering balances, transport, process interpretation and industrial biotechnology.
- Educational descriptions and benign illustrative datasets only. Do not include actionable pathogen cultivation, operational bioreactor optimisation for organisms or harmful products, genetic engineering protocols, or hazardous process recipes. Distinguish conceptual classroom models from qualified plant design, validated procedures and current regulations.
