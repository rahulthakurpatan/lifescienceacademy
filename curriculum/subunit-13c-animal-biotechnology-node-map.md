## Subunit contract

Connect animal cell systems and genetic technologies with breeding, germplasm preservation, genetic monitoring and veterinary applications.

**Planned nodes:** 6. See [master map](unit-13-advances-in-biotechnology-subunit-map.md) for syllabus and legacy traceability.

## Node contracts

### 13.C.01 — Animal Cell and Tissue Culture Applications

**Student-facing description:** Primary and established cell lines, tissue explants, 2D/3D models, anchorage dependence, cell identity, viability, contamination awareness and applications in animal biotechnology.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare culture model categories and interpret supplied viability and identity evidence.

**Progression:** Build on preceding relevant units and the subunit's prerequisite concepts. Prepare 13.C.02.

### 13.C.02 — Cell Cloning, Selection and Animal Reproductive Biotechnology

**Student-facing description:** Clonal populations, cell selection concepts, somatic cell nuclear transfer, embryo transfer, IVF concepts, cloning versus transgenesis, developmental limitations and animal welfare.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare historical cloning evidence and reproductive technology categories without procedural instructions.

**Progression:** Build on 13.C.01. Prepare 13.C.03.

### 13.C.03 — Transgenic and Genome-Modified Animals

**Student-facing description:** Transgenic, knockout, knock-in and conditional model concepts, animal models, recombinant product applications, phenotype validation and welfare and containment considerations.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret hypothetical model genotypes and phenotypes and assess whether evidence supports causal inference.

**Progression:** Build on 13.C.02. Prepare 13.C.04.

### 13.C.04 — Animal Breeding and Marker-Assisted Selection

**Student-facing description:** Selection, mating systems, crossbreeding, estimated breeding values, heritability, genomic selection, marker-assisted breeding and livestock trait trade-offs.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Work simple breeding-value and selection-response examples using Unit 8 foundations.

**Progression:** Build on 13.C.03. Prepare 13.C.05.

### 13.C.05 — Animal Germplasm Conservation and Genetic Health Monitoring

**Student-facing description:** Germplasm banks, semen, oocytes and embryos, cryopreservation concepts, pedigree and genomic diversity, inherited disorders, genetic erosion and conservation breeding.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare conservation choices and interpret pedigree or diversity evidence; no collection or preservation protocol.

**Progression:** Build on 13.C.04. Prepare 13.C.06.

### 13.C.06 — Veterinary Molecular Diagnosis, Medicine and Surgery

**Student-facing description:** Molecular diagnosis of animal pathogens, strain identification, genetic health screening, molecular medicine, biomaterials and molecularly informed surgery concepts, test accuracy and welfare.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret fictional diagnostic evidence and compare genomic and serological approaches without clinical or pathogen manipulation guidance.

**Progression:** Build on 13.C.05. Bridge to subunit 13.D.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only; no unpublished lesson URLs, sitemap edits or lesson-completion counter changes.
- Original friendly academic English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, connected narratives, original diagrams, classical evidence, modern applications, quantitative examples with assumptions and units, misconception checks, Part B and analytical Part C practice with solutions.
- Unit 13 owns biotechnology application and conceptual comparison; Unit 14 owns measurement principles, experimental evidence, instrumentation, statistical interpretation and ethical governance. Unit 3 owns molecular mechanisms, Unit 4 immunology, Unit 5 development, Unit 8 inheritance, Unit 10 ecology, Unit 11 algorithms and Unit 12 industrial engineering. Preview later methods when needed without duplicating full chapters.
- Use benign educational examples, abstract models and published high-level evidence. No actionable pathogen culture or engineering, harmful genetic modification, production recipes, invasive experimentation, radioisotope handling or medical treatment instructions. Research involving biological hazards, animals, humans or radiation requires trained personnel and authorised oversight.
- Verify current laws, biosafety rules, regulatory bodies and journal AI policies at actual publication; distinguish prediction from experimental or clinical validation. Maintain provenance and research integrity.
