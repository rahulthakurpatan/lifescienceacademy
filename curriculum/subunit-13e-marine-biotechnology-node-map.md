## Subunit contract

Connect oceanographic conditions and marine biological diversity with resource assessment, microbial functions, seafood quality, bioactive compounds and marine protection.

**Planned nodes:** 6. See [master map](unit-13-advances-in-biotechnology-subunit-map.md) for syllabus and legacy traceability.

## Node contracts

### 13.E.01 — Oceanography and Marine Environmental Foundations

**Student-facing description:** Physical, chemical and biological oceanography, salinity, temperature, pressure, currents, tides, stratification, upwelling, dissolved oxygen and marine ecological zones.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret ocean profiles and relate physical gradients to marine productivity.

**Progression:** Build on preceding relevant units and the subunit's prerequisite concepts. Prepare 13.E.02.

### 13.E.02 — Marine Organisms, Biology and Behaviour

**Student-facing description:** Plankton, nekton and benthos, algae, marine invertebrates, fishes and representative marine mammals, adaptation, reproduction, movement and behaviour.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare life strategies and habitat associations using representative species and ecological observations.

**Progression:** Build on 13.E.01. Prepare 13.E.03.

### 13.E.03 — Marine Resource Assessment and Population Protection

**Student-facing description:** Marine bioresources, biodiversity uses, fish-stock concepts, abundance and population assessment, sampling, sustainable harvest, habitat protection and marine conservation.

Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Progression:** Build on 13.E.02. Prepare 13.E.04.

**Student-facing description:** Seafood spoilage, foodborne hazards, quality indicators and safety principles, marine natural products, pharmacological discovery, bioactivity and evidence limitations.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. ### 13.E.06 — Marine Biofilms, Biofouling and Biocorrosion

**Student-facing description:** Biofilm formation concepts, surface colonisation, macrofouling, microbially influenced corrosion, environmental and economic effects and sustainable prevention approaches.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret high-level surface and corrosion evidence and compare prevention trade-offs without operational microbial manipulation.

**Progression:** Build on 13.E.05. Bridge to subunit 13.F.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only; no unpublished lesson URLs, sitemap edits or lesson-completion counter changes.
- Original friendly academic English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, connected narratives, original diagrams, classical evidence, modern applications, quantitative examples with assumptions and units, misconception checks, Part B and analytical Part C practice with solutions.
- Unit 13 owns biotechnology application and conceptual comparison; Unit 14 owns measurement principles, experimental evidence, instrumentation, statistical interpretation and ethical governance. Unit 3 owns molecular mechanisms, Unit 4 immunology, Unit 5 development, Unit 8 inheritance, Unit 10 ecology, Unit 11 algorithms and Unit 12 industrial engineering. Preview later methods when needed without duplicating full chapters.
- Use benign educational examples, abstract models and published high-level evidence. No actionable pathogen culture or engineering, harmful genetic modification, production recipes, invasive experimentation, radioisotope handling or medical treatment instructions. Research involving biological hazards, animals, humans or radiation requires trained personnel and authorised oversight.
- Verify current laws, biosafety rules, regulatory bodies and journal AI policies at actual publication; distinguish prediction from experimental or clinical validation. Maintain provenance and research integrity.
