## Subunit contract

Apply biological principles to wastewater, pollution control, resource recovery, environmentally friendly products and remediation while evaluating ecological outcomes.

**Planned nodes:** 6. See [master map](unit-13-advances-in-biotechnology-subunit-map.md) for syllabus and legacy traceability.

## Node contracts

### 13.F.01 — Wastewater Treatment and Biological Pollution Control

**Student-facing description:** Wastewater characteristics, BOD/COD, primary, secondary and tertiary treatment, activated sludge, trickling filters, anaerobic treatment concepts, nutrient removal and effluent assessment.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare conceptual treatment flows and calculate removal percentages from fictional monitoring data.

**Progression:** Build on preceding relevant units and the subunit's prerequisite concepts. Prepare 13.F.02.

### 13.F.02 — Bioremediation and Microbial Pollution Transformation

**Student-facing description:** Biodegradation, bioaugmentation and biostimulation concepts, in-situ/ex-situ remediation, organic contaminants, metals, biosorption, bioavailability, monitoring and limitations.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret contaminant trend and mass-balance evidence without operational microbial optimisation.

**Progression:** Build on 13.F.01. Prepare 13.F.03.

### 13.F.03 — Phytoremediation and Plant-Based Environmental Recovery

**Student-facing description:** Phytoextraction, phytostabilisation, rhizofiltration, phytodegradation, rhizosphere interactions, contaminant fate and plant-based restoration limitations.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare hypothetical contaminant uptake and stabilisation data and distinguish removal from immobilisation.

**Progression:** Build on 13.F.02. Prepare 13.F.04.

### 13.F.04 — Biosurfactants, Biofertilisers and Biopesticides

**Student-facing description:** Biological surfactants, nutrient-mobilising biofertilisers, nitrogen-fixing associations, biological pest control concepts, ecological benefits, specificity and safety evaluation.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare published benign product categories and evidence, without formulation, cultivation or application recipes.

**Progression:** Build on 13.F.03. Prepare 13.F.05.

### 13.F.05 — Integrated Waste Management, Biogas and Biofuels

**Student-facing description:** Waste hierarchy, segregation, composting, anaerobic digestion, biogas, biofuel from waste, circular economy, energy recovery and life-cycle assessment boundaries.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret fictional waste and energy budgets and compare recovery pathways with stated assumptions.

**Progression:** Build on 13.F.04. Prepare 13.F.06.

### 13.F.06 — Microbially Enhanced Oil Recovery and Sustainable Technology Assessment

**Student-facing description:** Concepts of microbial effects on oil displacement, biosurfactant roles, environmental constraints, integrated pollution control, resource recovery and sustainability assessment.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare high-level technology principles and assess environmental trade-offs without operational extraction or microbial engineering guidance.

**Progression:** Build on 13.F.05. Bridge to Unit 14 experimental methods.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only; no unpublished lesson URLs, sitemap edits or lesson-completion counter changes.
- Original friendly academic English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, connected narratives, original diagrams, classical evidence, modern applications, quantitative examples with assumptions and units, misconception checks, Part B and analytical Part C practice with solutions.
- Unit 13 owns biotechnology application and conceptual comparison; Unit 14 owns measurement principles, experimental evidence, instrumentation, statistical interpretation and ethical governance. Unit 3 owns molecular mechanisms, Unit 4 immunology, Unit 5 development, Unit 8 inheritance, Unit 10 ecology, Unit 11 algorithms and Unit 12 industrial engineering. Preview later methods when needed without duplicating full chapters.
- Use benign educational examples, abstract models and published high-level evidence. No actionable pathogen culture or engineering, harmful genetic modification, production recipes, invasive experimentation, radioisotope handling or medical treatment instructions. Research involving biological hazards, animals, humans or radiation requires trained personnel and authorised oversight.
- Verify current laws, biosafety rules, regulatory bodies and journal AI policies at actual publication; distinguish prediction from experimental or clinical validation. Maintain provenance and research integrity.
