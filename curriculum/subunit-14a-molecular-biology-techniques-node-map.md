## Subunit contract

Learn the principles and interpretation of macromolecule isolation, separation, purification and analytical methods, choosing appropriate techniques and quality controls.

**Planned nodes:** 6. See [master map](unit-14-methods-in-biology-subunit-map.md) for syllabus and legacy traceability.

## Node contracts

### 14.A.01 — Biological Sample Preparation and Macromolecule Analysis

**Student-facing description:** Sample integrity, fractionation concepts, recovery, purity, concentration, analytical controls, contamination and representative DNA, RNA, protein, carbohydrate and lipid measurements.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare sample-quality and recovery data and distinguish purity, quantity and integrity.

**Progression:** Build on preceding relevant units and the subunit's prerequisite concepts. Prepare 14.A.02.

### 14.A.02 — Isolation and Analysis of DNA and RNA

**Student-facing description:** Genomic and plasmid DNA, RNA isolation and purification principles, nucleic acid integrity, absorbance and fluorescence quantification, hybridisation, specific sequence detection and quality controls.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret supplied nucleic-acid spectra and gels; compare genomic/plasmid DNA and RNA evidence.

**Progression:** Build on 14.A.01. Prepare 14.A.03.

### 14.A.03 — Protein, Carbohydrate and Lipid Isolation and Analysis

**Student-facing description:** Protein solubility and fractionation, carbohydrate and lipid extraction/separation principles, colorimetric assays, protein activity and standards, lipid and carbohydrate analytical approaches.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Read standard curves and compare recovery and selectivity using benign samples.

**Progression:** Build on 14.A.02. Prepare 14.A.04.

### 14.A.04 — Centrifugation and Biological Fractionation

**Student-facing description:** Relative centrifugal force, sedimentation, differential and density-gradient centrifugation, rate-zonal versus isopycnic separation, analytical ultracentrifugation concepts.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare fractionation diagrams and calculate textbook RCF or sedimentation relationships from supplied values.

**Progression:** Build on 14.A.03. Prepare 14.A.05.

### 14.A.05 — Chromatographic Separation and Analytical Interpretation

**Student-facing description:** Partition, adsorption, ion exchange, affinity, size exclusion, TLC, GC and HPLC principles, retention, resolution, efficiency and calibration.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret illustrative chromatograms, compute retention/resolution and choose a separation principle for a benign analyte.

**Progression:** Build on 14.A.04. Prepare 14.A.06.

### 14.A.06 — Electrophoresis and Molecular Profiling

**Student-facing description:** Agarose and PAGE, native and SDS-PAGE, one- and two-dimensional electrophoresis, isoelectric focusing, molecular markers and electropherogram interpretation.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret supplied band patterns, pI-based separation and migration relationships; distinguish identity from size evidence.

**Progression:** Build on 14.A.05. Bridge to subunit 14.B.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only; no unpublished lesson URLs, sitemap edits or lesson-completion counter changes.
- Original friendly academic English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, connected narratives, original diagrams, classical evidence, modern applications, quantitative examples with assumptions and units, misconception checks, Part B and analytical Part C practice with solutions.
- Unit 13 owns biotechnology application and conceptual comparison; Unit 14 owns measurement principles, experimental evidence, instrumentation, statistical interpretation and ethical governance. Unit 3 owns molecular mechanisms, Unit 4 immunology, Unit 5 development, Unit 8 inheritance, Unit 10 ecology, Unit 11 algorithms and Unit 12 industrial engineering. Preview later methods when needed without duplicating full chapters.
- Use benign educational examples, abstract models and published high-level evidence. No actionable pathogen culture or engineering, harmful genetic modification, production recipes, invasive experimentation, radioisotope handling or medical treatment instructions. Research involving biological hazards, animals, humans or radiation requires trained personnel and authorised oversight.
