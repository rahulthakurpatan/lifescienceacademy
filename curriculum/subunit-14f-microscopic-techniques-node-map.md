## Subunit contract

Develop microscopy literacy from optical resolution and specimen contrast to advanced live imaging, electron microscopy, specimen preparation and quantitative image analysis.

**Planned nodes:** 6. See [master map](unit-14-methods-in-biology-subunit-map.md) for syllabus and legacy traceability.

## Node contracts

### 14.F.01 — Optical Foundations and Resolving Power of Microscopes

**Student-facing description:** Magnification, resolution, numerical aperture, diffraction, Abbe and Rayleigh criteria, working distance, contrast and sampling.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate illustrative resolving power and distinguish magnification from resolution.

**Progression:** Build on preceding relevant units and the subunit's prerequisite concepts. Prepare 14.F.02.

### 14.F.02 — Light Microscopy and Contrast Methods

**Student-facing description:** Bright-field, dark-field, phase contrast, differential interference contrast, polarising microscopy, cell and organelle visualisation and method selection.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare representative images of benign specimens and explain contrast mechanisms.

**Progression:** Build on 14.F.01. Prepare 14.F.03.

### 14.F.03 — Fluorescence, Confocal and Advanced Live-Cell Microscopy

**Student-facing description:** Widefield fluorescence, confocal optical sectioning, multiphoton and light-sheet concepts, super-resolution overview, time-lapse microscopy, live-cell imaging and phototoxicity.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret image stacks and time-series examples and compare resolution, depth and temporal trade-offs.

**Progression:** Build on 14.F.02. Prepare 14.F.04.

### 14.F.04 — Transmission and Scanning Electron Microscopy

**Student-facing description:** Electron optics, TEM and SEM contrast, internal ultrastructure versus surface morphology, resolution and representative biological images.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

### 14.F.05 — Microscopy Sample Preparation and Structural Preservation

**Student-facing description:** Fixation and staining concepts, sectioning, dehydration and embedding, negative staining, freeze-fracture, freeze-etch, cryogenic preservation and preparation artefacts.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare published conceptual preparation approaches and identify artefacts without hazardous reagent or instrument procedures.

**Progression:** Build on 14.F.04. Prepare 14.F.06.

### 14.F.06 — Digital Image Processing and Quantitative Microscopy

**Student-facing description:** Pixels and calibration, sampling, noise, background correction, segmentation, thresholding, colocalisation, morphometry, image integrity and reproducibility.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Measure illustrative calibrated images and compare legitimate processing with misleading manipulation.

**Progression:** Build on 14.F.05. Bridge to subunit 14.G.
