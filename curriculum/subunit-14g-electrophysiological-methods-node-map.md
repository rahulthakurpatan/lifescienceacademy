## Subunit contract

Relate electrical recordings, neural perturbation evidence and brain imaging to physiological mechanisms and experimental interpretation.

**Planned nodes:** 6. See [master map](unit-14-methods-in-biology-subunit-map.md) for syllabus and legacy traceability.

## Node contracts

### 14.G.01 — Bioelectric Signals and Single-Neuron Recording

**Student-facing description:** Membrane potential, electrodes, intracellular and extracellular recording concepts, single-unit spikes, signal conditioning, noise and recording interpretation.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Read supplied neuronal traces and distinguish action potentials from recording artefacts.

**Progression:** Build on preceding relevant units and the subunit's prerequisite concepts. Prepare 14.G.02.

### 14.G.02 — Patch-Clamp Recording and Ion Channel Analysis

**Student-facing description:** Voltage and current clamp, cell-attached, whole-cell, inside-out and outside-out configurations, current–voltage relations, conductance and reversal potential.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret fictional channel-current traces and I–V plots without invasive experimental instructions.

**Progression:** Build on 14.G.01. Prepare 14.G.03.

### 14.G.03 — ECG and Integrated Physiological Recording

**Student-facing description:** ECG lead concepts, P–QRS–T waves, intervals, heart-rate calculation, EMG context, recording artefacts and physiological interpretation.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Analyse schematic ECG intervals and distinguish educational inference from clinical diagnosis.

**Progression:** Build on 14.G.02. Prepare 14.G.04.

### 14.G.04 — Brain Activity Recording and Functional Neurophysiology

**Student-facing description:** EEG, evoked potentials, neural population activity, MEG context, oscillatory bands, temporal/spatial resolution and artefacts.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. ### 14.G.05 — Brain Lesion, Stimulation and Pharmacological Testing

**Student-facing description:** Conceptual lesion and stimulation studies, pharmacological perturbation, reversible versus permanent effects, controls, behavioural endpoints, causality and animal ethics.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

### 14.G.06 — PET, MRI, fMRI and Computed Tomography

**Student-facing description:** PET molecular imaging, MRI magnetic resonance contrast, fMRI BOLD response, CT/CAT X-ray attenuation, structural versus functional imaging and resolution limits.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare representative modality outputs and distinguish indirect functional signals from direct neural recordings.

**Progression:** Build on 14.G.05. Bridge to subunit 14.H.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only; no unpublished lesson URLs, sitemap edits or lesson-completion counter changes.
- Original friendly academic English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, connected narratives, original diagrams, classical evidence, modern applications, quantitative examples with assumptions and units, misconception checks, Part B and analytical Part C practice with solutions.
- Unit 13 owns biotechnology application and conceptual comparison; Unit 14 owns measurement principles, experimental evidence, instrumentation, statistical interpretation and ethical governance. Preview later methods when needed without duplicating full chapters.
- Use benign educational examples, abstract models and published high-level evidence. No actionable pathogen culture or engineering, harmful genetic modification, production recipes, invasive experimentation, radioisotope handling or medical treatment instructions. Research involving biological hazards, animals, humans or radiation requires trained personnel and authorised oversight.
- Verify current laws, biosafety rules, regulatory bodies and journal AI policies at actual publication; distinguish prediction from experimental or clinical validation. Maintain provenance and research integrity.
