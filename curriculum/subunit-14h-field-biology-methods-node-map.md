## Subunit contract

Develop field sampling and observation literacy for plant and animal populations, movements, behaviour and habitat characterisation.

**Planned nodes:** 5. See [master map](unit-14-methods-in-biology-subunit-map.md) for syllabus and legacy traceability.

## Node contracts

### 14.H.01 — Field Study Design and Ecological Sampling

**Student-facing description:** Sampling frames, random, systematic and stratified sampling, replication, detection probability, observer bias, spatial/temporal scales and ethical field research.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Choose a sampling design for a hypothetical ecological question and identify pseudoreplication.

**Progression:** Build on preceding relevant units and the subunit's prerequisite concepts. Prepare 14.H.02.

### 14.H.02 — Plant Population Density and Vegetation Assessment

**Student-facing description:** Quadrats, line and belt transects, density, frequency, cover, abundance, dominance, importance values and vegetation sampling limitations.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate vegetation measures from supplied quadrat records and compare sampling effort.

**Progression:** Build on 14.H.01. Prepare 14.H.03.

### 14.H.03 — Animal Population Density and Abundance Estimation

**Student-facing description:** Direct counts, indirect signs, distance sampling, mark–recapture principles, camera-trap indices, occupancy and imperfect detection.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare textbook abundance estimators and interpret detection-adjusted fictional survey data.

**Progression:** Build on 14.H.02. Prepare 14.H.04.

### 14.H.04 — Ranging Patterns and Behavioural Observation

**Student-facing description:** Home range, territory, movement paths, direct and indirect observations, telemetry and GPS concepts, focal/scan sampling, ethograms and activity budgets.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret anonymised movement maps and behavioural datasets without wildlife capture or tracking instructions.

**Progression:** Build on 14.H.03. Prepare 14.H.05.

### 14.H.05 — Habitat Characterisation, Remote Sensing and GIS

**Student-facing description:** Ground surveys, environmental variables, satellite imagery, spectral signatures, NDVI, spatial resolution, land-cover classification, GIS and ground-truth validation.

**Core scope:** Develop each concept in the description as an internal section, beginning with definitions and prerequisites, then mechanism or measurement principle, comparison, applications, controls, assumptions and limitations. Connect the topics using a representative benign example and evidence appropriate to the node.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Read illustrative habitat maps and assess classification accuracy, scale and sampling bias.

**Progression:** Build on 14.H.04. Bridge to subunit 14.I.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only; no unpublished lesson URLs, sitemap edits or lesson-completion counter changes.
- Original friendly academic English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, connected narratives, original diagrams, classical evidence, modern applications, quantitative examples with assumptions and units, misconception checks, Part B and analytical Part C practice with solutions.
- Unit 13 owns biotechnology application and conceptual comparison; Unit 14 owns measurement principles, experimental evidence, instrumentation, statistical interpretation and ethical governance. Unit 3 owns molecular mechanisms, Unit 4 immunology, Unit 5 development, Unit 8 inheritance, Unit 10 ecology, Unit 11 algorithms and Unit 12 industrial engineering. Preview later methods when needed without duplicating full chapters.
- Use benign educational examples, abstract models and published high-level evidence. No actionable pathogen culture or engineering, harmful genetic modification, production recipes, invasive experimentation, radioisotope handling or medical treatment instructions. Research involving biological hazards, animals, humans or radiation requires trained personnel and authorised oversight.
- Verify current laws, biosafety rules, regulatory bodies and journal AI policies at actual publication; distinguish prediction from experimental or clinical validation. Maintain provenance and research integrity.
