# Life Science Academy — Subunit 1.A: Chemical and Biophysical Foundations

Establish the chemical and physicochemical foundations of living systems, connecting atomic structure, molecular interactions, biological solutions, reaction kinetics and thermodynamics.

Five approved learning nodes. See [Unit 1 master map](unit-01-structure-function-biomolecules-subunit-map.md) for source references and the original A–J crosswalk.

## Node contracts

### 1.A.01 — Atomic Structure and Chemical Bonding

**Student-facing description:** Atoms, isotopes, ions, electronic configuration, orbitals, periodic properties, chemical bonds, hybridisation, resonance and molecular geometry.

**Core scope:** Integrate atomic number, mass number, isotopic abundance, electron configuration and quantum numbers, valence and periodic trends; ionic, covalent and coordinate bonds; electronegativity, bond polarity, hybridisation, VSEPR and resonance in biomolecules. Limit historical atomic models and nuclear chemistry to biologically relevant prerequisites.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Determine particle counts and valence, predict representative molecular shapes and polarity, and relate bonding to biologically important ions.

**Progression:** Begin with essential chemical prerequisites. Prepare 1.A.02.

### 1.A.02 — Water and Molecular Stabilising Interactions

**Student-facing description:** Water properties, polarity, hydration, electrostatic interactions, hydrogen bonds, van der Waals forces and hydrophobic effects.

**Core scope:** Water structure and solvent properties, dielectric screening, Coulomb interactions, salt bridges, hydrogen bonding, dispersion and dipole interactions, hydrophobic effect and cooperative stabilisation; distinguish hydrophobic association from a chemical bond.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare interaction strengths in aqueous contexts, explain solvation and predict the effect of changing solvent conditions on biomolecular associations.

**Progression:** Build on 1.A.01. Prepare 1.A.03.

### 1.A.03 — Biological Solutions, pH and Buffer Systems

**Student-facing description:** Concentration units, colligative properties, osmotic pressure, acids, bases, pKa, titration curves, Henderson–Hasselbalch equation and biological buffers.

**Core scope:** Molarity, molality, mole fraction, osmolarity/osmolality, vapour pressure, boiling-point elevation, freezing-point depression, osmotic pressure and tonicity; acid/base dissociation, pH/pOH, polyprotic acids, pKa, buffer capacity, phosphate, bicarbonate and protein buffers.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Solve solution, osmotic pressure, pH and buffer problems with assumptions and interpret titration curves.

**Progression:** Build on 1.A.02. Prepare 1.A.04.

### 1.A.04 — Chemical Kinetics and Reaction Dynamics

**Student-facing description:** Reaction rates, rate laws, reaction order, molecularity, half-life, activation energy and Arrhenius equation.

**Core scope:** Rate definitions, differential and integrated rate laws for zero-, first- and second-order cases, units of rate constants, elementary reactions versus overall mechanisms, activation barriers, Arrhenius plots and temperature dependence; introduce catalysis before 1.D.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Infer reaction order and rate constants from supplied data, calculate half-life and interpret energy profiles.

**Progression:** Build on 1.A.03. Prepare 1.A.05.

### 1.A.05 — Thermodynamics, Chemical Equilibrium and Redox Principles

**Student-facing description:** Thermodynamic laws, enthalpy, entropy, Gibbs free energy, equilibrium constants, redox potentials and Nernst equation.

**Core scope:** State functions, first and second laws, entropy, enthalpy, Gibbs energy, standard and actual free-energy change, reaction quotient and equilibrium, chemical potential, oxidation states, redox couples, electron transfer, Nernst equation and electrochemical gradients; distinguish spontaneity from rate.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate free energy, equilibrium and redox quantities, explain sign conventions and compare kinetic and thermodynamic predictions.

**Progression:** Build on 1.A.04. Bridge to subunit 1.B.
