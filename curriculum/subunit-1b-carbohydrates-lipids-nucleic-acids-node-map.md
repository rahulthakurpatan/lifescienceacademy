# Life Science Academy — Subunit 1.B: Carbohydrates, Lipids and Nucleic Acids

Explore the composition, chemical properties, structural organisation, stability and biological functions of carbohydrates, lipids and nucleic acids.

Five approved learning nodes. See [Unit 1 master map](unit-01-structure-function-biomolecules-subunit-map.md) for source references and the original A–J crosswalk.

## Node contracts

### 1.B.01 — Carbohydrate Chemistry and Structural Organisation

**Student-facing description:** Monosaccharides, stereochemistry, anomers, epimers, mutarotation, reducing sugars, disaccharides, polysaccharides and complex carbohydrates.

**Core scope:** Aldoses and ketoses, D/L and stereochemistry, cyclic hemiacetals, glycosidic linkages, reducing sugars, disaccharides and oligosaccharides, starch, glycogen, cellulose, chitin, glycoproteins, proteoglycans and glycosaminoglycans; link structure to storage, support and recognition.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret Fischer and Haworth representations, identify linkages and reducing ends, and compare structure–function relationships.

**Progression:** Build on preceding biomolecular concepts. Prepare 1.B.02.

### 1.B.02 — Lipid Chemistry and Biological Organisation

**Student-facing description:** Fatty acids, neutral lipids, phospholipids, glycolipids, sphingolipids, cholesterol, steroids, eicosanoids, micelles and lipid bilayers.

**Core scope:** Saturated/unsaturated and essential fatty acids, cis/trans geometry, triacylglycerols and waxes, glycerophospholipids, sphingolipids, glycolipids, sterols and lipid derivatives; amphipathicity, micelles, liposomes, bilayers, fluidity and lipoprotein organisation; detailed membranes in Unit 2.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Classify lipid structures and predict how composition and temperature influence packing and assembly.

**Progression:** Build on 1.B.01. Prepare 1.B.03.

### 1.B.03 — Nucleotide Chemistry and DNA Conformation

**Student-facing description:** Bases, nucleosides, nucleotides, phosphodiester bonds, double helix, base pairing and A-, B- and Z-DNA.

**Core scope:** Purines/pyrimidines, ribose/deoxyribose, nucleosides and nucleotides, 5′–3′ directionality, phosphodiester bonds, base pairing, major/minor grooves, base stacking, double helix evidence, A/B/Z conformations and nucleotide functions.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret nucleotide structures, base composition and helical features; distinguish evidence from models.

**Progression:** Build on 1.B.02. Prepare 1.B.04.

### 1.B.04 — RNA Structure and Functional Conformations

**Student-facing description:** RNA folding, secondary and tertiary structures, hairpins, pseudoknots, mRNA, tRNA, rRNA, regulatory RNAs and ribozymes.

**Core scope:** RNA backbone and 2′-OH, canonical and noncanonical pairing, stems, loops, bulges, pseudoknots and tertiary contacts; structural roles of mRNA, tRNA, rRNA, miRNA, siRNA, riboswitches and ribozymes; regulatory mechanisms in Unit 3.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Read RNA secondary-structure diagrams, explain structural adaptations and compare DNA and RNA properties.

**Progression:** Build on 1.B.03. Prepare 1.B.05.

### 1.B.05 — Nucleic Acid Topology and Molecular Stability

**Student-facing description:** Linking number, twist, writhe, supercoiling, base stacking, melting temperature, denaturation, renaturation and hybridisation.

**Core scope:** Topology of closed or constrained DNA, Lk=Tw+Wr, positive/negative supercoils and topoisomerase context; hydrogen bonding and stacking, GC content, salt and sequence effects, melting curves, hyperchromicity, denaturation, renaturation and hybridisation kinetics.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Solve elementary topology problems, interpret melting and reassociation curves, distinguish stability from sequence identity.

**Progression:** Build on 1.B.04. Bridge to subunit 1.C.
