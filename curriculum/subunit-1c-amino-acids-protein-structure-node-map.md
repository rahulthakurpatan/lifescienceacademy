# Life Science Academy — Subunit 1.C: Amino Acids and Protein Structure

Trace protein organisation from amino acid chemistry and peptide formation to higher-order conformations, folding, stability and biological functions.

Five approved learning nodes. See [Unit 1 master map](unit-01-structure-function-biomolecules-subunit-map.md) for source references and the original A–J crosswalk.

## Node contracts

### 1.C.01 — Amino Acid Chemistry and Peptide Formation

**Student-facing description:** Amino acid classification, stereochemistry, zwitterions, pKa, isoelectric point, titration and peptide bonds.

**Core scope:** Side-chain chemistry and essential/nonessential context, L/D convention and glycine exception, ionisation and charge, zwitterions, pKa, pI, titration, peptide linkage, termini and sequence direction.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate net charge and pI of representative amino acids and peptides, interpret titration curves.

**Progression:** Build on preceding biomolecular concepts. Prepare 1.C.02.

### 1.C.02 — Protein Backbone Conformation and Secondary Structure

**Student-facing description:** Primary structure, peptide planarity, phi and psi angles, Ramachandran plots, alpha helices, beta sheets, turns and loops.

**Core scope:** Primary sequence, peptide bond resonance and planarity, cis/trans bonds, phi/psi angles and steric constraints, Ramachandran regions, alpha helices, parallel/antiparallel sheets, turns and loops.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret Ramachandran plots and secondary-structure diagrams, connect hydrogen bonding and sequence to conformation.

**Progression:** Build on 1.C.01. Prepare 1.C.03.

### 1.C.03 — Protein Tertiary and Quaternary Organisation

**Student-facing description:** Motifs, domains, folds, structural classification, subunit interactions, protein assemblies and stabilising forces.

**Core scope:** Supersecondary structures, motifs, domains, folds, alpha/beta structural classes, hydrophobic cores, disulfides, noncovalent interactions, quaternary assembly, interfaces, symmetry and oligomerisation; distinguish domain, motif and subunit.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Read structural representations and predict consequences of changes in interfaces or stabilising interactions.

**Progression:** Build on 1.C.02. Prepare 1.C.04.

### 1.C.04 — Protein Folding, Stability and Denaturation

**Student-facing description:** Folding energetics, Anfinsen's experiment, chaperones, folding landscapes, denaturation, renaturation and aggregation.

**Core scope:** Folding thermodynamics and kinetics, native ensemble, Anfinsen evidence and limitations, energy landscapes, folding intermediates, chaperones, effects of temperature, pH and denaturants, reversibility and aggregation.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret folding and denaturation curves, compare thermodynamic stability and kinetic trapping, evaluate evidence for sequence-dependent folding.

**Progression:** Build on 1.C.03. Prepare 1.C.05.

### 1.C.05 — Protein Structure–Function Relationships

**Student-facing description:** Fibrous and globular proteins, collagen, keratin, myoglobin, haemoglobin, ligand binding, cooperativity and allostery.

**Core scope:** Fibrous versus globular architecture, collagen and keratin, haem groups, myoglobin and haemoglobin, oxygen-binding curves, fractional saturation, cooperativity, allosteric transitions, Bohr effect as biochemical context; physiology in Unit 7.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare binding curves, calculate simple fractional saturation and explain how structure supports function.

**Progression:** Build on 1.C.04. Bridge to subunit 1.D.
