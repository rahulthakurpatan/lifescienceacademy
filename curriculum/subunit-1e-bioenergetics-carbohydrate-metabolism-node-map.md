# Life Science Academy — Subunit 1.E: Bioenergetics and Carbohydrate Metabolism

Explain cellular energy conversion and carbohydrate metabolism through interconnected biochemical pathways, respiratory mechanisms and metabolic regulation.

Five approved learning nodes. See [Unit 1 master map](unit-01-structure-function-biomolecules-subunit-map.md) for source references and the original A–J crosswalk.

## Node contracts

### 1.E.01 — Bioenergetics and Biological Energy Transduction

**Student-facing description:** ATP, coupled reactions, group-transfer potential, high-energy compounds, biological electron carriers and energy conversion.

**Core scope:** Standard versus cellular free energies, coupling, ATP hydrolysis and group transfer, phosphorylated intermediates, thioesters, NAD/NADP, flavins, electron transfer, substrate-level phosphorylation and biological energy transducers; thermodynamic foundations in 1.A.05.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate coupled reaction energies and compare ATP and redox carrier roles.

**Progression:** Build on preceding biomolecular concepts. Prepare 1.E.02.

### 1.E.02 — Glycolysis, Pyruvate Metabolism and Fermentation

**Student-facing description:** Glycolytic reactions, energetics, regulation, aerobic and anaerobic metabolism, pyruvate dehydrogenase and acetyl-CoA formation.

**Core scope:** Preparatory/payoff steps, stoichiometry, substrate-level phosphorylation, hexokinase/PFK/pyruvate kinase regulation, NAD regeneration, lactate/alcohol fermentation, pyruvate dehydrogenase components and cofactors, regulation and pyruvate fates.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace carbon and ATP/NADH balances and interpret pathway regulation and enzyme-defect reasoning.

**Progression:** Build on 1.E.01. Prepare 1.E.03.

### 1.E.03 — Citric Acid Cycle and Metabolic Integration

**Student-facing description:** TCA cycle reactions, regulation, energetic yield, amphibolic functions and anaplerotic reactions.

**Core scope:** Cycle intermediates, enzymes, decarboxylation and redox steps, substrate-level phosphorylation, regulation, carbon tracing, amphibolic use, anaplerosis and glyoxylate-cycle comparison at relevant scope.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate stoichiometric yields, trace representative carbon labels and reason about replenishment of intermediates.

**Progression:** Build on 1.E.02. Prepare 1.E.04.

### 1.E.04 — Electron Transport and Oxidative Phosphorylation

**Student-facing description:** Respiratory complexes, chemiosmosis, proton motive force, ATP synthase, respiratory control, inhibitors, uncouplers and mitochondrial shuttles.

**Core scope:** Complexes I–IV, ubiquinone and cytochrome c, proton translocation, membrane potential and pH gradient, chemiosmotic evidence, ATP synthase mechanism, P/O ratios and assumptions, respiratory control, uncoupling, inhibitor interpretation and malate–aspartate/glycerol phosphate shuttles; plant-specific respiration in Unit 6.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret respiratory and ATP data, compare electron-entry routes, estimate ATP yields under stated assumptions and distinguish inhibition from uncoupling.

**Progression:** Build on 1.E.03. Prepare 1.E.05.

### 1.E.05 — Glucose Homeostasis and Alternative Carbohydrate Pathways

**Student-facing description:** Gluconeogenesis, glycogenesis, glycogenolysis, Cori cycle, pentose phosphate pathway, fructose and galactose metabolism.

**Core scope:** Gluconeogenic bypasses and reciprocal regulation, glycogen synthesis/branching and degradation, Cori and glucose–alanine cycles, oxidative/nonoxidative PPP, NADPH and ribose production, fructose/galactose entry, integrated hormonal and tissue context; physiology in Unit 7.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare pathway costs and products, interpret fed/fasted biochemical patterns and reason about NADPH and glucose supply.

**Progression:** Build on 1.E.04. Bridge to subunit 1.F.
