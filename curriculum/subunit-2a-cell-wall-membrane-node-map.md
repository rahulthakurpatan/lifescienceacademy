## Ten connected learning nodes

### 2.A.01 — Cell Walls: Structure, Diversity and Biological Functions

**Driving question:** Why do many cells need a structural boundary outside the plasma membrane, and why do different kingdoms build it from different materials?

**Required scientific coverage:** Primary and secondary plant cell walls; cellulose, hemicellulose, pectins, lignin and wall remodeling; bacterial peptidoglycan, Gram-positive and Gram-negative envelope organization, wall-associated structures; fungal chitin/glucan walls; archaeal S-layers and selected pseudomurein systems (not universal); protection, morphology, osmotic resistance, porosity and physiological consequences.

**Evidence, mathematics and CSIR/SET reasoning:** Conceptual wall-strength and osmotic-lysis comparisons; basic effects of lysozyme and cell-wall-targeting antibiotics, with precise bacterial target distinctions.

**Boundary against duplication:** Do not duplicate the detailed classification of microorganisms, plant development or cell-wall-based antimicrobial resistance mechanisms scheduled for other subunits.

### 2.A.02 — Membrane Lipids and Formation of the Lipid Bilayer

**Driving question:** How do amphipathic molecules spontaneously create a stable yet flexible boundary in water?

**Required scientific coverage:** Amphipathic nature; fatty acyl chain length and unsaturation; glycerophospholipids, sphingolipids, glycolipids, sterols; hydrophobic effect, free-energy minimization, micelles versus bilayers versus liposomes; bilayer thickness, leaflet arrangement, lipid asymmetry and the energetic barrier to transbilayer movement.

**Evidence, mathematics and CSIR/SET reasoning:** Thermodynamic reasoning, temperature/composition predictions and lipid classification examples; avoid simplistic claims that all lipids form bilayers.

**Boundary against duplication:** Formal historic membrane models and experimental measurement of dynamics belong to 2.A.03.

### 2.A.03 — Membrane Models, Architecture and Dynamics

**Driving question:** How did evidence transform the membrane from a hypothetical lipid film into the dynamic modern bilayer model?

**Required scientific coverage:** Langmuir monolayers; Gorter–Grendel reasoning and its limitations; Davson–Danielli model; Singer–Nicolson fluid mosaic framework and modern revisions; liposomes, planar bilayers and model systems; prokaryotic versus eukaryotic membranes; fluidity, phase transitions, lateral diffusion, asymmetry, microdomains and context-sensitive lipid raft concepts.

**Evidence, mathematics and CSIR/SET reasoning:** Interpret freeze-fracture images, FRAP curves, lateral diffusion and limitations of simplified liposome experiments; distinguish experimentally supported conclusions from models.

**Boundary against duplication:** Membrane-protein topology, structure and detailed functions belong to 2.A.04.

### 2.A.04 — Membrane Proteins: Structure, Organization and Functions

**Driving question:** How are proteins positioned within membranes, and how does their topology enable the membrane to perform selective tasks?

**Required scientific coverage:** Integral, multipass, single-pass, peripheral and lipid-anchored proteins; alpha-helical and beta-barrel topologies; membrane-spanning hydrophobic domains, orientation and sidedness; lipid–protein interactions, protein mobility and clustering; glycocalyx, membrane carbohydrates, surface recognition and structural anchors; basic classification of channels, carriers, pumps and receptors without preempting their dedicated mechanisms.

**Evidence, mathematics and CSIR/SET reasoning:** Hydropathy plots, protease protection, detergent extraction, FRAP interpretation and topology diagrams.

**Boundary against duplication:** Dedicated channel kinetics, pump energetics and signaling pathways receive separate treatment later.

### 2.A.05 — Diffusion, Membrane Permeability and Facilitated Transport

**Driving question:** Which molecules cross the bilayer passively, and what changes when a transport protein is involved?

**Required scientific coverage:** Brownian movement, concentration and chemical-potential gradients; Fick's first law with symbols/units/assumptions and qualitative second-law context; permeability coefficient, partitioning and thickness effects; simple diffusion; carrier-mediated facilitated diffusion, saturation, specificity, competitive inhibition, bidirectional transport and GLUT examples; distinguish passive carriers from channels.

**Evidence, mathematics and CSIR/SET reasoning:** Transport-rate graphs, permeability comparisons and dimensionally checked Fick-law problems with explicit boundary assumptions.

**Boundary against duplication:** Water-specific osmotic consequences belong to 2.A.06; ion channels and active pumping belong to 2.A.07–08.

### 2.A.06 — Osmosis, Water Transport and Osmotic Balance

**Driving question:** Why can water movement change cell volume even when water concentration is not directly measured?

**Required scientific coverage:** Chemical potential of water; osmosis and semipermeability; osmolarity versus osmolality; tonicity versus osmolarity, nonpenetrating versus penetrating solutes; van't Hoff relation and ideal dilute assumptions; plant water potential, turgor, plasmolysis, deplasmolysis; animal cell crenation and lysis; aquaporins, osmotic adjustment and effects of wall support.

**Evidence, mathematics and CSIR/SET reasoning:** Tonicity experiments, volume-response predictions and correctly qualified osmotic pressure/water-potential calculations.

**Boundary against duplication:** Avoid duplicating whole-plant water transport and kidney physiology taught in System Physiology units.

### 2.A.07 — Ion Channels: Selectivity, Gating and Ion Movement

**Driving question:** How do channels permit rapid and selective passive ion movement without hydrolyzing ATP?

**Required scientific coverage:** Ion pore versus carrier; ion hydration, dehydration and selectivity filters; potassium, sodium, calcium and chloride channels; voltage-, ligand- and mechanically gated channels; open, closed and inactivated states, gating probabilities, conductance, channel kinetics, qualitative electrochemical driving forces; basic channel recording and channelopathy reasoning.

**Evidence, mathematics and CSIR/SET reasoning:** Selectivity/gating graphs, current–voltage reasoning, channel versus pump comparison; introduce gradients conceptually and reserve Nernst/Goldman derivations for 2.A.09.

**Boundary against duplication:** Full synaptic signaling and detailed whole-neuron physiology belong to later units.

### 2.A.08 — Active Transport, Cotransporters and Membrane Pumps

**Driving question:** How can cells move solutes against electrochemical gradients without violating thermodynamics?

**Required scientific coverage:** Chemical/electrochemical work and energy coupling; primary versus secondary active transport; P-type ATPases, sodium–potassium ATPase, calcium ATPases, V-type proton pumps, F-type ATP synthase in energy-transduction context, ABC transporters; symport, antiport, uniport definitions; SGLT, Na+/H+ exchange and proton-coupled transport examples; electrogenic versus electroneutral exchange, inhibition and regulation.

**Evidence, mathematics and CSIR/SET reasoning:** ATP-coupling calculations and gradient/stoichiometry case studies, with directional conventions and units checked.

**Boundary against duplication:** Transporter-specific electrophysiological calculations follow in 2.A.09; organelle-specific metabolic machinery is not duplicated.

### 2.A.09 — Electrical Properties of Membranes and Membrane Potential

**Driving question:** How do selective permeabilities and ion gradients create an electrical potential across an insulating membrane?

**Required scientific coverage:** Membrane charge separation, capacitance, resistance, conductance; electrical potential, chemical and electrochemical potential; Nernst equation and equilibrium/reversal potential; Goldman–Hodgkin–Katz framework and its simplifying assumptions; resting membrane potential, driving force, electrogenic pumps; qualitative depolarization, repolarization and action-potential mechanisms; voltage-clamp versus current-clamp fundamentals.

**Evidence, mathematics and CSIR/SET reasoning:** Nernst/Goldman numerical problems with temperature, concentrations, sign conventions and units verified; equivalent circuit interpretations.

**Boundary against duplication:** Detailed neuronal circuit and muscle electrophysiology belong to System Physiology, not repeated here.

### 2.A.10 — Intracellular Membrane Sorting, Vesicular Transport and Regulation

**Driving question:** How does a cell deliver membrane proteins and lipids to the correct compartment while preserving membrane identity?

**Required scientific coverage:** Protein localization signals, topology and membrane orientation; basic secretory versus endocytic routes; ER-to-Golgi and Golgi sorting principles; COPII, COPI and clathrin coats; adaptor cargo selection, Rab GTPases, tethering and SNARE-mediated fusion; endocytosis, exocytosis, recycling and retrograde traffic; regulation by signaling, cargo and membrane composition; sorting defects and cellular consequences.

**Evidence, mathematics and CSIR/SET reasoning:** Interpret protein localization mutants, pulse–chase experiments and vesicle-trafficking block diagrams, distinguishing cargo signals from transport machinery.

**Boundary against duplication:** Full structure of nucleus, ER, Golgi, lysosomes and other organelles belongs to 2.B; transcription/translation and protein processing details belong to Unit 3.

## Progression and teaching hand-offs

1. **Boundary:** A mechanically protective wall (2.A.01) contrasts with a selectively permeable lipid boundary (2.A.02).
2. **Architecture:** Chemistry of lipids makes experimental membrane models (2.A.03) comprehensible; membrane proteins (2.A.04) explain how the barrier acquires selective functions.
3. **Movement:** Passive molecular diffusion (2.A.05) leads naturally to water and osmotic gradients (2.A.06), then to channel-mediated ion flow (2.A.07).
4. **Energy:** Passive flux clarifies why pumps and coupled transport (2.A.08) must do work; together ion gradients and permeabilities explain membrane electricity (2.A.09).
5. **Inside the cell:** After crossing barriers, membrane cargo must reach precise compartments through sorting, vesicles and regulation (2.A.10). The student can then study **2.B — Intracellular Organelles** from an informed mechanistic foundation.

## Cross-unit content boundaries

- **2.B:** Deep anatomy and individual functions of intracellular organelles; 2.A.10 covers transport systems rather than writing complete organelle chapters.
- **2.C:** Gene organization, chromatin, operons and transposons.
- **2.D:** Mitosis, meiosis, checkpoint regulation, apoptosis, necrosis and autophagy.
- **2.E:** Microbial growth, cell-division strategies, stress and antimicrobial resistance; cell-wall antibiotic targets in 2.A.01 are only introductory examples.
- **3:** Biosynthesis, processing and regulation of DNA/RNA/proteins, including detailed protein synthesis/processing; 2.A covers membrane organization and sorting.
- **4:** Full receptor-mediated signaling and cell adhesion; 2.A.04 covers structural membrane-protein foundations, not complete signaling cascades.
- **Plant/animal physiology:** Whole-organism transport and excitability; 2.A addresses principles at the membrane.
