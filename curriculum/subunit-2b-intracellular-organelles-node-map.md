# Life Science Academy — Subunit 2.B: Intracellular Organelles

Cell wall is in merged NET Unit 2.A and the existing Academy `2.A.01` chapter design. ## Final ten-node progression

### 2.B.01 — Nucleus: Structure, Organization and Dynamics

**Learning question:** How does the nucleus organize and protect cellular genetic material while remaining connected to the cytoplasm?

**Coverage to teach:** Nuclear double membrane and perinuclear space; continuity with ER; nuclear pore complexes and selective nucleocytoplasmic exchange as an organelle function; nuclear lamina; nucleoplasm, nucleolus and basic ribosomal subunit assembly; nuclear organization, shape and changes during the cell cycle at an introductory structural level.

**NET/SET reasoning and evidence:** Labeled nuclear-envelope diagrams, transport-direction logic, nuclear pore/lamina functional comparisons, nucleolus-versus-nucleus questions.

**Boundary to prevent scope drift and duplication:** Detailed DNA packaging and chromosome territories belong to 2.C; cell-cycle regulatory mechanisms belong to 2.D; transcription, RNA processing and ribosome synthesis pathways belong to Unit 3.

### 2.B.02 — Endoplasmic Reticulum: Structure and Cellular Functions

**Learning question:** Why is the endoplasmic reticulum divided into connected regions with different cellular tasks?

**Coverage to teach:** ER membrane network and lumen; continuity with outer nuclear membrane; rough versus smooth ER; roles in protein entry/folding and quality control at overview level; lipid and steroid synthesis, detoxification and Ca2+ storage/release; ER shape, specialization and basic functional adaptations.

**NET/SET reasoning and evidence:** Rough-versus-smooth ER micrographs, tissue-specific ER abundance, effects of disrupted ER functions, compartment diagrams.

**Boundary to prevent scope drift and duplication:** The complete mechanism of translation, post-translational processing and protein quality control belongs to Unit 3; vesicular coat mechanisms are primarily assigned to 2.A.10.

### 2.B.03 — Golgi Apparatus: Organization, Modification and Sorting

**Learning question:** How does the Golgi apparatus impose order on processing and distribution without making the proteins itself?

**Coverage to teach:** Golgi stacks/cisternae, cis-face, trans-face and trans-Golgi network; relation to ER and secretory pathway; overview of glycosylation and product maturation; organelle polarity, secretory product packaging, lysosomal enzyme targeting principle and tissue-specific functions.

**NET/SET reasoning and evidence:** Label cis/trans faces, infer movement from compartment-block experiments, distinguish ER versus Golgi roles, predict secretion defects.

**Boundary to prevent scope drift and duplication:** Detailed glycan chemistry and biosynthetic enzyme pathways are not separate research-level treatments; full coat/SNARE sorting machinery was introduced in 2.A.10 and protein processing is in Unit 3.

### 2.B.04 — Lysosomes: Structure, Digestion and Cellular Recycling

**Learning question:** How can a membrane-bound compartment safely perform breakdown reactions that would be harmful elsewhere?

**Coverage to teach:** Lysosomal single membrane, acidic lumen, V-type proton pump as a context-specific example, hydrolytic enzymes, endosome–lysosome relationship, intracellular digestion, heterophagy, autophagic cargo delivery as an overview, recycling of breakdown products, basic lysosomal storage disorder examples.

**NET/SET reasoning and evidence:** Compare lysosomes with Golgi, vacuoles and peroxisomes; predict effects of pH/hydrolase defects; interpret basic organelle-function cases.

**Boundary to prevent scope drift and duplication:** Autophagy regulation and cell-death decisions receive full treatment in 2.D; no advanced disease-pathway review.

### 2.B.05 — Vacuoles: Storage, Osmoregulation and Cell Homeostasis

**Learning question:** How can the same general type of membrane-bounded compartment support storage, digestion and water balance in different cells?

**Coverage to teach:** Vacuole membrane (tonoplast), cellular contents and diversity; central plant vacuole, vacuolar storage of solutes/pigments/defense compounds, hydrolytic activity and pH; turgor in the context of cell architecture; fungal vacuoles and contractile vacuoles in protists; comparisons with animal lysosomes.

**NET/SET reasoning and evidence:** Predict consequences of tonoplast or vacuole dysfunction; distinguish osmoregulatory and storage functions; compare cell types.

**Boundary to prevent scope drift and duplication:** Osmosis, water potential and transport calculations belong to 2.A.06; whole-plant water transport belongs to the plant physiology unit.

### 2.B.06 — Peroxisomes: Oxidation, Detoxification and Specialized Roles

**Learning question:** Why are certain oxidation reactions confined to peroxisomes rather than performed throughout the cytoplasm?

**Coverage to teach:** Peroxisome structure and single-membrane organization; oxidases and catalase; formation and detoxification of hydrogen peroxide; fatty-acid oxidation at conceptual level, plant peroxisomal and glyoxysomal functions; brief organelle biogenesis, import and contrast with lysosomes and mitochondria.

**NET/SET reasoning and evidence:** Catalase/oxidase reasoning, organelle identification, peroxisome-versus-lysosome distinctions, cell-function interpretation.

**Boundary to prevent scope drift and duplication:** Detailed metabolic cycles and biochemical engineering are other syllabus areas; avoid specialist peroxisome genetic disorders and frontier biology.

### 2.B.07 — Mitochondria: Structure, Energy Functions and Dynamics

**Learning question:** How does mitochondrial architecture make ATP production and other essential cell functions possible?

**Coverage to teach:** Double membrane, intermembrane space, cristae, matrix and respiratory membrane location; basic mitochondrial functions including aerobic ATP generation at organelle level, metabolism and calcium buffering; mitochondrial DNA and ribosomes, endosymbiotic origin as a foundational model; fission, fusion and organelle inheritance at overview level.

**NET/SET reasoning and evidence:** Relate respiratory membranes to function, identify organelle components in micrographs, compare mitochondrial and chloroplast architecture, interpret simplified functional defects.

**Boundary to prevent scope drift and duplication:** Oxidative phosphorylation chemistry and detailed Krebs-cycle mechanisms belong to Unit 1; plant respiration belongs to Unit 6; apoptotic signaling belongs to 2.D, and extranuclear inheritance to the genetics unit.

### 2.B.08 — Plastids and Chloroplasts: Diversity, Structure and Functions

**Learning question:** How do plastids specialize for photosynthesis, pigment formation or storage within plant cells?

**Coverage to teach:** Proplastids, chloroplasts, chromoplasts and leucoplast variants (including amyloplasts); plastid differentiation and interconversion as introductory cell biology; chloroplast double envelope, stroma, thylakoid membrane and lumen, grana, chloroplast DNA and ribosomes; light absorption and carbon fixation as compartment functions.

**NET/SET reasoning and evidence:** Identify plastid types from functions, interpret chloroplast cross-sections, distinguish mitochondrial and chloroplast inner membrane systems.

**Boundary to prevent scope drift and duplication:** Detailed light reactions, photophosphorylation, Calvin cycle and C3/C4/CAM mechanisms belong to Plant System Physiology; plastid gene inheritance belongs to Genetics.

### 2.B.09 — Cytoskeleton: Actin, Microtubules and Intermediate Filaments

**Learning question:** How can a cell remain mechanically organized while continuously remodeling its internal structure?

**Coverage to teach:** Actin microfilaments and associated proteins at basic level; microtubules built from tubulin and their polarity/dynamic instability; intermediate filament types and mechanical roles; cytoskeleton-membrane connections; centrosomes and microtubule organizing centers; cell shape, organelle positioning, cellular polarity and cytoskeletal remodeling.

**NET/SET reasoning and evidence:** Classify filaments by subunit, diameter and function, interpret basic drug perturbations, compare structure with function, distinguish microtubules from microfilaments.

**Boundary to prevent scope drift and duplication:** Full mitotic spindle mechanics and checkpoints are in 2.D; detailed molecular mechanics or high-resolution structural research excluded.

### 2.B.10 — Cytoskeletal Motility: Molecular Motors, Cilia and Flagella

**Learning question:** How do cytoskeletal filaments and ATP-dependent motors generate organized movement inside and outside a cell?

**Coverage to teach:** Myosin–actin, kinesin–microtubule and dynein–microtubule motility at standard exam level; directional movement, cargo transport and cell shape changes; actin-based cell migration; cilia and eukaryotic flagella including axoneme 9+2 architecture, dynein-driven bending and basal bodies; cytoplasmic streaming, cellular transport and motility comparisons.

**NET/SET reasoning and evidence:** Predict motor direction, identify 9+2 components, reason about disrupted dynein or actin, distinguish eukaryotic cilia/flagella from bacterial flagellar mechanisms.

**Boundary to prevent scope drift and duplication:** Detailed muscle contraction physiology and neuron-specific axonal transport physiology are elsewhere; bacterial flagellar movement is not to be mistaken for the eukaryotic axoneme.

## Why the progression connects

1. **Nuclear envelope → ER (01–02):** The outer nuclear membrane is continuous with the ER; the transition is structural and natural, not an arbitrary ordering.
2. **ER → Golgi (02–03):** Organelles are understood as linked parts of the secretory system; the detailed universal coat machinery was introduced in 2.A.10.
3. **Golgi → lysosomes → vacuoles (03–05):** Sorting and delivery lead to lysosomal digestion and vacuolar storage, degradation and water-balance functions.
4. **Vacuoles → peroxisomes → mitochondria (05–07):** The learner compares chemically specialized compartments, then examines oxidative metabolism and ATP-linked organelle architecture.
5. **Mitochondria → plastids/chloroplasts (07–08):** Comparison of double-membrane organelles clarifies similarities, distinctions and energy-related specialization.
6. **Organelles → cytoskeleton → motility (09–10):** The cytoskeleton positions and moves the organelles already understood; its motors explain directional movement, cilia, flagella and cell migration.

**Progression is pedagogical/causal, not a claim that these organelles evolved or were discovered in this exact order.** Historical experiments should appear within a chapter only where they genuinely improve exam-level understanding.
