## Final ten connected reading chapters

### 2.D.01 — Cell Cycle: Phases, Organization and Biological Significance

**Conceptual question:** What happens to a cell from one division to the next, and how do DNA content and chromosome number change?

**In-syllabus scientific scope:** Cell cycle purpose in growth and replacement; interphase versus M phase; G1, S, G2, M and G0; cell-cycle entry and exit, quiescence versus permanent differentiation at basic level; what DNA synthesis means; distinctions among chromosome number, chromatid number and C-value; standard 2C/4C diagrams and example cell-count calculations.

**NET/SET reasoning:** Progressively solve 2n, n, 2C and 4C questions at each phase; interpret labeled DNA-content flow-cytometry histograms without specialist instrument operation; common misconception that S phase doubles the number of chromosomes.

**Boundary against syllabus drift:** DNA polymerases and full replication mechanism belong to Unit 3; developmental cell-fate programs belong to Unit 5.

### 2.D.02 — Molecular Regulation of the Cell Cycle: Cyclins and CDKs

**Conceptual question:** How does a cell make transitions between phases happen in the correct order?

**In-syllabus scientific scope:** Cyclin-dependent protein kinase concept, activation and cyclin abundance; G1/S, S, and mitotic cyclin–CDK complexes with organism-specific names carefully distinguished; phosphorylation as control principle, basic inhibitors p21/p27, regulators such as CAK, Wee1 and Cdc25 at exam level; ubiquitin-linked cyclin turnover, SCF and APC/C at a conceptual level; purpose of once-per-cycle ordering.

**NET/SET reasoning:** Interpret time-course plots of cyclin levels and CDK activity; predict general phase arrest from cyclin/CDK disruption; distinguish protein abundance from kinase activity.

**Boundary against syllabus drift:** Cancer-specific oncogene/tumor-suppressor pathways are fully developed in Unit 4; do not add regulatory protein catalogs without examination value.

### 2.D.03 — Cell-Cycle Checkpoints and Control Mechanisms

**Conceptual question:** How can a cell delay division until DNA and chromosomes are ready?

**In-syllabus scientific scope:** Concept and logic of checkpoints; restriction point/G1-S decision; DNA replication monitoring and intra-S response; G2/M response to unreplicated/damaged DNA; p53–p21 and Rb–E2F as classical familiar regulators at a bounded, clear level; spindle assembly checkpoint as first introduction to metaphase-anaphase permission; repair, arrest, senescence or apoptotic outcome as concepts.

**NET/SET reasoning:** Map cell-cycle defects to checkpoint phases, infer outcomes in simple mutants, separate checkpoint from phase itself; distinguish cyclin switches (2.D.02) from monitoring functions (2.D.03).

**Boundary against syllabus drift:** Full biochemical DNA damage/repair pathways belong to Unit 3; detailed cell death biochemistry belongs to 2.D.08; cancer etiology belongs to Unit 4.

### 2.D.04 — Mitosis: Chromosome Behavior and Sequential Stages

**Conceptual question:** How is a duplicated genome separated into two daughter nuclei while preserving chromosome number?

**In-syllabus scientific scope:** Mitosis biological importance; preparatory chromosome duplication as a brief prerequisite; chromosome condensation, nuclear envelope breakdown, kinetochore attachment, alignment, sister chromatid disjunction and nuclear reformation; prophase, prometaphase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase; plant versus animal mitotic apparatus at an introductory level; chromosome and chromatid bookkeeping by stage.

**NET/SET reasoning:** Recognize mitotic stages from diagrams and micrographs, calculate daughter-cell chromosome counts; correct anaphase conventions where separated chromatids become individual chromosomes.

**Boundary against syllabus drift:** Spindle control, molecular segregation machinery and cytokinesis are treated in 2.D.05; meiosis and homolog pairing belong to 2.D.06–07.

### 2.D.05 — Mitotic Regulation, Spindle Function and Cytokinesis

**Conceptual question:** What coordinates chromosome segregation with the physical separation of one cell into two?

**In-syllabus scientific scope:** Spindle poles and microtubule classes as mitosis-specific application; kinetochore attachment and bi-orientation, cohesion release (separase/securin concept), APC/C activation and spindle checkpoint satisfaction; anaphase A/B as standard observations, avoiding advanced force models; cleavage furrow, actin–myosin contractile ring, animal abscission overview; phragmoplast and cell plate development in plants; basic regulation and consequences of division failure.

**NET/SET reasoning:** Distinguish abnormal metaphase arrest from cytokinesis defects, identify spindle attachments, compare animal/plant division in labeled figures; demonstrate ploidy after failed cytokinesis at basic level.

**Boundary against syllabus drift:** Detailed cytoskeletal filament biochemistry and motor structure belong to 2.B.09–10; advanced mitotic mechanics or chromosomal instability research not covered.

### 2.D.06 — Meiosis I: Homologous Chromosomes and Reduction Division

**Conceptual question:** Why does meiosis need an additional pairing and reductional division that mitosis lacks?

**In-syllabus scientific scope:** Diploid and haploid prerequisites; premeiotic DNA replication; homologs versus sister chromatids; synapsis and synaptonemal complex at exam level; leptotene, zygotene, pachytene, diplotene, diakinesis; crossing-over outcomes as chromosome-level observation and genetic variation, chiasmata; metaphase I/anaphase I disjunction of homologs, telophase I and brief interkinesis; reductional division.

**NET/SET reasoning:** Interpret bivalents/tetrads, explain chiasma timing, solve meiosis-I 2n/n and C-value counting, compare homolog and sister behavior with mitosis.

**Boundary against syllabus drift:** Molecular strand-exchange/recombination enzymes belong to Unit 3; population genetics consequences belong to Genetics; meiosis-II sequence is reserved for 2.D.07.

### 2.D.07 — Meiosis II: Chromatid Separation and Meiotic Regulation

**Conceptual question:** How do two meiotic divisions produce haploid cells, and why do homologs and sisters separate at different times?

**In-syllabus scientific scope:** Prophase II to telophase II and cytokinesis; equational division, lack of a new S phase between I and II in normal meiosis; meiosis-specific cohesion protection and release in conceptual terms; comparison of spindle/chromatid arrangement between the two divisions; basic meiotic checkpoint ideas; crossing-over versus independent assortment (simple cell-biological connection); nondisjunction in meiosis I versus II and resulting gamete chromosome counts; mitosis-versus-meiosis comparative synthesis.

**NET/SET reasoning:** Quantitative DNA and chromosome tracking across both divisions, identify meiotic stage from images, analyze basic nondisjunction outcomes; avoid ambiguous ploidy language.

**Boundary against syllabus drift:** Full Mendelian pedigrees, inheritance, genetic linkage mapping, gametogenesis and fertilization belong to Units 5 and 8; detailed meiotic recombination biochemistry belongs to Unit 3.

### 2.D.08 — Apoptosis: Programmed Cell Death and Its Regulation

**Conceptual question:** How can a cell activate an orderly self-destruction pathway without the same tissue injury pattern as traumatic lysis?

**In-syllabus scientific scope:** Apoptotic morphology (shrinkage, chromatin condensation, blebbing, apoptotic bodies), controlled clearance; initiator versus executioner caspases, intrinsic mitochondria-linked signaling and Bcl-2 family as basic principles; extrinsic death-receptor pathway; caspase activation and cleavage; physiological functions such as cell-number homeostasis and removal of damaged cells; apoptotic vs non-apoptotic distinctions.

**NET/SET reasoning:** Compare intrinsic and extrinsic pathway schematics, predict broad effects of caspase inhibition and Bcl-2 up/down changes, read morphological images; separate morphology from molecular evidence.

**Boundary against syllabus drift:** Full receptor signal cascades and cancer therapeutic interventions belong to Unit 4; specialized apoptosis proteomics and disease-specific pathways are beyond NET/SET necessity.

### 2.D.09 — Necrosis: Cellular Injury and Mechanisms of Cell Death

**Conceptual question:** What distinguishes damage-associated necrotic cell death from controlled apoptotic removal?

**In-syllabus scientific scope:** Classical necrosis definition and contexts such as severe hypoxia, toxins and membrane injury; ATP depletion, ionic imbalance, swelling, loss of plasma-membrane integrity and release of cellular contents; tissue inflammation as a typical consequence; necrotic morphology and simple observation methods; distinguish necrosis, apoptosis and viable cell stress at basic level; briefly acknowledge that some necrotic-appearing deaths can be regulated without specialist pathway expansion.

**NET/SET reasoning:** Identify likely process from microscopy/LDH-type leakage examples, compare membrane integrity and inflammatory context, explain why single markers alone may be imperfect.

**Boundary against syllabus drift:** Necroptosis/pyroptosis/ferroptosis complex pathway networks are not required standalone research chapters here; full injury pathology belongs to specialist domains.

### 2.D.10 — Autophagy: Cellular Recycling, Survival and Homeostasis

**Conceptual question:** How can a cell survive by digesting its own components, and how does that differ from cell death?

**In-syllabus scientific scope:** Autophagy as lysosome-dependent cellular recycling; macroautophagy stages: initiation, phagophore, autophagosome, fusion with lysosome and degradation; brief distinction from microautophagy and chaperone-mediated autophagy; homeostatic roles in nutrient stress, quality control and damaged organelle clearance; broad mTOR/AMPK nutrient-input concepts without detailed pathways; relation to survival, apoptosis and necrosis; emphasize that autophagy is not automatically a cell-death mechanism.

**NET/SET reasoning:** Distinguish lysosome, autophagosome and autolysosome, interpret simple flux-block diagrams, compare autophagy with apoptosis and necrosis using NET/SET-style cases.

**Boundary against syllabus drift:** Lysosomal organelle architecture covered in 2.B.04; disease-specific molecular autophagy networks, specialized protocols and research debates excluded.

## Progressive teaching logic

1. **2.C → 2.D.01:** Students already recognize chromosome architecture; now they follow its DNA content through interphase, mitosis and quiescence.
2. **2.D.01 → 2.D.02–03:** Describing phases motivates the question of how transitions are scheduled and how errors prevent premature progression.
3. **2.D.03 → 2.D.04–05:** After the checkpoint framework, mitotic stages are intelligible as controlled chromosome behavior, followed by spindle function and physical cytokinesis.
4. **2.D.05 → 2.D.06–07:** Mitosis establishes a comparison for why meiosis has homolog pairing, a reduction division, and a second chromatid-separation division; regulation is embedded at the relevant stages.
5. **2.D.07 → 2.D.08–10:** Having studied orderly proliferation, learners now compare programmed cell elimination, injury-associated necrosis, and survival-associated intracellular recycling.
6. **Next subunit:** E addresses **microbial** cell growth/division physiology, using cell-division basics but not relabeling prokaryotic binary fission as mitosis.

This is a **progressive academic order**, not a claim that these processes always occur chronologically within one cell. Apoptosis, necrosis and autophagy represent different responses and may occur in varied contexts.

## Detailed quality and examination checks

- Check chromosome number, chromatid number, ploidy and DNA content in each cell-cycle/meiosis exercise independently, including anaphase and meiosis-I conventions.
- Use correct checkpoints and regulatory relationships without treating p53 as a necessary feature of all eukaryotic cycles or overextending animal-specific examples to plants.
- Keep every standard stage of mitosis and meiosis I/II visible in the relevant chapter; distinguish five prophase-I substages.
- Distinguish apoptosis from necrotic injury without categorical claims: inflammation and mechanisms depend on context. Autophagy primarily supports recycling and survival and must not be unconditionally called programmed cell death.
- Include representative illustrations, phase photographs/diagrams, quantitative graphs and properly explained original practice where appropriate, but never arbitrary PhD-level detail.
- Every microtopic and practice item maps to one of the syllabus elements above; omit specialist repair mechanisms, full oncogenesis, research-level cell-death variants, gametogenesis and advanced cytogenetics.
- Each published node must be a fully written, naturally flowing single reading page with in-page navigation and scientific, accessibility and link QA. Do not use length inflation, formulaic AI prose or placeholders.
