## Final twelve connected learning nodes

### 3.A.01 — Evidence for DNA Replication and the Replicon Concept

**Content and microtopic responsibility:** Conservative, semiconservative and dispersive models; Meselson–Stahl result and brief eukaryotic experimental confirmation; replication origin, bubble, forks, replicon, bidirectional versus unidirectional copying. Assign core evidence and vocabulary here.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET reasoning:** Predict labeled-DNA density bands; compare models and count simple origin/fork diagrams.

**Other-unit and depth boundary:** Complete origin-initiation enzymes belong to 3.A.02.

### 3.A.02 — DNA Replication Origins and Initiation

**Content and microtopic responsibility:** Bacterial oriC and DnaA as E. coli examples; local melting, helicase loading, primase recruitment; eukaryotic multiple origins, ORC and MCM licensing/firing principles; the one-initiation-per-cycle idea.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET reasoning:** Recognize replication origins, bubbles and initiation defects, including differences between bacterial and eukaryotic origins.

**Other-unit and depth boundary:** Full core enzyme biochemistry is in 3.A.03; cell-cycle checkpoints are in 2.D.

### 3.A.03 — Replication Fork Architecture and Core Replication Enzymes

**Content and microtopic responsibility:** Antiparallel templates; 5′→3′ polymerase direction, required primer 3′-OH; helicase, SSB/RPA, primase, polymerase, sliding clamp and loader, ligase, topoisomerases, supercoiling; replisome, continuous and discontinuous synthesis, Okazaki fragments and primer removal.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET reasoning:** Label replication forks and 3′/5′ strand ends; infer results of enzyme inhibition; compare processivity and enzyme function.

**Other-unit and depth boundary:** Actual bacterial/eukaryotic system-specific mechanisms follow in 3.A.04–05.

### 3.A.04 — Bacterial DNA Replication: Elongation, Okazaki Fragments and Termination

**Content and microtopic responsibility:** Representative E. coli DNA Pol III holoenzyme, β sliding clamp; leading/lagging strand execution, Pol I primer removal and ligation; fork convergence, E. coli Tus–Ter as a model not a universal feature; DNA gyrase/topoisomerases, decatenation; Pol I, II and III overview.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET reasoning:** Identify polymerase and termination defects, explain lagging-strand processing and predict outcomes of fork arrest.

**Other-unit and depth boundary:** No duplication of binary fission/segregation taught in 2.E; avoid organism-agnostic E. coli claims.

### 3.A.05 — Eukaryotic DNA Replication: Fork Progression and Chromosome Ends

**Content and microtopic responsibility:** Pol α–primase, Pol δ and Pol ε with appropriately qualified strand assignments; PCNA/RFC, RPA, RNase H/FEN1, ligase I; multiple origins, fork convergence, nucleosome reassembly; end-replication problem for linear chromosomes and telomerase mechanism.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET reasoning:** Compare bacterial and eukaryotic polymerases, reason about origin/fork diagrams and consequences of absent telomerase.

**Other-unit and depth boundary:** Histone structure and telomere anatomy in 2.C; research-level replication-stress biology excluded.

### 3.A.06 — Extrachromosomal Replicons and Their Replication Strategies

**Content and microtopic responsibility:** Extrachromosomal DNA as independently replicating replicons; plasmid origin, copy number and elementary incompatibility; mitochondrial/plastid DNA in context; suitable viral/bacteriophage DNA examples; basic theta and rolling-circle intermediates; episome versus plasmid.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET reasoning:** Distinguish theta from rolling-circle intermediates, independent replicons from chromosomes and interpret simple copy-number scenarios.

**Other-unit and depth boundary:** Detailed cloning/vector protocols in Methods, organelle inheritance in Unit 8, viral gene regulation in 3.D.

### 3.A.07 — Replication Fidelity, Polymerase Proofreading and Mismatch Repair

**Content and microtopic responsibility:** Complementarity and polymerase selection, proofreading, error rate at order-of-magnitude level; mismatch repair and bacterial MutS/MutL/MutH as qualified examples, eukaryotic MSH/MLH principles; strand discrimination, consequences of unrepaired copying errors; distinguish replication fidelity from chemical damage repair.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET reasoning:** Compare mismatch-repair and proofreading mutants, distinguish exonuclease directions and evaluate elementary error-frequency problems.

**Other-unit and depth boundary:** Other DNA lesion-removal mechanisms belong to 3.A.09–10; detailed clinical syndromes excluded.

### 3.A.08 — DNA Damage: Sources, Lesions and Consequences

**Content and microtopic responsibility:** Hydrolysis, depurination, deamination, oxidative bases, alkylation, UV pyrimidine dimers, ionizing radiation, strand breaks and crosslinks; endogenous/exogenous causes, lesion versus mutation, consequences for replication and transcription.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET reasoning:** Recognize lesions from structural diagrams, map damage sources to likely effects and explain lesion versus mutation.

**Other-unit and depth boundary:** Detailed mutation/inheritance problems in Unit 8, toxicology and specialist chemical research excluded.

### 3.A.09 — DNA Repair I: Direct Reversal, Base Excision and Nucleotide Excision

**Content and microtopic responsibility:** Direct reversal with photolyase in organisms possessing it and methyltransferase-type repair; BER: DNA glycosylase, AP endonuclease, polymerase, ligase; NER: damage recognition, excision patch, DNA synthesis and ligation; UvrABC bacterial example and eukaryotic NER overview.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET reasoning:** Classify lesions by repair route, order steps, infer enzyme-specific defects in short exam cases.

**Other-unit and depth boundary:** Mismatch repair is in 3.A.07; specialist disease biology excluded.

### 3.A.10 — DNA Repair II: Strand Break Repair and DNA Damage Tolerance

**Content and microtopic responsibility:** Single-strand break joining at a conceptual level; double-strand breaks, non-homologous end joining (NHEJ), homologous template-guided repair in comparative outline and cell-cycle context; translesion DNA synthesis and bacterial SOS response as limited standard illustrations; bypass versus removal.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET reasoning:** Distinguish error-prone from template-guided break repair and damage tolerance from lesion elimination; interpret simple break-repair cases.

**Other-unit and depth boundary:** Homologous strand exchange mechanisms are assigned to 3.A.11; no research-level repair pathway networks.

### 3.A.11 — Homologous Recombination: Strand Exchange and Holliday Junctions

**Content and microtopic responsibility:** Requirements of homologous DNA, basic bacterial RecA/eukaryotic Rad51 functions, homologous pairing, strand invasion, Holliday junction model, branch migration and resolution, crossover and noncrossover outcomes, gene conversion overview and connection with DNA-break repair.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET reasoning:** Trace DNA strands in simple Holliday schemes, recognize crossovers and distinguish homologous exchange from end joining.

**Other-unit and depth boundary:** Meiosis stages in 2.D, linkage/gene mapping in Unit 8 and specialized advanced recombination chemistry excluded.

### 3.A.12 — Site-Specific Recombination: Integration, Excision and Inversion

**Content and microtopic responsibility:** Site-specific versus homologous recombination; target sites, recombinases; λ-phage integration/excision as a standard example; direct versus inverted site orientation and corresponding DNA segment outcomes where substrate diagram is explicit; Cre–loxP/FLP–FRT introductory examples; basic tyrosine/serine recombinase distinction.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET reasoning:** Predict excision and inversion products from site diagrams; contrast homologous and site-specific sequence recognition.

**Other-unit and depth boundary:** Transposon organization in 2.C, cloning methods in Unit 14, and phage expression regulation in 3.D.

## Progressive academic flow

1. **3.A.01:** Why and how DNA replication is semiconservative; what a single replicon means.
2. **3.A.02–03:** How copying starts and how enzymes coordinate continuous and discontinuous synthesis on antiparallel strands.
3. **3.A.04–05:** Implement the same general principles in bacterial chromosomes and in eukaryotic linear chromosomes with multiple origins.
4. **3.A.06:** Extend replicon reasoning to plasmids, organelles and selected viral DNA.
5. **3.A.07:** Explain why the copying system is accurate, including proofreading and postreplicative mismatch correction.
6. **3.A.08–10:** Explain lesions and damage, then repair by direct reversal, excision, end joining, template-directed restoration or damage bypass as appropriate.
7. **3.A.11–12:** Distinguish homologous DNA exchange from short-sequence-specific rearrangement.

This is a **logical teaching chronology**, not a claim that damage or recombination occurs only after DNA replication in a living cell.

## Mandatory scientific and scope checks

- DNA polymerases extend **5′→3′**, usually from a primer 3′-OH; distinguish polymerase proofreading 3′→5′ from Pol I primer removal 5′→3′.
- Clearly mark E. coli-based model components (Tus–Ter, MutH, oriC-dependent DnaA), which are not universal across all bacteria and archaea.
- Avoid treating every bacterial chromosome as circular, every eukaryotic replicon as single-origin or every viral genome as using theta/rolling-circle replication.
- Eukaryotic telomere completion applies to linear chromosome ends; fully worked replication timing, chromatin regulation and genome-wide mapping go outside this subunit.
- **Error ≠ lesion ≠ fixed mutation.** Damage bypass may permit replication without repairing the original lesion.
- Match repair type to the lesion: mismatch repair is owned by 3.A.07; direct/BER/NER by 3.A.09; break repair and bypass by 3.A.10; homologous strand-exchange chemistry by 3.A.11.
- Homologous and site-specific recombination must be treated distinctly; transposition is a different mechanism and its structure is already in 2.C.10.
- Examples, equations, images, historical evidence and questions must support a specific official-syllabus point. No specialist doctoral coverage or thin content disguised as tables.
- When writing the full chapters, preserve adjacent 3.B–D and Unit 2 menus, current GitHub main and scheduled Unit 1 progress; do not publish partial pages.
