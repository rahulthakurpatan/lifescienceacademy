## Full 13-node sequential curriculum

### 3.B.01 — Fundamentals of Transcription: From DNA Template to RNA

**Required microtopic scope:** DNA-directed RNA synthesis; difference between replication and transcription; RNA nucleotides, ribonucleoside triphosphates and phosphodiester formation; DNA template versus coding strand; 5′→3′ synthesis, template 3′→5′ reading; transcription unit, promoter, transcription start site (+1), terminator and RNA transcript; initiation, elongation and termination as a conceptual overview; absence of DNA primer requirement for RNA polymerase.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET evidence and reasoning:** Given a DNA template, write the correct RNA 5′→3′ sequence; identify strands and +1 location, infer transcript direction from a gene map; distinguish promoter, start codon, transcription start site and terminator.

**Overlap and depth boundary:** Gene/operon architecture is in 2.C.02–03; polymerase system components and concrete mechanisms are in 3.B.02–06; translation is 3.C.

### 3.B.02 — Prokaryotic RNA Polymerase, Promoters and Transcription Initiation

**Required microtopic scope:** Bacterial multisubunit RNA polymerase core and holoenzyme; sigma factors and promoter recognition; classical -10/-35 promoter elements as E. coli-derived example, with exceptions; closed/open initiation complexes, DNA melting, initial RNA synthesis, abortive initiation and promoter clearance at exam level; basic effects of promoter/sigma variation.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET evidence and reasoning:** Interpret promoter orientation and -10/-35 mutation diagrams; distinguish core enzyme from holoenzyme and predict initiation defects from sigma factor changes.

**Overlap and depth boundary:** RNA polymerase regulatory networks, detailed sigma-factor regulons and bacterial stress pathways belong to gene-expression control 3.D or microbial physiology 2.E; no specialized transcription structural chemistry.

### 3.B.03 — Prokaryotic Transcription Elongation, Termination and Inhibition

**Required microtopic scope:** Bacterial transcription elongation complex, template movement and RNA chain growth; transcription bubble and RNA–DNA hybrid as standard models; intrinsic (rho-independent) terminators with hairpin and U-rich region, rho-dependent termination and ATP-dependent rho action at a basic level; elementary pausing; rifampicin as an example of bacterial transcription inhibition, actinomycin D as a DNA-binding transcription inhibitor with correct target distinction.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET evidence and reasoning:** Distinguish terminators from sequence cartoons, predict effects of rho loss or hairpin disruption, identify inhibitor action versus translation inhibitor action.

**Overlap and depth boundary:** Phage antitermination circuits, the trp operon attenuation system and broad differential regulation belong to 3.D; detailed RNA stability and export are later.

### 3.B.04 — Eukaryotic RNA Polymerases and General Transcription Machinery

**Required microtopic scope:** Nuclear RNA polymerases I/II/III and their principal products, accurate distinction of 5S rRNA from most other rRNAs; essential basal factors for Pol II including TFIID/TBP, TFIIA, TFIIB, TFIIF, TFIIE and TFIIH at functional overview level; promoter/core elements such as TATA or initiator as examples not universal requirements; different Pol I/III factor organization in a non-catalog fashion; Pol II CTD as bridge to RNA processing.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET evidence and reasoning:** Classify gene type by RNA polymerase, relate basal factors to basic initiation functions and interpret simple Pol I/II/III inhibitor scenarios.

**Overlap and depth boundary:** Cell-specific activators/repressors and promoter recruitment dynamics are in 3.B.05; chromatin-based epigenetic regulation is in 3.D.

### 3.B.05 — Eukaryotic Transcription Initiation, Activators and Repressors

**Required microtopic scope:** Basic pre-initiation complex assembly, TATA-binding protein, general factors and Pol II promoter opening; activator and repressor proteins, regulatory DNA elements, coactivator/Mediator as conceptual bridging factors, how factors change promoter access or initiation efficiency; importance of transcription-factor binding specificity and context; compare bacterial promoter recognition with eukaryotic assembly without duplicating either.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET evidence and reasoning:** Reconstruct a simple Pol II initiation diagram, distinguish general factors from gene-specific activators and infer results from activator-binding-site or promoter mutations.

**Overlap and depth boundary:** Full regulatory logic of bacterial operons, phages, viruses, enhancer networks, chromatin remodeling, epigenetics and RNA-mediated silencing are reserved for 3.D; only direct transcription-machinery mechanisms here.

### 3.B.06 — Eukaryotic Transcription Elongation, Termination and Inhibitors

**Required microtopic scope:** Promoter escape and Pol II elongation, CTD phosphorylation and its coupling to co-transcriptional maturation at basic level; pausing and elongation support factors at exam level; cleavage/polyadenylation-coupled Pol II termination as a mechanistic outline, contrast with Pol I/III termination principles; alpha-amanitin sensitivity of polymerases (context and concentration differences), actinomycin D and rifampicin as representative inhibitors; major prokaryotic/eukaryotic contrasts.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET evidence and reasoning:** Distinguish initiation inhibitors from elongation inhibitors and transcript consequences, interpret Pol II CTD function schematics, compare bacterial versus eukaryotic termination.

**Overlap and depth boundary:** The full processing reactions (cap, poly(A), splicing) belong to 3.B.07–09; detailed termination models beyond NET/SET excluded.

### 3.B.07 — Eukaryotic mRNA End Processing: 5′ Capping and 3′ Polyadenylation

**Required microtopic scope:** Timing and chemistry of 5′ 7-methylguanosine cap via 5′–5′ linkage at standard level, enzymes as functional steps; roles in protection, nuclear export and translation; 3′ end processing signal such as AAUAAA in a typical example (not universally absolute), cleavage and poly(A) polymerase, poly(A)-binding proteins; biological roles in stability and maturation; distinguish poly(A) tail from DNA-encoded poly(A) tract and from bacterial RNA 3′ tail contexts.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET evidence and reasoning:** Interpret cap structures, cleavage signals and processing mutants, distinguish transcription termination from 3′ end cleavage and polyadenylation.

**Overlap and depth boundary:** mRNA degradation and translational control belong principally to 3.C/3.D, and promoter/termination machinery remain in 3.B.05–06.

### 3.B.08 — Pre-mRNA Splicing: Introns, Spliceosome and Alternative Splicing

**Required microtopic scope:** Pre-mRNA introns and exons as prerequisites, typical 5′ GU/3′ AG splice sites with variations, branch point and polypyrimidine tract; snRNPs U1/U2/U4/U5/U6 and spliceosome as exam-level components; two transesterification reactions and lariat intermediate; constitutive versus alternative splicing, exon skipping and basic transcript diversity; selected self-splicing introns introduced briefly as bridge to 3.B.13.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET evidence and reasoning:** Trace exon/intron and lariat diagrams, interpret splice-site mutations and count simple alternative-exon products under stated assumptions.

**Overlap and depth boundary:** Structural organization of interrupted genes is 2.C.03; full splicing-regulation networks, cancer splice-variants and specialist spliceosome research excluded; ribozyme catalysis in 3.B.13.

### 3.B.09 — RNA Editing and Maturation of rRNA and tRNA

**Required microtopic scope:** RNA editing as post-transcriptional sequence alteration, canonical examples A-to-I and C-to-U; note RNA editing systems differ across taxa and are not ubiquitous; rRNA precursor cleavage and nucleotide modification with snoRNA guidance as appropriate; tRNA precursor trimming, 3′ CCA addition where required, intron removal in selected tRNAs and base modification; organelle RNA editing as a representative context; clearly distinguish RNA editing from mutation and alternative splicing.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET evidence and reasoning:** Compare genomic DNA, pre-RNA and edited/mature RNA examples; classify cap/splice/edit versus tRNA/rRNA processing; interpret simple precursor-to-product diagrams.

**Overlap and depth boundary:** Exhaustive RNA-editing enzyme families, organelle RNA research, tRNA aminoacylation and codon identity belong to 3.C or outside exam scope.

### 3.B.10 — RNA Export, Intracellular Transport and Quality Control

**Required microtopic scope:** Nuclear RNA export via nuclear pore as an RNA-specific cargo process, mRNP assembly and maturation-dependent export, broad exportin-mediated tRNA transport as comparison; mRNA cytoplasmic localization/transport at overview level; nuclear retention of incompletely processed RNA; basic RNA quality control and degradation principles including nonsense-mediated decay as a concise familiar example, without rewriting full RNA turnover mechanisms.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET evidence and reasoning:** Interpret localization consequences of defective processing/export, distinguish transcript synthesis from transport defects, compare nuclear RNAs with cytoplasmic products.

**Overlap and depth boundary:** Detailed nuclear-pore anatomy in 2.B.01, membrane cargo sorting in 2.A.10, RNA-dependent gene silencing in 3.D and protein translation in 3.C.

### 3.B.11 — Major RNA Types: Structure, Function and Cellular Roles

**Required microtopic scope:** Structure/function of mRNA, rRNA and tRNA at the level relevant to transcription outputs, RNA secondary structures, ribonucleoprotein concepts; distinction between prokaryotic and eukaryotic mRNA processing/polycistronic organization; snRNA and snoRNA roles in splicing and modification; representative guide RNAs and other functional RNA species only if clearly relevant; summarize where major RNA classes are made and function.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET evidence and reasoning:** Identify RNA class by structural feature or functional description, distinguish snRNA from snoRNA and interpret general RNA types in cell diagrams.

**Overlap and depth boundary:** tRNA charging and ribosome translation mechanics belong to 3.C; dedicated regulatory non-coding RNAs in 3.B.12, and full RNA-mediated silencing pathways in 3.D.

### 3.B.12 — Non-coding RNAs: Classes and Biological Functions

**Required microtopic scope:** Meaning of coding versus non-coding RNA; overview of small regulatory RNAs including miRNA, siRNA, piRNA and lncRNA, representative biogenesis and main targets at structural/functional scope; basic guide-RNA/protein-complex idea where relevant; distinguish RNA types by origin, approximate size categories and biological role without pretending all species share identical machinery; connect sn/snoRNAs from 3.B.11 with wider ncRNA diversity.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET evidence and reasoning:** Classify RNA examples and compare miRNA/siRNA/piRNA conceptual functions; identify whether an ncRNA encodes protein or acts as a molecule itself.

**Overlap and depth boundary:** Molecular mechanisms of RNA interference, chromatin gene silencing and epigenetic regulation are owned by 3.D; no clinical RNA therapeutics or research-only ncRNA catalog.

### 3.B.13 — Catalytic and Regulatory RNAs: Ribozymes and Riboswitches

**Required microtopic scope:** Ribozymes as catalytic RNAs; examples RNase P, self-splicing introns and ribosomal peptidyl-transferase RNA, with correct activity assignments; sequence/structure relation to RNA catalysis and Mg2+ roles in simple terms; riboswitch location and ligand-binding aptamer/expression platform as a general model; transcription termination or translation initiation modulation in bacteria as representative functions; comparisons among ribozyme, riboswitch, ordinary mRNA, and protein enzyme.

**Standard CSIR-NET/SET evidence and reasoning:** Identify catalytic RNA from examples, predict basic ligand-dependent RNA conformational consequences, distinguish an RNA catalyst from a cis-regulatory RNA switch.

**Overlap and depth boundary:** Complete biochemical kinetics and RNA structure determination are beyond NET/SET; full gene expression regulation circuits and riboswitch-regulated gene programs are in 3.D.

## Causal learning journey and hand-offs

1. **3.B.01:** DNA information must first be represented as a correctly oriented RNA transcript; transcription units, template selection and direction are prerequisites to molecular mechanisms.
2. **3.B.02–03:** Start with the bacterial polymerase, sigma-directed initiation, then follow an elongating complex to intrinsic/rho-dependent termination and simple inhibitor effects.
3. **3.B.04–06:** Compare eukaryotic polymerases, basal transcription machinery, initiation-complex formation, activator/repressor effects, elongation, termination and inhibitors.
4. **3.B.07–09:** Shift from transcription to maturation: 5′ capping and 3′ end formation, splicing/alternative splicing, then RNA editing and rRNA/tRNA processing.
5. **3.B.10:** Explain export and quality control once a functional mature RNA product has been made.
6. **3.B.11–12:** Classify major coding, structural and regulatory RNA classes and the functions enabled by their structures; reserve full gene-silencing pathway mechanisms for 3.D.
7. **3.B.13:** Conclude by showing that RNA can catalyze reactions (ribozymes) or respond to metabolites to control expression (riboswitches), then transition into protein synthesis in 3.C.

The order is pedagogical and causal, **not** a literal claim that RNA classes or catalytic RNAs appear only after export in a real cell.

## Accuracy and examination-level QA requirements

- RNA polymerases synthesize **5′→3′** and read DNA template **3′→5′**; do not conflate transcription start (+1) with translation start codon.
- Treat E. coli sigma and -10/-35 sequences as bacterial models, not universal promoter sequences. Treat eukaryotic TATA boxes as context-dependent rather than mandatory on all promoters.
- Distinguish bacterial **intrinsic and rho-dependent termination** from eukaryotic Pol II termination; inhibitors differ in target and organism.
- Pol I generally produces the large pre-rRNA; Pol III produces 5S rRNA and tRNAs; Pol II produces pre-mRNA and several other RNAs. Note biologically important exceptions without expanding into specialist taxonomy.
- Capping, splicing, cleavage/polyadenylation and RNA editing are **different molecular processes**. Present accurate 5′ cap orientation and RNA lariat mechanism, with exceptions to canonical splice-site sequences acknowledged.
- RNA editing changes an RNA sequence after transcription and is not identical to DNA mutation. Not all organisms use the same RNA-editing mechanisms.
- RNA export is selective and linked to maturation; do not recast 2.B's nuclear-pore anatomy as a new organelle chapter.
- Distinguish **noncoding RNA classes** from the separate Unit 3.D question of how gene-silencing circuits regulate particular genes.
- Ribozymes catalyze reactions; riboswitches bind ligands to regulate expression, and the two terms are not synonyms.
- No microtopic, mathematical example, experiment or practice question may exceed CSIR-NET/SET or lack an assignable syllabus need. Coherent textbook narrative, standard evidence and explained original exam problems outrank any word-count or chapter-count target.
- Protect existing 3.A, 3.C, 3.D menus and all earlier units. When eventual reading chapters are built, perform scientific and technical reviews and update navigation/sitemap only for real published URLs.
