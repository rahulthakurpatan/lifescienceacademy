## Fourteen connected learning-node briefs

### 3.C.01 — Genetic Code and Principles of Translational Decoding

**Precisely assigned in-syllabus microtopics:** mRNA codons as nucleotide triplets; 64 possible codons, standard amino-acid/stop assignment and correct caveats about codon variants; reading frames, start/stop codons, degeneracy, unambiguity and near universality; wobble principle, codon–anticodon complementarity and why synonymous codons exist; differences between transcription start and translation start; straightforward translation of provided sequences.

**CSIR-NET and State SET reasoning:** Correctly translate a short mRNA in a specified reading frame and distinguish synonymous, missense, nonsense and frameshift effects only as decoding examples; reason about wobble and codon tables.

**Strict overlap/depth boundary:** Mutation inheritance belongs to Unit 8; RNA synthesis and editing to 3.B; avoid extended codon optimization and synthetic-genome research.

### 3.C.02 — tRNA Identity, Aminoacylation and Translational Proofreading

**Precisely assigned in-syllabus microtopics:** tRNA cloverleaf/three-dimensional L-shape as functional prerequisites; anticodon and CCA amino-acid attachment; identity elements and correct recognition by aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases; class I/II synthetases only at broad illustrative level; two-step aminoacylation using ATP, aminoacyl-AMP intermediate and charged tRNA; pre-transfer/post-transfer editing as standard proof-reading concepts; charging errors versus ribosomal codon-recognition errors.

**CSIR-NET and State SET reasoning:** Distinguish correct anticodon pairing from correct amino-acid attachment; use ATP and enzyme-step diagrams to spot charging/proofreading defects, and predict consequences of a mischarged tRNA.

**Strict overlap/depth boundary:** tRNA precursor processing in 3.B.09; full amino-acid metabolism in Unit 1; protein engineering/specialist kinetic mechanisms excluded.

### 3.C.03 — Ribosome Architecture and the Molecular Basis of Translation

**Precisely assigned in-syllabus microtopics:** Prokaryotic 70S (30S+50S) and cytosolic eukaryotic 80S (40S+60S); sedimentation values do not add arithmetically; rRNA and ribosomal proteins, A/P/E sites, small-subunit decoding and large-subunit peptide-bond formation, peptidyl-transferase activity of rRNA; mRNA/tRNA placement, polysomes, broad organelle ribosome caveats; basics of polysomal translation without advanced ribosome structure.

**CSIR-NET and State SET reasoning:** Identify sites/subunits, place initiator versus elongator tRNAs and interpret why the ribosome is a ribozyme; distinguish ribosome sizes and their inhibitor selectivity.

**Strict overlap/depth boundary:** Detailed RNA classes and RNA catalysis in 3.B; mitochondrial/chloroplast organelle structures in 2.B; no cryo-EM structural specialization.

### 3.C.04 — Prokaryotic Translation Initiation and Initiation Factors

**Precisely assigned in-syllabus microtopics:** Ribosome-binding site and Shine–Dalgarno interaction in standard bacterial examples, its exceptions; initiator tRNA charged with N-formylmethionine in typical bacteria; IF1, IF2-GTP and IF3 functional roles; start-codon recognition, 30S preinitiation steps, 50S joining and initiation-factor release; differences between initiator and elongator tRNAs; coupling of transcription and translation as basic context, without detailed regulatory circuitry.

**CSIR-NET and State SET reasoning:** Arrange initiation factors/events and distinguish ribosome-binding region from transcription promoter; predict the result of a defective start site, IF2 or initiator-tRNA recognition.

**Strict overlap/depth boundary:** Prokaryotic RNA transcription apparatus is 3.B; operon regulatory mechanisms in 3.D; bacterial exceptions acknowledged without becoming taxonomy.

### 3.C.05 — Eukaryotic Translation Initiation and Its Key Factors

**Precisely assigned in-syllabus microtopics:** Eukaryotic cytosolic initiator Met-tRNAi (usually unformylated); cap-associated eIF4F and scanning 43S/48S complexes at exam-level resolution, eIF2-GTP, eIF3, eIF4A/eIF4E/eIF4G roles; Kozak context and start-codon selection; 60S joining and formation of active 80S initiation complex; comparison with bacterial initiation and recognition that not all eukaryotic translation is strictly cap-dependent.

**CSIR-NET and State SET reasoning:** Recognize initiation-complex diagrams, compare scanning and Shine–Dalgarno mechanisms, and predict broad outcomes of altered eIF2 or cap recognition.

**Strict overlap/depth boundary:** Cellular regulation of initiation factors belongs to 3.C.08; detailed viral IRES strategies belong to 3.D only if required; specialist translation initiation complexes excluded.

### 3.C.06 — Translation Elongation, Translocation and Decoding Accuracy

**Precisely assigned in-syllabus microtopics:** A-site tRNA delivery by bacterial EF-Tu or eukaryotic eEF1A, correct codon–anticodon sampling and kinetic proofreading as ordinary NET/SET concepts; peptide bond formation, peptidyl transferase, EF-G/eEF2-driven translocation, A→P→E tRNA movements; direction of mRNA reading and N→C polypeptide extension, basic ATP/GTP energy bookkeeping and error avoidance; compare bacterial and eukaryotic factors.

**CSIR-NET and State SET reasoning:** Follow a ribosome cartoon after each step; identify effects of blocking tRNA entry or translocation; solve standard peptide length/energy-use questions with clearly declared initiation/termination assumptions.

**Strict overlap/depth boundary:** tRNA synthetase editing is 3.C.02 and not identical to ribosomal decoding fidelity; inhibitor examples concentrated in 3.C.09.

### 3.C.07 — Translation Termination, Ribosome Recycling and Polysomes

**Precisely assigned in-syllabus microtopics:** Stop codons recognized by proteins rather than normal tRNAs; bacterial RF1/RF2 and RF3 with factor-specific recognition as standard examples, eukaryotic eRF1/eRF3; hydrolysis of peptidyl-tRNA linkage, factor release and subunit recycling; polysomes as an efficiency illustration, differences between completion and premature termination; clear comparison between normal termination and stall-related quality concerns.

**CSIR-NET and State SET reasoning:** Distinguish start versus stop site, predict outcomes of abnormal release factors, interpret polyribosome distributions and count ribosomes/translation products in simple diagrams.

**Strict overlap/depth boundary:** Advanced ribosome rescue and surveillance research excluded; transcript-level quality control was introduced in 3.B.10.

### 3.C.08 — Regulation of Translation in Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells

**Precisely assigned in-syllabus microtopics:** Relative contributions of initiation, mRNA availability and stability to protein output; bacterial translation control through ribosome-binding-site accessibility and basic regulatory RNA examples; eukaryotic eIF2 phosphorylation and nutrient-sensitive mTOR–eIF4E control at mechanistic overview level, upstream open reading frames and mRNA accessibility as basic examples; stress-responsive changes and regulation of ribosomal activity, without duplicating global gene-expression networks.

**CSIR-NET and State SET reasoning:** Interpret broad protein-output changes with constant mRNA level, infer effects of altered eIF2 activity or ribosome-binding-site availability and distinguish translational from transcriptional control.

**Strict overlap/depth boundary:** Phage/viral networks, miRNA silencing mechanisms, epigenetics and integrated gene-expression regulatory programs are reserved for 3.D; full signal transduction cascades are in Unit 4.

### 3.C.09 — Translational Inhibitors and Their Sites of Action

**Precisely assigned in-syllabus microtopics:** Exam-relevant bacterial-target examples: tetracyclines (aa-tRNA entry), chloramphenicol (peptidyl transferase), macrolides such as erythromycin (exit-tunnel/elongation), streptomycin (decoding accuracy); eukaryotic-target cycloheximide as a standard inhibitor, puromycin as premature chain release in both systems; diphtheria toxin's eEF2 inactivation as a clear non-antibiotic mechanism example; organism/compartment distinctions, limitations and common mechanism-to-effect logic.

**CSIR-NET and State SET reasoning:** Match drug/toxin mechanism with ribosome stage and expected peptide output; reason about selective inhibition without oversimplifying antibiotic specificity.

**Strict overlap/depth boundary:** Do not teach clinical prescribing, antimicrobial treatment regimens or AMR epidemiology (2.E.09–10). Detailed drug chemistry and clinical toxicology excluded.

### 3.C.10 — Nascent Protein Folding, Chaperones and Maturation

**Precisely assigned in-syllabus microtopics:** Nascent-chain emergence and cotranslational/posttranslational folding; primary sequence and folding, chaperones including Hsp70 and chaperonins as standard examples, avoidance of aggregation, disulfide-bond formation in appropriate compartments, proteolytic cleavage of precursor proteins as maturation examples, assembly of protein complexes; distinguish folding from covalent post-translational modification and compartment targeting; misfolding as an entry point to degradation.

**CSIR-NET and State SET reasoning:** Interpret effects of altered chaperones or disrupted disulfide formation, classify folding/processing/targeting and predict whether a polypeptide is functional solely after synthesis.

**Strict overlap/depth boundary:** Protein thermodynamic folding and structural classifications belong to Unit 1; clinical protein-folding disorders and PhD chaperone biochemistry excluded.

### 3.C.11 — Post-translational Modifications: Types and Biological Functions

**Precisely assigned in-syllabus microtopics:** Definitions and purposes of protein post-translational modification; representative phosphorylation/dephosphorylation, N-linked/O-linked glycosylation in appropriate compartments, acetylation, methylation, limited lipidation and proteolytic maturation; reversibility versus irreversible processing; effects on stability, localization, activity and interactions; introduction to ubiquitination as a tag with E1/E2/E3 mechanism reserved to 3.C.14, diagrams connecting location with modification type.

**CSIR-NET and State SET reasoning:** Match modifications to residue/compartment/function in basic scenarios, classify maturation versus modification and reason about changing protein fate after loss of a modifying enzyme.

**Strict overlap/depth boundary:** Full kinase signaling networks belong to Unit 4, specialized glycan biosynthetic chemistry to other biochemistry chapters, histone epigenetic modification mechanisms to 3.D; avoid cataloging rare PTMs.

### 3.C.12 — Protein Targeting to the ER and Secretory Pathway

**Precisely assigned in-syllabus microtopics:** Free versus ER-bound ribosomes are the same ribosome population operating at different locations; signal peptide recognition, signal recognition particle (SRP) and SRP receptor, ribosome docking and Sec61 translocon at exam-appropriate scope; cotranslational translocation, luminal versus membrane insertion, simple transmembrane orientation and stop-transfer concepts; protein folding/processing in ER and progression through Golgi for secretion; signal sequence cleavage and recognition of retained versus exported products.

**CSIR-NET and State SET reasoning:** Predict localization from signal sequence, interpret SRP/translocon blocking experiments and distinguish membrane insertion from cytosolic translation.

**Strict overlap/depth boundary:** ER/Golgi organelle architecture is 2.B.02–03, and COP/SNARE-mediated vesicle sorting is 2.A.10; full secretion biology/receptor signaling research excluded.

### 3.C.13 — Protein Import into Nucleus, Mitochondria, Plastids and Peroxisomes

**Precisely assigned in-syllabus microtopics:** Nuclear localization signal and importin-mediated import through pores as broad principles, nuclear export motif as context; mitochondrial presequence and TOM/TIM import, typical energy/unfolding requirement and cleavage; chloroplast transit peptide and TOC/TIC overview; peroxisome targeting signals (PTS1, e.g., terminal SKL, and PTS2) and Pex receptors as introductory examples; contrast import from cytosolic ribosomes with ER-mediated secretory targeting; proteins can vary in route and organization.

**CSIR-NET and State SET reasoning:** Match targeting peptide to destination, identify likely location after import-signal deletion, distinguish translocation into ER versus mitochondria versus nucleus.

**Strict overlap/depth boundary:** Organelle structural anatomy belongs to 2.B and universal intracellular membrane sorting to 2.A.10; full mitochondrial respiratory physiology and specialized import motor chemistry excluded.

### 3.C.14 — Protein Degradation: Ubiquitin–Proteasome and Lysosomal Pathways

**Precisely assigned in-syllabus microtopics:** Biological reasons for selective degradation and protein half-life; ubiquitin activation/conjugation/ligation by E1/E2/E3, polyubiquitin chains and standard degradation outcomes; ATP-dependent 26S proteasome composition and unfolding at concept level; deubiquitination and recycling; lysosomal degradation of extracellular/endocytosed or selected intracellular proteins, autophagic delivery as a relevant contrast; distinguish proteasome versus lysosome routes and protein quality-control selection, avoid categorical claims that all ubiquitin marks cause proteasomal degradation.

**CSIR-NET and State SET reasoning:** Interpret ubiquitination-deficient mutant cases and predict accumulation of proteins from proteasome/lysosome blockade; classify turnover routes and distinguish synthesis defects from excessive breakdown.

**Strict overlap/depth boundary:** Lysosome structure is in 2.B.04, autophagy sequence in 2.D.10, detailed cargo vesicle sorting in 2.A.10; clinical proteasome inhibitors and research-only degradation pathways excluded.

## Progressive flow from RNA to active protein and controlled turnover

1. **3.C.01–02:** First decode the messenger's triplet vocabulary, then explain how tRNA delivers the *correct amino acid* through charging and editing.
2. **3.C.03:** Introduce the ribosome as the molecular platform on which loaded tRNAs interpret mRNA.
3. **3.C.04–05:** Compare separately the bacterial and eukaryotic initiation complexes so organism-specific factors are not indiscriminately mixed.
4. **3.C.06–07:** Follow an initiated ribosome through aminoacyl-tRNA entry, peptide bond formation, movement, termination and reuse.
5. **3.C.08–09:** Once normal translation is understood, show how translation is regulated and how standard inhibitors interrupt specific steps.
6. **3.C.10–11:** A completed or growing polypeptide must fold, mature and often undergo covalent post-translational modification.
7. **3.C.12–13:** Explain how newly made proteins are sent through the ER/secretory route or imported into other compartments according to their targeting signals.
8. **3.C.14:** Complete the cellular protein life cycle with proteasomal and lysosomal breakdown, naturally leading to integrated expression controls in 3.D.

**Teaching chronology, not strict cellular timing:** Folding, modification and targeting can happen co-translationally as well as after release. Protein degradation, RNA production and regulation happen throughout a cell's life; the ordered chapters simplify understanding without asserting an erroneous biological chronology.

## Mandatory technical, exam and scientific QA

- Code recognition, tRNA aminoacylation and ribosomal decoding are related **but separate accuracy steps**. A correctly paired anticodon does not guarantee the right amino acid was charged.
- Normal cytosolic bacterial translation commonly begins with fMet; eukaryotic cytosolic translation with Met. Distinguish both from organelle-specific cases.
- A bacterial 70S ribosome is 30S + 50S, and a eukaryotic 80S ribosome is 40S + 60S; Svedberg coefficients are not simple arithmetic sums.
- Translation reads mRNA 5′→3′ and generally builds polypeptide N→C. Distinguish an mRNA codon from its antiparallel tRNA anticodon.
- Include standard IF/eIF, EF/eEF and RF/eRF factors with correct functions. Classic GTP/ATP accounting must make its assumptions explicit.
- Distinguish a **translational** effect from an RNA production/transcription effect. Local mechanisms reside here; integrated phage/viral, epigenetic, RNA-silencing control resides in 3.D.
- Inhibitor comparisons are mechanistic, not medical recommendations. Antibiotics and toxins differ in target and taxonomic specificity.
- Signal recognition particle, translocons and targeting peptides explain **protein targeting**, while the complete COP/SNARE vesicle machinery has already been assigned to 2.A.10.
- Explain post-translational modification as a category distinct from folding, trafficking and degradation, without artificially insisting it occurs after translation has completely ended.
- Ubiquitination does not uniformly trigger degradation; compare proteasomal and lysosomal fates without collapsing mechanisms into one.
- Every chapter must contain naturally connected explanations, relevant experiments or worked diagrams, clear exam reasoning and appropriately justified depth, not thin bullets or research-only digressions.
- Prior subunits (2.A–2.E, 3.A–3.B) and 3.D remain untouched during initial menu publication. Notes and sitemap are updated only when real full-depth teaching chapters are published.
