## Complete progressive node scopes

### 4.A.01 — Principles of Cell Signaling: Signals, Receptors and Responses

**Student-facing menu description:** Understand signal molecules, receptor specificity, signal reception, transduction, amplification and the cellular response.

**Complete NET/SET teaching scope:** Why signaling is needed; chemical/physical extracellular cues; specificity, affinity and saturability of ligand–receptor binding; occupancy and qualitative dose–response; signal reception → transduction → effector → response; amplification and signaling duration as concepts; autocrine, paracrine and endocrine signaling only as short context. Distinguish signals from second messengers and receptors from downstream effectors.

**Explained questions and diagram-based reasoning:** Interpret elementary dose–response/affinity diagrams, order signal-transduction steps and explain why different cells respond differently to the same ligand.

**Scope boundary — prevent duplication and research-level tangents:** Detailed cell–cell communication modes and adhesion belong primarily to 4.B; hormone receptor classification begins in 4.A.02; do not teach specialist pharmacology or quantitative ligand-binding research.


### 4.A.02 — Hormones and Receptor Classes: Cell-Surface and Intracellular Signaling

**Student-facing menu description:** Compare peptide, amine and steroid or thyroid hormone signaling through GPCRs, enzyme-linked and ion-channel receptors or intracellular receptors.

**Complete NET/SET teaching scope:** Representative chemical classes of hormones and location of their receptors; membrane-impermeant peptide/catecholamine examples; membrane-permeant steroid/thyroid hormone receptors and ligand-dependent transcription at conceptual level; GPCR, receptor tyrosine kinase, kinase-associated receptors, receptor serine/threonine kinase, receptor guanylyl cyclase and ligand-gated ion-channel receptor classes as a map, not a separate advanced catalogue; receptor distribution and specificity; different response timings.

**Explained questions and diagram-based reasoning:** Predict receptor location from ligand chemistry with exceptions, classify receptors from simple structural drawings and contrast immediate second-messenger responses with slower transcription-dependent outcomes.

**Scope boundary — prevent duplication and research-level tangents:** Full endocrine regulation belongs to Unit 7 and plant hormone physiology to Unit 6; detailed individual membrane signaling mechanisms are in nodes 03–07; ion-channel biophysics belongs to 2.A.


### 4.A.03 — G-Protein-Coupled Receptors and Heterotrimeric G-Protein Activation

**Student-facing menu description:** Follow GPCR ligand binding, seven-transmembrane receptor activation, GDP–GTP exchange, Gαβγ effectors and the G-protein cycle.

**Complete NET/SET teaching scope:** Typical seven-transmembrane GPCR structure; receptor as a guanine-nucleotide exchange factor on Gα; Gαβγ heterotrimers, GDP release/GTP binding, Gα–GTP and Gβγ effectors, signaling shutoff by GTP hydrolysis; common Gs, Gi/o and Gq/11 coupling examples with context; basic receptor desensitization concept only, developed later; distinguish heterotrimeric G proteins from small GTPase Ras.

**Explained questions and diagram-based reasoning:** Arrange GPCR cycle stages, predict effects of inactive GTPase or GDP-locked Gα, distinguish Gs/Gi/Gq outputs from pathway diagrams.

**Scope boundary — prevent duplication and research-level tangents:** Cyclic nucleotide pathway details are in 4.A.04; PLC/IP3/DAG details in 4.A.05; full receptor desensitization integration in 4.A.08; no specialist GPCR structures or biased agonism research.


### 4.A.04 — Cyclic Nucleotide Second Messengers: cAMP, cGMP and Their Effectors

**Student-facing menu description:** Trace cAMP and cGMP formation, protein kinase activation and messenger removal in representative signaling pathways.

**Complete NET/SET teaching scope:** Adenylyl cyclase conversion of ATP to cAMP; Gs/Gi effects as prerequisites; cAMP activation of PKA and a standard CREB-linked response; cAMP-dependent regulatory protein as simple example; phosphodiesterases and signal termination; guanylyl cyclases, cGMP, PKG and representative nitric-oxide-mediated cGMP signaling, clearly distinguishing intracellular NO sensing from membrane guanylyl cyclase receptors; cyclic-nucleotide-gated channels only as relevant principle.

**Explained questions and diagram-based reasoning:** Predict effect of activated adenylyl cyclase versus phosphodiesterase inhibition, order cAMP/PKA outputs and distinguish cAMP from cGMP cascades.

**Scope boundary — prevent duplication and research-level tangents:** GPCR GTP exchange is taught in 4.A.03; NO production details and vascular/endocrine physiology belong to Unit 7; no full clinical drug mechanisms.


### 4.A.05 — Phosphoinositide, IP₃, DAG and Calcium Signaling

**Student-facing menu description:** Explain phospholipase C activation, PIP₂ cleavage, IP₃-triggered calcium release, DAG–PKC signaling and calcium-dependent proteins.

**Complete NET/SET teaching scope:** Gq or other appropriate receptor activation of phospholipase C; PIP₂ → IP₃ and DAG, different localizations and roles of these messengers; IP₃ receptor-mediated Ca²⁺ release from ER; Ca²⁺-binding proteins, calmodulin and CaM-kinases as standard downstream effectors; DAG/Ca²⁺-dependent PKC, calcium pumps and buffering as restoration concepts; amplitude and temporal calcium signals at simple exam level.

**Explained questions and diagram-based reasoning:** Identify which messenger reaches ER, predict roles of PLC/PKC or calcium chelation, compare membrane-localized DAG with soluble IP₃ and Ca²⁺ signaling.

**Scope boundary — prevent duplication and research-level tangents:** Membrane transport and channel biophysics from 2.A should not be retaught; GPCR Gq activation from 4.A.03 is prerequisite, not repeated as a full chapter; no specialist calcium imaging or oscillation mathematical modeling.


### 4.A.06 — Receptor Tyrosine Kinases and the Ras–MAPK Signaling Cascade

**Student-facing menu description:** Follow growth-factor receptor activation, tyrosine phosphorylation, adaptor recruitment, Ras and MAPK cascades, and a basic PI3K–Akt branch.

**Complete NET/SET teaching scope:** Ligand-induced RTK activation/dimerization in standard examples; trans-autophosphorylation, phosphotyrosine docking and adaptor proteins; Grb2–SOS–Ras, Ras GDP/GTP switch, Raf → MEK → ERK and changes in transcription or proliferation; PI3K–PIP₃–Akt as a concise additional RTK-related signaling branch and role in survival/growth; avoid implying every RTK shares identical activation sequence.

**Explained questions and diagram-based reasoning:** Order kinase steps, predict dominant-active Ras or inactive Raf effects, identify signaling sequence differences between RTK pathways and GPCRs.

**Scope boundary — prevent duplication and research-level tangents:** Cell-cycle regulation in 2.D, cancer molecular oncogenes in 4.E, phosphoinositide as second messenger context in 4.A.05; no clinical targeted-therapy or advanced kinase domain research.


### 4.A.07 — Cytokine and Serine–Threonine Kinase Receptor Pathways

**Student-facing menu description:** Compare JAK–STAT signaling with TGF-β-family receptor–SMAD signaling as additional enzyme-associated transduction mechanisms.

**Complete NET/SET teaching scope:** Cytokine receptors lacking intrinsic receptor tyrosine kinase activity in standard JAK–STAT systems; associated JAK activation, receptor and STAT phosphorylation, STAT dimerization and nuclear signaling; receptor serine/threonine kinases in the TGF-β family and receptor-regulated SMAD/SMAD4 signal transfer; compare receptor-associated kinases with intrinsically active RTKs; note representative inhibitory feedback at conceptual level.

**Explained questions and diagram-based reasoning:** Distinguish RTK, JAK-associated and TGF-β receptor types; reconstruct simplified STAT/SMAD sequences and predict effect of pathway blockage.

**Scope boundary — prevent duplication and research-level tangents:** Detailed immune-cell activation is 4.C, full gene regulation is 3.D, and cell fate pathways in Developmental Biology Unit 5. No exhaustive cytokine families or advanced structural signaling.


### 4.A.08 — Regulation and Integration of Cell-Signaling Pathways

**Student-facing menu description:** Understand signal amplification, feedback, receptor desensitization, phosphatases, messenger removal and pathway cross-talk.

**Complete NET/SET teaching scope:** Pathway amplification and signaling specificity; positive/negative feedback; G-protein GTP hydrolysis, RGS-type control, GPCR phosphorylation and arrestin-associated desensitization, receptor internalization/downregulation as suitable examples; phosphatases, dephosphorylation and second-messenger phosphodiesterases, calcium clearance; scaffold proteins and pathway cross-talk; transient versus sustained signaling and adaptation from elementary time-response plots.

**Explained questions and diagram-based reasoning:** Predict loss of phosphatase or degradation effects, distinguish signal strength versus duration and reason from time-course/feedback diagrams.

**Scope boundary — prevent duplication and research-level tangents:** Do not duplicate full individual GPCR, MAPK or PLC cascades, and do not expand into clinical resistance networks, advanced control theory or signaling network omics.


### 4.A.09 — Bacterial Two-Component Systems: Histidine Kinases and Response Regulators

**Student-facing menu description:** Explore stimulus detection, histidine-to-aspartate phosphotransfer and bacterial transcriptional or behavioral responses.

**Complete NET/SET teaching scope:** Sensor histidine kinases and response regulators, autophosphorylation at His followed by phosphate transfer to Asp, phosphatase-mediated reset; typical membrane or cytoplasmic sensing and response-output domain; EnvZ–OmpR or other textbook example for osmotic response, compared with a classic nutrient response at broad level; distinction from eukaryotic Ser/Thr/Tyr signaling; simple phosphorelay architecture and environmental adaptation.

**Explained questions and diagram-based reasoning:** Draw His→Asp phosphotransfer, interpret inactive sensor or constitutive response regulator and identify sensor/input/output layers.

**Scope boundary — prevent duplication and research-level tangents:** Prokaryotic transcription mechanism belongs to 3.B/3.D and microbial stress physiology to 2.E; chemotaxis-specific Che systems have detailed ownership in 4.A.12.


### 4.A.10 — Plant Two-Component Signaling and Cytokinin Phosphorelays

**Student-facing menu description:** Follow cytokinin perception through plant histidine-kinase receptors, histidine phosphotransfer proteins and response regulators.

**Complete NET/SET teaching scope:** Plant two-component-like multistep signaling versus classical bacterial two-protein systems; cytokinin receptor AHK as a clear Arabidopsis model; His→Asp→His→Asp phosphorelay via AHP to type-B ARR; type-A ARR feedback in common systems, resulting cytokinin-responsive transcription; other plant two-component-like elements only as brief comparison, no implication that all plant photoreceptors or ethylene receptors use identical active phosphorelays.

**Explained questions and diagram-based reasoning:** Trace the order of phosphotransfers and compare response-regulator mutant phenotypes; contrast bacterial direct His→Asp with plant multistep example.

**Scope boundary — prevent duplication and research-level tangents:** Plant hormone synthesis, physiological effects and tissue-specific regulation belong to Unit 6; light sensing is 4.A.11. Do not build a whole botanical growth-pathway unit.


### 4.A.11 — Light Signaling in Plants: Phytochromes and Blue-Light Receptors

**Student-facing menu description:** Explain light perception by phytochromes, cryptochromes and phototropins and their control of basic light-responsive signaling.

**Complete NET/SET teaching scope:** Red and far-red light detection by phytochromes, interconversion of Pr and Pfr as classic exam concept, phytochrome signal to PIF and COP1/HY5-related regulation at explanatory level; blue-light cryptochromes and phototropins as distinct receptor classes and their principal signaling roles; photomorphogenesis versus darkness responses as brief consequences; receptor-to-transcription response, wavelength-specific and photoreversibility reasoning.

**Explained questions and diagram-based reasoning:** Predict red/far-red reversal experiments, recognize photoreceptor classes by wavelength, distinguish light-induced signaling from photosynthetic light reactions.

**Scope boundary — prevent duplication and research-level tangents:** Photosynthesis and full plant developmental physiology belong to Units 1/5/6; no exhaustive photoperiodism, circadian regulatory networks or research-level photoreceptor complexes.


### 4.A.12 — Bacterial Chemotaxis: Sensory Receptors, Che Proteins and Adaptation

**Student-facing menu description:** Trace detection of attractants or repellents to CheA–CheY signaling, flagellar behavior and sensory adaptation.

**Complete NET/SET teaching scope:** Bacterial chemical gradient sensing and run/tumble concept in explicitly named E. coli model; methyl-accepting chemotaxis proteins (MCPs), CheW/CheA activation, CheY phosphorylation and flagellar-switch responses; CheZ reset and CheR/CheB receptor methylation-based adaptation; comparing attractant binding with clockwise/counterclockwise behavioral changes in E. coli and caveat that species differ; contrast chemotaxis and growth/chemokinesis.

**Explained questions and diagram-based reasoning:** Trace attractant vs repellent signals through CheA/CheY and flagellar rotation and predict receptor-adaptation mutant behavior.

**Scope boundary — prevent duplication and research-level tangents:** Bacterial motility ultrastructure and general microbiology in Unit 2/9; general two-component pathway learned in 4.A.09, avoid duplicating it at length or universalizing E. coli details.


### 4.A.13 — Quorum Sensing: Autoinducers and Population-Dependent Responses

**Student-facing menu description:** Explain bacterial autoinducer production, sensing and feedback through classic LuxI–LuxR and peptide-sensing examples.

**Complete NET/SET teaching scope:** Quorum sensing as cell-density-related chemical signaling; autoinducer synthesis and accumulation, thresholds and response; Gram-negative acyl-homoserine-lactone LuxI–LuxR in Vibrio fischeri as a named model, positive feedback and bioluminescence as illustrative output; Gram-positive peptide autoinducers and histidine-kinase response regulator detection at principle level; AI-2 as a contrasting representative system; simplified coordination of biofilm formation or virulence with clear organism specificity.

**Explained questions and diagram-based reasoning:** Interpret cell density versus reporter expression, predict effects of signal synthase/receptor defects, distinguish quorum sensing from chemotaxis and direct two-component sensor signaling.

**Scope boundary — prevent duplication and research-level tangents:** Microbial growth curves in 2.E, pathogenesis and host effects in 4.D, population ecology in Unit 10. Do not add specialist virulence circuits, antibiotic therapy or synthetic biology protocols.

## Academic progression and downstream connections

1. **4.A.01–02:** Start with signals, receptors and hormone-receptor chemistry; establish how a signal becomes a cell-specific response.
2. **4.A.03–05:** Explain a GPCR's G-protein switch, then decode the two main textbook groups of downstream second-messenger pathways: cyclic nucleotides and PLC/Ca²+.
3. **4.A.06–07:** Compare enzyme-linked and enzyme-associated receptor families using the exam-relevant RTK/Ras–MAPK, JAK–STAT and TGF-β/SMAD examples.
4. **4.A.08:** Consolidate why pathways have temporary outputs, feedback, cross-talk and receptor desensitization before moving to non-animal examples.
5. **4.A.09–10:** Introduce the bacterial His→Asp two-component principle and then the longer plant cytokinin phosphorelay.
6. **4.A.11:** Study photoreception and signaling from wavelength to gene-expression responses as a separate plant light-sensing topic.
7. **4.A.12–13:** Finish with bacterial chemotaxis as behavior directed by a chemical gradient, then quorum sensing as density-dependent chemical communication.

This is a **pedagogical causal chronology**, not a historical claim that one biochemical system evolved after another or that every cell expresses every pathway.

## Scientific precision and hard editorial limits

- Receptors, ligands and second messengers are different functional entities. GPCRs typically have seven transmembrane helices; heterotrimeric G proteins (αβγ) are distinct from monomeric Ras.
- cAMP/PKA, cGMP/PKG, IP₃/DAG, Ca²+ and PKC are defined by their actual biochemical roles. Distinguish the membrane-localized DAG from soluble IP₃.
- RTKs have intrinsic Tyr-kinase activity in standard examples; JAK-associated cytokine receptors recruit non-receptor kinases; TGF-β receptors are Ser/Thr kinases. These are different receptor classes.
- Classic pathways are representative examples under the official generic phrase *signal transduction pathways*, not claims that CSIR names every kinase or factor as a separately required syllabus item.
- Amplification, desensitization, phosphatases and phosphodiesterases should be taught as functional regulation rather than as a specialist pharmaceutical catalogue.
- Histidine-kinase/response-regulator systems transfer phosphates via His→Asp. In a representative plant cytokinin pathway, the relay can be His→Asp→His→Asp; do not mistake all ethylene/phytochrome-related proteins for equivalent active bacterial-like relays.
- Plant light signaling includes red/far-red phytochrome and blue-light cryptochrome/phototropin signaling, but **plant physiology** (Unit 6), full photoperiodic development and photosynthesis should not be retaught.
- E. coli CheA–CheY chemotaxis is a species-labeled model, not the complete mechanistic diversity of all bacteria. Preserve accurate attractant/motor response signs.
- Quorum sensing is chemical population-linked coordination (e.g., LuxI–LuxR), not simply chemotaxis or a synonym for biofilm growth. Distinguish Gram-negative AHL examples and Gram-positive peptide examples while acknowledging exceptions.
- Cell adhesion/ECM, neurotransmission and blood-cell production belong to **4.B**; adaptive immunity to **4.C**, pathogen entry to **4.D**, cancer progression to **4.E**, detailed plant hormone physiology to **Unit 6**, systemic endocrine physiology to **Unit 7**.
- Do not pad with optional research-only pathways (specialized Hedgehog/Wnt/Notch networks, modern clinical kinase-inhibitor regimens, high-throughput phosphoproteomics) unless an official unit clearly requires them elsewhere.
- Each actual future reading page must independently pass scientific, learning-prose, original-example, explained MCQ, navigation, SEO and deployment quality audits; a menu node is not a published chapter.
