# Life Science Academy — Unit 04, Subunit 4.C: Innate and Adaptive Immune System

## Approved 13-chapter academic blueprint (consolidation of all original scope)

- **Final reading-node count:** Exactly **13**, codes `4.C.01` through `4.C.13`. There is no hidden or separate fifteenth/twenty-fifth chapter. Earlier 25 planned topic clusters are preserved as **in-page microtopic responsibilities** inside the thirteen chapters, not as extra pages.
- **Syllabus authority:** Revised CSIR–UGC–DBT LS&BT, Unit **4.C** (2026), printed p. 5: https://www.csirhrdg.res.in/SiteContent/ManagedContent/ContentFiles/20260121115025355Merged_Syllabus_CSIR-DBT_21Jan2026.pdf

Revised syllabus uses 4.C lettering, which is authoritative for the Academy menu.
- **Complete coverage promise:** None of the official statements were omitted during consolidation; include innate/adaptive cells and molecules; antigen/epitope properties; antibodies, diversity, monoclonals and basic engineering; antigen–antibody interactions; MHC and presentation; B/T receptors, activation, differentiation, humoral and cellular responses; primary/secondary immune modulation; complement, TLRs, cellular effectors and inflammation; hypersensitivity, autoimmunity; bacterial/parasitic/viral response models, primary/acquired immunodeficiency and vaccines.
- **Named legacy/State SET contextual examples:** tuberculosis, malaria and HIV are all retained at immunity-focused level. Do not turn these into separate infectious-disease/pathogen-life-cycle syllabi.
- **Equal rigor, not equal length:** Broad integrated chapters may be longer than narrower mechanism chapters. Each chapter is a **complete readable, navigable textbook page**, with sections, original explanatory diagrams, worked examples and explained exam-level questions. A max-13 menu is **not** a word-count cap; substantial complete content is required for each page.
- **Strict boundaries:** No PhD-only frontier immunology, clinical treatment protocols, detailed antibody-production techniques, broad immune pharmacology, deep pathogen taxonomy or advanced diagnostic methods. 4.A owns generic kinases/signaling, 4.B the hematopoietic cell niche, 4.D host–pathogen entry/pathogenesis and 4.E cancer. Full disease therapies are not in scope.
- **Deployment meaning:** This curriculum file and menu contain blueprints, not published reading pages. No fake lesson links, sitemap changes, or placeholder articles until full chapters are researched and QA-approved.

## Final 13 learning nodes with every original microtopic explicitly assigned

### 4.C.01 — Immune System Organization, Barriers and Innate Recognition

**Student-facing menu description:** Map immune organs and cells, innate versus adaptive defense, physical barriers, microbial patterns and Toll-like receptor recognition.

**Chronological teaching journey:** First establish the major immune tissues, cells and protection layers; then learn how innate receptors detect threats.

**Required internal microtopic sections and inherited science contracts:**

#### Organization of the Immune System: Cells, Tissues and Defense Layers

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Physical and chemical barriers as initial protection; innate versus adaptive response timing, specificity and memory; leukocytes—neutrophils, monocytes/macrophages, dendritic cells, NK cells, mast cells, eosinophils, basophils and B/T lymphocytes; bone marrow, thymus, lymph nodes, spleen and mucosal lymphoid tissues in simple functional terms; antigen transport and cooperation among cells.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Classify leukocytes and organs, identify which cells initiate/present/execute immune responses, compare innate and adaptive response timelines.
- **Scope boundary:** Detailed hematopoietic stem-cell hierarchy and cytokine regulation are 4.B.10–11; organ anatomy and broad animal physiology are not repeated.

#### Innate Recognition: Barriers, Pattern-Recognition Receptors and Toll-Like Receptors

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Epithelial, mucus, antimicrobial peptide and soluble barrier functions; pathogen-associated and damage-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs/DAMPs); PRRs and key membrane/endosomal TLR examples; receptor localization and broad MyD88/TRIF-to-inflammatory-response logic at exam level; distinguish innate germline-encoded receptors from clonally rearranged antigen receptors; avoid presenting a fixed TLR ligand list as universal across species.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Interpret TLR localization and basic bacterial/viral ligand sensing cases, predict weak innate activation upon loss of recognition, contrast PRR versus BCR/TCR.
- **Scope boundary:** No comprehensive intracellular NF-κB biochemistry or general signaling cascades already covered in 4.A; downstream effector and inflammation in nodes 03/05.



### 4.C.02 — Innate Effector Cells, Inflammation and the Innate–Adaptive Bridge

**Student-facing menu description:** Follow phagocytes, natural killer cells, microbial killing, inflammatory mediators, leukocyte recruitment and activation of adaptive immunity.

**Chronological teaching journey:** After pathogen recognition, understand how phagocytes and NK cells act, how inflammation recruits defenders and how dendritic cells prime adaptive responses.

**Required internal microtopic sections and inherited science contracts:**

#### Innate Cellular Effectors: Phagocytes, Natural Killer Cells and Microbial Killing

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Neutrophil, macrophage and dendritic-cell roles; chemotaxis/recruitment only where functionally relevant, phagosome–lysosome maturation, oxidative burst/ROS and selected antimicrobial enzymes at NET/SET level; NK cells, missing-self recognition, inhibitory/activating receptors, perforin/granzyme as a representative innate cytotoxic mechanism; coordination with cytokines and antigen presentation as brief bridges.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Trace phagocytosis and killing, compare macrophage versus dendritic-cell roles and NK versus T-cell receptor specificity; interpret simple NK missing-self examples.
- **Scope boundary:** T-cell receptor-dependent CTL killing is in node 17, complement-mediated opsonization in 04 and inflammation processes in 05; host entry mechanisms belong to 4.D.

#### Inflammation and the Innate-to-Adaptive Immune Response

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Initiating tissue damage/microbial recognition; cytokines and chemokines, histamine, prostaglandins as selected mediators; vascular changes, neutrophil rolling, adhesion, extravasation and recruitment as a functional review; acute inflammation signs and resolution; antigen-presenting cell maturation/co-stimulation and migration to lymph nodes, cytokine roles as bridge to adaptive immunity; distinguish beneficial protection from excessive tissue damage.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Order inflammatory events and distinguish receptor recognition from tissue response; predict cell recruitment defects from basic adhesion/chemokine changes.
- **Scope boundary:** Detailed CAM biochemistry belongs to 4.B.02–05; signal-transduction cascades to 4.A, pathogen entry to 4.D; no exhaustive inflammatory disease pathology.



### 4.C.03 — Complement System: Pathways, Effector Functions and Regulation

**Student-facing menu description:** Compare classical, lectin and alternative complement activation, C3/C5 convertases, opsonization, inflammation, membrane attack and control.

**Chronological teaching journey:** Consolidate the innate soluble defense system and the antibody-triggered link to adaptive responses.

**Required internal microtopic sections and inherited science contracts:**

#### The Complement System: Activation, Effector Functions and Regulation

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Classical, lectin and alternative activation triggers; C3/C5 convertases at NET/SET diagrammatic level with named examples, C3b opsonization, C3a/C5a anaphylatoxins, terminal membrane-attack complex; appropriate regulation of host-self complement to prevent damage; note complement as innate effector with classical adaptive antibody linkage.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Follow simplified pathway activation charts, identify effects of early/late component deficiencies, distinguish complement opsonization, chemotaxis and direct membrane lysis.
- **Scope boundary:** Antibody isotypes in 07 and Ab-antigen binding in 10, immune disorders from complement deficiency in 24; no specialist clinical complement-drug protocols.



### 4.C.04 — Antigens, Epitopes, Antibody Structure and Antigen–Antibody Binding

**Student-facing menu description:** Understand antigenicity, immunogenicity, B/T epitopes, immunoglobulin structure and isotypes, affinity, avidity and antibody-binding reactions.

**Chronological teaching journey:** Begin with what is recognized, build the Ig binding molecule and then explain how epitope–paratope interactions produce specificity and effector binding.

**Required internal microtopic sections and inherited science contracts:**

#### Antigens, Immunogenicity and B- and T-Cell Epitopes

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Antigen and immunogen definitions; antigenicity versus immunogenicity; determinants of immunogenicity including foreignness, size, chemical complexity and administration context at general level; haptens/carriers; immunodominance as a basic concept; conformational and linear B-cell epitopes, peptide–MHC-associated T-cell epitopes; distinction between native antigen recognition and processed antigen presentation.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Identify epitope types from protein/peptide diagrams, predict hapten–carrier responses and classify antigen without assuming every antigen is strongly immunogenic.
- **Scope boundary:** Antibody physical interaction in node 10 and MHC presentation in 11–12; no advanced vaccine-epitope design or computational prediction.

#### Antibody Structure, Isotypes and Biological Functions

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Typical Y-shaped Ig domains and disulfide bonds, heavy/light chains, variable/constant regions and CDRs; Fab versus Fc, membrane and secreted antibodies, IgG/IgM/IgA/IgE/IgD structural differences and major effector roles, isotype vs allotype vs idiotype at textbook level; bivalency and typical polymer forms with appropriate exceptions; antigen neutralization, opsonization, Fc-receptor functions and complement activation concept.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Label an Ig schematic, match isotypes with representative functions, distinguish Fc versus Fab and serum/mucosal roles.
- **Scope boundary:** V(D)J mechanisms in 08, binding thermodynamics in 10, primary/secondary response patterns in 14 and complement mechanisms in 04.

#### Antigen–Antibody Interactions: Specificity, Affinity and Avidity

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Noncovalent interactions (hydrogen bonding, electrostatic, hydrophobic and van der Waals) in epitope–paratope recognition; monovalent affinity versus multivalent avidity, equilibrium concept without advanced biophysics, antigen specificity/cross-reactivity and binding-site geometry; precipitation and agglutination as standard qualitative examples, zone of equivalence/prozone as exam-relevant; basic neutralization and opsonization as consequences.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Reason about affinity versus avidity, interpret antigen/antibody ratio curves or simple cross-reaction cases, identify why precipitation requires lattice formation.
- **Scope boundary:** Detailed immunoassay methods such as ELISA, immunoblot and flow cytometry belong to Methods; antibody domains were in 07, complement effector biochemistry in 04.



### 4.C.05 — Antibody Diversity, Monoclonal Antibodies and Basic Engineering

**Student-facing menu description:** Explain V(D)J recombination, junctional diversity, somatic changes, hybridoma monoclonals and basic chimeric, humanized or recombinant antibodies.

**Chronological teaching journey:** Once antibody structure is understood, explain how nature generates receptor diversity and how the monoclonal concept is applied.

**Required internal microtopic sections and inherited science contracts:**

#### Generation of Antibody Diversity: V(D)J Recombination and Somatic Changes

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Heavy- and light-chain V/D/J segment organization, RAG1/2 recombination, junctional diversification and TdT as selected factors, combinatorial association of heavy and light chains; allelic exclusion at concept level, somatic hypermutation and AID-driven class-switch recombination as separate mechanisms in activated B cells; clarify isotype class switch preserves antigen specificity but alters effector region; contrast generation of naive diversity with antigen-selected affinity maturation.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Predict simple V-D-J segment arrangements and clone diversity qualitatively, interpret RAG/AID defects, distinguish recombination versus somatic hypermutation and class switching.
- **Scope boundary:** Gene-level rearrangement machinery should be NET/SET level only; TCR rearrangement comparisons in 15; avoid exhaustive genome-locus and molecular cloning methods.

#### Monoclonal Antibodies and Basic Antibody Engineering

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Polyclonal versus monoclonal antibody populations; classic hybridoma principle (immunization, B cell–myeloma fusion, selection and screening described conceptually, no laboratory protocol); epitope-specific clones; chimeric/humanized/fully human antibodies and engineered Fab/scFv formats at classification level; major uses in recognition, research/diagnostics and general therapy principles, limitations including specificity/immune response.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Distinguish monoclonal and polyclonal profiles, identify humanization stages and match antibody formats with basic binding/recognition needs.
- **Scope boundary:** Avoid cell-fusion engineering procedures, industrial antibody-manufacturing methods or detailed clinical indications; laboratory assays in Methods, general interactions in 10.



### 4.C.06 — MHC Molecules, Antigen Processing and Presentation

**Student-facing menu description:** Compare MHC I and II structure, expression and restriction, endogenous or endosomal antigen processing, peptide loading and cross-presentation.

**Chronological teaching journey:** Move from antibody recognition of native antigen to peptide–MHC display, preparing for conventional T-cell recognition.

**Required internal microtopic sections and inherited science contracts:**

#### Major Histocompatibility Complex: Classes, Structure and Restriction

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** MHC class I heavy chain/β2-microglobulin, MHC class II α/β chains, peptide binding grooves and typical peptide-binding preferences, polymorphism and codominant expression at standard level; class I expression on most nucleated cells and class II on professional antigen-presenting cells with induction caveats; MHC restriction, CD8 and CD4 associations and HLA/H-2 examples.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Match peptide–MHC class to T-cell subset, identify class I/II structures and cells, solve basic inheritance/restriction scenarios.
- **Scope boundary:** Antigen processing pathways assigned to 12; MHC-specific disease associations and transplantation clinical management not within this subunit scope.

#### Antigen Processing and Presentation: MHC I, MHC II and Cross-Presentation

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Conventional endogenous/proteasome→TAP→ER loading MHC I pathway, invariant chain/CLIP and HLA-DM-mediated MHC II endosomal peptide loading; dendritic cell, macrophage and B-cell presenting roles, costimulation as transition to T-cell activation; cross-presentation as a basic important exception and autophagy-mediated presentation only as brief nuance if needed; peptides bind MHC while TCR senses peptide–MHC.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Order processing organelles and molecules, predict effect of TAP/invariant-chain defects, distinguish MHC II uptake from MHC I endogenous processing without ignoring cross-presentation.
- **Scope boundary:** MHC structure in 11, molecular mechanism of TCR activation in 16; specialist immunoproteasome regulation or transplant processing is unnecessary.



### 4.C.07 — B-Cell Receptors, Development, Activation and Humoral Immunity

**Student-facing menu description:** Trace BCR signaling, B-cell maturation, T-dependent and T-independent activation, plasma cells, class switching, antibody responses and memory.

**Chronological teaching journey:** Follow the B-cell life history from the receptor and development to antigen-triggered activation, antibody secretion, primary and secondary responses.

**Required internal microtopic sections and inherited science contracts:**

#### B-Cell Receptors, B-Cell Development and Antigen Recognition

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** BCR as membrane immunoglobulin complex with Igα/Igβ (CD79a/b), signaling recruitment as overview; pro-B/pre-B/immature/mature B-cell development stages, light-chain rearrangement and central tolerance concept, basic allelic exclusion; mature naive B cells recognize native antigen and can present processed peptides to T helpers; distinguish BCR from secreted antibody and from MHC-restricted TCR.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Identify B-cell maturation sequence, infer basic consequences of BCR signaling/selection defects, distinguish BCR antigen binding from antigen presentation to T cells.
- **Scope boundary:** Deep V(D)J enzymology already owned by 08; B-cell activation and class switching consequences are 14; detailed kinase pathways in 4.A.

#### B-Cell Activation, Humoral Immunity and Immune Memory

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** BCR cross-linking and antigen uptake, CD4 T-cell help via CD40–CD40L and cytokines, T-dependent versus T-independent activation in standard examples; germinal center as a site for affinity maturation and class switching, differentiation into plasma and memory B cells; humoral effector functions (neutralization, opsonization, classical complement), primary vs secondary antibody response time courses and typical IgM/IgG patterns with exceptions; passive versus active immunity as preview to vaccines.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Plot and interpret primary/booster immune response curves, compare T-dependent and independent responses, predict loss of T-cell help on affinity maturation and memory.
- **Scope boundary:** Ig molecular structure in 07, AID chemistry in 08, complement in 04; immunization formats fully taught in 25.



### 4.C.08 — T-Cell Receptors, Thymic Selection, Activation and Differentiation

**Student-facing menu description:** Trace TCR–CD3 recognition, positive and negative thymic selection, costimulation, clonal expansion and major helper, regulatory and cytotoxic lineages.

**Chronological teaching journey:** Start with the MHC-restricted TCR and thymic development; then learn activation, co-stimulation, clonal growth and specialized T-cell roles.

**Required internal microtopic sections and inherited science contracts:**

#### T-Cell Receptors, Thymic Development and MHC Selection

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Typical αβ TCR and CD3 signal complex, difference from soluble antibody/BCR and its requirement for peptide–MHC recognition; V(D)J diversity as analogous to 08 with caveats, double-negative/double-positive and single-positive thymocytes, positive MHC restriction and negative self-tolerance selection, CD4/CD8 lineage context; distinguish γδ T cells in brief without specialist coverage.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Match MHC/co-receptor class to lineage, interpret positive/negative thymic selection and compare TCR versus BCR antigen recognition.
- **Scope boundary:** T-cell full activation/cytokines follow in 16; class I/II MHC structures in 11; do not repeat exhaustive V(D)J biochemistry.

#### T-Cell Activation and Differentiation: Helper, Regulatory and Cytotoxic Lineages

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Naive T-cell activation by peptide–MHC/TCR (signal 1), CD28–B7 costimulation (signal 2) and cytokine cues (signal 3) as a clear model; IL-2 expansion, anergy when activation signals are insufficient, differentiation into CD4 Th1, Th2, Th17, Tfh and Treg and CD8 cytotoxic effectors; functions and distinguishing cytokines at NET/SET-level examples without claiming fixed absolute cytokine identities.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Predict results of missing co-stimulation, assign Th subsets to simplified pathogens and B-cell help, distinguish activation from differentiation.
- **Scope boundary:** Cytotoxic effector actions in 17, broader immune modulation/tolerance in 18; full kinase and transcription-factor networks not required.



### 4.C.09 — Cell-Mediated Immunity, Immune Modulation and Self-Tolerance

**Student-facing menu description:** Explain cytotoxic T-cell killing, macrophage activation, cytokine networks, inhibitory checkpoints, regulatory T cells and immune tolerance.

**Chronological teaching journey:** Examine effector T-cell outputs first, then the feedback, inhibitory and tolerance mechanisms that prevent excessive or misdirected immunity.

**Required internal microtopic sections and inherited science contracts:**

#### Cell-Mediated Immunity: Cytotoxicity and Macrophage Activation

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** CD8 CTL recognition of target peptide–MHC I, perforin/granzyme cytotoxic pathway and Fas–FasL-mediated apoptosis as standard examples; Th1 cytokines and macrophage antimicrobial activation, delayed-type hypersensitivity as an effector example distinct from pathologic classification later; Th17 recruitment support in extracellular microbial defense; compare cellular killing to humoral antibody neutralization and innate NK function.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Trace cytotoxic target-cell recognition and killing, determine which pathway addresses intracellular pathogens, interpret CD8/class I loss effects.
- **Scope boundary:** Innate NK mechanism in 03; hypersensitivity clinical classification in 19, specific TB example in 21; do not repeat basic apoptotic cascade from 2.D.

#### Immune Modulation, Cytokine Networks and Self-Tolerance

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Immune modulation as increase/decrease/control of a response; innate/adaptive cytokines at representative level (interleukins, interferons, TNF) and chemokines, simple positive/negative response feedback; antigen presentation/co-stimulation modulation, inhibitory receptors such as CTLA-4/PD-1 as basic physiological regulation, Treg control and IL-10/TGF-β examples; central and peripheral tolerance and immune contraction; distinguish physiological regulation from primary/secondary response patterns already in 14.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Analyze loss-of-tolerance or inhibitory-signal effects, contrast central and peripheral tolerance and classify basic immunomodulatory influences.
- **Scope boundary:** Detailed signal-transduction chemistry in 4.A and full autoimmune mechanisms in 20; do not develop cancer immunotherapy regimens or clinical drug protocols.



### 4.C.10 — Hypersensitivity and Autoimmunity: Immune-Mediated Damage

**Student-facing menu description:** Distinguish types I–IV hypersensitivity and their effectors from loss of self-tolerance, autoantibodies and systemic or organ-specific autoimmunity.

**Chronological teaching journey:** Apply normal effector and tolerance mechanisms to four hypersensitivity pathways and to self-reactive disease mechanisms.

**Required internal microtopic sections and inherited science contracts:**

#### Hypersensitivity: Types I–IV and Their Immune Mechanisms

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Gell–Coombs I–IV mechanisms at exam depth: type I IgE/mast-cell degranulation and immediate allergic reaction; type II IgG/IgM directed toward cell/ECM antigens with complement/effector consequences; type III immune complex deposition/inflammation; type IV T-cell-driven delayed responses; temporal and effector distinctions and one or two standard examples such as anaphylaxis, hemolytic reaction, serum sickness and tuberculin response.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Assign clinical-style textbook vignettes to hypersensitivity class, recognize antibody versus T-cell origin and distinguish type II versus III targets.
- **Scope boundary:** Avoid medical diagnosis, desensitization prescriptions and disease management; keep examples biological and NET/SET relevant.

#### Autoimmunity: Breakdown of Self-Tolerance and Immune Damage

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Self versus non-self discrimination, clonal deletion, regulatory mechanisms, immune-privileged concepts only where relevant; central/peripheral tolerance failures, molecular mimicry as a representative trigger, HLA genetic predisposition at principle level; organ-specific versus systemic autoimmunity using one or two familiar examples such as type 1 diabetes or systemic lupus; roles of autoantibodies, complement and autoreactive T cells; distinguish autoimmunity from allergy and immunodeficiency.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Classify autoantibody versus T-cell-driven scenarios, connect self-tolerance defects to disease and contrast with hypersensitivity classification.
- **Scope boundary:** Specific clinical diagnostic antibody panels and therapeutic protocols excluded; tolerance concepts rooted in 15/18 and general mechanisms not repeated wholesale.



### 4.C.11 — Immune Responses to Bacterial and Parasitic Infections

**Student-facing menu description:** Compare defense against extracellular or intracellular bacteria, tuberculosis, malaria stages and helminth infection using appropriate innate and adaptive arms.

**Chronological teaching journey:** Compare extracellular and intracellular bacterial immunity (TB as a model), then contrast immune defense by parasite stage (malaria and helminths).

**Required internal microtopic sections and inherited science contracts:**

#### Immune Responses to Bacterial Infection: Tuberculosis as a Model

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Extracellular bacteria: innate phagocytosis, complement, opsonizing antibodies and helper mechanisms; intracellular bacteria: macrophage defense, IL-12–IFN-γ/Th1 signaling principles and cell-mediated control; Mycobacterium tuberculosis as MP SET/legacy CSIR illustrative case, phagocyte persistence, granuloma containment as an immune phenomenon, immune-mediated injury and delayed-type sensitivity at conceptual level; no pathogen-culture or disease treatment details.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Predict relative vulnerability with phagocyte/Th1 defects, compare intracellular versus extracellular bacterial defense and identify key cells/cytokines.
- **Scope boundary:** Pathogen entry, virulence mechanisms and bacterial taxonomy belong to 4.D or diversity; complement and CTL pathways previously taught, used in integrated cases rather than retaught.

#### Immune Responses to Parasitic Infection: Malaria and Helminth Defense

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Diversity of parasite locations as prerequisite; malaria as MP SET/legacy CSIR representative: innate sensing, sporozoite/liver-stage and blood-stage immune defense at broad mechanistic level, roles of antibodies, macrophages, T cells and evasion as immunological concepts; helminth defense with Th2, IL-4/IL-5/IL-13, IgE and eosinophils as contrasting parasite responses; distinction between protective immunity and immunopathology.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Match parasite stage to appropriate cellular/humoral responses, compare Th1/Th2 emphasis and interpret simple antigen variation examples.
- **Scope boundary:** Detailed Plasmodium life cycle, parasite taxonomy and pathogenesis are outside 4.C and belong to other units; no antimalarial treatment or parasite culture.



### 4.C.12 — Antiviral Immunity, HIV and Immunodeficiency Disorders

**Student-facing menu description:** Trace interferons, NK cells, CTLs and antiviral antibodies; understand HIV-associated CD4 loss and congenital or acquired immunodeficiencies.

**Chronological teaching journey:** Integrate antiviral immunity, use HIV to explain acquired immune deficiency, then compare inherited and other secondary defects by the lost immune component.

**Required internal microtopic sections and inherited science contracts:**

#### Immune Responses to Viral Infection: Interferons, CTLs and HIV

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Type I interferon production and antiviral state at overview level, NK cells and infected-cell detection, CTL recognition via MHC I, neutralizing antibodies, memory responses; viral evasion as immunological concept; HIV as explicit MP SET/legacy example: infection of CD4 T cells, progressive immune disruption, consequences for opportunistic susceptibility and role of immune responses at general level; do not conflate immune response to HIV with HIV treatment or detailed replication mechanism.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Identify key antiviral arms, predict susceptibility after impaired CD8 or interferon response, explain why CD4 depletion affects both B- and T-cell defense.
- **Scope boundary:** Viral entry/replication and host manipulation belong to 4.D; HIV immunodeficiency comparison also referenced but not retaught in 24; no anti-retroviral regimens.

#### Congenital and Acquired Immunodeficiencies: Mechanisms and Consequences

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Definitions of congenital/primary vs acquired/secondary deficiency; representative B-cell, T-cell, combined, phagocyte and complement deficiencies (SCID, X-linked agammaglobulinemia, chronic granulomatous disease as textbook examples) and associated infection susceptibility patterns at basic mechanistic level; acquired immune dysfunction due to HIV, malnutrition or immunosuppression in broad comparison; distinguish immune deficiency from autoimmunity/hypersensitivity; basic principle of laboratory immune-component assessment without diagnostics protocols.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Classify simple loss-of-function immune vignettes as B-cell/combined/phagocyte/complement defects, primary vs secondary; connect deficient effector to likely microbial challenge.
- **Scope boundary:** HIV antiviral immune response in 23; no clinical genetic diagnosis, specialist immune genetic syndromes or treatment guidelines.



### 4.C.13 — Vaccination, Active–Passive Immunization and Immune Memory

**Student-facing menu description:** Explain active and passive immunization, vaccine types, adjuvants, boosters and how protective B- and T-cell memory develops.

**Chronological teaching journey:** End by applying the foundation of antigen recognition, memory and immune modulation to immunization and vaccine platforms.

**Required internal microtopic sections and inherited science contracts:**

#### Vaccines and Immunological Memory: Types, Mechanisms and Protection

- **Full scientific content responsibility:** Vaccination as controlled induction of protective adaptive memory; active vs passive immunization, primary/secondary response in vaccine context, memory B/T cells and antibody quality; live-attenuated, inactivated, subunit/recombinant, toxoid, conjugate and mRNA/viral-vector vaccine platforms as mechanism-level comparisons where relevant, adjuvant and immune-stimulation principles, booster doses and herd immunity at introductory level; correlate route, antigen type and expected immune arms without ungrounded universal claims.
- **Required NET/SET reasoning practice:** Classify vaccine platforms and active/passive immunity, interpret booster antibody curves, distinguish conjugate vaccine T-cell help from independent polysaccharide response.
- **Scope boundary:** No production protocols, current vaccine schedules, effectiveness claims or medical advice; deeper vaccine manufacturing is biotechnology, public-health modeling is ecology.

## No-loss and no-overlap audit

- All **25 previous topic contracts** are retained word-for-word in the microtopic responsibilities and exam checks above and assigned to exactly one of the 13 final chapters. No old extra node IDs appear in the new menu or curriculum headings.
- **Innate sequence:** immune organs/cell classes and PRR/TLR recognition (4.C.01) → phagocytic/NK/inflammatory defense (4.C.02) → complement cascades (4.C.03).
- **Antibody sequence:** antigenicity/epitopes, Ig structure/isotypes and physical binding (4.C.04) → V(D)J diversity, monoclonals and basic engineering (4.C.05).
- **Adaptive specificity:** MHC and endogenous/exogenous processing (4.C.06) → B-cell humoral maturation and memory (4.C.07) → TCR thymic selection and activation (4.C.08).
- **Effector and dysregulation:** CTL/macrophage effects and immune modulation/tolerance (4.C.09) → hypersensitivity and autoimmunity (4.C.10).
- **Contextual infection response:** bacterial/TB and parasite/malaria immune responses (4.C.11) → viral/HIV immune response and congenital/acquired defects (4.C.12). Preserve distinctions among the different organism classes; do not reduce their coverage to disease name-dropping.
- **Application:** vaccine classes, adjuvants, priming/boosters and immunological memory (4.C.13). Basic B-cell primary/secondary kinetics are learned in 4.C.07; vaccine application is not redundant coverage.
- Keep the revised NET official heading **Innate and Adaptive Immune System**. Individual pages must not be claimed live while only menu titles exist.
- Every official scope item is assigned an owner with sufficient microtopics and exam-level conceptual comparisons; if future reading content gets thin, **expand the in-page sections** rather than re-splitting this menu.
