## Twenty-five complete reading-node briefs

### 4.C.01 — Organization of the Immune System: Cells, Tissues and Defense Layers

**Student-facing menu description:** Meet innate and adaptive immune cells, primary and secondary lymphoid organs, immune recognition, specificity and memory.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Physical and chemical barriers as initial protection; innate versus adaptive response timing, specificity and memory; leukocytes—neutrophils, monocytes/macrophages, dendritic cells, NK cells, mast cells, eosinophils, basophils and B/T lymphocytes; bone marrow, thymus, lymph nodes, spleen and mucosal lymphoid tissues in simple functional terms; antigen transport and cooperation among cells.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Classify leukocytes and organs, identify which cells initiate/present/execute immune responses, compare innate and adaptive response timelines.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** Detailed hematopoietic stem-cell hierarchy and cytokine regulation are 4.B.10–11; organ anatomy and broad animal physiology are not repeated.

### 4.C.02 — Innate Recognition: Barriers, Pattern-Recognition Receptors and Toll-Like Receptors

**Student-facing menu description:** Explain microbial and damage signals, Toll-like receptors, pattern recognition and the first steps that initiate innate defense.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Epithelial, mucus, antimicrobial peptide and soluble barrier functions; pathogen-associated and damage-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs/DAMPs); PRRs and key membrane/endosomal TLR examples; receptor localization and broad MyD88/TRIF-to-inflammatory-response logic at exam level; distinguish innate germline-encoded receptors from clonally rearranged antigen receptors; avoid presenting a fixed TLR ligand list as universal across species.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Interpret TLR localization and basic bacterial/viral ligand sensing cases, predict weak innate activation upon loss of recognition, contrast PRR versus BCR/TCR.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** No comprehensive intracellular NF-κB biochemistry or general signaling cascades already covered in 4.A; downstream effector and inflammation in nodes 03/05.

### 4.C.03 — Innate Cellular Effectors: Phagocytes, Natural Killer Cells and Microbial Killing

**Student-facing menu description:** Follow phagocytosis, macrophage and neutrophil killing, dendritic-cell linking functions and NK-cell cytotoxic recognition.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Neutrophil, macrophage and dendritic-cell roles; chemotaxis/recruitment only where functionally relevant, phagosome–lysosome maturation, oxidative burst/ROS and selected antimicrobial enzymes at NET/SET level; NK cells, missing-self recognition, inhibitory/activating receptors, perforin/granzyme as a representative innate cytotoxic mechanism; coordination with cytokines and antigen presentation as brief bridges.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Trace phagocytosis and killing, compare macrophage versus dendritic-cell roles and NK versus T-cell receptor specificity; interpret simple NK missing-self examples.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** T-cell receptor-dependent CTL killing is in node 17, complement-mediated opsonization in 04 and inflammation processes in 05; host entry mechanisms belong to 4.D.

### 4.C.04 — The Complement System: Activation, Effector Functions and Regulation

**Student-facing menu description:** Compare classical, lectin and alternative complement pathways through C3 activation, opsonization, inflammation and membrane attack.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Classical, lectin and alternative activation triggers; C3/C5 convertases at NET/SET diagrammatic level with named examples, C3b opsonization, C3a/C5a anaphylatoxins, terminal membrane-attack complex; appropriate regulation of host-self complement to prevent damage; note complement as innate effector with classical adaptive antibody linkage.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Follow simplified pathway activation charts, identify effects of early/late component deficiencies, distinguish complement opsonization, chemotaxis and direct membrane lysis.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** Antibody isotypes in 07 and Ab-antigen binding in 10, immune disorders from complement deficiency in 24; no specialist clinical complement-drug protocols.

### 4.C.05 — Inflammation and the Innate-to-Adaptive Immune Response

**Student-facing menu description:** Trace inflammatory mediators, leukocyte recruitment, acute inflammation and dendritic-cell activation leading to adaptive immunity.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Initiating tissue damage/microbial recognition; cytokines and chemokines, histamine, prostaglandins as selected mediators; vascular changes, neutrophil rolling, adhesion, extravasation and recruitment as a functional review; acute inflammation signs and resolution; antigen-presenting cell maturation/co-stimulation and migration to lymph nodes, cytokine roles as bridge to adaptive immunity; distinguish beneficial protection from excessive tissue damage.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Order inflammatory events and distinguish receptor recognition from tissue response; predict cell recruitment defects from basic adhesion/chemokine changes.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** Detailed CAM biochemistry belongs to 4.B.02–05; signal-transduction cascades to 4.A, pathogen entry to 4.D; no exhaustive inflammatory disease pathology.

### 4.C.06 — Antigens, Immunogenicity and B- and T-Cell Epitopes

**Student-facing menu description:** Distinguish antigens from immunogens, haptens and epitopes and compare what B-cell and T-cell receptors recognize.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Antigen and immunogen definitions; antigenicity versus immunogenicity; determinants of immunogenicity including foreignness, size, chemical complexity and administration context at general level; haptens/carriers; immunodominance as a basic concept; conformational and linear B-cell epitopes, peptide–MHC-associated T-cell epitopes; distinction between native antigen recognition and processed antigen presentation.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Identify epitope types from protein/peptide diagrams, predict hapten–carrier responses and classify antigen without assuming every antigen is strongly immunogenic.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** Antibody physical interaction in node 10 and MHC presentation in 11–12; no advanced vaccine-epitope design or computational prediction.

### 4.C.07 — Antibody Structure, Isotypes and Biological Functions

**Student-facing menu description:** Examine immunoglobulin heavy and light chains, variable and constant domains, Fab/Fc regions and the five major antibody classes.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Typical Y-shaped Ig domains and disulfide bonds, heavy/light chains, variable/constant regions and CDRs; Fab versus Fc, membrane and secreted antibodies, IgG/IgM/IgA/IgE/IgD structural differences and major effector roles, isotype vs allotype vs idiotype at textbook level; bivalency and typical polymer forms with appropriate exceptions; antigen neutralization, opsonization, Fc-receptor functions and complement activation concept.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Label an Ig schematic, match isotypes with representative functions, distinguish Fc versus Fab and serum/mucosal roles.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** V(D)J mechanisms in 08, binding thermodynamics in 10, primary/secondary response patterns in 14 and complement mechanisms in 04.

### 4.C.08 — Generation of Antibody Diversity: V(D)J Recombination and Somatic Changes

**Student-facing menu description:** Explain how immunoglobulin genes generate diverse B-cell receptors through segment recombination, junctional changes and affinity maturation.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Heavy- and light-chain V/D/J segment organization, RAG1/2 recombination, junctional diversification and TdT as selected factors, combinatorial association of heavy and light chains; allelic exclusion at concept level, somatic hypermutation and AID-driven class-switch recombination as separate mechanisms in activated B cells; clarify isotype class switch preserves antigen specificity but alters effector region; contrast generation of naive diversity with antigen-selected affinity maturation.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Predict simple V-D-J segment arrangements and clone diversity qualitatively, interpret RAG/AID defects, distinguish recombination versus somatic hypermutation and class switching.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** Gene-level rearrangement machinery should be NET/SET level only; TCR rearrangement comparisons in 15; avoid exhaustive genome-locus and molecular cloning methods.

### 4.C.09 — Monoclonal Antibodies and Basic Antibody Engineering

**Student-facing menu description:** Understand hybridoma-based monoclonal antibody production and the rationale for chimeric, humanized and recombinant antibody designs.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Polyclonal versus monoclonal antibody populations; classic hybridoma principle (immunization, B cell–myeloma fusion, selection and screening described conceptually, no laboratory protocol); epitope-specific clones; chimeric/humanized/fully human antibodies and engineered Fab/scFv formats at classification level; major uses in recognition, research/diagnostics and general therapy principles, limitations including specificity/immune response.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Distinguish monoclonal and polyclonal profiles, identify humanization stages and match antibody formats with basic binding/recognition needs.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** Avoid cell-fusion engineering procedures, industrial antibody-manufacturing methods or detailed clinical indications; laboratory assays in Methods, general interactions in 10.

### 4.C.10 — Antigen–Antibody Interactions: Specificity, Affinity and Avidity

**Student-facing menu description:** Follow noncovalent antigen–antibody binding, specificity, affinity, avidity, cross-reactivity and classic reaction patterns.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Noncovalent interactions (hydrogen bonding, electrostatic, hydrophobic and van der Waals) in epitope–paratope recognition; monovalent affinity versus multivalent avidity, equilibrium concept without advanced biophysics, antigen specificity/cross-reactivity and binding-site geometry; precipitation and agglutination as standard qualitative examples, zone of equivalence/prozone as exam-relevant; basic neutralization and opsonization as consequences.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Reason about affinity versus avidity, interpret antigen/antibody ratio curves or simple cross-reaction cases, identify why precipitation requires lattice formation.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** Detailed immunoassay methods such as ELISA, immunoblot and flow cytometry belong to Methods; antibody domains were in 07, complement effector biochemistry in 04.

### 4.C.11 — Major Histocompatibility Complex: Classes, Structure and Restriction

**Student-facing menu description:** Compare MHC class I and II molecules, antigen-binding grooves, tissue distribution and CD8/CD4 recognition.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** MHC class I heavy chain/β2-microglobulin, MHC class II α/β chains, peptide binding grooves and typical peptide-binding preferences, polymorphism and codominant expression at standard level; class I expression on most nucleated cells and class II on professional antigen-presenting cells with induction caveats; MHC restriction, CD8 and CD4 associations and HLA/H-2 examples.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Match peptide–MHC class to T-cell subset, identify class I/II structures and cells, solve basic inheritance/restriction scenarios.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** Antigen processing pathways assigned to 12; MHC-specific disease associations and transplantation clinical management not within this subunit scope.

### 4.C.12 — Antigen Processing and Presentation: MHC I, MHC II and Cross-Presentation

**Student-facing menu description:** Trace cytosolic and endosomal antigen-processing pathways to peptide display by MHC molecules on antigen-presenting cells.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Conventional endogenous/proteasome→TAP→ER loading MHC I pathway, invariant chain/CLIP and HLA-DM-mediated MHC II endosomal peptide loading; dendritic cell, macrophage and B-cell presenting roles, costimulation as transition to T-cell activation; cross-presentation as a basic important exception and autophagy-mediated presentation only as brief nuance if needed; peptides bind MHC while TCR senses peptide–MHC.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Order processing organelles and molecules, predict effect of TAP/invariant-chain defects, distinguish MHC II uptake from MHC I endogenous processing without ignoring cross-presentation.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** MHC structure in 11, molecular mechanism of TCR activation in 16; specialist immunoproteasome regulation or transplant processing is unnecessary.

### 4.C.13 — B-Cell Receptors, B-Cell Development and Antigen Recognition

**Student-facing menu description:** Understand the membrane immunoglobulin B-cell receptor, its signaling helpers, major developmental checkpoints and antigen recognition.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** BCR as membrane immunoglobulin complex with Igα/Igβ (CD79a/b), signaling recruitment as overview; pro-B/pre-B/immature/mature B-cell development stages, light-chain rearrangement and central tolerance concept, basic allelic exclusion; mature naive B cells recognize native antigen and can present processed peptides to T helpers; distinguish BCR from secreted antibody and from MHC-restricted TCR.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Identify B-cell maturation sequence, infer basic consequences of BCR signaling/selection defects, distinguish BCR antigen binding from antigen presentation to T cells.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** Deep V(D)J enzymology already owned by 08; B-cell activation and class switching consequences are 14; detailed kinase pathways in 4.A.

### 4.C.14 — B-Cell Activation, Humoral Immunity and Immune Memory

**Student-facing menu description:** Follow T-dependent and T-independent B-cell activation to plasma cells, antibody responses, class switching and memory.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** BCR cross-linking and antigen uptake, CD4 T-cell help via CD40–CD40L and cytokines, T-dependent versus T-independent activation in standard examples; germinal center as a site for affinity maturation and class switching, differentiation into plasma and memory B cells; humoral effector functions (neutralization, opsonization, classical complement), primary vs secondary antibody response time courses and typical IgM/IgG patterns with exceptions; passive versus active immunity as preview to vaccines.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Plot and interpret primary/booster immune response curves, compare T-dependent and independent responses, predict loss of T-cell help on affinity maturation and memory.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** Ig molecular structure in 07, AID chemistry in 08, complement in 04; immunization formats fully taught in 25.

### 4.C.15 — T-Cell Receptors, Thymic Development and MHC Selection

**Student-facing menu description:** Examine αβ T-cell receptor structure, CD3-associated recognition, thymic maturation and positive or negative selection.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Typical αβ TCR and CD3 signal complex, difference from soluble antibody/BCR and its requirement for peptide–MHC recognition; V(D)J diversity as analogous to 08 with caveats, double-negative/double-positive and single-positive thymocytes, positive MHC restriction and negative self-tolerance selection, CD4/CD8 lineage context; distinguish γδ T cells in brief without specialist coverage.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Match MHC/co-receptor class to lineage, interpret positive/negative thymic selection and compare TCR versus BCR antigen recognition.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** T-cell full activation/cytokines follow in 16; class I/II MHC structures in 11; do not repeat exhaustive V(D)J biochemistry.

### 4.C.16 — T-Cell Activation and Differentiation: Helper, Regulatory and Cytotoxic Lineages

**Student-facing menu description:** Trace antigen recognition, costimulation, clonal expansion and cytokine-guided differentiation into major T-cell functional groups.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Naive T-cell activation by peptide–MHC/TCR (signal 1), CD28–B7 costimulation (signal 2) and cytokine cues (signal 3) as a clear model; IL-2 expansion, anergy when activation signals are insufficient, differentiation into CD4 Th1, Th2, Th17, Tfh and Treg and CD8 cytotoxic effectors; functions and distinguishing cytokines at NET/SET-level examples without claiming fixed absolute cytokine identities.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Predict results of missing co-stimulation, assign Th subsets to simplified pathogens and B-cell help, distinguish activation from differentiation.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** Cytotoxic effector actions in 17, broader immune modulation/tolerance in 18; full kinase and transcription-factor networks not required.

### 4.C.17 — Cell-Mediated Immunity: Cytotoxicity and Macrophage Activation

**Student-facing menu description:** Explain effector T-cell killing, helper-driven macrophage activation and the functions of cell-mediated defenses.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** CD8 CTL recognition of target peptide–MHC I, perforin/granzyme cytotoxic pathway and Fas–FasL-mediated apoptosis as standard examples; Th1 cytokines and macrophage antimicrobial activation, delayed-type hypersensitivity as an effector example distinct from pathologic classification later; Th17 recruitment support in extracellular microbial defense; compare cellular killing to humoral antibody neutralization and innate NK function.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Trace cytotoxic target-cell recognition and killing, determine which pathway addresses intracellular pathogens, interpret CD8/class I loss effects.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** Innate NK mechanism in 03; hypersensitivity clinical classification in 19, specific TB example in 21; do not repeat basic apoptotic cascade from 2.D.

### 4.C.18 — Immune Modulation, Cytokine Networks and Self-Tolerance

**Student-facing menu description:** Learn how regulatory cytokines, co-stimulation, immune checkpoints and tolerance prevent excessive immune responses.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Immune modulation as increase/decrease/control of a response; innate/adaptive cytokines at representative level (interleukins, interferons, TNF) and chemokines, simple positive/negative response feedback; antigen presentation/co-stimulation modulation, inhibitory receptors such as CTLA-4/PD-1 as basic physiological regulation, Treg control and IL-10/TGF-β examples; central and peripheral tolerance and immune contraction; distinguish physiological regulation from primary/secondary response patterns already in 14.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Analyze loss-of-tolerance or inhibitory-signal effects, contrast central and peripheral tolerance and classify basic immunomodulatory influences.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** Detailed signal-transduction chemistry in 4.A and full autoimmune mechanisms in 20; do not develop cancer immunotherapy regimens or clinical drug protocols.

### 4.C.19 — Hypersensitivity: Types I–IV and Their Immune Mechanisms

**Student-facing menu description:** Classify immediate allergy, antibody-mediated, immune-complex and T-cell-mediated hypersensitivity with representative examples.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Gell–Coombs I–IV mechanisms at exam depth: type I IgE/mast-cell degranulation and immediate allergic reaction; type II IgG/IgM directed toward cell/ECM antigens with complement/effector consequences; type III immune complex deposition/inflammation; type IV T-cell-driven delayed responses; temporal and effector distinctions and one or two standard examples such as anaphylaxis, hemolytic reaction, serum sickness and tuberculin response.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Assign clinical-style textbook vignettes to hypersensitivity class, recognize antibody versus T-cell origin and distinguish type II versus III targets.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** Avoid medical diagnosis, desensitization prescriptions and disease management; keep examples biological and NET/SET relevant.

### 4.C.20 — Autoimmunity: Breakdown of Self-Tolerance and Immune Damage

**Student-facing menu description:** Explain how defective central/peripheral tolerance and genetic or environmental factors lead to organ-specific and systemic autoimmune responses.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Self versus non-self discrimination, clonal deletion, regulatory mechanisms, immune-privileged concepts only where relevant; central/peripheral tolerance failures, molecular mimicry as a representative trigger, HLA genetic predisposition at principle level; organ-specific versus systemic autoimmunity using one or two familiar examples such as type 1 diabetes or systemic lupus; roles of autoantibodies, complement and autoreactive T cells; distinguish autoimmunity from allergy and immunodeficiency.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Classify autoantibody versus T-cell-driven scenarios, connect self-tolerance defects to disease and contrast with hypersensitivity classification.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** Specific clinical diagnostic antibody panels and therapeutic protocols excluded; tolerance concepts rooted in 15/18 and general mechanisms not repeated wholesale.

### 4.C.21 — Immune Responses to Bacterial Infection: Tuberculosis as a Model

**Student-facing menu description:** Compare immunity to extracellular and intracellular bacteria, using macrophage and Th1 responses in tuberculosis as a standard example.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Extracellular bacteria: innate phagocytosis, complement, opsonizing antibodies and helper mechanisms; intracellular bacteria: macrophage defense, IL-12–IFN-γ/Th1 signaling principles and cell-mediated control; Mycobacterium tuberculosis as MP SET/legacy CSIR illustrative case, phagocyte persistence, granuloma containment as an immune phenomenon, immune-mediated injury and delayed-type sensitivity at conceptual level; no pathogen-culture or disease treatment details.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Predict relative vulnerability with phagocyte/Th1 defects, compare intracellular versus extracellular bacterial defense and identify key cells/cytokines.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** Pathogen entry, virulence mechanisms and bacterial taxonomy belong to 4.D or diversity; complement and CTL pathways previously taught, used in integrated cases rather than retaught.

### 4.C.22 — Immune Responses to Parasitic Infection: Malaria and Helminth Defense

**Student-facing menu description:** Understand parasite-stage-specific immunity using malaria and contrast it with type-2 responses to helminths.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Diversity of parasite locations as prerequisite; malaria as MP SET/legacy CSIR representative: innate sensing, sporozoite/liver-stage and blood-stage immune defense at broad mechanistic level, roles of antibodies, macrophages, T cells and evasion as immunological concepts; helminth defense with Th2, IL-4/IL-5/IL-13, IgE and eosinophils as contrasting parasite responses; distinction between protective immunity and immunopathology.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Match parasite stage to appropriate cellular/humoral responses, compare Th1/Th2 emphasis and interpret simple antigen variation examples.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** Detailed Plasmodium life cycle, parasite taxonomy and pathogenesis are outside 4.C and belong to other units; no antimalarial treatment or parasite culture.

### 4.C.23 — Immune Responses to Viral Infection: Interferons, CTLs and HIV

**Student-facing menu description:** Follow antiviral innate sensing, interferons, cytotoxic T-cell clearance and antibody protection, with HIV as an acquired-immunodeficiency example.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Type I interferon production and antiviral state at overview level, NK cells and infected-cell detection, CTL recognition via MHC I, neutralizing antibodies, memory responses; viral evasion as immunological concept; HIV as explicit MP SET/legacy example: infection of CD4 T cells, progressive immune disruption, consequences for opportunistic susceptibility and role of immune responses at general level; do not conflate immune response to HIV with HIV treatment or detailed replication mechanism.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Identify key antiviral arms, predict susceptibility after impaired CD8 or interferon response, explain why CD4 depletion affects both B- and T-cell defense.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** Viral entry/replication and host manipulation belong to 4.D; HIV immunodeficiency comparison also referenced but not retaught in 24; no anti-retroviral regimens.

### 4.C.24 — Congenital and Acquired Immunodeficiencies: Mechanisms and Consequences

**Student-facing menu description:** Compare primary inherited immune defects with acquired immunodeficiency and infer consequences from the impaired immune component.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Definitions of congenital/primary vs acquired/secondary deficiency; representative B-cell, T-cell, combined, phagocyte and complement deficiencies (SCID, X-linked agammaglobulinemia, chronic granulomatous disease as textbook examples) and associated infection susceptibility patterns at basic mechanistic level; acquired immune dysfunction due to HIV, malnutrition or immunosuppression in broad comparison; distinguish immune deficiency from autoimmunity/hypersensitivity; basic principle of laboratory immune-component assessment without diagnostics protocols.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Classify simple loss-of-function immune vignettes as B-cell/combined/phagocyte/complement defects, primary vs secondary; connect deficient effector to likely microbial challenge.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** HIV antiviral immune response in 23; no clinical genetic diagnosis, specialist immune genetic syndromes or treatment guidelines.

### 4.C.25 — Vaccines and Immunological Memory: Types, Mechanisms and Protection

**Student-facing menu description:** Connect immune memory and booster responses to vaccination, active or passive immunization, vaccine classes and adjuvants.

**Required graduate/NET/SET scientific scope:** Vaccination as controlled induction of protective adaptive memory; active vs passive immunization, primary/secondary response in vaccine context, memory B/T cells and antibody quality; live-attenuated, inactivated, subunit/recombinant, toxoid, conjugate and mRNA/viral-vector vaccine platforms as mechanism-level comparisons where relevant, adjuvant and immune-stimulation principles, booster doses and herd immunity at introductory level; correlate route, antigen type and expected immune arms without ungrounded universal claims.

**Exam reasoning, diagrams and applied understanding:** Classify vaccine platforms and active/passive immunity, interpret booster antibody curves, distinguish conjugate vaccine T-cell help from independent polysaccharide response.

**No-overlap and research-depth boundary:** No production protocols, current vaccine schedules, effectiveness claims or medical advice; deeper vaccine manufacturing is biotechnology, public-health modeling is ecology.

## Sequential teaching stages

1. **4.C.01–05 — The innate foundation:** Cells and lymphoid sites → pattern recognition/TLRs → phagocytes/NK → complement → inflammation and antigen-presenting-cell activation.
2. **4.C.06–10 — Antigen and antibody principles:** Immunogenicity and B/T epitopes → Ig structure and function → antibody diversity → monoclonals and basic engineering → antigen–antibody binding principles.
3. **4.C.11–12 — Bridging to T-cell specificity:** MHC I versus II, restriction, then classical antigen-processing/presentation pathways and recognized exceptions.
4. **4.C.13–14 — Humoral adaptive arm:** BCR recognition and maturation → activation, plasma cells, primary and secondary humoral responses and memory.
5. **4.C.15–17 — Cell-mediated adaptive arm:** TCR selection → T-cell activation/differentiation → CTL and Th-dependent effector mechanisms.
6. **4.C.18–20 — Immune balance:** Cytokine regulation and self-tolerance → hypersensitivity classes → autoimmune dysfunction.
7. **4.C.21–23 — Immunity during infection:** Bacterial (TB), parasitic (malaria/helminths), then viral (HIV) defense models, each limited to the immune response rather than pathogen life cycles.
8. **4.C.24–25 — Failure and protection:** Congenital/acquired immunodeficiencies → vaccine mechanisms and induced immune memory.

The order is *instructional*; it does not imply immune phenomena occur one after another exclusively in biological time.

## Scientific QA safeguards for full-page chapters

- Distinguish **antigenicity** (ability to be recognized/bound) from **immunogenicity** (ability to elicit a response). BCR/antibodies can recognize native antigens, whereas conventional αβ TCRs recognize a peptide bound to MHC; account for known exceptions only when necessary.
- **TLRs** are innate PRRs; their ligands, localization and adaptor mechanisms depend on TLR type and organism. Do not convert 4.C.02 into a repeat of Unit 4.A pathways.
- **Complement** classical/lectin/alternative initiation differs, but all converge on C3 and can lead to opsonization, inflammation and terminal lysis. Host regulation prevents tissue damage.
- **Ig classes** have distinct structural and effector functions. Somatic hypermutation, V(D)J rearrangement and class-switch recombination are three different processes. A class switch changes effector region/isotype, not the original V-region antigen specificity.
- **MHC I** usually displays endogenous peptides to CD8 T cells; **MHC II** commonly displays exogenous-processed peptides to CD4 T cells. Cross-presentation and other genuine exceptions mean these simplified statements must not be taught as absolute universal laws.
- **B- versus T-cell recognition:** BCR binds antigen directly, and mature B cells can process/present antigen on MHC II; TCR needs peptide–MHC for conventional αβ T cells. Positive and negative selection happen in thymic development of T cells.
- **Primary/secondary response curves** differ in timing/magnitude/affinity; do not claim IgM is the sole primary response or that every booster raises exactly the same isotype.
- **Cell-mediated immunity** includes T-cell-mediated macrophage activation and cytotoxic killing. Keep the NK innate pathway from 4.C.03 distinct from CD8 CTL recognition in 4.C.17.
- **Immunomodulation** and physiological tolerance are not identical to hypersensitivity or clinical immunotherapy. Differentiating inhibitory regulation from disease is central to chapter order.
- **Tuberculosis, malaria and HIV** are specifically named older CSIR/MP SET comparisons; use them as immunological models without teaching clinical management, detailed pathogen biology, treatment or modern population surveillance.
- **Hypersensitivity I–IV** distinguishes IgE immediate, antibody/cell-target, immune-complex and T-cell-mediated mechanisms. Autoimmunity concerns self-reactive responses; deficiency concerns insufficient function.
- **Vaccines** teach induced memory and principles. Avoid vaccination schedules, real-time product comparisons, individual health recommendations, effectiveness estimates or treatment claims.
