## The twelve chronological node contracts

### 5.C.01 — Cell Aggregation and Differentiation in Dictyostelium

**Student-facing menu description:** Follow starvation-triggered cAMP signaling, chemotactic aggregation, mound and slug formation, prestalk–prespore differentiation and fruiting-body development.

**Driving developmental question:** How can separate amoebae coordinate movement and specialize into different cell types?

**Full in-page scientific microtopic responsibility:** Dictyostelium discoideum is a social amoeba, not an animal; starvation and developmental transition; extracellular cAMP pulses, chemotaxis, relay and aggregation streams; adhesion, mound, slug and migratory stages; prestalk versus prespore fate allocation, culminant stalk/spore and cooperative function; cAMP and selected differentiation factors as examples. Contrast aggregation with cleavage-derived animal multicellularity.

**NET/SET evidence, diagram and problem-solving responsibility:** Label a developmental life cycle; predict defective cAMP relay, chemotaxis, adhesion or stalk/spore differentiation in simple perturbations.

**Scope boundary, overlap prevention and scientific guardrails:** 4.A signaling and 5.A specification are foundations; do not repeat complete GPCR biochemistry or misclassify Dictyostelium as a metazoan.

### 5.C.02 — Drosophila Axis Formation: Maternal Gradients and Embryonic Polarity

**Student-facing menu description:** Trace maternal-effect Bicoid, Nanos, Torso and Dorsal signals that establish anterior–posterior and dorsal–ventral embryonic axes.

**Driving developmental question:** How does a syncytial insect embryo acquire its anterior, posterior, dorsal and ventral positions?

**Full in-page scientific microtopic responsibility:** Maternal determinants and syncytial blastoderm; anterior Bicoid and posterior Nanos, Hunchback and Caudal in appropriate context; Torso terminal signals; dorsoventral patterning through ventral Toll pathway and nuclear Dorsal gradient; species/stage-specific mechanisms, with emphasis that high nuclear Dorsal is ventral; maternal-effect phenotypes and localized maternal transcript experiments. Explain geometry of axis formation before discussing zygotic segmentation.

**NET/SET evidence, diagram and problem-solving responsibility:** Interpret localization maps and concentration patterns; predict loss or ectopic Bicoid/Nanos function or dorsal/ventral fate shifts.

**Scope boundary, overlap prevention and scientific guardrails:** General maternal determinants and gradients were introduced in 5.A; 5.C.03 owns gap, pair-rule, polarity and Hox development.

### 5.C.03 — Drosophila Segmentation, Segment Polarity and Hox Gene Identity

**Student-facing menu description:** Follow gap, pair-rule, segment-polarity and homeotic gene activities that define repeated embryonic segments and distinct regional identities.

**Driving developmental question:** Once axes are established, how do repeated segments form and acquire different identities?

**Full in-page scientific microtopic responsibility:** Maternal coordinate inputs → gap gene broad domains → pair-rule periodic stripes → segment-polarity boundary refinements; examples Krüppel, even-skipped, engrailed and Wingless/Hedgehog. Segment versus parasegment; Hox/homeotic selector genes such as Antennapedia and Ultrabithorax, homeobox meaning and Hox function in segment identity; classic mutant series as causal evidence. Emphasize that Hox identity is not the same as initial segment count.

**NET/SET evidence, diagram and problem-solving responsibility:** Diagnose gap, pair-rule, segment-polarity or homeotic mutations from diagrams; order gene-cascade stages and predict ectopic organ identity.

**Scope boundary, overlap prevention and scientific guardrails:** 5.C.02 already explains maternal gradients; Unit 3.D covers broad gene regulation, and no doctoral gene-network catalog is needed.

### 5.C.04 — Amphibian Axis Formation: Cortical Rotation, Nieuwkoop Center and Organizer

**Student-facing menu description:** Connect fertilization-associated cortical rotation with dorsal specification, the Nieuwkoop center and Spemann–Mangold organizer-directed embryonic patterning.

**Driving developmental question:** How does a vertebrate egg establish a dorsal organizer and recruit neighboring tissues to form an axis?

**Full in-page scientific microtopic responsibility:** Amphibian/Xenopus egg polarity, sperm-entry-linked cortical rotation, gray crescent, dorsal β-catenin and vegetally located Nieuwkoop center as inductive inputs; organizer formation at dorsal blastopore lip, grafts and secondary axis induction, contribution from both donor and host tissues; Nodal-related and BMP antagonist activities at overview level; neural induction as an organogenesis bridge. Explain competence and organizer control without retelling all gastrulation movements.

**NET/SET evidence, diagram and problem-solving responsibility:** Interpret gray-crescent displacement, organizer graft, Nieuwkoop-center alteration and dorsal marker experiments; distinguish Nieuwkoop center from organizer.

**Scope boundary, overlap prevention and scientific guardrails:** 5.A covers general induction and 5.B covers gastrulation; do not claim an ectopic axis comes entirely from donor cells.

### 5.C.05 — Chick Embryo Axis Formation: Primitive Streak, Hensen's Node and Patterning

**Student-facing menu description:** Follow avian blastoderm polarity, primitive-streak formation, Hensen's node, axis elongation and the emergence of somitic patterns.

**Driving developmental question:** How does a disc-shaped bird embryo construct a patterned vertebrate body axis?

**Full in-page scientific microtopic responsibility:** Discoidal avian blastoderm and epiblast/hypoblast, posterior marginal zone and Koller's sickle as context, primitive streak and Hensen's node organizer activity, ingression and nascent mesoderm at overview level, axial elongation and notochord; somitogenesis, segmentation rhythm and representative left/right patterning signals. Contrast chick organizer and amphibian dorsal lip, without equating anatomical structures.

**NET/SET evidence, diagram and problem-solving responsibility:** Label chick organizer structures; reason about ectopic Hensen node and somitic defects; compare avian primitive-streak and amphibian organizer evidence.

**Scope boundary, overlap prevention and scientific guardrails:** 5.B owns general germ-layer formation; 5.C.04 amphibian axis, 5.C.10 neural identity and differentiation.

### 5.C.06 — C. elegans Vulva Formation: Induction, Lateral Signaling and Cell Fate

**Student-facing menu description:** Explain anchor-cell LIN-3/EGF induction, Ras/MAPK signaling, LIN-12/Notch lateral communication and vulval precursor-cell patterning.

**Driving developmental question:** How do six equivalent-competence precursor cells adopt different fates in a reproducible organ?

**Full in-page scientific microtopic responsibility:** C. elegans vulval precursor cells P3.p–P8.p, gonadal anchor cell, P6.p primary fate through LIN-3/EGF–LET-23 and downstream Ras/MAPK, P5.p/P7.p secondary fate through LIN-12/Notch lateral signaling, remaining tertiary cells and developmental competence; canonical 3°–3°–2°–1°–2°–3° pattern under standard context, further divisions and vulval morphogenesis, anchor-cell ablation and pathway mutant experiments, multivulva versus vulvaless patterns.

**NET/SET evidence, diagram and problem-solving responsibility:** Work 1°/2°/3° cell-fate diagrams and anchor-cell removal/EGF/Notch perturbation predictions; distinguish initial induction and later lateral signaling.

**Scope boundary, overlap prevention and scientific guardrails:** 4.A teaches generic pathway biochemistry, 5.A differentiation principles; do not cover the entire nematode lineage.

### 5.C.07 — Vertebrate Eye Formation and Lens Induction

**Student-facing menu description:** Trace optic-vesicle interactions with competent head ectoderm through lens placode, lens vesicle and lens-cell differentiation.

**Driving developmental question:** How can neural and surface ectoderm coordinate development of the eye and lens?

**Full in-page scientific microtopic responsibility:** Eye field and optic vesicle positioning, competence of head surface ectoderm including early prepatterning, optic-vesicle and ectoderm reciprocal induction, classical transplantation and tissue-stage differences, lens placode→pit→vesicle and fiber-cell differentiation, optic cup and retina versus surface-ectoderm-derived lens, Pax6 and Sox2 as familiar regulators; caution that optic-vesicle contact is not universally the earliest or only inductive event.

**NET/SET evidence, diagram and problem-solving responsibility:** Order organogenetic stages, identify lens/retina embryonic source, predict effects of grafts to ectoderm of different competence or Pax6 defects.

**Scope boundary, overlap prevention and scientific guardrails:** 5.A induction fundamentals already covered; Unit 07 vision physiology and specialist eye disease are outside scope.

### 5.C.08 — Vertebrate Limb Development: Limb Bud, AER, ZPA and Positional Identity

**Student-facing menu description:** Follow limb-bud initiation and interacting AER, ZPA, FGF, Shh and Wnt signals along proximodistal, anteroposterior and dorsoventral axes.

**Driving developmental question:** How does a limb bud assign its length, digit sequence and upper/lower identities?

**Full in-page scientific microtopic responsibility:** Tetrapod limb fields and bud initiation, mesenchymal FGF10 and apical ectodermal ridge FGF reciprocity, outgrowth and proximodistal stylopod–zeugopod–autopod patterning, posterior zone of polarizing activity and Sonic hedgehog for anteroposterior digit organization, Wnt7a–Lmx1 dorsoventral polarity as standard example, limb Hox inputs and interdigital cell elimination as context; classic AER excision and ZPA graft effects, with modern contextual caveats.

**NET/SET evidence, diagram and problem-solving responsibility:** Interpret limb axes and manipulations, predict AER removal effects and ZPA graft mirror-image duplication; distinguish growth from positional specification.

**Scope boundary, overlap prevention and scientific guardrails:** 5.C.09 is regeneration, 5.E cell death, 4.A generic biochemical cascades; no specialist evolutionary limb development.

### 5.C.09 — Vertebrate Limb Regeneration: Wound Response, Blastema and Pattern Restoration

**Student-facing menu description:** Understand wound epidermis, regenerative blastema, nerve dependence, positional memory and tissue redifferentiation during limb regrowth.

**Driving developmental question:** How can an amputated limb in some vertebrates restore organized missing parts?

**Full in-page scientific microtopic responsibility:** Axolotl/newt limb regeneration as standard model; wound closure, wound epithelium/apical epithelial cap, progenitor recruitment or cell dedifferentiation as contextual possibilities, blastema formation, growth and pattern restoration, nerve dependence and positional memory. Compare primary limb development and regeneration, regeneration versus repair/scar formation, variable frog and mammalian regeneration capabilities; classic denervation and proximal/distal amputation experiments.

**NET/SET evidence, diagram and problem-solving responsibility:** Order wound→blastema→patterning→redifferentiation and interpret denervation and positional-memory scenarios.

**Scope boundary, overlap prevention and scientific guardrails:** 5.C.08 owns embryonic limb patterning; 5.A stem cells; no clinical regeneration or tissue-engineering treatment procedures.

### 5.C.10 — Neuronal Differentiation: Neural Induction, Cell Fates and Axon Guidance

**Student-facing menu description:** Connect neural plate and tube induction to neural-crest specification, neurogenesis, neuronal migration and developing axon pathways.

**Driving developmental question:** How does embryonic ectoderm produce distinct neurons and establish directed connections?

**Full in-page scientific microtopic responsibility:** Vertebrate ectoderm specification into neural versus epidermal fates, amphibian organizer/BMP context, neural plate folding and tube formation, neural crest migration and selected derivatives; dorsal and ventral neural tube patterning with BMP/Shh gradient examples, neural progenitors, proneural factors and Notch-mediated lateral inhibition, neuronal/glial fate decisions, neuron migration and growth-cone response to netrin/slit/semaphorin/ephrin classes at broad level; distinguish neuronal development from transmission at mature synapses.

**NET/SET evidence, diagram and problem-solving responsibility:** Label neural tube versus crest derivatives, interpret Notch/gradient and growth-cone attraction/repulsion perturbations.

**Scope boundary, overlap prevention and scientific guardrails:** Unit 07 owns neuronal action potentials and circuit physiology; 4.B chemical neurotransmission, 5.C.04 organizer signals.

### 5.C.11 — Postembryonic Development: Larvae, Metamorphosis and Environmental Regulation

**Student-facing menu description:** Follow direct versus indirect development, larval differentiation, insect and amphibian metamorphosis, endocrine cues and environmental plasticity.

**Driving developmental question:** How does a larva become an adult, and why can developmental timing depend on its environment?

**Full in-page scientific microtopic responsibility:** Direct and indirect development, larval body plan and postembryonic morphogenesis; insect hemimetabolous versus holometabolous routes, ecdysone and juvenile hormone as developmental timing signals, imaginal discs in Drosophila; amphibian tadpole metamorphosis and thyroid hormone, tissue remodeling and developmental cell death as links. Environmental influences on normal development: nutrition, temperature, photoperiod, density, stress and stage-sensitive plasticity using appropriate model examples; differences among adaptive timing shifts, diapause and permanent malformation.

**NET/SET evidence, diagram and problem-solving responsibility:** Interpret stage/hormone plots, predict metamorphic response to correctly specified hormone manipulation and explain normal plasticity versus genetic mutation.

**Scope boundary, overlap prevention and scientific guardrails:** Detailed hormone feedback/physiology in Unit 07, PCD in 5.E, ecological population questions in ecology units; avoid using environment as an unconnected topic.

### 5.C.12 — Sex Determination and Sexual Differentiation in Animals

**Student-facing menu description:** Compare XX/XY, ZZ/ZW and other genetic systems, mammalian SRY–SOX9 development, fly dosage mechanisms and temperature-dependent sex determination.

**Driving developmental question:** How do animals establish sex-specific developmental pathways through genetic or environmental information?

**Full in-page scientific microtopic responsibility:** Distinguish sex determination from sexual differentiation; XX/XY and ZZ/ZW chromosomal patterns with species caveats, mammalian SRY and SOX9 testis-development cascade, ovarian development signals such as WNT4/RSPO1 at conceptual level; Drosophila X:A ratio, Sex-lethal/transformer/doublesex genetic splicing and role of Y in fertility rather than primary sex determination; C. elegans XX/XO as compact comparative example; temperature-dependent sex determination in selected reptiles, temperature-sensitive windows and species-dependent directions; hormone-mediated downstream differentiation only as necessary developmental context.

**NET/SET evidence, diagram and problem-solving responsibility:** Interpret sex-chromosome patterns, X:A ratios and reptile temperature data; distinguish a primary sex-determination signal from later hormone-mediated sexual differentiation.

**Scope boundary, overlap prevention and scientific guardrails:** Unit 08 sex-linked inheritance; Unit 07 full gonadal/endocrine physiology; 5.B gamete production. No human medical diagnosis or clinical advice.

## Cross-node progress and proof of continuity

1. **Comparative beginning (01).** A social amoeba demonstrates cell aggregation, chemical coordination and fates without confusing it with an animal embryo.
2. **Animal axes (02–05).** Maternal information sets initial directions; zygotic segmentation specifies regions; vertebrate organizers, primitive streak and node interactions create coordinated embryonic body plans.
3. **Organs from positional information (06–08).** VPC lateral and inductive communication, eye-field and lens reciprocity, and limb-bud patterning are separate organogenesis mechanisms with comparable causal tests.
4. **Restoration and specialization (09–10).** Regenerative blastema formation and neuron specification/axon guidance demonstrate later cell-fate and tissue-patterning needs.
5. **Postembryonic change (11).** Larval forms, endocrine metamorphosis and environmental regulation are integrated into one causal chapter rather than isolated short pages.
6. **Sex-related developmental pathways (12).** Genetic versus environmental determinants and differentiation are separated, with organism-specific systems explicitly labeled.

## Nonnegotiable accuracy and anti-overlap checks

- *Dictyostelium discoideum* is not an animal; starvation and cAMP-mediated aggregation precede prespore and prestalk differentiation.
- In Drosophila, maternal polarity signals precede gap→pair-rule→segment-polarity expression and homeotic segment identity. Do not conflate Dorsal protein nuclear localization with the anatomical dorsal side.
- The amphibian Nieuwkoop center and Spemann organizer are related but are not the same embryonic territory; graft-induced secondary axes have donor **and** host contributions.
- Hensen's node is an avian organizer-related structure in the context of a primitive streak; do not claim its morphogenesis is anatomically identical to the amphibian dorsal lip.
- The common *C. elegans* vulval pattern includes P6.p primary and P5.p/P7.p secondary fates with anchor-cell LIN-3/EGF and Notch-mediated lateral signaling.
- Eye lens induction depends on developmental competence and successive interactions; direct optic vesicle contact is not the only possible early inductive event in every model.
- AER and ZPA are separate limb patterning zones; the PD, AP and DV axes must not be mixed. Regeneration after amputation differs from ordinary limb embryogenesis.
- Neural tube and neural crest have different derivatives; guidance-cue comparisons should remain at developmental—not mature electrophysiological—depth.
- Metamorphosis hormones affect developmental timing, but nutrition/temperature can modify normal trajectories; do not generalize a single insect hormone rule to all amphibians.
- Drosophila primary sex determination depends on X:A-related regulation rather than the presence of Y, while temperature effects in reptiles vary by species.
