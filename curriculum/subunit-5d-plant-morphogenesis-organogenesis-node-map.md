- **Exact revised syllabus topics:** Organization of shoot and root apical meristem; shoot and root development; leaf development and phyllotaxy; transition to flowering; floral meristems; organogenesis and floral development.
- **Legacy CSIR and MP SET explicit examples:** The flowering/developmental model systems **Arabidopsis** and **Antirrhinum** must be retained—especially floral homeotic mutations and gene-function comparisons. The 2026 revised wording is broader, not license to drop those classical examples.
- **Node count:** **11** coherent, causal learning nodes `5.D.01–5.D.11`. This number balances academic responsibilities and independent NET/SET reasoning questions, rather than imposing equal word count or padding short chapters.
- **Narrative logic:** germinating plant's continuing organ formation → shoot apex organization → shoot architecture → root apex organization → root tissues/branches → leaf positioning → leaf shape and differentiated tissues → flowering signals → floral meristem identity → organ identity → Arabidopsis/Antirrhinum experimental evidence.
- **Structural note:** Shoot and root modules are **parallel organ systems**, not a claim that shoot development must biologically finish before roots develop. Chapters are organized pedagogically from common meristem concepts to independent morphogenic applications and finally reproductive transition.
- **Academic standard:** Complete MSc/CSIR NET/State SET conceptual depth with accessible continuous English, real experiment-to-mechanism reasoning, original diagrams and explained questions. No specialist PhD biology, obscure transcriptome inventories, detailed laboratory gene-editing methods or plant breeding protocols.
- **Publication status:** These eleven nodes are **planned lesson titles and summaries**, not finished reading pages. Keep their menu entries nonclickable, with no invented lesson URLs or sitemap updates.
- **Overlap prevention:** Unit 5.B owns embryogenesis/seed germination and male/female gametophyte development; 5.A stem cell, induction and patterning principles; 5.E developmental death and aging; Unit 06 hormones, photoperiod receptors and detailed plant physiology; 4.A general receptor signaling; Unit 08 detailed gene inheritance. Teach only the **developmental function** of these mechanisms in 5.D.

## Eleven complete future textbook-page contracts

### 5.D.01 — Foundations of Plant Organogenesis: Meristems, Axes and Indeterminate Growth

**Student-visible menu description:** Connect the germinating embryo with shoot and root meristems, plant body axes, primary growth, cell differentiation and repeated organ formation.

**Question driving the lesson:** How can a plant continuously generate roots, shoots, leaves and reproductive organs after embryogenesis?

**Complete mandatory internal microtopics and scientific examples:** Distinguish plant organogenesis from animal organ formation, primary apical versus lateral meristems, embryonic establishment of SAM and RAM as brief 5.B context; indeterminate and determinate developmental outcomes, apical–basal and radial axes, growth by patterned cell divisions and enlargement while walls constrain cell movement; primary tissues and stem-cell niches; differences among meristem, primordium, mature tissue and organ; sequence seedling emergence→vegetative axis growth→leaf production→flower meristem; primary versus secondary growth as orientation only.

**NET/SET reasoning, classic experiments, illustrations and worked questions:** Identify meristem/primordium/differentiated organ in diagrams, compare shoot/root and embryonic/postembryonic axes, predict effect of missing apical meristem on continued primary organ initiation.

**No-overlap and scientific-depth boundary:** 5.B.11–13 owns embryo polarity, seed development and germination; detailed cambium/secondary vascular anatomy is background, not a whole new chapter; 5.E owns senescence and developmental cell death.

### 5.D.02 — Shoot Apical Meristem: Zones, Stem-Cell Maintenance and Organ Initiation

**Student-visible menu description:** Explore the shoot apical meristem’s central, peripheral and rib zones and how WUSCHEL–CLAVATA feedback maintains a renewing stem-cell pool.

**Question driving the lesson:** How does the shoot apex keep stem cells while continually supplying new organ primordia?

**Complete mandatory internal microtopics and scientific examples:** Tunica and corpus concepts (L1/L2/L3), central zone, organizing center, peripheral zone and rib zone as complementary descriptions; SAM stem-cell renewal and daughter recruitment into shoot organs; WUSCHEL (WUS) organizing center, CLAVATA3 peptide and CLV receptors as negative feedback, SHOOT MERISTEMLESS/KNOX developmental identity at exam level; meristem size, proliferative domain and differentiating periphery; distinguish meristem maintenance from auxin-induced local initiation at flanks. Arabidopsis wus versus clv meristem phenotypes with their developmental interpretations.

**NET/SET reasoning, classic experiments, illustrations and worked questions:** Label longitudinal SAM diagrams, compare central versus peripheral zones, predict WUS loss, CLV down-regulation, and organ-initiation changes without treating CLV as a general growth promoter.

**No-overlap and scientific-depth boundary:** Generic receptor and peptide signaling belongs to 4.A; full auxin chemistry/hormone biosynthesis Unit 06; shoot organ elongation/branching in 5.D.03 and leaf positioning in 5.D.06.

### 5.D.03 — Shoot Development: Stem Elongation, Nodes, Buds and Branching

**Student-visible menu description:** Follow shoot primordia into stems, nodes, internodes and axillary meristems, connecting apical growth with branch formation and shoot architecture.

**Question driving the lesson:** How does an actively growing shoot produce repeated organs and branching patterns?

**Complete mandatory internal microtopics and scientific examples:** Shoot development from the SAM, nodal versus internodal structure, epidermal/ground/vascular tissue differentiation and coordinated cell division/elongation as conceptual processes; axillary meristem specification in leaf axils and emergence of axillary buds; apical dominance and bud outgrowth as physiologic context with auxin/cytokinin/strigolactone used only as broad integrative signals; branching architecture as outcome of meristem placement and growth; comparison of terminal and axillary meristems, indeterminate primary shoot growth and transitions in architecture; use simple Arabidopsis or grass shoot comparisons with species-specific caveats.

**NET/SET reasoning, classic experiments, illustrations and worked questions:** Interpret shoot anatomy and branch diagrams, predict suppressed axillary meristem effects versus inhibited bud outgrowth, distinguish making a branch meristem from activating an existing bud.

**No-overlap and scientific-depth boundary:** Detailed plant hormonal transport/signaling and tropisms belong to Unit 06; phyllotactic positioning and leaf initiation are 5.D.06, floral transition 5.D.08.

### 5.D.04 — Root Apical Meristem: Quiescent Center, Stem-Cell Niche and Radial Patterning

**Student-visible menu description:** Study the root apical meristem, quiescent center and root-cap initials, including the WOX5, PLETHORA and SHORTROOT–SCARECROW patterning framework.

**Question driving the lesson:** How can roots grow forward while maintaining the stem cells and ordered tissues at the tip?

**Complete mandatory internal microtopics and scientific examples:** RAM structure: root cap/columella, quiescent center, surrounding stem-cell initials, proximal meristem, root apical organization; formative asymmetric divisions and stem-cell niche; WOX5, PLETHORA (PLT) gradients and SHR/SCR signaling as standard functions in QC maintenance/radial development, not specialist gene networks; root zones and auxin gradients as conceptual morphogenic information; contrasting SAM and RAM stem-cell organizing centers (WUS versus WOX5) with caveat their detailed roles are not identical; Arabidopsis root-tip labeling/ablation or simple mutant phenotypes as causal test ideas.

**NET/SET reasoning, classic experiments, illustrations and worked questions:** Label RAM zone/QC/initial diagrams, predict QC loss or perturbed root-cap initials, assign radial layers from initial stem-cell populations and compare SAM/RAM architectures.

**No-overlap and scientific-depth boundary:** 5.D.05 owns tissue differentiation, root hairs and lateral roots; 5.B embryo root polarity and Unit 06 root nutrient transport and hormonal chemistry are separate.

### 5.D.05 — Root Development: Tissue Differentiation, Root Hairs and Lateral Roots

**Student-visible menu description:** Trace primary root elongation and radial tissue formation, then explain root-hair differentiation and pericycle-driven lateral-root initiation.

**Question driving the lesson:** How do stem-cell descendants build an organized root system with specialized tissues and new branches?

**Complete mandatory internal microtopics and scientific examples:** Root division, elongation and maturation zones; epidermis, cortex, endodermis, pericycle and vascular cylinder in roots; ground-tissue formative divisions and functional differentiation; root hair versus nonhair epidermal fates with positional cues as representative context, root-cap cell progression; lateral roots initiated internally from pericycle cells (commonly xylem-pole pericycle in Arabidopsis), early primordium divisions and emergence through parent tissues; auxin maxima and local signal integration for lateral-root positioning; primary versus lateral/adventitious roots, the distinction between root apical growth and branch initiation.

**NET/SET reasoning, classic experiments, illustrations and worked questions:** Identify root zones/tissues, trace the lateral root's pericycle origin, distinguish root hair from lateral root, predict effect of inhibited pericycle primordia or reduced root tip division.

**No-overlap and scientific-depth boundary:** 4.A general signal-transduction mechanisms and Unit 06 root water/nutrient transport and hormonal biosynthesis are prerequisites, not repeated in full.

### 5.D.06 — Leaf Primordium Initiation and Phyllotaxy at the Shoot Apex

**Student-visible menu description:** Explain how localized auxin maxima and PIN transport position new leaf primordia in alternate, opposite, whorled or spiral arrangements.

**Question driving the lesson:** How does a shoot apex choose where the next leaf begins and avoid producing every leaf in the same position?

**Complete mandatory internal microtopics and scientific examples:** Leaf initiation at the SAM peripheral zone, primordium outgrowth and separation from meristem state; localized auxin response maxima and PIN-FORMED1-directed polar transport as positioning cues, inhibitory fields and feedback as explanatory models; phyllotaxy concepts: alternate/distichous, opposite/decussate, whorled and spiral, common divergence angle around 137.5 degrees for some spiral species without asserting universal phyllotaxy; geometric/positional diagrams and plastochron notion at exam level; dynamic phyllotactic patterns across species, limited effects of altered auxin transport.

**NET/SET reasoning, classic experiments, illustrations and worked questions:** Classify leaf arrangements from plant drawings, predict PIN polarity/auxin-maximum disturbance, calculate or interpret divergence angles as model geometry not universal law, distinguish a leaf primordium from a floral primordium.

**No-overlap and scientific-depth boundary:** SAM WUS–CLV stem maintenance is 5.D.02; subsequent leaf polarity/lamina tissue formation 5.D.07; Unit 06 owns broader auxin transport/physiology.

### 5.D.07 — Leaf Development: Polarity, Lamina Growth and Tissue Differentiation

**Student-visible menu description:** Follow leaf primordia through adaxial–abaxial patterning, proximodistal and mediolateral growth, blade expansion and differentiated tissues.

**Question driving the lesson:** How does a tiny primordium become a flat leaf with an upper side, lower side and vascular pattern?

**Complete mandatory internal microtopics and scientific examples:** Leaf shape axes adaxial–abaxial, proximal–distal and medial–lateral; polarity specification from SAM-derived context, simple HD-ZIP III/REVOLUTA, KANADI, AS1/AS2 examples at NET/SET level; blade expansion and margin formation, dorsiventral patterning, compound versus simple leaves as comparisons; epidermis, mesophyll and veins as developmental tissues, vascular continuity/auxin canalization as conceptual model, stomatal lineage only as relevant representative differentiated cell; leaf maturation and heteroblasty examples linked to later phase change, not a full anatomy or photosynthesis course.

**NET/SET reasoning, classic experiments, illustrations and worked questions:** Label leaf axes, infer radialized/abaxialized leaf phenotypes, compare changed leaf-blade growth, connect lamina shape to differential expansion and polarity.

**No-overlap and scientific-depth boundary:** Leaf initiation/phyllotaxy is 5.D.06, Unit 06 owns photosynthesis, transpiration and hormone transport; 5.E senescence/abscission.

### 5.D.08 — Vegetative-to-Reproductive Transition: Developmental Timing of Flowering

**Student-visible menu description:** Understand how developmental age and photoperiod or cold cues converge through FT, SOC1 and other regulators to initiate flowering.

**Question driving the lesson:** How does a vegetative shoot become developmentally competent to form reproductive structures?

**Complete mandatory internal microtopics and scientific examples:** Juvenile versus adult vegetative phase, developmental competence and the switch to reproductive fate; inductive light/photoperiod and vernalization signals as short physiological input concepts, internal age pathway miR156–SPL–miR172 as an outline, flowering repressors such as FLC and integrators FT/florigen and SOC1, representative CONSTANS–FT pathway at exam level; systemic signaling from leaf to shoot apex and developmental reprogramming, seasonal variability and species/photoperiod caveats. Distinguish 'floral transition' from organ identity and formation of a mature flower; frame Arabidopsis as standard model.

**NET/SET reasoning, classic experiments, illustrations and worked questions:** Interpret early/late flowering mutants, predict FT/SOC1 or flowering repressor changes, distinguish an inductive photoperiod signal from irreversible meristem fate change.

**No-overlap and scientific-depth boundary:** Unit 06 sensory photobiology owns phytochrome/cryptochrome light reception and full circadian/photoperiod mechanisms; 5.D.09 owns floral meristem establishment, 5.D.10 organ identities.

### 5.D.09 — Inflorescence and Floral Meristems: Identity, Position and Determinacy

**Student-visible menu description:** Trace the transition from a shoot to inflorescence and floral meristems using LFY, AP1 and TFL1 and their roles in flower initiation.

**Question driving the lesson:** How is a determinate flower-producing meristem specified while an inflorescence meristem may continue growing?

**Complete mandatory internal microtopics and scientific examples:** Vegetative SAM→inflorescence meristem (IM)→floral meristem (FM) sequence in standard model, indeterminate versus determinate meristem fate, architecture of raceme and floral meristem initiation; spatial induction of floral identity by LFY/LEAFY and AP1/APETALA1, CAL and TFL1 functional examples; overlap/feedback between flowering time regulators and meristem identity; loss-of-function lfy/ap1 and tfl1 phenotypes at NET/SET concept level, not unconditional gene-to-one-phenotype claims; floral-meristem size and organ primordium formation as bridge to next chapter; distinguish floral meristem identity genes from ABC organ identity genes.

**NET/SET reasoning, classic experiments, illustrations and worked questions:** Match branching/cauliflower-like inflorescence phenotypes to LFY/AP1/CAL/TFL1 logic, distinguish conversion of shoot meristem to flower from conversion of sepal identity to petal.

**No-overlap and scientific-depth boundary:** 5.D.08 flowering cues, 5.D.10 floral organ identity, full plant flowering physiology Unit 06; no crop horticulture protocols.

### 5.D.10 — Floral Organogenesis: Whorls, ABC/ABCE Identity and Genetic Control

**Student-visible menu description:** Explore sepals, petals, stamens, carpels and the combinatorial ABC/ABCE model that specifies organ identity during flower formation.

**Question driving the lesson:** How can four concentric regions in one flower develop into distinctly different organs?

**Complete mandatory internal microtopics and scientific examples:** Floral meristem organ primordium initiation and whorl positions 1–4, homeotic organ transformations and genetic reasoning, classic ABC model A (sepal), A+B (petal), B+C (stamen), C (carpel), with overlapping domains and A/C antagonism in the classic simplified framework; updated ABCE/E SEPALLATA cofactor requirement and D ovule-identity discussion as limited expansion; typical Arabidopsis A candidates AP1/AP2, B AP3/PI, C AG and E SEP genes with scientifically necessary caveats about AP2 and model limitations; MADS-box floral homeotic transcription factors as a family (AP2 not MADS), the floral quartet principle only at overview level; homeotic identity versus growth or pollen/embryo-sac formation.

**NET/SET reasoning, classic experiments, illustrations and worked questions:** Predict organ-type transformations when A, B or C function is absent, read whorl gene-expression diagrams, separate ABC identity from meristem identity and recognize AP2 exception to MADS classification.

**No-overlap and scientific-depth boundary:** 5.D.11 applies model to real Arabidopsis and Antirrhinum mutants; 5.B owns pollen/ovule gametogenesis and fertilization; no advanced network biochemistry.

### 5.D.11 — Arabidopsis and Antirrhinum: Floral Mutants and Comparative Development

**Student-visible menu description:** Apply organ-identity principles to Arabidopsis APETALA/AGAMOUS and Antirrhinum DEFICIENS/GLOBOSA mutants and compare floral developmental outcomes.

**Question driving the lesson:** How do classical mutants in two different plant species establish that gene combinations specify floral organ identity?

**Complete mandatory internal microtopics and scientific examples:** Why Arabidopsis thaliana and Antirrhinum majus (snapdragon) are classic genetic systems; Arabidopsis APETALA1/APETALA2, APETALA3, PISTILLATA and AGAMOUS (and selected SEP) phenotypes with mutation class, changed organ identity and whorl logic; Antirrhinum DEFICIENS (DEF), GLOBOSA (GLO), PLENA (PLE), FARINELLI (FAR) where appropriate, functional comparison with Arabidopsis B/C genes without pretending strict one-to-one equivalence; landmark floral homeosis, organ identity rescue or ectopic expression as conceptual experiments, use genotype→gene-domain→changed organ→evidence reasoning; contrast a homeotic shift with reduced growth, differentiation arrest and meristem determinacy defects; examples of species-specific differences and modern ABC(E) interpretation.

**NET/SET reasoning, classic experiments, illustrations and worked questions:** Work organ-homeosis mutation vignettes for ap3/pi/AG and DEF/GLO, compare mutant-whorl drawings, explain evidence for conserved B-class functions and avoid claiming every gene has identical functions across flowering plants.

**No-overlap and scientific-depth boundary:** Basic ABC(E) model in 5.D.10, meristem identity in 5.D.09; flowering taxonomy, full genetic engineering protocols and flower breeding are excluded.

## Progressive, interconnected lesson sequence

1. **Plant module and axes (5.D.01):** Move from 5.B seed/embryo to indeterminate primary vegetative organogenesis, defining meristems, developmental axes and differentiating tissues.
2. **Shoot anatomy and function (5.D.02–03):** First identify SAM stem-cell zoning and WUS–CLV regulation, then follow organ output into stem, nodes, internodes and axillary branching.
3. **Root anatomy and function (5.D.04–05):** Contrast the RAM/QC stem-cell organization with the SAM, then follow initials into radial tissue layers, root hairs and internally initiated lateral roots.
4. **Leaves (5.D.06–07):** Understand where leaves form through auxin maxima and phyllotaxy, then how primordia acquire upper/lower identity, lamina and differentiated tissues.
5. **Reproductive transition (5.D.08–09):** Integrate developmental age/environmental flowering cues and florigen in a vegetative apex before establishing inflorescence and floral meristem identity.
6. **Floral organ fate (5.D.10–11):** Assign organs to whorls via ABC/ABCE logic and use real Arabidopsis/Antirrhinum homeotic mutants to test predictions and reveal general/specific functions.

## Scientific accuracy, balance and study-quality controls

- **Meristem terminology:** SAM central/peripheral/rib zones and tunica–corpus layers are distinct but overlapping organizational descriptions, not interchangeable. RAM QC and organizer are not identical to SAM organizing center.
- **WUS–CLV feedback:** WUS maintains SAM stem cells while CLV restricts the WUS domain through negative regulation; simplified exam model should not claim every species uses identical signal wiring.
- **Shoot growth:** Axillary meristem initiation and release of an already formed bud from dormancy/apical dominance are different phenomena.
- **Root architecture:** Lateral roots usually arise endogenously from the pericycle in a model like Arabidopsis, not from individual epidermal root hairs. QC maintenance and mature root differentiation are separate steps.
- **Phyllotaxy:** Polar auxin transport helps place leaf primordia; Fibonacci/137.5-degree spiral geometry is a typical model, not universal for every angiosperm.
- **Leaf development:** Leaf primordium positioning, adaxial–abaxial polarity and lamina growth are different phases. Not every variation in leaf shape indicates defective auxin transport.
- **Floral transition:** FT/florigen and SOC1 are developmentally relevant; full photoperiodic light receptor pathways and hormone production belong to Unit 06.
- **Floral meristem vs organ identity:** LFY/AP1/TFL1 influence inflorescence and floral meristem fate, while AP3/PI/AG and related ABC(E) functions assign whorl identities. Do not equate flowering time, meristem fate and sepal/petal fate.
- **ABC model caveats:** The textbook A+B+C model is a useful idealization. MADS-box TFs include AP1, AP3, PI and AG; AP2 is not a MADS-box factor. E-function SEP cofactors broaden the classic model and organ identity can vary by plant group.
- **Antirrhinum:** DEF and GLO are B-class homologues of Arabidopsis AP3 and PI; PLENA and FARINELLI C-related roles are not universally identical to a single Arabidopsis AGAMOUS function. Avoid false one-to-one equivalence.
