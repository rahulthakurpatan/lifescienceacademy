# Life Science Academy — Unit 06, Subunit 6.D: Plant Hormones

## Official syllabus scope and chapter status

- Revised **UGC–CSIR–DBT Life Sciences & Biotechnology 2026** syllabus, Unit 06.D, printed page 7: https://www.csirhrdg.res.in/SiteContent/ManagedContent/ContentFiles/20260121115025355Merged_Syllabus_CSIR-DBT_21Jan2026.pdf
- Separate CSIR Life Sciences 2026 syllabus: https://www.csirhrdg.res.in/SiteContent/ManagedContent/ContentFiles/20260129163159108LS_Sylbs_29Jan2026.pdf
- MP SET Life Sciences Paper II syllabus: https://cdn-images.prepp.in/public/image/mp-set-paper-2-life-scinces-1760795800.pdf
- **Official 6.D description:** Biosynthesis, storage, breakdown and transport; physiological effects and mechanisms of action. The official syllabus **does not** prescribe a fixed number or titles of chapter nodes.
- **Academy-approved teaching architecture:** 10 progressively linked future textbook chapters, `6.D.01–6.D.10`. One foundations chapter followed by nine major small-molecule hormone classes. Peptide hormones are introduced in the foundation as an important additional signaling class; plant biology has other signal families and this numbering is not a universal taxonomy.
- **Systematic learning order:** Production/transport/perception → local growth (auxin, CK, GA, BR) → architecture/nutrient collaboration (strigolactones) → dormancy and life-stage timing (ABA, ethylene) → protection/cross-talk (JA, SA). This is a **teaching progression**, not a claim that hormones act sequentially in an actual plant. They act simultaneously and influence each other.
- **Standard chapter contract:** Each hormone is taught through source and biosynthesis; physiologically relevant activation, conjugation/storage; degradation; transport and distribution; receptor and signal transduction; growth, development or defense outcomes; classic experiments and NET/SET reasoning. Some hormones (e.g. ethylene gas) have different handling of storage or transport. Do not force one false template onto all classes.
- **Current publication stage:** 2/10 chapters linked on production branch; remaining nodes retain unlinked curriculum entries. GitHub Pages live verification is separate.
- **Anti-duplication:** Unit 4.A owns general signaling machinery, 5.B/5.D plant development, 5.E senescence, 6.E photoreceptor biology, 6.F hydraulics/translocation, 6.G specialized products and 6.H complete plant stress/innate immunity. 6.D owns the specific hormones' synthesis, homeostasis, transport, perception and physiological regulation.

## Ten chapter microtopic and exam contracts

### 6.D.01 — Fundamentals of Plant Hormones: Biosynthesis, Transport and Signaling

**Student menu description:** Establish how plant hormones are synthesized, activated, stored or conjugated, transported, perceived and inactivated, and compare local and long-distance regulation of growth and stress responses.

**Driving scientific question:** How can extremely low hormone concentrations create distinct growth, developmental and defensive outcomes across plant organs?

**Complete required teaching coverage:** Define hormones versus nutrients, primary metabolites and environmental signals; nine well-established small-molecule classes (auxin, cytokinins, GA, BR, SL, ABA, ethylene, JA, SA), with peptide hormones recognized as another important signaling category. Synthesis, bioactive precursors, activation, reversible or irreversible conjugation, catabolism, local versus long-distance movement, diffusion versus transporter-mediated movement, receptor competence, response sensitivity, feedback and cross-talk; introduce intracellular co-receptor and SCF–proteasome repressor degradation, membrane kinase receptors, two-component signaling and kinase–phosphatase switches without teaching every route twice. Distinguish concentration from sensitivity, hormone ratio from fixed phenotype, biosynthetic mutant from perception mutant. Note that ethylene gas is not stored like an auxin/ABA conjugate; no universal transporter operates for every hormone.

**Classic evidence, diagrams and NET/SET worked reasoning:** Trace Darwin/Boysen-Jensen/Went auxin evidence as a preview, hormone gradients/dose curves, tissue-competence assays, receptor vs synthesis mutant application and short- versus long-range signal interpretation.

**Academic overlap boundary:** Hormone chemistry/mechanisms belong here; Unit 4.A generic signaling, Unit 5 plant organogenesis and cell death, 6.E light sensing, 6.F full solute transport and 6.H complete stress physiology are separate.

### 6.D.02 — Auxins: Polar Transport, Cell Elongation and Developmental Regulation

**Student menu description:** Follow indole-3-acetic acid biosynthesis, conjugation and oxidation, PIN/AUX1-directed transport and TIR1/AFB signaling to explain cell elongation, rooting, apical dominance and tropic growth.

**Driving scientific question:** How does spatial redistribution of auxin cause asymmetric growth while the hormone also influences rooting and buds?

**Complete required teaching coverage:** Auxin IAA structure/context, TAA/TAR tryptophan-dependent indole-3-pyruvate and YUCCA oxidation, alternative routes as qualified exceptions, GH3 conjugation, DAO oxidative inactivation, free-versus-conjugated forms and cellular homeostasis. AUX1/LAX influx, PIN polar efflux and ABCB, chemiosmotic model with extracellular pH, polar auxin flux and local maxima. TIR1/AFB–Aux/IAA–ARF co-receptor and ubiquitin-dependent degradation, transcriptional response and rapid mechanisms context. Acid-growth hypothesis (H+-ATPase, expansin, wall loosening and turgor), phototropism/gravitropism, apical dominance with cytokinin and SL cross-talk, lateral/adventitious rooting and dose-dependent differential sensitivity. Distinguish auxin influence on organ positioning from full organ morphogenesis.

**Classic evidence, diagrams and NET/SET worked reasoning:** Darwin/Went coleoptile tests, agar-block polar transport, inhibitors of PIN-mediated transport, YUCCA versus TIR1 receptor phenotype and auxin dose-response diagrams.

**Academic overlap boundary:** 5.D leaf/meristem patterning, 6.E phototropin perception, 6.F water movement, 6.D.06 strigolactone-mediated bud suppression. Do not duplicate whole developmental organ modules.

### 6.D.03 — Cytokinins: Cell Division, Meristem Activity and Organ Formation

**Student menu description:** Trace cytokinin production, activation, transport and CKX degradation through AHK–AHP–ARR signaling, linking it to cell division, shoot initiation, nutrient responses and delayed leaf senescence.

**Driving scientific question:** How does cytokinin combine with auxin to influence tissue proliferation, organ fate and nutrient-regulated growth?

**Complete required teaching coverage:** Adenine-derived iP, trans-zeatin and other cytokinin types; IPT synthesis, side-chain hydroxylation and LOG activation; cytokinin ribosides/ribotides and glycosylation (reversible vs irreversible forms vary); CKX oxidation/catabolism, xylem-mediated root-to-shoot signals and transporters. AHK histidine-kinase receptors, phosphorelay AHP, transcriptional type-B ARR positive regulation and type-A ARR negative feedback; cytokinin versus animal signaling distinctions. Effects on cell division, SAM activity, regeneration in tissue culture, nutrient assimilation/crosstalk, delayed leaf senescence and organ initiation with context sensitivity.

**Classic evidence, diagrams and NET/SET worked reasoning:** Classic Skoog–Miller auxin/cytokinin root–shoot tissue culture ratios, AHK receptor and CKX overexpression mutants, delayed leaf senescence assays, type-A vs type-B ARR interpretation.

**Academic overlap boundary:** 5.D meristem anatomical development, 5.E plant senescence pathways, 6.C nutrient metabolism and Unit 4.A two-component biochemical fundamentals.

### 6.D.04 — Gibberellins: Stem Growth, Seed Germination and DELLA Signaling

**Student menu description:** Follow bioactive gibberellin synthesis and GA2ox-mediated inactivation, GID1–DELLA signaling, stem elongation, seed germination and cereal aleurone enzyme induction.

**Driving scientific question:** How does the removal of GA-responsive repressors release stem growth and mobilization of seed reserves?

**Complete required teaching coverage:** Diterpenoid GGPP→ent-kaurene via CPS/KS, KO/KAO, late GA20ox/GA3ox formation of active GA species and GA2ox inactivation, conjugation/storage and tissue-dependent distribution. GID1 receptor binds bioactive GA, recognizes DELLA, SCF SLY1/GID2 targets DELLA for proteolysis; signaling output is release of growth repression. Stem elongation and dwarf mutants, bolting, some flowering effects, cereal aleurone GA-induced α-amylase production and germination, interactions with ABA seed dormancy and BR/auxin growth, no blanket statement that GA promotes flowering in every species.

**Classic evidence, diagrams and NET/SET worked reasoning:** Dwarf GA biosynthetic versus GID1/DELLA-insensitive mutants and exogenous GA rescue, barley aleurone α-amylase induction, GA–ABA antagonism and seed germination timecourses.

**Academic overlap boundary:** 5.B seed development, 5.D floral transitions, 6.D.07 ABA signaling and Unit 01 starch chemistry remain separate.

### 6.D.05 — Brassinosteroids: Steroid Hormones and Cell Expansion

**Student menu description:** Explain sterol-derived brassinosteroid synthesis and inactivation, BRI1–BAK1 receptor signaling and BIN2–BES1/BZR1 control of cell expansion and vascular differentiation.

**Driving scientific question:** How can a plant steroid hormone act through a plasma-membrane kinase receptor and transcriptional regulators?

**Complete required teaching coverage:** Sterol/campesterol-derived BRs, representative CYP90/CYP85 enzymes and active castasterone/brassinolide forms, local biosynthesis, hormone homeostasis via metabolism/conjugation and transport caveat (not all BRs are classical long-distance transport signals). BRI1 receptor kinase, BAK1/SERK partner, BIN2 kinase inhibition via BSU1-like relay, BES1/BZR1 phosphorylation and nuclear transcription; differences from animal intracellular steroid receptors. Cell expansion, hypocotyl length, vascular differentiation, fertility, photomorphogenesis and interactions with auxin/GA; concentration and organ-dependent growth/defense outcomes.

**Classic evidence, diagrams and NET/SET worked reasoning:** Compare BR-deficient dwarf and BRI1 receptor-impaired dwarf, exogenous BR response vs resistance, BIN2 gain and BES1 phosphorylation, relate phenotype to receptor localization.

**Academic overlap boundary:** 4.A receptor-kinase common biochemistry, 5.D vascular morphogenesis, 6.G specialized steroid biosynthesis and 6.H broad environmental stress.

### 6.D.06 — Strigolactones: Shoot Branching, Root Architecture and Symbiotic Signaling

**Student menu description:** Trace carotenoid-derived strigolactone synthesis and D14–MAX2–SMXL signaling, connecting axillary bud suppression with nutrient-dependent root architecture and mycorrhizal cues.

**Driving scientific question:** How does one carotenoid-derived family adjust plant branches in response to nutrient supply and communicate with soil fungi?

**Complete required teaching coverage:** D27 carotenoid isomerization, CCD7/CCD8 formation of carlactone, MAX1/CYP711A modification and diversity of active SLs, hormone homeostasis, release in root exudates and plant tissue movement not universally mapped. D14 receptor/hydrolase, SCF MAX2/D3 and SMXL/D53 repressors targeted for degradation, selected species-dependent responses. Axillary-bud control, apical dominance interaction with auxin/cytokinin, phosphate deficiency, root morphology and arbuscular mycorrhizal fungal signals, parasitic seed germination as environmental consequence. Explain SL signaling distinguishes fungal mutualism from rhizobial nitrogen fixation.

**Classic evidence, diagrams and NET/SET worked reasoning:** max biosynthetic, d14 receptor and max2 signaling mutant branching phenotypes, hormone application rescue logic, low-phosphate root exudate experiments and D14–MAX2–SMXL pathway drawing.

**Academic overlap boundary:** 5.D axillary meristem initiation, 6.C rhizobial nodules, 6.F phosphate uptake and 6.H wider nutrient stress response are distinct.

### 6.D.07 — Abscisic Acid: Seed Dormancy and Water-Stress Signaling

**Student menu description:** Explain ABA biosynthesis, conjugation, CYP707A catabolism and PYR/PYL–PP2C–SnRK2 signaling, linking dormancy with drought response and stomatal control.

**Driving scientific question:** Why does ABA help a dormant seed delay germination but help mature guard cells conserve water rapidly?

**Complete required teaching coverage:** Carotenoid cleavage of 9-cis-violaxanthin/neoxanthin by NCED in plastids, xanthoxin and later cytosolic synthesis through ABA2/AAO3, ABA glucose-ester conjugation/storage, hydrolysis, CYP707A oxidative catabolism and root–shoot transport/ABCG or NPF examples. PYR/PYL/RCAR receptor blocks PP2C (ABI1/ABI2) inhibition of SnRK2 kinases, responses through AREB/ABF transcription factors and guard-cell anion efflux, K+ movement and turgor loss. Seed desiccation tolerance and dormancy, germination suppression relative to GA, drought signaling and rapid/slow physiological outputs, stress/growth tradeoff without treating ABA as always growth-inhibitory.

**Classic evidence, diagrams and NET/SET worked reasoning:** Analyze nced/aba-deficient versus pyr/pyl insensitive seeds, PP2C gain and SnRK2 mutants, dehydration response and stomatal aperture graphs, ABA/GA dose interactions.

**Academic overlap boundary:** 5.B seed structure/germination morphology; 6.E blue-light stomatal opening, 6.F water potential and 6.H broad drought stress pathways.

### 6.D.08 — Ethylene: Gaseous Signaling, Ripening and Senescence

**Student menu description:** Follow methionine–SAM–ACC-dependent ethylene production and ETR–CTR1–EIN2–EIN3 signaling through the triple response, ripening, abscission and stress responses.

**Driving scientific question:** How can a gaseous hormone influence growth, fruit ripening and organ aging in neighboring cells?

**Complete required teaching coverage:** Methionine–SAM–ACC via ACC synthase ACS and ACC oxidase ACO leading to ethylene, Yang cycle recycling, ACC conjugation/transport as precursor homeostasis, ethylene as freely diffusible gas rather than stored conjugate. ETR receptors are negative regulators of ethylene responses when unbound, CTR1 kinase and downstream EIN2/EIN3/EIL1 signaling, transcriptional defense/growth pathways and adaptive feedback. Etiolated seedling triple response, climacteric fruit ripening and selected non-climacteric caveats, flower senescence, abscission-zone responses, flooding-associated ethylene accumulation and oxygen sensitivity of ACC oxidase, interactions with auxin and JA.

**Classic evidence, diagrams and NET/SET worked reasoning:** Triple-response etr1 vs ctr1/ein2 mutant logic, ethylene receptor blockade via 1-MCP as interpretation only, ACC/ethylene treatment experiments, ripening timecourse and precursor versus gas transport distinction.

**Academic overlap boundary:** 5.E senescence/PCD, 6.H flood stress and plant immunity, 5.D flower organogenesis and 6.D.02 auxin interactions.

### 6.D.09 — Jasmonates: Wound Signaling and Herbivore Defense

**Student menu description:** Trace oxylipin-derived jasmonate production, JA–Ile homeostasis and COI1–JAZ–MYC signaling through wound defense, herbivory responses and growth–defense tradeoffs.

**Driving scientific question:** How does leaf damage activate fatty-acid-derived signaling and transcriptional defense without permanently stopping growth?

**Complete required teaching coverage:** α-linolenic acid→LOX/AOS/AOC→OPDA in chloroplast, OPDA peroxisome reduction and β-oxidation to JA, JAR1 conversion to JA–Ile, JA/JA–Ile breakdown by oxidation/hydroxylation and conjugation and signal spread through tissue with membrane electrical/Ca2+ signals also involved. JA–Ile promotes SCF COI1–JAZ co-receptor assembly, JAZ repressor degradation and MYC-mediated gene activation. Wound response, chewing herbivore defense, plant fertility in some species, induction of proteinase inhibitors and specialized products, defense-growth tradeoff, cooperation/antagonism with SA and ethylene according to pathogen and context.

**Classic evidence, diagrams and NET/SET worked reasoning:** COI1-insensitive or stabilized JAZ repressor phenotypes, JA synthesis-vs-receptor mutant treatments, damage vs hormone timecourse, local-to-distal response graphs and ecological tradeoff scenarios.

**Academic overlap boundary:** 6.H complete PTI/ETI and insect immunity, 6.G defensive secondary metabolites, Unit 4.A general proteolysis signaling.

### 6.D.10 — Salicylic Acid: Defense Signaling and Hormonal Crosstalk

**Student menu description:** Trace salicylic acid biosynthesis, conjugation and NPR-centered defense signaling, then integrate SA–JA, ABA–ethylene and growth–defense hormonal interactions.

**Driving scientific question:** How does salicylate coordinate pathogen defense and communicate with growth and other immune-related hormone pathways?

**Complete required teaching coverage:** SA as phenolic hormone, chorismate/isoc horismate route (ICS1/SID2, EDS5 export, PBS3/EPS1 conversions in Arabidopsis) with appropriate species-specific pathway alternatives, phenylalanine contribution in some species. SA glycosylation/storage (SAG), methyl salicylate MeSA as mobile derivative and oxidative catabolism such as DMR6, free-SA homeostasis. NPR1/NPR3/NPR4 family in SA-regulated defense perception and downstream TGA transcription factor PR gene induction, redox and protein-turnover effects with caution against one invariant direct NPR1 binding model. Systemic acquired resistance (SAR), SA responses to many biotroph-associated infections, how SA vs JA antagonism/cooperation changes with pathogen lifestyle, ABA–ethylene signaling and hormone cross-talk in growth and survival. End comprehensive nine-hormone matrix of origin, homeostasis, transport, receptor, examples and mutant evidence.

**Classic evidence, diagrams and NET/SET worked reasoning:** ICS1-deficient versus npr1 response mutants, PR1 expression and systemic signal tests, MeSA mobile SA evidence, data involving SA–JA interactions that do not enforce universal antagonism, comparative NET/SET case interpretation.

**Academic overlap boundary:** 6.H pathogen PRR/PTI/NLR/ETI and full immune response, 6.G phenylpropanoids, 5.E plant senescence, 5.D development; teach salicylate chemistry, receptor signaling and integrative hormone logic.

## Continuous learning story

1. **6.D.01 Foundations:** A hormone must be synthesized, protected or inactivated, reach responsive tissue, be perceived by its receptor and trigger a regulated output.
2. **6.D.02 Auxin:** Bring that story into a spatial gradient where polar membrane carriers and response proteins create asymmetric tissue expansion.
3. **6.D.03 Cytokinins:** Couple auxin's patterning context with cell division and developmental competence through an alternative two-component-like kinase/phosphorelay mechanism.
4. **6.D.04 Gibberellins:** Remove a DELLA growth repressor to show elongation and germination as a dynamic hormone-regulated balance.
5. **6.D.05 Brassinosteroids:** Contrast a plant steroid acting through a membrane receptor kinase with intracellular auxin and GA repressor pathways.
6. **6.D.06 Strigolactones:** Move from local cell expansion to whole-plant branching and phosphate-responsive rhizosphere signals.
7. **6.D.07 ABA:** Explain dormancy and water conservation through receptor-mediated phosphatase/kinase switching in seed and guard cell.
8. **6.D.08 Ethylene:** Introduce volatile gas perception and how it times seedling responses, ripening and organ senescence.
9. **6.D.09 Jasmonates:** Use wound-derived oxylipins and targeted degradation of JAZ repressors to explain herbivore defense and costs.
10. **6.D.10 Salicylic acid:** Study SA defense regulation, then synthesize growth/defense interactions and limits of one-hormone-one-outcome explanations.

## Nonnegotiable scientific safeguards

- Not every hormone follows the same production, conjugation, breakdown, transport and receptor logic; ethylene is a volatile gas, while its ACC precursor may be transported or conjugated.
- Auxin IAA is a principal regulator, but local concentration and tissue sensitivity strongly affect whether an organ elongates or development changes. PIN localization and distribution matter.
- CK regulation uses multistep AHK–AHP–ARR phosphorelay with type-A vs type-B ARR feedback; cytokinin–auxin tissue culture ratio is useful experimentally but not a deterministic universal organ code.
- GID1–DELLA signaling releases growth repression; not all GAs are bioactive and hormones do not direct every plant flowering response identically.
- BR acts through BRI1/BAK1 receptor kinase modules, not the classic mammalian nuclear steroid receptor mechanism.
- SL forms, perception and roles vary across species; D14–MAX2–SMXL is an established representative signaling module. Mycorrhizal symbiosis is different from rhizobial nitrogen fixation.
- ABA PYR/PYL–PP2C–SnRK2 perception couples slower gene regulation with rapid stomatal ion-channel effects; hormone concentration alone is not a full hydraulic explanation.
- Ethylene is formed by methionine/SAM→ACC→ethylene and should not be said to be stored in the same way as glycosylated ABA/IAA.
- JA–Ile (not every free JA molecule) prominently acts through COI1–JAZ co-receptor biology; SA/NPR regulation is more complex than a single universal 'SA binds NPR1' statement.
- SA–JA antagonism is context dependent, not automatic across all pathogen types. Plant hormone immune signaling here is distinct from detailed PRR/NLR-based plant innate immunity of 6.H.
