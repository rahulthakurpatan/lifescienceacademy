- **Revised UGC–CSIR–DBT NET Life Sciences & Biotechnology, 2026**, Unit **6.E**, printed page 7/PDF index 6: https://www.csirhrdg.res.in/SiteContent/ManagedContent/ContentFiles/20260121115025355Merged_Syllabus_CSIR-DBT_21Jan2026.pdf

- **Official required scope:** “Light perception, structure, function and mechanisms of action of phytochromes, cryptochromes and phototropins, stomatal movement, photoperiodism and biological clock.”
- **Academy-approved breakdown:** Seven independent yet connected future reading nodes `6.E.01–6.E.07` created from the official breadth, **not officially mandated seven subdivisions** and not a mechanical chapter-volume quota.
- **Chronological / causal learning story:** Why light is information → phytochrome red/far-red perception → cryptochrome blue perception → phototropin blue perception for direction/movement → guard-cell stomatal ion movement as integrated output → endogenous timekeeping to track environmental cycles → seasonal photoperiod sensing integrating receptor and oscillator outputs.
- **Scientific chronology caveat:** These receptors and biological clocks operate **simultaneously**, not in a literal evolutionary, cell-biological or seasonal order. The sequence is an educational prerequisite chain; photoperiodic flowering depends on clock timing, so clock precedes photoperiodism in the lessons.
- **Quality target:** substantial MSc/CSIR NET/MP SET-level connected explanatory prose and experiments; realistic diagrams and action spectra; fully reasoned evidence-interpretation and mutant questions. Avoid thin chapters, misleading oversimplification, forced equality of word counts, specialist research-only gene inventories and fake chapter URLs.
- **Current publication type:** 6.E.04 is an authored textbook chapter; its menu link and sitemap entry are included in the publication commit. Remaining nodes are nonclickable until individually published.
- **Cross-unit boundaries:** Photosynthetic energy capture and pigment photoprotection belong to 6.A, hormone biosynthesis and downstream hormone pathways to 6.D, water potential/transpiration to 6.F, whole-plant stresses to 6.H, floral organ identity to 5.D and general receptor/kinase signaling principles to Unit 4.A. Here the focus remains **photoreception, signaling outputs, endogenous timing and photoperiodic integration**.

## Seven complete teaching-node contracts

### 6.E.01 — Foundations of Plant Light Perception: Signals, Spectra and Responses

**Student-facing menu description:** Understand how light quality, intensity, duration and direction become developmental information, and distinguish photoreceptor action spectra from photosynthetic light harvesting.

**Driving biological question:** How can a plant detect the color, direction and timing of light even before it captures any significant energy for photosynthesis?

**Complete required concept, mechanisms and experimental context:** Introduce photons and wavelength-energy qualitative context (red, far-red, blue, UV-A, UV-B), irradiance/intensity, spectral quality and red:far-red ratio, direction and day/night timing; chlorophyll-driven photosynthesis vs light perception and signaling. Explain absorption spectrum vs action spectrum, chromophore, apoprotein, holoprotein and excitation/conformation as concepts; plant phytochromes (red/far-red, bilin), cryptochromes (blue/UV-A, flavin), phototropins (blue, LOV-flavin) and peripheral awareness of UVR8 (UV-B) without adding an off-syllabus UVR8 node. Define photomorphogenesis vs skotomorphogenesis, de-etiolation, hypocotyl inhibition, cotyledon opening, shade avoidance, directional growth, chloroplast positioning, germination and stomatal responses as examples to be assigned to later chapters. Define threshold/fluence, fluence-rate dependence, red/far-red reversibility, low-fluence/high-irradiance responses at overview level; temporal input/output, receptor vs downstream transcription factor, local stimulus vs systemic physiology, model Arabidopsis and seedling classic assays. Show how light as ecological information is fundamentally different from ATP/NADPH generation by light reactions.

**NET/SET practical diagram and explained reasoning targets:** Compare equal-energy red/blue light action spectra, interpret dark-grown vs light-grown seedlings, classify whether photon input acts as energy source or environmental information, use light-pulse/photoreversal experimental design and a wavelength-response plot to infer receptor class.

**Academic overlap boundary and exceptions:** 6.A owns thylakoid photosystems, photosynthetic electron transport and quantum yield; 4.A owns general signal-transduction fundamentals. 6.E.02–04 handle individual receptor biochemistry, 6.E.05 stomatal output, 6.E.06 circadian timing and 6.E.07 photoperiodism.

### 6.E.02 — Phytochromes: Red/Far-Red Perception, Photoconversion and Developmental Signaling

**Student-facing menu description:** Trace phytochrome Pr–Pfr photoconversion, bilin chromophores, PHY–PIF nuclear signaling and red/far-red responses in germination, de-etiolation and shade avoidance.

**Driving biological question:** How can a seedling use red and far-red wavelengths to infer whether it is in sunlight, shade or darkness?

**Complete required concept, mechanisms and experimental context:** Phytochrome photoreceptor apoprotein domains and covalently associated linear tetrapyrrole/phytochromobilin chromophore, functional dimer and N-terminal photosensory module vs C-terminal signaling domains as exam-level structure; red-absorbing Pr and far-red-absorbing Pfr, photoreversibility with environmental constraints, Pfr normally a signaling-active state in seed plants but note phyA far-red high-irradiance and context-specific responses, dark reversion and phytochrome photostationary state. Phytochrome family members phyA light-labile/type I and phyB and other type II forms (Arabidopsis model), red/far-red pulse logic and seed-germination examples, red:far-red effects in canopy, shade-avoidance elongation. Pfr nuclear accumulation, interaction with PHYTOCHROME INTERACTING FACTORs (PIF transcription regulators) and degradation/inactivation in relevant pathways, coordination with COP1/SPA and HY5 photomorphogenesis as overview, interconnection with auxin growth output but don't reteach auxin biosynthesis; distinguish light-sensing phytochrome from chlorophyll reaction centers and physiological outcome from receptor conversion.

**NET/SET practical diagram and explained reasoning targets:** Classic lettuce-seed red/far-red reversal and last-light quality experiment with dose/saturation caveat; predict phyA vs phyB mutant phenotype qualitatively, interpret PIF stability time series, shade R:FR graphs and red versus far-red treatments.

**Academic overlap boundary and exceptions:** 6.E.01 defines action spectra and light input, 6.E.03 cryptochrome blue-light signaling, 6.E.06 oscillator light entrainment, 6.E.07 flowering photoperiodic output; 6.D auxin biosynthesis/signaling itself, 5.D floral developmental organ identity.

### 6.E.03 — Cryptochromes: Blue-Light Sensing and Photomorphogenesis

**Student-facing menu description:** Explore flavin-containing CRY photoreceptor structure, blue-light activation and COP1–SPA/HY5-linked signaling that influences seedling development and biological timing.

**Driving biological question:** How do plant cells detect blue light and use the information to change transcription, seedling shape and flowering-related timing?

**Complete required concept, mechanisms and experimental context:** CRY family origin as flavoproteins and photolyase-homologous N-terminal PHR domain with FAD chromophore (and antenna cofactor considerations), plant CRY1/CRY2 and C-terminal extension/regulatory region; blue/UV-A light perception and photochemical/conformational activation, cryptochrome dimerization/oligomerization in representative models and nucleus/cytosol cellular dynamics as needed. Blue-light inhibition of seedling hypocotyl elongation, de-etiolation and cotyledon growth; light-dependent changes in COP1–SPA ubiquitin-mediated repressor activity, HY5 stability as an integrator, selected CRY2–CIB/CO flowering context without retelling full photoperiodic output. Distinguish cryptochrome from phototropin: both are blue-light receptors but cryptochromes primarily inform developmental transcriptional processes whereas phototropins are LOV-kinases strongly linked to directional movement; neither group produces chloroplast photosynthetic NADPH. Clarify photolyase-related fold does not mean all plant cryptochromes are DNA-repair photolyases; species and light-dose mechanisms differ.

**NET/SET practical diagram and explained reasoning targets:** Interpret cry1/cry2 loss-of-function hypocotyl length under blue vs red light, compare protein HY5 accumulation after blue irradiation, discriminate COP1 signaling from actual receptor photon absorption, draw FAD/PHR–regulatory domain map, explain CRY vs PHOT double-receptor response.

**Academic overlap boundary and exceptions:** 6.E.02 phytochrome red/far-red mechanisms, 6.E.04 phototropin movement and kinase signaling, 6.E.06–07 timing/photoperiodic integration. Unit 03 DNA repair photolyases are not this chapter's main biology.

### 6.E.04 — Phototropins: LOV Kinase Signaling, Phototropism and Chloroplast Movement

**Student-facing menu description:** Explain phototropin LOV1/LOV2 flavin photochemistry, kinase activation, NPH3/PIN-dependent directional auxin responses and light-controlled chloroplast positioning.

**Driving biological question:** How does a one-sided blue light beam lead to directional bending, and why do chloroplasts change location as light intensity rises?

**Complete required concept, mechanisms and experimental context:** Phototropin phot1 and phot2 blue-light sensing by N-terminal LOV1 and LOV2 domains binding FMN, light-induced LOV2 conformational change/autophosphorylation activating C-terminal serine/threonine protein kinase; distinction between receptor excitation and downstream phosphorylation. Spatial/temporal photoactivation and phototropism through NPH3/RPT2 family signaling and auxin redistribution via PIN transport, differential shoot hypocotyl/coleoptile growth (link to 6.D.02), directional blue-light low-vs-high intensity effects, phot1 versus phot2 partial redundancy/species-specific sensitivity. Chloroplast accumulation response in weak light and avoidance response in high-intensity light with phot2 prominently mediating avoidance in classic Arabidopsis, phototropin-regulated cytoskeletal/CHUP1 interactions at overview level, leaf positioning and maximizing photon interception vs damage. Mention phototropin also perceives blue light in guard cells but guard-cell H+-ATPase, BLUS1/CBC and osmotic mechanism belong to 6.E.05. Distinguish phototropism (directional growth), photonasty (nondirectional movement), circadian periodicity and chloroplast intracellular relocation.

**NET/SET practical diagram and explained reasoning targets:** Darwin coleoptile tip/directional illumination history in conjunction with 6.E.01; phot1/phot2 mutant bending and chloroplast positioning graphs, compare red vs blue beam and weak vs strong blue light, interpret NPH3 vs PIN perturbations, diagram LOV1/LOV2–kinase domain arrangement.

**Academic overlap boundary and exceptions:** 6.D.02 owns auxin production and receptor transcription pathway; 6.E.05 owns stomatal mechanics; 6.A.05 xanthophyll photoprotection is a different non-phototropin protection mechanism; Unit 2 cytoskeletal cell machinery is background.

### 6.E.05 — Light-Regulated Stomatal Movement: Guard Cells, Ion Flux and Turgor

**Student-facing menu description:** Follow guard-cell blue-light sensing via phototropin and BLUS1 to H+-ATPase activation, membrane ion flux, osmotic water entry and stomatal opening, contrasted with closure signals.

**Driving biological question:** How do guard cells convert blue light into a reversible pore opening while balancing CO2 entry against water loss?

**Complete required concept, mechanisms and experimental context:** Stomata as paired guard-cell pores with subsidiary cells in many species, guard-cell shape/wall mechanics and reversible turgor control; photosynthetic mesophyll demand for CO2 and water-cost context, blue-light-specific response vs red-light/photosynthesis-mediated stomatal opening. Guard cell phot1/phot2 receptors, BLUS1 and associated kinase signaling, 14-3-3 regulatory interactions, plasma-membrane H+-ATPase phosphorylation/activation, membrane hyperpolarization, K+ influx with counterions malate/Cl− and osmotically driven water uptake producing opening; light-to-dark transition and K+/anion outward movement for closure, differences between passive water flow vs active ion flux; ABA–PYR/PYL/SnRK2 and SLAC1-type anion activation as concise counterexample for closure, CO2, vapor-pressure deficit/drought, temperature and circadian modulation, no universal 'all light opens all stomata' claim. Discuss guard cell chloroplasts and red-light effects without reducing them to phototropins alone, stomatal conductance vs transpiration as linked but distinct physiological measures; species-specific stomatal behaviors and CAM nocturnal opening as exception cross-reference.

**NET/SET practical diagram and explained reasoning targets:** Map phototropin→BLUS1→H+-ATPase→ion gradient→water influx; reason response to H+-ATPase inhibition, blue-light removal and K+-channel inhibition, compare blue versus red light under CO2-controlled conditions, interpret aperture and conductance timecourses and ABA closure mutants.

**Academic overlap boundary and exceptions:** 6.E.04 owns phototropin LOV structure and phototropism, 6.D.07 full ABA hormone biosynthesis/receptor cascade, 6.F transpiration/water-potential hydraulics, 6.A C3/C4/CAM photosynthesis and 6.H drought stress/immune stomatal defense.

### 6.E.06 — Plant Circadian Clock: Entrainment, Feedback Loops and Daily Rhythms

**Student-facing menu description:** Explain self-sustaining near-24-hour rhythms, light/temperature entrainment and Arabidopsis CCA1–LHY, PRR/TOC1 and ELF3–ELF4–LUX oscillator circuitry.

**Driving biological question:** Why can leaf and gene-expression rhythms persist in constant conditions and still synchronize to local day–night cycles?

**Complete required concept, mechanisms and experimental context:** Definitions period, phase, amplitude, rhythm, endogenous/free-running circadian oscillator and approximate 24-hour period; contrast directly light-driven diurnal response from persistent rhythms under constant light/dark and phase shifts after a zeitgeber cue. Entrainment by dawn/dusk light or temperature, PRC (phase response curve) as NET level interpretation, clock input/output and tissue-specific oscillator communication; in Arabidopsis core morning CCA1/LHY transcription factors, sequential PSEUDO-RESPONSE REGULATORS PRR9/PRR7/PRR5 and evening PRR1/TOC1, evening complex ELF3–ELF4–LUX, interconnected repression/activation loops and timed protein turnover. Avoid simplistic single positive TOC1→CCA1 activation as an invariable model; photoreceptor PHY and CRY pathways influence entrainment without being the molecular clock itself, clock influence on photosynthesis, stomatal conductance, growth, hormone response, leaf movement and flowering readiness. Use luciferase promoter reporters, leaf movement, constant-condition rhythms and mutant short/long-period experiments. Chronobiological integration sets the molecular clock prerequisite for photoperiodism in 6.E.07.

**NET/SET practical diagram and explained reasoning targets:** Interpret free-running rhythmic traces and entrainment/phase shift, compare a purely light-activated gene to CCA1-LUC self-sustaining rhythms, predict cca1/lhy/TOC1 perturbations conceptually, draw morning→day→evening oscillator timeline without asserting every gene regulates all outputs identically.

**Academic overlap boundary and exceptions:** 6.E.07 day-length flowering external-coincidence mechanism, 6.E.02–03 specific receptor photoactivation, 6.D hormonal effects and 6.F full stomatal water economics. This is not a universal chronobiology course across animals.

### 6.E.07 — Photoperiodism: Day-Length Sensing, CO–FT Signaling and Flowering Time

**Student-facing menu description:** Connect circadian phase with light/dark duration and CONSTANS–FT signaling to explain long-day and short-day flowering, night-break responses and systemic floral induction.

**Driving biological question:** How can an Arabidopsis or rice plant use day length as seasonal information and flower at an appropriate time of year?

**Complete required concept, mechanisms and experimental context:** Photoperiodism as response to relative light–dark cycle duration, long-day, short-day and day-neutral plant categories as species- and developmental-stage-dependent, critical photoperiod and critical uninterrupted night in classic short-day studies, night-break red/far-red reversible responses in suitable systems and why 'short day' can correspond to long dark period. Garner–Allard photoperiod experimental logic and night-interruption tests; phytochrome red/far-red photoreception and circadian phase from 6.E.02/6.E.06, external coincidence model (clock-controlled sensitivity overlaps light), distinguish clock phase from total daily photosynthetic energy. Arabidopsis long-day model: photoreceptor input, GIGANTEA (GI) and FKF1 regulation of CDF repressors, CONSTANS (CO) accumulation/stability dependent on timing/light, CO-dependent FT activation in leaf companion/vascular-associated tissue, FT (florigen) protein movement via phloem to shoot apex and interaction with FD to trigger flowering-related gene expression; cover at exam depth without giving a false simple 'CO always active at night' rule. Rice short-day models Hd1/Hd3a/RFT1 and species-specific sign differences as comparative context, not rigid one-to-one Arabidopsis template; distinguish photoperiodic flowering signal/FT transport from floral-meristem patterning and ABC organ identity. Integrate dawn/dusk shifts, night-break, seasonality and flowering mutant interpretation with exceptions (temperature/vernalization/age can alter timing).

**NET/SET practical diagram and explained reasoning targets:** Classify plants by flowering under long/short days, calculate night duration from time schedules, interpret classic red and far-red night-break reversal, read CO/FT transcript/protein timing graphs and predict co/ft/gi or altered circadian phase flowering in Arabidopsis, compare rice short-day response without extrapolating one gene universally.

**Academic overlap boundary and exceptions:** 5.D.08–11 owns floral meristem identity, organ development and flower homeotic mutants; 6.E.06 owns the detailed oscillator feedback loops, 6.D GA/other hormone effects, 6.F general phloem mass flow. This chapter owns integration of photoreceptor, clock, external coincidence, leaf FT and reproductive timing.

## Learning continuity and lesson hand-offs

1. **6.E.01 → 6.E.02:** Light is defined as information, not only as photosynthetic energy. The first photoreceptor family demonstrates photochemical switching by red and far-red light, governing seedlings' germination/shape.
2. **6.E.02 → 6.E.03:** Blue-light cryptochromes add a structurally distinct FAD sensor and changes to photomorphogenic gene expression; compare with bilin-dependent phytochrome signaling rather than treating all 'light receptors' as photosystems.
3. **6.E.03 → 6.E.04:** Phototropins share blue-light reception but employ FMN LOV domains and a kinase to direct growth and chloroplast repositioning, contrasting with cryptochrome development-related transcription.
4. **6.E.04 → 6.E.05:** Guard-cell phototropin activation becomes an example of a measurable physiological output: H+-ATPase activation, ion uptake, water movement and reversible aperture changes, modified by CO2, ABA and circadian cues.
5. **6.E.05 → 6.E.06:** A guard-cell or leaf response can also have daily timing. Introduce autonomous oscillator feedback loops and entrainment, distinguishing direct responses to the current light from rhythms continuing in constant conditions.
6. **6.E.06 → 6.E.07:** An internally timed sensitivity window interacts with daily light/dark duration; CO and FT turn day-length information into a mobile flowering signal. Biological clocks and seasonal photoperiod responses are connected but **not synonyms**.

## Mandatory scientific verification and guardrails

- **Photosynthesis vs sensory photobiology:** Reaction-center chlorophyll in 6.A converts photon energy to redox chemistry; phytochrome, cryptochrome and phototropin turn light information into development, movement or timing signals.
- **Wavelength specificity:** Phytochromes chiefly perceive red and far-red, cryptochromes and phototropins blue/UV-A (with class-specific differences), UVR8 perceives UV-B. Photoreceptor spectra may overlap, so not one receptor per exclusive wavelength.
- **Phytochrome:** Pr and Pfr are spectrally interconvertible states; Pfr usually mediates many physiological effects, but phyA far-red responses are not adequately described by a universal 'red=Pfr active, far-red=everything off' rule.
- **Phytochrome signaling:** PIF interactions, nuclear localization and repression/degradation in appropriate light contexts, with other transcriptional regulators; no fictitious hormone biosynthesis or strict one-step mechanism.
- **Cryptochrome:** CRY photolyase-homologous domain and FAD are not proof the plant CRY directly repairs DNA in the manner of photolyases. COP1–SPA/HY5 and CRY2 flowering-related outputs are appropriately distinguished.
- **Phototropin:** Phot1/phot2 are FMN-binding LOV-domain receptor kinases, not flavin DNA photolyases; NPH3 and auxin transport affect bending, chloroplast movement can be controlled by phototropin and actin.
- **Stomatal movement:** Guard-cell phototropin/BLUS1/H+-ATPase can stimulate opening under blue light, but stomata also respond to photosynthetic red light, CO2, humidity/drought, ABA and circadian timing. Dark closure or ABA closure is not simply the H+-ATPase pathway played backward with no extra signals.
- **Water and ion direction:** Opening requires guard-cell membrane hyperpolarization and solute accumulation followed by osmotic water entry; distinguish membrane flux from stomatal pore movement, with appropriate cell physiology and species dependence.
- **Circadian clock:** Oscillations may persist under constant conditions with period approximately 24 h; circadian oscillator, photoreceptor inputs, entrainment and clock-controlled outputs are distinct. CCA1/LHY morning, PRRs daytime/evening and ELF3/ELF4/LUX evening complex comprise connected feedback loops, not a single unchanged activation circuit.
- **Photoperiodism:** Long-day and short-day are relative flowering classes, not guaranteed every species; many short-day plants respond to **night duration**, and night interruption can alter response. Light timing plus clock phase matters, not simply total daily photosynthetic carbon gain.
- **FT/florigen:** For Arabidopsis long-day induction, CO-dependent FT is often expressed in leaf vascular tissues, and FT protein can travel to shoot apex; full floral-meristem initiation and flower-whorl organ identity are in 5.D. Rice and other short-day crops differ in photoperiodic mechanisms.
- **No duplication:** Reuse prerequisite concepts briefly, while comprehensive auxin mechanism remains 6.D.02, ABA mechanism 6.D.07, transpiration and water potential 6.F, C3/C4/CAM stomatal strategies 6.A.08, and flowering-meristem patterning 5.D.
