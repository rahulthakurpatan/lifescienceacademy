- Official scope: biosynthesis and roles of terpenes, phenolics, alkaloids, phenylpropanoids and nitrogenous compounds; metabolic engineering in plants.
- Ten Academy learning nodes, not official syllabus numbering. Equal conceptual workload rather than identical word counts. Sequence: primary metabolic precursors → terpene backbone → terpene diversification → aromatic precursors → phenylpropanoid branches → flavonoids and tannins → alkaloids → other nitrogenous compounds → regulation/ecological integration → pathway engineering. This is prerequisite order; plant pathways coexist and branch.
- Publish student-facing titles and descriptions only; no fabricated lesson links, sitemap entries or changes to published lesson counters. Each future chapter must meet the editorial quality gate, with connected accessible English, mechanisms, evidence, original diagrams, explained NET/SET reasoning and boundary checks. No specialist laboratory procedures.
- Prerequisites: 6.A–6.C carbon and nitrogen assimilation, 6.F allocation, Unit 1 biochemical foundations. Boundaries: 6.D hormone action, 6.H integrated stress and innate immunity, Unit 13 engineering methods. Avoid duplicating these.

## Node contracts

### 6.G.01 — Foundations of Plant Specialized Metabolism

**Student-facing description:** Connect photosynthetic carbon and assimilated nitrogen with specialized metabolite classification, precursor supply and plant chemical diversity.

**Core scope:** Primary versus specialized metabolism; shared precursors acetyl-CoA, pyruvate, glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate, PEP, erythrose-4-phosphate, malonyl-CoA and amino acids; carbon/nitrogen allocation; biosynthetic versus structural classification, pathway branching and evolutionary diversification. Distinguish essential functions from historical terminology.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Construct precursor-to-class map; interpret conceptual isotope labeling and classify overlapping compound families.

**Progression:** Build from 6.A–6.C and 6.F. Prepare 6.G.02.

### 6.G.02 — Biosynthesis of Terpenes: MVA and MEP Pathways

**Student-facing description:** Trace cytosolic mevalonate and plastidial MEP/DOXP pathways to IPP and DMAPP, then explain prenyl diphosphate formation and terpene carbon skeletons.

**Core scope:** MVA from acetyl-CoA via HMG-CoA and HMGR; MEP from pyruvate and glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate via DXS, DXR and downstream intermediates to IPP/DMAPP; energy and reducing equivalents, IPP isomerase; GPP, FPP, GGPP and carbon accounting; compartmentation and possible cross-talk.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare precursor labeling, carbon counts and consequences of conceptual pathway inhibition; map compartment-specific biosynthesis.

**Progression:** Build on 6.G.01; cross-reference shared precursors as needed. Prepare 6.G.03.

### 6.G.03 — Terpenoid Diversity and Biological Functions

**Student-facing description:** Follow prenyl precursors into volatile mono- and sesquiterpenes, diterpenes, triterpenes, sterols, saponins, carotenoids and polyterpenes, linking their structures to plant functions.

**Core scope:** Hemiterpenes C5, monoterpenes C10, sesquiterpenes C15, diterpenes C20, sesterterpenes C25 (brief), triterpenes C30, tetraterpenes C40, polyterpenes; terpene synthases, cyclization, oxidation and glycosylation; limonene, menthol, resin, squalene, phytosterols, saponins, carotenoids and natural rubber; volatile signals, defense, pigmentation and photoprotection. Cross-link hormone derivatives to 6.D, not full hormone pathways.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Predict class from carbon skeleton, compare GPP/FPP/GGPP products and explain diversity from shared precursors.

**Progression:** Build on 6.G.02; cross-reference shared precursors as needed. Prepare 6.G.04.

### 6.G.04 — Shikimate Pathway and Biosynthetic Origins of Phenolics

**Student-facing description:** Trace shikimate-derived aromatic amino acids and acetate–malonate contributions to plant phenolics, distinguishing their biosynthetic origins and chemical properties.

**Core scope:** PEP and erythrose-4-phosphate through DAHP, dehydroquinate, dehydroshikimate, shikimate, shikimate-3-phosphate, EPSP and chorismate; phenylalanine, tyrosine and tryptophan branches; phenolic hydroxyl groups, simple phenols, benzoic acids, gallic acid and acetate–malonate/polyketide contribution; distinguish phenolic class from phenylpropanoid origin; bridge to 6.G.05 and 6.G.06.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace aromatic precursor fate, reason through chorismate branch changes and compare shikimate and malonate contributions.

**Progression:** Build on 6.G.03; cross-reference shared precursors as needed. Prepare 6.G.05.

### 6.G.05 — Phenylpropanoid Biosynthesis and Structural Phenolics

**Student-facing description:** Follow phenylalanine through PAL, C4H and 4CL to hydroxycinnamates, lignin, lignans, coumarins and stilbenes, linking pathway branches with plant structure and defense.

**Core scope:** Phenylalanine deamination, cinnamate, p-coumarate, p-coumaroyl-CoA; hydroxycinnamate modification, monolignols and oxidative lignification, H/G/S units, lignans versus lignin, coumarins, stilbene synthase and resveratrol; cell-wall reinforcement, water transport, antimicrobial roles; branch competition with flavonoids.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Draw pathway branch map; explain altered lignin composition and distinguish structural polymers from soluble phenolics.

**Progression:** Build on 6.G.04; cross-reference shared precursors as needed. Prepare 6.G.06.

### 6.G.06 — Flavonoids, Anthocyanins and Tannins

**Student-facing description:** Trace chalcone formation into flavonoid branches and anthocyanins, then compare condensed and hydrolysable tannins in pigmentation, protection and defense.

**Core scope:** Chalcone synthase uses p-coumaroyl-CoA plus malonyl-CoA; CHI, flavones, flavonols, flavanones, isoflavonoids, anthocyanidins versus anthocyanins, glycosylation, vacuolar pH and pigment variation; proanthocyanidins and hydrolysable gallotannins/ellagitannins from gallic acid; UV screening, pollinator attraction, protein binding, defense and antioxidant context.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret pigment mutants and pH effects; compare tannin origins and explain competition at phenylpropanoid branch points.

**Progression:** Build on 6.G.05; cross-reference shared precursors as needed. Prepare 6.G.07.

### 6.G.07 — Alkaloids: Classification, Biosynthesis and Functions

**Student-facing description:** Explain the origins of alkaloid nitrogen and representative biosynthetic families, distinguishing true alkaloids, protoalkaloids and pseudoalkaloids and their biological functions.

**Core scope:** Amino acid-derived families from ornithine/arginine, lysine, tyrosine and tryptophan; representative tropane, pyridine, quinolizidine, benzylisoquinoline and monoterpene indole alkaloids; purine-derived caffeine as a distinct example; broad pathway logic of decarboxylation, condensation, methylation and oxidation without procedural production instructions; classification caveats, localization, defense and ecological effects.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Match precursors to structural families; explain why nitrogenous compounds are not all alkaloids and compare mixed terpenoid–amino acid origins.

**Progression:** Build on 6.G.06; cross-reference shared precursors as needed. Prepare 6.G.08.

### 6.G.08 — Other Nitrogenous Specialized Metabolites

**Student-facing description:** Explore cyanogenic glycosides, glucosinolates, non-protein amino acids and betalains, relating their biosynthetic origins to plant defense and pigmentation.

**Core scope:** Amino acid origins and broad biosynthetic logic of cyanogenic glycosides and glucosinolates; compartmentalized activation by endogenous hydrolases, conceptual defense roles; sulfur-containing glucosinolates and myrosinase, representative non-protein amino acids such as canavanine, tyrosine-derived betalains and their distinction from anthocyanins; selected specialized peptides as brief classification context, not generic protein synthesis.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare nitrogen and sulfur allocation, distinguish glycosides from alkaloids, explain why separation of defensive compounds and enzymes matters.

**Progression:** Build on 6.G.07; cross-reference shared precursors as needed. Prepare 6.G.09.

### 6.G.09 — Regulation and Ecological Roles of Specialized Metabolites

**Student-facing description:** Connect tissue-specific biosynthesis, storage, transport and environmental regulation with constitutive and induced defenses, signaling, allelopathy and ecological interactions.

**Core scope:** Trichomes, resin ducts, vacuoles, transport and glycosylation; enzyme activity, transcription factors and feedback; light, development, nutrient status and stress regulation; phytoanticipins versus phytoalexins, herbivore and pathogen defense, volatile signaling, pollination/seed dispersal, rhizosphere interactions and allelopathy with evidence caveats; growth–defense allocation. Cross-link JA/SA to 6.D and immunity to 6.H.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret tissue and induction data; compare constitutive versus induced defenses and distinguish ecological evidence from correlation.

**Progression:** Build on 6.G.08; cross-reference shared precursors as needed. Prepare 6.G.10.

### 6.G.10 — Metabolic Engineering in Plants

**Student-facing description:** Apply pathway knowledge to plant metabolic engineering through precursor supply, enzyme regulation, branch competition and product accumulation, using Golden Rice and pigment engineering as examples.

**Core scope:** Define flux versus concentration, bottlenecks, competing pathways, rate-limiting steps and feedback; conceptual overexpression, suppression, gene editing, tissue-specific expression and organelle targeting; carotenoid biofortification in Golden Rice and flavonoid/pigment modification; evaluate yield, fitness, stability and unintended effects; distinguish plant engineering from microbial production; leave molecular protocols to biotechnology units.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret simple flux diagrams, predict effects of altered enzymes and evaluate engineering tradeoffs, rather than laboratory instructions.

**Progression:** Build on 6.G.09; cross-reference shared precursors as needed. Bridge to 6.H stress physiology and later biotechnology.
