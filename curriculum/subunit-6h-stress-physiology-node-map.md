## Node contracts

### 6.H.01 — Foundations of Plant Stress Physiology

**Student-facing description:** Distinguish abiotic and biotic stress, physiological injury and homeostatic responses, then compare avoidance, escape, tolerance, resistance, acclimation and evolutionary adaptation.

**Core scope:** Stress intensity, duration, developmental stage and genotype; primary and secondary injury; reversible stress and irreversible damage; escape, avoidance, tolerance and resistance; acclimation versus heritable adaptation; growth, yield, survival and recovery.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret dose–response curves and recovery data.

**Progression:** Integrate 6.A–6.G. Prepare 6.H.02.

### 6.H.02 — Stress Perception and Molecular Signaling

**Student-facing description:** Follow environmental perturbations through cellular sensing, calcium, ROS and kinase signaling to transcriptional reprogramming and hormonal coordination.

**Core scope:** Membrane and osmotic perturbations, mechanosensitive and osmotic sensing; calcium signatures, calmodulin, CPK and CBL–CIPK, MAPK cascades, ROS and nitric oxide; ABA-dependent and independent responses; DREB, NAC and WRKY regulators, local and systemic signals; no universal stress receptor.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret time courses and signaling mutant phenotypes.

**Progression:** Build on 6.H.01 and earlier relevant mechanisms. Prepare 6.H.03.

### 6.H.03 — Oxidative Stress and Cellular Protection

**Student-facing description:** Explain reactive oxygen species production, oxidative injury and signaling, then trace enzymatic and nonenzymatic antioxidant systems that preserve cellular redox balance.

**Core scope:** Superoxide, hydrogen peroxide, hydroxyl radical and singlet oxygen; chloroplast, mitochondrion, peroxisome and RBOH sources; lipid peroxidation, protein and DNA damage; SOD, catalase, APX, ascorbate–glutathione cycle, GR, DHAR, MDHAR; carotenoids, tocopherols, glutathione, repair and ROS signaling.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Map ROS conversion and interpret antioxidant activity, electrolyte leakage and fluorescence data.

**Progression:** Build on 6.H.02 and earlier relevant mechanisms. Prepare 6.H.04.

### 6.H.04 — Drought and Dehydration Stress

**Student-facing description:** Connect soil water deficit with plant water potential, ABA-mediated stomatal responses, osmotic adjustment, protective proteins and drought avoidance and tolerance.

**Core scope:** Soil versus atmospheric drought, hydraulic and chemical signals; ABA–PYL/PP2C/SnRK2/SLAC1, DREB, stomatal and photosynthesis tradeoffs; root allocation, aquaporins, proline, sugars, glycine betaine, LEA proteins and dehydrins; desiccation tolerance as a special case; recovery and hydraulic limitations. Cross-link 6.D and 6.F.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare relative water content, osmotic potential and gas exchange; distinguish active adjustment from passive concentration.

**Progression:** Build on 6.H.03 and earlier relevant mechanisms. Prepare 6.H.05.

### 6.H.05 — Salinity and Ionic Stress

**Student-facing description:** Separate osmotic and ionic components of salt stress, then explain sodium exclusion, potassium homeostasis, SOS signaling, vacuolar compartmentation and salt tolerance.

**Core scope:** Osmotic effects and ion accumulation, Na⁺/Cl⁻ toxicity, K⁺ competition; SOS3/CBL4–SOS2/CIPK24–SOS1, HKT-mediated sodium retrieval in appropriate species, NHX exchangers and proton gradients, K⁺ and pH regulation; osmolytes, halophytes, glycophytes, salt glands; saline versus sodic soils.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret Na⁺/K⁺ data and compare salt with iso-osmotic nonionic stress.

**Progression:** Build on 6.H.04 and earlier relevant mechanisms. Prepare 6.H.06.

### 6.H.06 — Heat, Chilling and Freezing Stress

**Student-facing description:** Compare heat-induced protein and membrane injury with chilling and freezing damage, then explain heat-shock responses, cold acclimation and cellular protection.

**Core scope:** Heat effects on proteins, membranes, photosynthesis and reproduction; HSF–HSP chaperones and acquired thermotolerance; chilling versus freezing, extracellular ice and dehydration, membrane unsaturation; ICE/CBF/DREB1–COR framework, dehydrins, sugars and antifreeze proteins in selected plants; acclimation and deacclimation.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare heat pretreatment, cold acclimation, electrolyte leakage and photosynthetic performance.

**Progression:** Build on 6.H.05 and earlier relevant mechanisms. Prepare 6.H.07.

### 6.H.07 — Flooding, Hypoxia and Other Abiotic Stresses

**Student-facing description:** Trace oxygen limitation during waterlogging and submergence, then compare adaptations to nutrient imbalance, toxic metals and excess radiation.

**Core scope:** Waterlogging versus submergence, hypoxia/anoxia, ATP limitation, glycolysis and ethanol fermentation, ADH/PDC, ethylene, aerenchyma and adventitious roots; rice SUB1A quiescence versus escape, oxygen-dependent ERF-VII stability, reoxygenation ROS; nutrient deficiency and toxicity, soil pH, aluminum and cadmium stress, phytochelatins and sequestration; excess light and UV-B with cross-links to 6.A and 6.E.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare flooding and reoxygenation data, fermentation and adaptation strategies.

**Progression:** Build on 6.H.06 and earlier relevant mechanisms. Prepare 6.H.08.

### 6.H.08 — Biotic Stress and Plant Defense Barriers

**Student-facing description:** Compare major plant attackers and infection strategies, then examine preformed physical and chemical barriers and inducible local defenses.

**Core scope:** Bacterial, fungal, oomycete, viral and nematode threats; biotrophs, necrotrophs and hemibiotrophs, compatible/incompatible interactions, host/nonhost resistance; disease triangle; cuticle, wall, trichomes, callose, lignification, PR proteins, chitinases, glucanases, phytoalexins versus phytoanticipins; antiviral RNA silencing as distinct defense; link 6.G and 4.D.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare attacker lifestyles and barrier-deficient mutants; distinguish resistance from tolerance.

**Progression:** Build on 6.H.07 and earlier relevant mechanisms. Prepare 6.H.09.

### 6.H.09 — Plant Innate Immunity: Pattern-Triggered Immunity

**Student-facing description:** Follow microbial and damage-associated pattern perception by cell-surface receptors through early signaling to stomatal defense, callose deposition and antimicrobial gene expression.

**Core scope:** PAMP/MAMP and DAMP, PRRs, RLKs and RLPs; FLS2–flg22–BAK1, LysM-mediated chitin perception; BIK1, calcium, RBOHD, ROS burst, MAPKs, transcription, callose and stomatal responses; PTI as interacting immune layer; no animal-style adaptive immunity.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret elicitor response and receptor mutant data.

**Progression:** Build on 6.H.08 and earlier relevant mechanisms. Prepare 6.H.10.

### 6.H.10 — Effector-Triggered Immunity and Disease Resistance

**Student-facing description:** Explain pathogen effector action, intracellular NLR-mediated recognition, gene-for-gene relationships and hypersensitive responses, then connect PTI and ETI as cooperating immune mechanisms.

**Core scope:** Effector suppression of defense conceptually; Flor gene-for-gene model, R/avr, direct and indirect recognition, guard/decoy, NLR/NB-LRR domains and CNL/TNL examples; hypersensitive response is not universal and may benefit necrotrophs; historical zig-zag model versus reciprocal PTI–ETI reinforcement; resistance tradeoffs.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret R/avr compatibility matrices and distinguish cell death from effective resistance.

**Progression:** Build on 6.H.09 and earlier relevant mechanisms. Prepare 6.H.11.

### 6.H.11 — Herbivory, Wounding and Insect Resistance

**Student-facing description:** Trace wound and herbivore-associated signals through jasmonate-centered responses to physical, chemical and indirect defenses against insects.

**Core scope:** Chewing and piercing-sucking herbivores, DAMPs and HAMPs, calcium and electrical signals; JA-Ile–COI1–JAZ–MYC, SA/ethylene context; spines, trichomes, protease inhibitors, defensive metabolites and herbivore-induced volatiles, natural enemies; systemic wound defense, tolerance and compensatory growth.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare wounding and herbivory treatments, jasmonate mutants and direct versus indirect defense.

**Progression:** Build on 6.H.10 and earlier relevant mechanisms. Prepare 6.H.12.

### 6.H.12 — Systemic Resistance and Integrated Stress Adaptation

**Student-facing description:** Connect local defense to systemic acquired and induced systemic resistance, then integrate hormonal cross-talk, defense priming and combined-stress responses with growth and recovery.

**Core scope:** SAR, SA/NPR1, PR expression, pipecolic acid and N-hydroxypipecolic acid; ISR by beneficial root microbes, often JA/ethylene dependent with exceptions; distal signals, priming, SA–JA–ET–ABA cross-talk, combined stress, growth–defense tradeoffs, stress memory and limits of transgenerational claims; integrate 6.A–6.G.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare local/distal challenge, SAR/ISR, primed response curves and factorial combined-stress evidence.

**Progression:** Build on 6.H.11 and earlier relevant mechanisms. Conclude Unit 6 by integrating whole-plant physiology.
