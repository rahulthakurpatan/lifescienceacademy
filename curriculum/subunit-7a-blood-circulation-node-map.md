## Eight node contracts

### 7.A.01 — Blood Composition and Plasma Physiology

**Student-facing description:** Explore blood as a specialised connective tissue, distinguishing plasma, serum and formed elements. Explain haematocrit, plasma proteins, buffering, transport and colloid osmotic pressure in maintaining internal stability.

**Core scope:** Blood composition and functions; physical properties, viscosity, pH and osmolality; plasma water, electrolytes, nutrients, metabolites and proteins; albumin, globulins and fibrinogen, colloid osmotic pressure, buffering and transport; serum versus plasma, haematocrit, relative plasma and cell fractions; comparative examples including nucleated vertebrate erythrocytes and invertebrate haemolymph without conflating blood and haemolymph.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret centrifuged blood, haematocrit changes and plasma-protein effects on fluid distribution; distinguish measured percentages from absolute cell numbers.

**Progression:** Establish the composition of the circulating tissue. Prepare 7.A.02.

### 7.A.02 — Haematopoiesis and Blood Cell Differentiation

**Student-facing description:** Trace blood cell formation from haematopoietic stem cells through myeloid and lymphoid differentiation, connecting bone marrow niches and growth factors with regulated production of erythrocytes, leukocytes and platelets.

**Core scope:** Embryonic and adult sites of haematopoiesis; stem-cell self-renewal and differentiation; conventional myeloid/lymphoid lineage framework with caveat that real differentiation is not strictly binary; erythroid, granulocytic, monocytic, lymphoid and megakaryocytic maturation; marrow microenvironment; erythropoietin, thrombopoietin, colony-stimulating factors and feedback; reticulocytes and basic lineage markers as explanatory context.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret lineage trees and blood cell counts after altered growth-factor signals; connect hypoxia and erythropoietin without repeating renal or respiratory chapters.

**Progression:** Build on 7.A.01 and earlier relevant concepts. Prepare 7.A.03.

### 7.A.03 — Erythrocytes and Haemoglobin

**Student-facing description:** Connect erythrocyte structure, membrane mechanics, metabolism and life cycle with haem and globin synthesis, cooperative oxygen binding, functional haemoglobin variants and haemoglobin recycling.

**Core scope:** Biconcave shape, deformability, spectrin/ankyrin cytoskeleton, mature mammalian erythrocyte organelle loss; anaerobic glycolysis, pentose phosphate pathway and redox protection, 2,3-BPG; erythropoiesis and iron availability, erythrocyte ageing and splenic removal; haem synthesis overview, iron insertion and globin assembly, adult HbA and fetal HbF, tetrameric structure, T/R states, cooperativity and allostery, methemoglobin and representative HbS mechanism; haem breakdown, bilirubin and iron recycling; brief oxygen-affinity determinants, reserve gas exchange and full dissociation-curve analysis for 7.C.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Explain structure-function relationships, consequences of enzyme or haemoglobin changes and reticulocyte responses; interpret simple binding/cooperativity examples without duplicating 7.C.

**Progression:** Build on 7.A.02 and earlier relevant concepts. Prepare 7.A.04.

### 7.A.04 — Leukocytes and Blood-Mediated Immunity

**Student-facing description:** Compare circulating granulocytes and agranulocytes, tracing leukocyte recruitment, phagocytosis and inflammatory responses alongside antibody- and complement-mediated protection in blood.

**Core scope:** Neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils, monocytes and lymphocyte classes; morphology, counts and physiological roles; leukocyte rolling, adhesion, diapedesis and chemotaxis; phagocytosis and antimicrobial mechanisms; inflammatory recruitment and circulating humoral protection, complement overview; distinguish circulating cells from tissue-resident macrophages and mast cells; primary immune concepts only, detailed antigen recognition, adaptive responses and complement pathways reserved for Unit 4.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret differential counts and recruitment/adhesion defects; connect cell types with function without diagnostic or therapeutic protocols.

**Progression:** Build on 7.A.03 and earlier relevant concepts. Prepare 7.A.05.

### 7.A.05 — Platelets and Primary Haemostasis

**Student-facing description:** Follow platelet production and vascular injury through vasoconstriction, platelet adhesion, activation and aggregation, explaining the formation and regulation of the initial haemostatic plug.

**Core scope:** Megakaryocyte-derived platelets and granules; intact endothelium and antiplatelet signals including NO and prostacyclin; vascular constriction; exposed collagen and von Willebrand factor, GPIb-mediated adhesion, GPVI signaling, ADP and thromboxane A2, integrin alphaIIb beta3 (GPIIb/IIIa), fibrinogen bridging and platelet aggregation; primary versus secondary haemostasis; representative von Willebrand disease and platelet-function defects at mechanism level.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret platelet count versus function and adhesion versus aggregation experiments; explain how primary plug formation prepares a surface for coagulation.

**Progression:** Build on 7.A.04 and earlier relevant concepts. Prepare 7.A.06.

### 7.A.06 — Blood Coagulation and Fibrinolysis

**Student-facing description:** Trace clotting-factor activation through the classical intrinsic, extrinsic and common pathways and the cell-based model of coagulation, then explain fibrin stabilisation, endogenous anticoagulation and fibrinolysis.

**Core scope:** Tissue factor–VIIa initiation, intrinsic/contact pathway as a classical laboratory framework, IXa–VIIIa and Xa–Va complexes, prothrombin/thrombin, fibrinogen/fibrin and factor XIII crosslinking; Ca²⁺, phospholipids and vitamin K-dependent factors; initiation, amplification and propagation in vivo; clot retraction, antithrombin, protein C/S and TFPI; tPA, plasminogen, plasmin, PAI-1 and fibrin degradation; PT and aPTT as conceptual pathway tests; haemophilia and thrombosis as illustrative imbalance, not treatment.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare classical pathways with cell-based coagulation, predict factor-deficiency test patterns and distinguish clot formation from clot breakdown.

**Progression:** Build on 7.A.05 and earlier relevant concepts. Prepare 7.A.07.

### 7.A.07 — Blood Groups and Immunohaematology

**Student-facing description:** Explain ABO and Rh blood group antigens, inheritance and antigen–antibody interactions, then connect agglutination, compatibility, cross-matching and haemolytic reactions with blood transfusion and pregnancy.

**Core scope:** ABO alleles, codominance, H antigen and Bombay phenotype, natural anti-A/anti-B antibodies; RhD antigen and sensitization, maternal IgG and haemolytic disease of fetus/newborn; agglutination versus haemolysis; forward/reverse grouping, compatibility principles, red-cell versus plasma transfusion distinction, cross-matching and transfusion reaction mechanisms; other blood group systems only as brief context; avoid unqualified universal donor/recipient claims and clinical procedures.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Solve ABO inheritance and compatibility problems, predict agglutination patterns and explain Rh sensitization.

**Progression:** Build on 7.A.06 and earlier relevant concepts. Prepare 7.A.08.

### 7.A.08 — Blood Volume and Its Regulation

**Student-facing description:** Link plasma volume, red cell mass and body-fluid compartments with blood volume measurement, capillary fluid exchange and hormonal feedback, explaining compensatory responses that preserve circulatory homeostasis.

**Core scope:** Total blood volume, plasma volume and red cell volume, body-fluid compartments; indicator dilution and haematocrit-based estimates with assumptions; capillary hydrostatic/oncotic forces, endothelial glycocalyx and lymphatic return, avoid treating sustained venous reabsorption as universal; effective circulating volume versus osmolality, baroreceptors and osmoreceptors; RAAS, ADH, ANP, thirst and renal sodium/water handling as integrative overview; acute haemorrhage, fluid shifts, longer-term erythropoietin response; cross-link 7.B cardiovascular regulation and 7.F renal mechanisms.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate simple indicator-dilution volumes and interpret changes in haematocrit, fluid distribution and compensatory feedback after blood or water loss.

**Progression:** Build on 7.A.07 and earlier relevant concepts. Bridge to 7.B cardiovascular physiology.
