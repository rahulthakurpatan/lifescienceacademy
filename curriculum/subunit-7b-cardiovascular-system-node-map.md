## Eight node contracts

### 7.B.01 — Comparative Anatomy and Evolution of the Cardiovascular System

**Student-facing description:** Compare open and closed circulation and the organisation of invertebrate and vertebrate hearts, tracing single and double circulatory circuits, chambers, valves and coronary blood supply.

**Core scope:** Open versus closed circulation, haemolymph versus blood, representative arthropod, molluscan and annelid circulations including cephalopod exceptions; fish single circulation, amphibian and reptilian patterns including crocodilian four-chambered hearts and shunts, avian and mammalian double circulation; pulmonary and systemic circuits, atria, ventricles, septa, atrioventricular and semilunar valves, coronary circulation; structural variation as functional adaptation, not an inevitable evolutionary ladder.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare pressure and oxygenation across circuits; trace blood through representative hearts and explain the functional significance of separation and shunting.

**Progression:** Build from blood physiology in 7.A. Prepare 7.B.02.

### 7.B.02 — Myogenic Heart and Cardiac Electrophysiology

**Student-facing description:** Trace heartbeat generation from cardiac muscle and pacemaker cells through the sinoatrial node, atrioventricular node and His–Purkinje system, connecting electrical excitation with calcium-dependent contraction.

**Core scope:** Myogenic versus neurogenic hearts with comparative examples and exceptions; cardiomyocytes, intercalated discs, gap junctions and functional syncytium; SA node, AV delay, bundle of His, bundle branches and Purkinje fibres; pacemaker funny current, calcium and potassium currents; fast-response action potential, sodium upstroke, calcium plateau and repolarisation; refractoriness and prevention of tetanus; calcium-induced calcium release, troponin and relaxation, autonomic modulation as bridge to 7.B.07.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare nodal and ventricular action-potential traces, predict conduction delay and ion-channel perturbation effects, relate excitation–contraction coupling to pumping.

**Progression:** Build on 7.B.01 and earlier relevant concepts. Prepare 7.B.03.

### 7.B.03 — Electrocardiography: Principles and Physiological Significance

**Student-facing description:** Explain how cardiac depolarisation and repolarisation generate surface electrical potentials, then interpret ECG leads, waves, intervals, electrical axes and representative rhythm or conduction changes.

**Core scope:** Surface potentials and dipole/vector projections, limb leads I, II, III and Einthoven relation; augmented and precordial leads at conceptual level; P wave, PR interval, QRS complex, ST segment, T wave, QT interval; calibration, rate and rhythm calculations, electrical axis concept; sinus rhythm, bradycardia, tachycardia and representative AV block as physiology examples; distinguish electrical activity from mechanical events and avoid clinical diagnostic claims.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate rate and intervals from calibrated traces, apply Einthoven relation, predict wave polarity from vector direction and connect ECG timing to the cardiac cycle.

**Progression:** Build on 7.B.02 and earlier relevant concepts. Prepare 7.B.04.

### 7.B.04 — Cardiac Cycle and Pressure–Volume Relationships

**Student-facing description:** Follow atrial and ventricular systole and diastole through pressure gradients, valve movements, heart sounds and changing chamber volumes using Wiggers diagrams and pressure–volume loops.

**Core scope:** Ventricular filling, atrial systole, isovolumetric contraction, ejection, isovolumetric relaxation; pressure relations in atrium, ventricle and aorta; valve opening/closure, S1 and S2, physiological context of S3/S4; ECG alignment and timing, Wiggers diagram, ventricular pressure–volume loop, EDV, ESV, stroke volume and stroke work; compare right and left ventricular pressure patterns; effects of rate and altered filling or ejection as bridge to pump function.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Identify phases from pressure and volume traces, calculate stroke volume and explain valve timing, heart sounds and loop area.

**Progression:** Build on 7.B.03 and earlier relevant concepts. Prepare 7.B.05.

### 7.B.05 — Heart as a Pump and Cardiac Output

**Student-facing description:** Connect ventricular filling and contraction with stroke volume, ejection fraction and cardiac output, explaining preload, afterload, contractility, Frank–Starling behaviour, venous return and the Fick principle.

**Core scope:** Cardiac output = heart rate × stroke volume, stroke volume = EDV − ESV, ejection fraction; determinants of preload, afterload and intrinsic contractility; Frank–Starling length-dependent activation versus inotropic changes; venous return, skeletal muscle and respiratory pumps, mean systemic filling pressure as conceptual context, cardiac and venous-return curves and steady-state equality; Fick principle using oxygen uptake and arterial–venous oxygen content difference; cardiac reserve and limiting effects of excessive rate.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate CO, EF and Fick-derived flow with consistent units; interpret Starling curves and pressure–volume shifts; distinguish changes in preload, afterload and contractility.

**Progression:** Build on 7.B.04 and earlier relevant concepts. Prepare 7.B.06.

### 7.B.06 — Vascular Physiology and Haemodynamics

**Student-facing description:** Trace blood flow through arteries, arterioles, capillaries and veins, relating pressure gradients, vascular resistance, compliance, flow velocity and microcirculation to tissue perfusion.

**Core scope:** Elastic and muscular arteries, arteriolar resistance, capillary exchange and venous capacitance; flow = pressure difference/resistance; Poiseuille relation and radius-fourth-power dependence under ideal assumptions; series and parallel resistance, velocity versus total cross-sectional area, laminar/turbulent flow and qualitative Reynolds reasoning, viscosity and nonideal blood behaviour; compliance and Windkessel function; microcirculation, capillary exchange, glycocalyx and lymphatic return with cross-link to 7.A.08; local regulation as bridge to 7.B.08.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Solve resistance, flow, velocity and compliance problems; interpret pressure profiles and vascular changes; identify limitations of ideal tube-flow models.

**Progression:** Build on 7.B.05 and earlier relevant concepts. Prepare 7.B.07.

### 7.B.07 — Blood Pressure and Neural Regulation

**Student-facing description:** Explain systolic, diastolic, pulse and mean arterial pressure and their measurement, then trace baroreceptor, chemoreceptor and autonomic reflexes through cardiovascular control centres.

**Core scope:** Arterial pressure determinants, MAP approximation at ordinary resting rates and its limitations, pulse pressure, CO and systemic vascular resistance; direct versus indirect measurement concepts, cuff and Korotkoff sounds; carotid sinus and aortic arch baroreceptors, afferents via IX/X, nucleus tractus solitarius and medullary control, sympathetic and parasympathetic efferents, beta1 and alpha1 effects, vagal muscarinic effects; peripheral chemoreflex and cardiopulmonary receptors; short-term regulation, orthostatic response and reflex resetting; avoid clinical prescribing.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate pulse pressure and approximate MAP, predict reflex responses to pressure and posture changes, distinguish afferent detection from efferent action.

**Progression:** Build on 7.B.06 and earlier relevant concepts. Prepare 7.B.08.

### 7.B.08 — Chemical Regulation and Integrated Cardiovascular Homeostasis

**Student-facing description:** Integrate endocrine, endothelial and local metabolic control of circulation, explaining autoregulation and cardiovascular adjustments during exercise, posture changes and blood loss.

**Core scope:** Circulating catecholamines, receptor-dependent effects; renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system, ADH/vasopressin and ANP in volume and pressure control, with renal details reserved for 7.F; endothelial NO, prostacyclin and endothelin; local metabolites, myogenic autoregulation, active and reactive hyperaemia, tissue-specific perfusion including coronary circulation; integration with baroreflex and venous return in exercise, standing and haemorrhage; short- versus long-term feedback, compensatory limits and illustrative hypertension physiology without clinical management.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret perfusion and autoregulation curves, compare local and systemic regulation, integrate feedback responses across exercise and volume loss.

**Progression:** Build on 7.B.07 and earlier relevant concepts. Bridge to 7.C respiratory physiology and systemic oxygen supply.
