## Eight node contracts

### 7.C.01 — Foundations and Comparative Physiology of Respiration

**Student-facing description:** Compare respiratory surfaces and gas exchange across animals, from cutaneous and tracheal respiration to book lungs, fish gills and the pulmonary systems of amphibians, reptiles and birds.

**Core scope:** Distinguish cellular respiration from organismal gas exchange, external and internal respiration, surface area, diffusion distance and gradients, aquatic versus aerial oxygen availability; direct diffusion, cutaneous respiration, insect tracheae and spiracles, arachnid book lungs, fish gill lamellae, buccal–opercular ventilation and countercurrent exchange; amphibian skin and positive-pressure buccal pumping, reptilian lung ventilation, avian parabronchi, air sacs, unidirectional airflow and crosscurrent exchange, including the distinction between ventilatory air sacs and exchange surfaces; compare mammals as transition to 7.C.02. Avoid presenting comparative diversity as a linear evolutionary ladder.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret countercurrent versus concurrent exchange diagrams, compare costs and benefits of water and air ventilation, trace bird airflow over two breathing cycles.

**Progression:** Build on blood and circulation in 7.A and 7.B. Prepare 7.C.02.

### 7.C.02 — Mammalian Respiratory Anatomy and Functional Organisation

**Student-facing description:** Follow airflow through conducting and respiratory zones to the alveoli, connecting lung and pleural anatomy, pneumocytes, surfactant, the blood–air barrier and pulmonary circulation with gas exchange.

**Core scope:** Upper and lower respiratory tract, conducting versus respiratory zones, trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, respiratory bronchioles, alveolar ducts and alveoli; mucociliary clearance and airway protection, alveolar macrophages; lung lobes and pleural membranes, pleural fluid and mechanical coupling; type I pneumocytes, type II pneumocytes, surfactant production and alveolar stability; blood–air barrier, capillary network, pulmonary versus bronchial circulation and low-pressure pulmonary flow. Structure–function relationships rather than exhaustive clinical anatomy.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace airflow and blood flow, compare conducting and exchange epithelia, explain how barrier thickness and surfactant support function; bridge to mechanics in 7.C.03.

**Progression:** Build on 7.C.01 and relevant earlier mechanisms. Prepare 7.C.03.

### 7.C.03 — Mechanics of Breathing and Pulmonary Ventilation

**Student-facing description:** Explain inspiration and expiration through respiratory muscle activity, pressure–volume relationships, lung compliance, elastic recoil, surface tension and airway resistance.

**Core scope:** Quiet and forced breathing; diaphragm and intercostal muscles, accessory muscles in context; atmospheric, alveolar and intrapleural pressure, transpulmonary pressure, Boyle's law and assumptions, pressure changes over a respiratory cycle; lung and chest-wall compliance, elastic recoil, pressure–volume curves and hysteresis, surface tension and Laplace relation as an idealised model with limitations, surfactant and alveolar stability; airway resistance, radius and flow, dynamic compression concept, work of breathing, representative obstructive and restrictive mechanical patterns, without clinical diagnosis.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret pressure-time and compliance curves, calculate simple pressure–volume and resistance relationships, predict effects of altered surfactant or airway calibre.

**Progression:** Build on 7.C.02 and relevant earlier mechanisms. Prepare 7.C.04.

### 7.C.04 — Lung Volumes, Capacities and Respiratory Measurements

**Student-facing description:** Measure and interpret lung volumes and capacities, spirometric patterns, dead space, minute ventilation and alveolar ventilation to explain how breathing frequency and depth affect effective ventilation.

**Core scope:** Tidal volume, inspiratory and expiratory reserve volumes, residual volume, inspiratory capacity, functional residual capacity, vital capacity and total lung capacity; spirometry and its inability to directly measure residual-volume-containing capacities, gas dilution and body plethysmography principles; forced vital capacity and FEV1/FVC, reference-dependent interpretation and distinction between suspected restriction and confirmed low TLC; anatomical, alveolar and physiological dead space, minute and alveolar ventilation equations, classical Bohr equation with assumptions and distinction from Enghoff approximation; respiratory frequency versus depth and representative flow-volume loops.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate capacities, minute and alveolar ventilation and dead-space fraction from stated data, interpret spirometry and compare rapid shallow with slower deeper breathing.

**Progression:** Build on 7.C.03 and relevant earlier mechanisms. Prepare 7.C.05.

### 7.C.05 — Gas Exchange and Ventilation–Perfusion Relationships

**Student-facing description:** Trace alveolar and tissue gas exchange through partial-pressure gradients, gas solubility and diffusion, then explain diffusion capacity, ventilation–perfusion matching, dead space and shunts.

**Core scope:** Dalton's law, humidified inspired gas and partial pressures, Henry's law and solubility, Fick's diffusion law, respiratory membrane surface area and thickness; alveolar gas composition and simplified alveolar gas equation with assumptions; pulmonary and systemic tissue exchange gradients, diffusion capacity and transfer limitation, perfusion versus diffusion limitation; regional V/Q differences, hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction, low and high V/Q, limiting cases of shunt and dead space, physiological shunts, conceptual alveolar–arterial gradient. Keep haemoglobin chemistry in 7.A and transport in 7.C.06–07.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Use gas laws and diffusion relationships with units; interpret V/Q diagrams, partial-pressure tables and diffusion/perfusion limitation scenarios.

**Progression:** Build on 7.C.04 and relevant earlier mechanisms. Prepare 7.C.06.

### 7.C.06 — Oxygen Transport and Respiratory Pigments

**Student-facing description:** Explain oxygen carriage by respiratory pigments, focusing on haemoglobin saturation, oxygen content, dissociation curves, P50, the Bohr effect and factors that regulate oxygen delivery to tissues.

**Core scope:** Dissolved versus haemoglobin-bound oxygen, saturation versus content versus partial pressure; oxygen content equation and unit assumptions, cooperative sigmoid dissociation curve, P50, effects of pH, CO2, temperature and 2,3-BPG, Bohr effect; HbF affinity, myoglobin oxygen storage and hyperbolic binding, comparative haemocyanin and other representative respiratory pigments including haemerythrin and chlorocruorin; oxygen delivery as cardiac output times arterial oxygen content, extraction and tissue unloading; differentiate low oxygen content from low arterial PO2 with illustrative anaemia and hypoxaemia; avoid repeating haem synthesis and globin structure from 7.A.03.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret right/left shifts and P50, compute oxygen content and delivery, compare pigment-binding curves and explain effects of altered haemoglobin concentration.

**Progression:** Build on 7.C.05 and relevant earlier mechanisms. Prepare 7.C.07.

### 7.C.07 — Carbon Dioxide Transport, Waste Elimination and Acid–Base Balance

**Student-facing description:** Trace carbon dioxide carriage as dissolved gas, bicarbonate and carbamino compounds, explaining carbonic anhydrase, chloride shift, the Haldane effect, pulmonary elimination and respiratory acid–base balance.

**Core scope:** Metabolic CO2 production and tissue uptake, dissolved CO2, carbaminohaemoglobin and bicarbonate, carbonic anhydrase, intracellular buffering and AE1-mediated chloride shift; reverse reactions in lungs, Haldane effect distinguished from Bohr effect; alveolar ventilation–PaCO2 relationship at steady CO2 production, exhaled CO2 and water vapour as respiratory waste losses; bicarbonate buffer, Henderson–Hasselbalch equation with defined units, respiratory acidosis and alkalosis as physiological changes, acute versus compensated responses in outline; renal compensation cross-reference to 7.F, nitrogenous waste elimination belongs to excretory physiology.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare CO2 carriage in oxygenated and deoxygenated blood, calculate simple pH and ventilation relationships, interpret acid–base patterns without clinical treatment protocols.

**Progression:** Build on 7.C.06 and relevant earlier mechanisms. Prepare 7.C.08.

### 7.C.08 — Neural and Chemical Regulation of Respiration

**Student-facing description:** Connect medullary and pontine respiratory networks with central and peripheral chemoreceptors, pulmonary reflexes and feedback control, integrating respiratory adjustments during exercise, sleep and altitude exposure.

**Core scope:** Rhythm generation including pre-Bötzinger complex, medullary and pontine network coordination, motor output via phrenic and intercostal nerves; central chemoreception of CO2-related brain extracellular pH, carotid and aortic bodies and peripheral responses to arterial PO2, CO2 and pH; Hering–Breuer inflation reflex and airway protective reflexes, voluntary and behavioural modulation; ventilatory response curves, negative feedback, exercise hyperpnoea, sleep effects, acute altitude hyperventilation and acclimatization overview, distinguish respiratory control from detailed altitude stress adaptation in 7.H; integration with cardiovascular oxygen delivery.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret chemoreceptor and ventilation response graphs, predict feedback after changing CO2 or oxygen, compare exercise and altitude responses and integrate ventilation, gas exchange and tissue delivery.

**Progression:** Build on 7.C.07 and relevant earlier mechanisms. Bridge to 7.D nervous system and 7.F acid–base homeostasis.
