## Ten node contracts

### 7.D.01 — Foundations of Nervous System and Cellular Organisation

**Student-facing description:** Explore comparative nervous system organisation, neuronal structure and classification, neuroglia, myelination and axonal transport to establish the cellular basis of neural communication.

**Core scope:** Diffuse nerve nets, ganglia and centralisation in representative animals; CNS and PNS overview; neuronal polarity, soma, dendrites, axon hillock, initial segment, terminals; sensory neurons, interneurons, motor neurons, morphological classes; astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, Schwann cells, microglia, ependymal and satellite cells; myelin, nodes of Ranvier, anterograde and retrograde axonal transport, kinesin and dynein; structure–function relationships.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare neuron and glial cell roles, trace axonal cargo transport, relate myelin and neuronal geometry to communication.

**Progression:** Build on membrane physiology and the neural regulation encountered in 7.B and 7.C. Prepare 7.D.02.

### 7.D.02 — Resting Membrane Potential and Neuronal Excitability

**Student-facing description:** Explain how ion gradients and selective membrane permeability establish resting potential, using the Nernst and Goldman equations to connect electrochemical forces with graded neuronal responses.

**Core scope:** Intracellular/extracellular ion distributions, membrane capacitance, electrochemical gradients, equilibrium and reversal potential; Nernst equation with valence and temperature, Goldman–Hodgkin–Katz voltage equation with assumptions and chloride orientation; K+ leak and other resting conductances, electrogenic Na+/K+ ATPase and gradient maintenance, distinction between pump and immediate voltage generation; driving force, graded potentials, electrotonic spread, qualitative resistance and capacitance, threshold as transition.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate equilibrium and resting potentials with units and sign conventions, predict changes in extracellular K+ and permeability, interpret graded responses.

**Progression:** Build on 7.D.01 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.D.03.

### 7.D.03 — Action Potential and Nerve Impulse Conduction

**Student-facing description:** Trace threshold-dependent action potential generation through voltage-gated channel activity and refractory periods, then compare continuous and saltatory conduction and the determinants of propagation speed.

**Core scope:** Threshold and regenerative activation, Na+ activation/inactivation and delayed K+ conductance, depolarisation, repolarisation and afterhyperpolarisation; absolute and relative refractory periods, all-or-none firing, rate coding; squid giant axon and voltage-clamp evidence, Hodgkin–Huxley conductance model conceptually, patch clamp as complementary channel evidence; local circuit currents, axon diameter, myelin, saltatory conduction, temperature and demyelination as mechanism examples; distinguish action potential amplitude from firing frequency.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret voltage-clamp and action-potential traces, predict channel-block effects at conceptual level, compare conduction speed and refractory effects.

**Progression:** Build on 7.D.02 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.D.04.

### 7.D.04 — Synaptic Transmission and Neural Integration

**Student-facing description:** Compare electrical and chemical synapses, tracing transmitter release and receptor activation through excitatory and inhibitory postsynaptic potentials, summation and basic synaptic plasticity.

**Core scope:** Gap junctions and electrical synapses; presynaptic Ca2+ entry, vesicle docking and SNARE-mediated fusion, quantal release, neurotransmitter classes, ionotropic and metabotropic receptors, termination by uptake, diffusion or degradation; EPSPs and IPSPs, reversal potential, shunting inhibition, temporal and spatial summation, integration at axon initial segment, presynaptic inhibition, short-term facilitation/depression and basic LTP/LTD as physiological context. Avoid duplicating Unit 4 general signalling and vesicular trafficking.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret postsynaptic traces and miniature events, distinguish excitation from depolarisation alone, reason about summation, release probability and receptor changes.

**Progression:** Build on 7.D.03 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.D.05.

### 7.D.05 — Gross Neuroanatomy and Functional Organisation of the Brain

**Student-facing description:** Map the forebrain, midbrain and hindbrain to their principal functions, relating cerebral cortex, thalamus, hypothalamus, basal ganglia, cerebellum and brainstem to protection and integration.

**Core scope:** Major brain divisions and cerebral hemispheres, cortical lobes and functional regions, white matter and corpus callosum, thalamic relay and hypothalamic homeostasis, basal ganglia, limbic structures, midbrain, pons, medulla and cerebellum; meninges, ventricles, CSF production and circulation, blood–brain barrier and neurovascular support; gross anatomy and functional relationships, not exhaustive clinical nuclei or lesion localisation; motor circuits developed in 7.D.10.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Identify structures on labelled sections and infer broad functional consequences of disrupted pathways; trace CSF flow and distinguish cortical from subcortical roles.

**Progression:** Build on 7.D.04 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.D.06.

### 7.D.06 — Spinal Cord, Neural Pathways and Reflex Integration

**Student-facing description:** Connect spinal cord organisation and dorsal and ventral roots with ascending and descending pathways, reflex arcs, reciprocal inhibition and coordinated spinal responses.

**Core scope:** Spinal segments, grey horns, white columns, dorsal root ganglia, dorsal sensory and ventral motor roots, spinal nerves; ascending dorsal-column and anterolateral pathways and descending corticospinal pathways at gross functional level; reflex receptor, afferent, integration, efferent and effector; monosynaptic stretch reflex introduction, polysynaptic withdrawal and crossed-extensor reflexes, reciprocal inhibition, interneurons and descending modulation. Detailed proprioceptive feedback reserved for 7.D.09.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace reflex circuits, distinguish sensory and motor root disruption, infer responses from excitatory and inhibitory interneuron arrangements.

**Progression:** Build on 7.D.05 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.D.07.

### 7.D.07 — Peripheral and Autonomic Nervous Systems

**Student-facing description:** Trace cranial and spinal nerves, peripheral ganglia and afferent and efferent pathways, comparing somatic, sympathetic, parasympathetic and enteric organisation and neurotransmission.

**Core scope:** PNS organisation, cranial and spinal nerves with functional overview rather than exhaustive memorisation, sensory and motor components, peripheral ganglia; somatic motor pathway versus autonomic two-neuron chains, thoracolumbar sympathetic and craniosacral parasympathetic outflow, ganglion placement, pre- and postganglionic transmitters and nicotinic, muscarinic and adrenergic receptors; sweat gland and adrenal medulla exceptions; autonomic tone, dual innervation and exceptions, enteric plexuses, visceral reflex integration; cross-link 7.B, 7.C and 7.I.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare autonomic pathways and receptor effects, predict organ responses in representative physiological situations, distinguish somatic and autonomic organisation.

**Progression:** Build on 7.D.06 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.D.08.

### 7.D.08 — Neuromuscular Transmission and Motor Unit Physiology

**Student-facing description:** Follow motor neuron signals through the neuromuscular junction to skeletal muscle excitation and contraction, explaining motor units, recruitment and the graded control of muscle tension.

**Core scope:** Motor end plate, presynaptic acetylcholine release, nicotinic receptors, end-plate potential, muscle action potential and acetylcholinesterase; T-tubules, DHPR–RyR1 coupling, sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium, troponin and relaxation; sliding filament and cross-bridge cycle only as essential context; motor unit size, slow and fast units, size principle, recruitment, rate coding, twitch summation and tetanus in skeletal muscle, fatigue overview; distinguish end-plate potential from muscle action potential.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret end-plate and force traces, compare recruitment and firing-rate effects, connect calcium handling to contraction and relaxation.

**Progression:** Build on 7.D.07 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.D.09.

### 7.D.09 — Proprioception and Neural Control of Muscle Tone

**Student-facing description:** Explain how muscle spindles, Golgi tendon organs and alpha–gamma motor pathways provide feedback for stretch reflexes and regulate muscle tone during rest and movement.

**Core scope:** Proprioception, muscle spindles, intrafusal and extrafusal fibres, group Ia and II afferents, dynamic/static sensitivity, alpha and gamma motor neurons, gamma loop and alpha–gamma coactivation; stretch reflex and reciprocal inhibition in detail, Golgi tendon organs and Ib afferents, context-dependent spinal force feedback rather than an invariant inhibitory reflex; resting muscle tone, passive mechanical contributions, descending control, physiological consequences of altered tone; avoid clinical diagnostic protocols.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace spindle and tendon-organ feedback circuits, predict responses to stretch and gamma activation, distinguish length/velocity feedback from force feedback.

**Progression:** Build on 7.D.08 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.D.10.

### 7.D.10 — Central Motor Control, Posture and Coordinated Movement

**Student-facing description:** Integrate motor cortical and descending pathways with basal ganglia, cerebellar and vestibular functions to explain movement initiation, coordination, balance and postural control.

**Core scope:** Motor and premotor cortex, corticospinal and corticobulbar pathways, pyramidal and extrapyramidal terminology with limitations; basal ganglia direct/indirect pathway principles and dopamine modulation, cerebellar error correction and coordination, vestibular input and integration with visual and proprioceptive cues; vestibulospinal and reticulospinal pathways, anticipatory and reactive postural adjustments, spinal locomotor pattern generation as conceptual context; distinguish upper/lower motor organisation and tone changes at physiological level; vestibular receptor transduction cross-linked to 7.E.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare functions of motor cortex, cerebellum and basal ganglia, interpret postural perturbation and motor feedback experiments, integrate central and peripheral control.

**Progression:** Build on 7.D.09 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Bridge to 7.E sensory systems and subsequent homeostatic physiology.
