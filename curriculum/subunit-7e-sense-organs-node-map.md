## Six node contracts

### 7.E.01 — Foundations of Sensory Physiology and Signal Transduction

**Student-facing description:** Explain how specialised sensory receptors detect adequate stimuli and convert them into electrical signals, then connect receptor potentials, adaptation, receptive fields and neural coding with sensory perception.

**Core scope:** Receptor classification by stimulus (photoreceptors, mechanoreceptors, thermoreceptors, chemoreceptors and nociceptors as comparative context), location and cellular organisation; adequate stimulus, threshold, transduction, receptor/generator potentials, primary sensory neurons versus specialised receptor cells; graded receptor response, action-potential frequency and population coding, stimulus duration and intensity, tonic and phasic adaptation, receptive fields, labelled lines and lateral inhibition; sensory acuity and general psychophysical threshold concepts. Chemical senses are contextual examples only, not separate syllabus chapters.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare graded and spike responses, interpret receptor adaptation and receptive-field plots, predict effects of lateral inhibition on discrimination.

**Progression:** Build on neuronal excitability and synaptic transmission from 7.D. Prepare 7.E.02.

### 7.E.02 — Visual System: Eye Anatomy and Physiological Optics

**Student-facing description:** Compare representative animal visual organs and trace light through the mammalian eye, relating cornea, lens, pupil and retinal organisation to image formation, accommodation and visual acuity.

**Core scope:** Comparative eyespots, compound eyes and ommatidia, apposition and superposition principles, camera-type eyes; mammalian sclera, cornea, choroid, aqueous and vitreous humours, iris, pupil, lens, ciliary muscle and zonules; retina, fovea, optic disc and blind spot; refraction, focal length, dioptres and simplified thin-lens relationships with assumptions; real inverted retinal image, accommodation, pupil and depth of field, acuity and representative myopia, hyperopia, astigmatism and presbyopia mechanisms; pupillary reflex circuit in outline. Avoid clinical diagnostic or treatment protocols.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace light through optical media, solve simple optical power problems, interpret accommodation and explain why foveal acuity differs from peripheral vision.

**Progression:** Build on 7.E.01 and the shared sensory principles in 7.E.01. Prepare 7.E.03.

### 7.E.03 — Phototransduction and Neural Processing of Vision

**Student-facing description:** Trace photon absorption in rods and cones through retinal photoisomerisation, transducin and cGMP signalling, then explain adaptation, colour vision, retinal circuits and visual pathways.

**Core scope:** Rods versus cones, outer-segment disks and cone specialisation; rhodopsin and cone opsins, 11-cis to all-trans retinal, transducin, PDE6, reduced cGMP, cyclic-nucleotide-gated channel closure, reduced dark current, hyperpolarisation and glutamate release; recovery and calcium-dependent adaptation, retinal pigment epithelium and visual cycle, scotopic versus photopic vision, dark/light adaptation and Purkinje shift; S/M/L cones, trichromatic and opponent processing, colour-vision variants; ON/OFF bipolar cells, horizontal/amacrine cells, ganglion cells, centre-surround organisation, optic nerve, chiasm, tract, lateral geniculate nucleus, optic radiations and visual cortex; superior colliculus and pretectal reflexes as contextual links. Distinguish photoreceptor graded responses from ganglion-cell spikes.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret dark/light current and cGMP responses, compare rod/cone sensitivity curves, predict ON/OFF centre responses and trace visual hemifields through the pathway.

**Progression:** Build on 7.E.02 and the shared sensory principles in 7.E.01. Prepare 7.E.04.

### 7.E.04 — Auditory System: Ear Anatomy and Mechanics of Hearing

**Student-facing description:** Follow sound through the external and middle ear to the cochlea, explaining acoustic properties, impedance matching, fluid motion and basilar-membrane mechanics.

**Core scope:** Sound frequency, wavelength, amplitude, pressure and intensity, decibel relationships with stated reference and distinction between intensity and pressure ratios; comparative mechanosensory hearing adaptations including insect tympanal organs and vertebrate hair-cell systems; pinna, auditory canal, tympanic membrane, malleus/incus/stapes, oval and round windows, middle-ear impedance transformation and Eustachian tube; cochlear scala vestibuli, media and tympani, perilymph and endolymph, Reissner's and basilar membranes, organ of Corti, tectorial membrane; travelling wave, stiffness gradient and frequency-dependent peak displacement; introduce inner/outer hair-cell roles as bridge to 7.E.05.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate simple sound frequency/wavelength and decibel changes, trace sound transmission, compare low- and high-frequency basilar-membrane responses.

**Progression:** Build on 7.E.03 and the shared sensory principles in 7.E.01. Prepare 7.E.05.

### 7.E.05 — Auditory Transduction, Neural Processing and Equilibrium

**Student-facing description:** Explain hair-cell mechanotransduction, cochlear amplification and auditory coding, then connect ascending auditory pathways, sound localisation and vestibular signals with perception and equilibrium.

**Core scope:** Hair bundles, stereocilia, tip links and mechanotransduction channels, K+-rich endolymph and positive endocochlear potential, depolarisation by K+ entry, Ca2+-dependent transmitter release and adaptation; inner hair-cell sensory output and outer hair-cell prestin-mediated electromotility; place coding, temporal phase locking and intensity coding; spiral ganglion, cochlear nuclei, superior olivary complex, lateral lemniscus, inferior colliculus, medial geniculate body and auditory cortex; interaural timing and level differences; audiometry and otoacoustic emissions as conceptual evidence; semicircular canals, cristae and cupula for angular acceleration, utricle/saccule and otolith organs for linear acceleration and head orientation, vestibulo-ocular reflex overview. Keep central postural integration in 7.D.10.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret hair-cell polarity responses, tonotopic maps, auditory threshold plots and binaural cues; predict vestibular receptor responses to rotations and linear acceleration.

**Progression:** Build on 7.E.04 and the shared sensory principles in 7.E.01. Prepare 7.E.06.

### 7.E.06 — Tactile Sensation and Somatosensory Processing

**Student-facing description:** Compare cutaneous mechanoreceptors for touch, pressure, vibration and skin stretch, tracing mechanotransduction, adaptation, spatial discrimination and ascending somatosensory pathways.

**Core scope:** Merkel cell–neurite complexes, Meissner and Pacinian corpuscles, Ruffini endings and hair-follicle receptors; receptive-field size, superficial versus deep receptors, slowly and rapidly adapting responses, vibration frequency selectivity; Piezo2 and mechanically gated currents as key examples without implying identical mechanisms in all endings; afferent fibre classes and conduction, dorsal-column–medial-lemniscal pathway for discriminative touch and vibration, thalamic relay, primary somatosensory cortex and somatotopy; anterolateral crude-touch context; two-point discrimination, receptive-field overlap and lateral inhibition; comparative tactile adaptations. Pain, temperature and proprioception are brief boundaries; proprioceptive motor control belongs to 7.D.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret mechanoreceptor firing patterns and two-point thresholds, compare vibration responses, predict sensory pathway consequences and distinguish receptor adaptation from stimulus loss.

**Progression:** Build on 7.E.05 and the shared sensory principles in 7.E.01. Complete the sense-organ scope and reinforce links to nervous-system integration.
