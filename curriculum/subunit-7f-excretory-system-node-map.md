## Eight node contracts

### 7.F.01 — Comparative Physiology of Excretion and Osmoregulation

**Student-facing description:** Compare nitrogenous waste products and excretory structures across animals, connecting ammonia, urea and uric acid elimination with water conservation, osmotic regulation and aquatic or terrestrial adaptations.

**Core scope:** Distinguish excretion, egestion and osmoregulation; ammonia toxicity and water requirements, urea synthesis as brief metabolic context, uric acid and energetic/water trade-offs; ammonotelism, ureotelism and uricotelism with exceptions and life-stage variation; contractile vacuoles in protists as comparative context, protonephridia and flame cells, metanephridia, Malpighian tubules and rectal water recovery, crustacean antennal glands and vertebrate kidneys; osmoconformers versus osmoregulators, freshwater and marine teleost ion/water fluxes, gill ionocytes, elasmobranch urea retention and rectal glands, euryhalinity, terrestrial water conservation and salt glands. Avoid treating excretory diversity as a linear evolutionary ladder.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare nitrogenous waste trade-offs, interpret osmotic gradients and predict water and ion fluxes in freshwater, marine and terrestrial animals.

**Progression:** Build on blood composition, circulation and metabolism. Prepare 7.F.02.

### 7.F.02 — Kidney Anatomy and Functional Organisation of the Nephron

**Student-facing description:** Trace renal blood flow and filtrate movement through the vertebrate kidney, relating cortex, medulla, nephron segments, cortical and juxtamedullary nephrons and the juxtaglomerular apparatus to renal function.

**Core scope:** Comparative vertebrate kidney organisation and nephron diversity; adult mammalian kidney capsule, cortex, medulla, pyramids, papillae, calyces and pelvis; renal artery and vein, afferent and efferent arterioles, glomerular capillaries, peritubular capillaries and vasa recta; renal corpuscle, Bowman’s capsule, proximal tubule, loop of Henle, distal tubule, connecting tubule and collecting duct system; cortical and juxtamedullary nephron distinctions, macula densa, granular cells and extraglomerular mesangial cells; functional overview of filtration, reabsorption, secretion and excretion. Detailed transport follows in 7.F.04–05.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace blood and filtrate along labelled anatomical diagrams; predict functional implications of long and short nephron loops and different capillary networks.

**Progression:** Build on 7.F.01 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.F.03.

### 7.F.03 — Glomerular Filtration and Renal Haemodynamics

**Student-facing description:** Explain the glomerular filtration barrier, pressure-dependent filtration, renal blood flow and clearance, then connect myogenic autoregulation and tubuloglomerular feedback with stable filtration.

**Core scope:** Fenestrated endothelium and glycocalyx, glomerular basement membrane, podocyte slit diaphragm and mesangial support; size and other determinants of permeability without oversimplified charge-selectivity claims; hydrostatic and oncotic pressures, net filtration pressure and filtration coefficient, GFR; renal blood flow, renal plasma flow and filtration fraction, haematocrit relation, clearance Cx=UxV/Px, inulin and creatinine principles and limitations, PAH extraction and effective renal plasma flow; afferent/efferent resistance effects with qualifications, myogenic response, macula densa NaCl sensing, ATP/adenosine signalling, tubuloglomerular feedback and autoregulatory limits; sympathetic and hormonal modulation as bridge to 7.F.06.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate GFR, clearance, filtration fraction and renal plasma/blood flow with consistent units; interpret marker clearance and autoregulation curves, compare changes in arteriolar resistance.

**Progression:** Build on 7.F.02 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.F.04.

### 7.F.04 — Tubular Reabsorption, Secretion and Urine Formation

**Student-facing description:** Follow filtered substances through nephron segments, explaining selective reabsorption, secretion, transport maximum, renal threshold and the determinants of final urine composition.

**Core scope:** Filtered load, reabsorbed load, secreted load and excretion rate; transcellular and paracellular transport; basolateral Na+/K+ ATPase as driving force; proximal Na+-coupled glucose and amino acid transport (SGLT/GLUT), bicarbonate and water reclamation, organic anion/cation secretion; thin descending and ascending limbs, thick ascending NKCC2 and water impermeability, distal NCC, connecting tubule and collecting duct ENaC, ROMK and intercalated-cell overview; transport maximum, renal threshold and splay, glucose titration curves; secretion of H+, K+ and representative organic solutes; segmental changes in tubular fluid and typical urine constituents; micropuncture and clearance evidence. Detailed concentrating, electrolyte and acid–base regulation belongs to 7.F.05–07.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Solve filtered-load and excretion calculations, interpret glucose titration and micropuncture data, predict segmental effects of changes in transporter function.

**Progression:** Build on 7.F.03 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.F.05.

### 7.F.05 — Urine Concentration and Regulation of Water Balance

**Student-facing description:** Trace countercurrent multiplication and exchange, medullary osmotic gradients and urea recycling, then explain vasopressin, aquaporins, thirst and the production of concentrated or dilute urine.

**Core scope:** Osmolality and tonicity, water permeability along nephron; loop-of-Henle countercurrent multiplication, thick ascending limb active salt transport and single effect, medullary gradient and its limitations, vasa recta countercurrent exchange, urea recycling and inner-medullary concentration mechanisms without presenting unresolved details as settled; ADH/vasopressin V2–cAMP–PKA–AQP2 pathway, AQP3/4 and water reabsorption, osmoreceptors, thirst, volume-related ADH control, water diuresis and antidiuresis; osmolar clearance and free-water clearance with assumptions; comparative nephron architecture and concentrating ability in desert mammals and other vertebrates.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret corticomedullary gradients and ADH response curves; calculate osmolar and free-water clearance, distinguish multiplier from exchanger and predict urine osmolality under altered hydration.

**Progression:** Build on 7.F.04 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.F.06.

### 7.F.06 — Renal Regulation of Blood Volume, Blood Pressure and Electrolytes

**Student-facing description:** Connect renal sodium and water handling with circulating volume and arterial pressure, integrating RAAS, aldosterone, ANP and the regulation of major electrolytes.

**Core scope:** Effective arterial blood volume versus total body water and osmolality; renin release by granular cells, reduced afferent pressure, macula densa and sympathetic beta1 input; angiotensin II, aldosterone, sodium retention and potassium secretion, ENaC and Na+/K+ ATPase; ANP/BNP and natriuresis, pressure natriuresis and long-term pressure feedback, ADH and thirst cross-link; sodium and chloride balance, potassium intake, cellular redistribution and renal secretion, flow and aldosterone effects; calcium and phosphate handling, PTH, calcitriol and FGF23 at NET/SET depth, magnesium as concise supporting context; avoid confusing sodium concentration with sodium content or pressure regulation with filtration alone.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret feedback after haemorrhage or salt loading, distinguish sodium and water imbalance, predict electrolyte changes from altered hormonal signals and renal transport.

**Progression:** Build on 7.F.05 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.F.07.

### 7.F.07 — Renal Regulation of Acid–Base Balance

**Student-facing description:** Explain bicarbonate reclamation, hydrogen ion secretion, ammonium generation and titratable acid excretion, linking renal acid handling to respiratory compensation and systemic pH stability.

**Core scope:** Acid loads and bicarbonate buffering, Henderson–Hasselbalch relationship and respiratory cross-link; proximal Na+/H+ exchange, carbonic anhydrase and bicarbonate reclamation, distinction between reclaiming filtered bicarbonate and generating new bicarbonate; distal alpha and beta intercalated cells, H+-ATPase, H+/K+ ATPase, AE1 and pendrin; glutamine ammoniagenesis, NH3/NH4+ handling and trapping, phosphate and other titratable buffers, net acid excretion = ammonium + titratable acid − urinary bicarbonate; metabolic acidosis/alkalosis, respiratory compensation and renal compensation for respiratory disturbances, time-course differences and limits; anion gap as optional explanatory calculation, not a clinical diagnostic protocol.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate simple buffer and net-acid-excretion examples, interpret qualitative acid–base changes and compensatory responses, distinguish reclamation from new bicarbonate production.

**Progression:** Build on 7.F.06 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.F.08.

### 7.F.08 — Waste Elimination, Micturition and Integrated Renal Homeostasis

**Student-facing description:** Follow final metabolic waste elimination and urine transport from kidney to bladder and urethra, explaining the micturition reflex, renal endocrine functions and coordinated maintenance of internal homeostasis.

**Core scope:** Urea, creatinine and uric acid handling and distinctions between filtration, secretion and reabsorption; urine composition and excretion rates, ureteral peristalsis, vesicoureteral junction, bladder storage, detrusor and outlet smooth muscle, external urethral sphincter; stretch afferents, sacral parasympathetic, sympathetic and pudendal pathways, spinal and pontine micturition centres, voluntary control and storage/voiding coordination; renal erythropoietin, renin and calcitriol functions with cross-links; integrate GFR, transport, concentration, electrolytes, acid–base and volume responses to dehydration, water loading and metabolic challenges; representative impaired renal function as physiological context only.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace storage and voiding circuits, interpret integrated clearance and hydration scenarios, connect renal endocrine signals and homeostatic feedback without duplicating earlier mechanisms.

**Progression:** Build on 7.F.07 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Bridge to thermoregulation, stress adaptation and endocrine integration in subsequent subunits.
