## Five node contracts

### 7.G.01 — Foundations of Thermoregulation and Thermal Comfort Zone

**Student-facing description:** Compare ectothermy, endothermy, homeothermy and heterothermy, then explain thermal comfort, thermoneutrality, critical temperatures and the relationship between temperature and metabolic rate.

**Core scope:** Core and peripheral temperatures, regulated body temperature and daily variation; ectothermy/endothermy (heat source) versus homeothermy/poikilothermy (temperature stability), heterothermy and comparative examples; thermal comfort as a behavioural/subjective range distinguished from thermoneutral zone in endotherms, lower and upper critical temperatures, resting metabolic rate versus ambient temperature and evaporative heat loss; thermal performance curves, Q10 with assumptions, limits to tolerance, thermal gradients and homeostasis.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret thermoneutral-zone and thermal-performance graphs, calculate Q10, distinguish heat source from temperature variability and predict metabolic responses to ambient temperature.

**Progression:** Build on circulation, neural control and fluid homeostasis in earlier subunits. Prepare 7.G.02.

### 7.G.02 — Physical Regulation of Body Temperature

**Student-facing description:** Explain heat balance through conduction, convection, radiation and evaporation, linking body size, insulation, skin blood flow, sweating, panting and countercurrent heat exchange with heat conservation and dissipation.

**Core scope:** Heat storage as metabolic heat minus external work and net heat loss (define sign conventions for conduction, convection, radiation and evaporation); thermal gradients, heat-transfer coefficients as simple conceptual context, surface-area-to-volume ratio and body size, insulation by fur, feathers and fat; skin blood flow, vasoconstriction and vasodilation as effectors, sweating, panting and gular flutter, humidity and evaporative cooling, countercurrent heat exchangers and regional heat conservation; physical exchange may reverse direction when environment is warmer; dehydration trade-offs and behavioural modification of exposure.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Solve simple heat-budget and surface-area scaling problems, compare dry and evaporative heat loss, interpret countercurrent exchange diagrams and predict effects of humidity, wind and insulation.

**Progression:** Build on 7.G.01 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.G.03.

### 7.G.03 — Chemical Regulation and Metabolic Thermogenesis

**Student-facing description:** Trace metabolic heat production through shivering and non-shivering thermogenesis, connecting skeletal muscle, brown adipose tissue, mitochondrial UCP1 and hormonal control with energy expenditure.

**Core scope:** Basal metabolic heat and energy conversion, ATP turnover and mitochondrial proton gradients; shivering thermogenesis in skeletal muscle, non-shivering thermogenesis in brown adipose tissue, sympathetic noradrenaline, beta-adrenergic signalling and lipolysis, UCP1-mediated proton conductance and uncoupling of oxidative phosphorylation, thyroid hormone permissive and adaptive actions, catecholamines and tissue-specific contributions; age and species differences in brown fat, beige adipocytes as supporting context, non-UCP1 mechanisms briefly and with caveats; calorimetry and oxygen consumption as evidence. BMR measurement and endocrine biosynthesis remain in 7.I and 7.J.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret oxygen consumption, heat production and UCP1 perturbation data, compare coupled and uncoupled respiration, distinguish shivering from non-shivering mechanisms and identify their energy costs.

**Progression:** Build on 7.G.02 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.G.04.

### 7.G.04 — Neural Regulation of Body Temperature

**Student-facing description:** Follow peripheral and central thermal signals through hypothalamic circuits and feedback to autonomic, motor and behavioural responses, distinguishing regulated fever from unregulated hyperthermia.

**Core scope:** Peripheral cutaneous and central thermoreception, temperature-sensitive TRP channels (representative TRPM8 and TRPV1 with context), spinal and brainstem relays, hypothalamic preoptic integration, downstream hypothalamic and brainstem pathways at functional level, sympathetic vasomotor and thermogenic outputs, sweating and shivering, behavioural thermoregulation; negative feedback, thresholds and effector coordination, anticipatory signals, fever and pyrogen–cytokine–PGE2 signalling with altered defended temperature, hyperthermia without such regulated upward change, hypothermia and limits of compensation as physiological examples; avoid clinical diagnosis or treatment.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace thermoregulatory feedback circuits, predict responses to skin and core temperature changes, compare fever and hyperthermia curves and distinguish sensory input, integration and effector output.

**Progression:** Build on 7.G.03 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.G.05.

### 7.G.05 — Thermal Acclimatization and Comparative Adaptations

**Student-facing description:** Compare heat and cold acclimatization with evolved thermal adaptations, integrating seasonal changes, torpor, hibernation, aestivation and regional heterothermy across animal species.

**Core scope:** Acute response versus acclimation under controlled conditions, acclimatization under natural conditions and heritable evolutionary adaptation; heat acclimatization including earlier/more effective sweating, plasma volume changes and salt conservation with context, cold acclimatization and metabolic/insulative responses, cellular heat-shock protection briefly, seasonal pelage and migratory/behavioural strategies; torpor and hibernation, metabolic depression and arousal, aestivation as response to heat/drought, regional heterothermy and countercurrent examples, ectotherm behavioural basking, thermal tolerance and performance plasticity; representative examples from fish, insects, reptiles, birds and mammals; avoid universal claims about adaptations or detailed ecology duplication.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret before/after acclimatization curves, distinguish reversible plasticity from evolutionary adaptation, compare torpor and hibernation patterns and integrate heat balance, metabolism and neural responses.

**Progression:** Build on 7.G.04 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Bridge to 7.H stress and adaptation.
