## Five node contracts

### 7.H.01 — Foundations of Stress, Homeostasis and Adaptation

**Student-facing description:** Distinguish stressors from physiological stress responses, then connect homeostasis, allostasis, acute and chronic stress, tolerance, acclimation, acclimatization and evolutionary adaptation.

**Core scope:** Internal and external stressors, magnitude, duration, predictability and context; regulated variables, negative feedback, homeostasis and allostasis, stress as an adaptive challenge rather than necessarily harmful; acute versus repeated and chronic stress, Selye’s historical general adaptation syndrome (alarm, resistance and exhaustion) and limitations of its universal application; resistance, tolerance, resilience, physiological plasticity, controlled acclimation versus natural acclimatization and heritable adaptation; comparative examples in animals, including invertebrates. Distinguish a stressor, physiological response, performance outcome and disease state.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret dose–response and time-course curves; distinguish reversible plasticity from inherited adaptation; evaluate the historical evidence and limitations of general adaptation syndrome.

**Progression:** Build on earlier circulation, respiration, neural, renal and thermal homeostasis. Prepare 7.H.02.

### 7.H.02 — Neural and Endocrine Mechanisms of Stress Response

**Student-facing description:** Trace stress perception through sympathetic–adrenal–medullary and hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal signalling, explaining catecholamines, glucocorticoids, feedback and coordinated physiological responses.

**Core scope:** Sensory and central appraisal, hypothalamic and limbic integration at functional level; sympathetic–adrenal–medullary response, adrenal medullary catecholamines, receptor-specific effects on circulation, ventilation and fuel mobilisation; hypothalamic CRH and AVP, pituitary ACTH, adrenal glucocorticoids, cortisol in humans and corticosterone in many other vertebrates, negative feedback, pulsatile and circadian context; different timing of neural and endocrine responses, permissive and genomic/non-genomic glucocorticoid effects at conceptual level; stress axes across vertebrates (including teleost HPI axis) and distinct invertebrate neuroendocrine signals as examples, without implying mammalian HPA anatomy is universal. Hormone synthesis and general receptor mechanisms remain in 7.J.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace feedback diagrams, interpret ACTH/glucocorticoid time courses and experimental perturbations of feedback, compare rapid catecholamine and slower endocrine responses.

**Progression:** Build on 7.H.01 and relevant earlier mechanisms. Prepare 7.H.03.

### 7.H.03 — Cellular and Molecular Mechanisms of Stress Adaptation

**Student-facing description:** Explain how oxidative stress, protein damage and metabolic disturbance activate antioxidant defence, molecular chaperones, the unfolded protein response and cellular repair mechanisms.

**Core scope:** Reactive oxygen species from metabolism and environmental perturbation, physiological signalling versus oxidative injury; superoxide dismutase, catalase, glutathione-dependent defence and redox balance, NRF2 as representative regulation; protein misfolding, HSF1 and heat-shock proteins, chaperone-assisted folding and proteostasis; ER unfolded protein response (IRE1, PERK and ATF6 at conceptual depth), adaptive translational and transcriptional responses versus persistent stress; DNA damage and repair as a functional protective response, metabolic adjustments including AMPK and energy conservation, autophagy and cell survival/death trade-offs at contextual level; cross-reference Units 2–4 for detailed repair, signalling and apoptosis pathways. Distinguish molecular stress responses from whole-animal endocrine responses.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret protein-expression and oxidative-damage data, compare transient and sustained cellular responses, reason from altered antioxidant or chaperone activity to physiological consequences.

**Progression:** Build on 7.H.02 and relevant earlier mechanisms. Prepare 7.H.04.

### 7.H.04 — Environmental Stress and Comparative Physiological Adaptations

**Student-facing description:** Compare animal responses to hypoxia, altitude, diving, dehydration, osmotic stress, temperature extremes and food shortage, linking oxygen sensing, resource conservation and metabolic depression with survival.

**Core scope:** Oxygen availability versus oxygen delivery, hypoxia and altitude responses, HIF stabilisation through oxygen-dependent prolyl hydroxylation/VHL pathway, erythropoietin and longer-term adjustments with acute/chronic distinction; comparative diving response, oxygen stores including myoglobin, bradycardia and selective perfusion in diving vertebrates; dehydration and osmotic challenges, water conservation and compatible osmolytes as appropriate comparative examples; temperature extremes linked to 7.G rather than repeated; fasting/starvation, substrate shifts, metabolic depression, hypoxia/anoxia tolerance in representative animals (e.g. turtles), resource allocation and trade-offs. Distinguish adaptation, acclimatization, acute compensation and tolerance; avoid repeating detailed kidney, respiratory and thermoregulatory mechanisms.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare altitude and diving oxygen budgets, interpret HIF and metabolic-rate data, predict physiological adjustments to combined environmental stressors, distinguish alternative species-specific strategies.

**Progression:** Build on 7.H.03 and relevant earlier mechanisms. Prepare 7.H.05.

### 7.H.05 — Chronic Stress, Allostatic Load and Integrated Physiological Recovery

**Student-facing description:** Integrate prolonged stress effects on immune, metabolic, reproductive and cardiovascular function, then examine allostatic load, biomarkers, resilience, physiological trade-offs and recovery.

**Core scope:** Repeated or persistent stress, habituation and sensitisation, altered feedback and context-dependent glucocorticoid patterns (not invariably elevated); allostatic load as cumulative physiological cost rather than a single universally defined measurement; immune modulation, inflammation and context-dependent effects of glucocorticoids, energy mobilisation and chronic metabolic consequences, reproductive-axis suppression as a possible trade-off, cardiovascular effects; interactions among nervous, endocrine, immune and metabolic systems; stress biomarkers such as glucocorticoids, heart-rate measures and physiological performance, sampling-time and species confounders, baseline versus challenge and recovery; resilience, restoration of regulated variables, repair and limits of compensation, comparative life-history trade-offs. Avoid diagnostic cutoffs, treatment recommendations and unsupported causal generalisations.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret repeated-challenge and recovery curves, distinguish association from causation and biomarker from outcome, compare short-term benefit with long-term physiological cost and integrate responses across organ systems.

**Progression:** Build on 7.H.04 and relevant earlier mechanisms. Bridge to digestion, endocrine coordination and integrated metabolic homeostasis in later subunits.
