## Six node contracts

### 7.I.01 — Foundations and Comparative Physiology of Digestion

**Student-facing description:** Compare intracellular and extracellular digestion and the functional organisation of animal digestive systems, relating alimentary tract structure, dietary specialisation and microbial fermentation to nutrient acquisition.

**Core scope:** Digestion versus ingestion, absorption and assimilation; intracellular digestion in representative animals, gastrovascular cavities and complete alimentary canals; functional regionalisation of oral cavity, pharynx, oesophagus, stomach, small and large intestine and accessory organs; comparative feeding and digestive adaptations in invertebrates and vertebrates; herbivore, carnivore and omnivore strategies, foregut and hindgut fermentation, ruminant compartments, rumination, cellulose fermentation and volatile fatty acid absorption; comparative limitations and trade-offs. Microbiome ecology and host–microbe signalling are reserved for 7.K.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare digestive architectures and diet-related adaptations, trace food through ruminant and nonruminant tracts, explain why fermentation location affects nutrient utilisation.

**Progression:** Build on animal homeostasis and basic biochemical principles. Prepare 7.I.02.

**Publication record (7.I.01):** [Reading chapter](https://rahulthakurpatan.github.io/lifescienceacademy/foundations-and-comparative-physiology-of-digestion.html); academic and structural QA passed, production deployment verification tracked separately.

### 7.I.02 — Gastrointestinal Motility and Neuroendocrine Regulation

**Student-facing description:** Trace food movement through swallowing, peristalsis, segmentation and gastric emptying, explaining enteric neural circuits, digestive reflexes and gastrointestinal hormones that coordinate motility and secretion.

**Core scope:** Swallowing phases, oesophageal peristalsis and sphincters; gastric receptive relaxation, mixing and emptying; intestinal segmentation and propulsion, migrating motor complex, colonic movements and defecation reflex overview; smooth muscle, interstitial cells of Cajal, slow waves and excitability; myenteric and submucosal plexuses, autonomic modulation and local versus long reflexes; cephalic, gastric and intestinal phases, gastrin, secretin, CCK, GIP, GLP-1, motilin and somatostatin with stimulus and physiological effects, distinguish endocrine, paracrine and neural control. Detailed systemic endocrine physiology stays in 7.J.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret motility recordings and gastric-emptying responses, predict effects of meal composition and hormonal signals, connect neural and endocrine feedback to coordinated digestion.

**Progression:** Build on 7.I.01 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.I.03.

### 7.I.03 — Digestive Secretions and Enzymatic Digestion

**Student-facing description:** Follow salivary, gastric, pancreatic, biliary and intestinal secretions through the chemical breakdown of carbohydrates, proteins, lipids and nucleic acids into absorbable products.

**Core scope:** Saliva and amylase; gastric glands, parietal-cell H+/K+ ATPase, acid regulation by acetylcholine, gastrin and histamine, chief-cell pepsinogen and activation, intrinsic factor and mucosal protection; pancreatic acinar enzymes and ductal bicarbonate, zymogens, enteropeptidase–trypsin activation and protective mechanisms; liver bile formation, gallbladder storage and release, bile salts and emulsification, enterohepatic circulation; pancreatic amylase, proteases, lipase/colipase and nucleases, brush-border disaccharidases and peptidases, digestion products and appropriate luminal pH. Distinguish bile salts from enzymes; avoid detailed intracellular metabolism or clinical treatment.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret secretion and enzyme-activity data, predict changes after altered acid or bicarbonate delivery, trace each macronutrient to its absorbable digestion products.

**Progression:** Build on 7.I.02 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.I.04.

### 7.I.04 — Intestinal Absorption and Nutrient Transport

**Student-facing description:** Connect villi and microvilli with epithelial transport of sugars, amino acids, peptides, lipids, vitamins, minerals, water and electrolytes, tracing absorbed nutrients into portal blood or lymph.

**Core scope:** Absorptive surface and epithelial polarity, sodium gradients and Na+/K+ ATPase; SGLT1, GLUT5, GLUT2, amino acid transporters and PEPT1; micelle formation, lipid uptake, re-esterification, chylomicron assembly and lacteal transport, short/medium-chain fatty acid distinctions; fat-soluble and water-soluble vitamins, intrinsic-factor-dependent B12 uptake in terminal ileum, iron and calcium transport and regulation; water movement, sodium and chloride absorption, colonic recovery and secretion balance; portal circulation versus lymphatic routes, hepatic first-pass processing overview. Distinguish luminal digestion from epithelial absorption and avoid universal claims about transporter localisation in all species.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret tracer and transporter experiments, predict effects of disrupted sodium gradients or bile delivery, compare portal and lymphatic nutrient pathways and osmotic effects of unabsorbed solutes.

**Progression:** Build on 7.I.03 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.I.05.

### 7.I.05 — Nutrition, Energy Balance and Metabolic States

**Student-facing description:** Relate essential nutrients and dietary energy to energy intake, expenditure and storage, then compare absorptive, postabsorptive and fasting states in maintaining nutritional homeostasis.

**Core scope:** Macronutrients, essential amino and fatty acids, vitamins, minerals and water; gross versus metabolizable energy and approximate physiological energy values of carbohydrates, fats and proteins with assumptions; energy intake and expenditure, positive, negative and neutral energy balance, energy stores, distinction between short-term weight change and tissue energy stores; fed/postabsorptive transitions, glycogen, lipid and protein turnover, fasting and prolonged starvation overview, glucose conservation, ketone utilisation and context-dependent hormonal signals; nutritional sufficiency and deficiency as physiological context. Detailed biochemical reactions remain in Unit 1, hormone mechanisms in 7.J and integrated organ-to-organ signalling and metabolic disorders in 7.L.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate simple dietary energy and energy-balance examples, interpret fed/fasting substrate patterns, distinguish essential nutrients from energy-providing nutrients and explain storage trade-offs.

**Progression:** Build on 7.I.04 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.I.06.

### 7.I.06 — Basal Metabolic Rate and Measurement of Energy Expenditure

**Student-facing description:** Distinguish basal, resting and standard metabolic rates and explain direct and indirect calorimetry, respiratory quotient, respiratory exchange ratio, metabolic scaling and the determinants of total energy expenditure.

**Core scope:** BMR under standardized basal conditions (rest, postabsorptive state, thermoneutrality, awake and appropriate time/context), RMR as less restrictive, standard metabolic rate in ectotherms under specified temperature and conditions; direct heat calorimetry and indirect gas-exchange calorimetry, oxygen consumption, carbon dioxide production, cellular RQ versus measured RER and non-steady-state limitations, representative carbohydrate/fat/protein values with assumptions; caloric equivalent of oxygen and energy expenditure calculation, thermic effect of food, activity expenditure and adaptive thermogenesis, body size and composition, age, thyroid status, temperature and physiological state; allometric scaling as empirical relationship with variable exponents, not universal fixed law; distinguish BMR from total daily expenditure and avoid diet prescriptions.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate RQ/RER, oxygen-based energy expenditure and simple allometric comparisons with units; interpret calorimetry data, compare basal measurement conditions and explain components of energy expenditure.

**Progression:** Build on 7.I.05 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Bridge to 7.J endocrinology and reproduction, 7.K host–microbe interactions and 7.L metabolic integration.
