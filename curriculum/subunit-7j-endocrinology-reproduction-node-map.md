## Twelve node contracts

### 7.J.01 — Foundations of Endocrinology and Hormonal Communication

**Student-facing description:** Compare endocrine, neuroendocrine, paracrine and autocrine communication, then trace hormone classification, biosynthesis, secretion, transport and feedback regulation.

**Core scope:** Endocrine versus exocrine glands; peptide/protein, steroid and amino-acid-derived hormones, synthesis, storage and regulated release, binding proteins and free hormone, half-life, metabolism and clearance; pulsatile and circadian secretion, negative and positive feedback, target specificity, receptor sensitivity, permissive, synergistic and antagonistic actions; endocrine axes and comparative principles.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret hormone concentration and feedback curves, compare peptide and steroid secretion and transport, distinguish total from free hormone.

**Progression:** Build on cellular signalling and integrated animal homeostasis. Prepare 7.J.02.

### 7.J.02 — Molecular Mechanisms of Hormone Action

**Student-facing description:** Connect membrane and intracellular hormone receptors with second messengers, kinase cascades and transcriptional regulation, explaining how target cells amplify and modify endocrine signals.

**Core scope:** GPCR Gs/Gi–cAMP–PKA and Gq–PLC–IP3/DAG–Ca2+/PKC, receptor tyrosine kinases and insulin signalling, cytokine receptors and JAK–STAT (GH and prolactin), guanylyl cyclase and cGMP (ANP), steroid and thyroid nuclear receptors, transcriptional regulation, rapid non-genomic effects as context; amplification, desensitisation, receptor regulation and resistance. Cross-reference general signalling in Unit 4.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Predict responses to altered receptors and second messengers, compare response times and interpret dose–response and receptor-binding data.

**Progression:** Build on 7.J.01 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.J.03.

### 7.J.03 — Hypothalamus and Pituitary Gland

**Student-facing description:** Trace hypothalamic control of anterior and posterior pituitary functions, linking releasing hormones, portal circulation and pituitary hormones with growth, homeostasis and endocrine disorders.

**Core scope:** Hypothalamic releasing/inhibiting hormones CRH, TRH, GnRH, GHRH, somatostatin and dopamine; hypophyseal portal system, anterior pituitary cell types and GH, prolactin, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH; GH–IGF-1 axis, growth regulation and representative deficiency/excess, prolactin and tonic dopamine inhibition; posterior pituitary storage/release of hypothalamically synthesised ADH and oxytocin, supraoptic/paraventricular nuclei with overlapping roles; feedback and pulsatility, central versus peripheral endocrine disturbances, diabetes insipidus as contextual example. Detailed target glands follow in later nodes.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret pituitary-axis feedback patterns, gland ablation/replacement experiments and GH/IGF-1 data; distinguish synthesis from release in posterior pituitary.

**Progression:** Build on 7.J.02 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.J.04.

### 7.J.04 — Thyroid, Parathyroid and Calcium Homeostasis

**Student-facing description:** Explain thyroid hormone synthesis and feedback control, then integrate parathyroid hormone, calcitonin and vitamin D in calcium and phosphate homeostasis.

**Core scope:** Thyroid follicle, iodide uptake, thyroid peroxidase, thyroglobulin iodination/coupling, colloid, T4/T3 release and deiodinases, HPT axis, metabolic and developmental effects, hypo/hyperthyroidism, iodine deficiency and goitre; parathyroid calcium-sensing receptor, PTH, bone and renal responses, calcitriol activation and intestinal absorption, calcium/phosphate regulation, calcitonin with appropriately limited adult human homeostatic role, representative parathyroid disturbances. Cross-link 7.F renal mineral handling and 7.G thermogenesis.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace thyroid synthesis and feedback, predict hormone patterns in primary versus central disorders, interpret calcium/PTH responses.

**Progression:** Build on 7.J.03 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.J.05.

### 7.J.05 — Adrenal Gland and Corticosteroid Physiology

**Student-facing description:** Compare adrenal cortical zones and medullary function, tracing steroidogenesis, glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid regulation, catecholamine responses and representative adrenal disorders.

**Core scope:** Zona glomerulosa, fasciculata and reticularis, medulla and chromaffin cells; cholesterol and steroidogenic pathways including StAR, representative enzymes and products; aldosterone regulation by angiotensin II and potassium, cortisol by ACTH and feedback, adrenal androgen context; catecholamine synthesis and sympathetic control; metabolic, immune and electrolyte effects, Addison disease, Cushing syndrome (distinguish pituitary Cushing disease), primary aldosteronism as representative mechanisms. Avoid repeating 7.H stress responses and 7.F transport detail.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare zonal products and control, interpret ACTH/cortisol and renin/aldosterone patterns, distinguish primary and secondary endocrine defects.

**Progression:** Build on 7.J.04 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.J.06.

### 7.J.06 — Endocrine Pancreas and Glucose Homeostasis

**Student-facing description:** Explain pancreatic islet organisation, glucose-stimulated insulin secretion, glucagon and somatostatin action, and hormonal feedback underlying glucose homeostasis and diabetes.

**Core scope:** Islet alpha, beta, delta and PP cells, glucose metabolism, ATP-sensitive K+ channels, depolarisation, Ca2+ entry and insulin exocytosis; insulin receptor signalling and GLUT4, anabolic effects, glucagon–cAMP pathway and hepatic glucose output; paracrine regulation and incretin effect, fed/fasted feedback; type 1 and type 2 diabetes distinguished, insulin resistance versus insulin deficiency, hypoglycaemia and counterregulation, representative glucose tolerance data. Detailed interorgan disease integration remains in 7.L.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret glucose/insulin curves and beta-cell perturbations, compare hormone profiles and predict feedback changes in fasting and feeding.

**Progression:** Build on 7.J.05 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.J.07.

### 7.J.07 — Other Endocrine Organs and Integrated Hormonal Regulation

**Student-facing description:** Survey pineal, thymic and non-classical endocrine tissues, relating melatonin, renal, cardiac, gastrointestinal and adipose hormones to integrated and comparative animal endocrine regulation.

**Core scope:** Pineal melatonin and circadian/photoperiodic signalling; thymic peptide signals and immune context without overstating a single hormone axis; renal renin, erythropoietin and calcitriol, cardiac natriuretic peptides, gastrointestinal hormones and adipokines with cross-references to 7.F, 7.I and 7.L; endocrine coordination across tissues, representative vertebrate differences, insect ecdysteroids and juvenile hormone in developmental transitions and other comparative neuroendocrine examples. Avoid exhaustive lists or duplication of developmental pathways.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare classical glands and distributed endocrine tissues, interpret photoperiod and comparative hormone-response experiments, identify feedback connections across organ systems.

**Progression:** Build on 7.J.06 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.J.08.

### 7.J.08 — Comparative Reproductive Physiology and Neuroendocrine Regulation

**Student-facing description:** Compare animal reproductive strategies and gonadal organisation, then connect puberty, hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal signalling, GnRH pulsatility and seasonal cues with reproductive competence.

**Core scope:** Sexual and asexual reproduction as comparative context, gonochorism and hermaphroditism, oviparity and viviparity, reproductive tract anatomy overview, sexual maturation and puberty, hypothalamic GnRH pulse generation and kisspeptin, FSH/LH, gonadal steroid and inhibin feedback, pulsatile versus continuous GnRH effects, photoperiod and seasonal breeding, representative vertebrate and invertebrate reproductive endocrine strategies. Gametogenesis and cycles developed in 7.J.09–11; general developmental mechanisms remain in Unit 5.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace HPG-axis feedback, interpret hormone pulse and seasonal breeding data, compare reproductive strategies and distinguish endocrine coordination from gamete development.

**Progression:** Build on 7.J.07 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.J.09.

### 7.J.09 — Male Reproductive Physiology and Spermatogenesis

**Student-facing description:** Trace testicular organisation, Sertoli and Leydig cell functions, spermatogenesis and sperm maturation, linking testosterone, gonadotropins and inhibin with male reproductive function.

**Core scope:** Seminiferous tubules, Sertoli and Leydig cells, blood–testis barrier, spermatogonial renewal, meiosis, spermatids and spermiogenesis, spermiation, epididymal maturation, accessory glands and seminal fluid; LH-dependent testosterone synthesis, FSH/Sertoli support, inhibin B and feedback, temperature regulation and representative hormonal disturbances; distinguish spermatogenesis, epididymal maturation and capacitation. Cross-link Unit 5 for detailed gamete differentiation.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret germ-cell stages and ploidy, predict hormone changes and reproductive effects of altered Sertoli or Leydig function, read spermatogenesis diagrams.

**Progression:** Build on 7.J.08 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.J.10.

### 7.J.10 — Female Reproductive Physiology and Oogenesis

**Student-facing description:** Trace ovarian organisation, oocyte development and meiotic arrest through follicular maturation, linking granulosa and theca cell functions with estrogen and progesterone production.

**Core scope:** Ovarian cortex and follicles, primordial germ cells and ovarian reserve, primary oocyte prophase I arrest, meiotic resumption, secondary oocyte metaphase II arrest in humans and comparative differences; folliculogenesis from primordial to preovulatory stages, granulosa/theca compartments, follicular atresia, two-cell/two-gonadotropin model, aromatase, estrogen, progesterone and inhibin; oocyte–follicle coordination and hormonal regulation, ovarian reserve concepts with limitations. Cycle timing and ovulation follow in 7.J.11.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret follicle stages, meiotic chromosome/DNA content and steroid production patterns, predict consequences of altered FSH/LH and aromatase.

**Progression:** Build on 7.J.09 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.J.11.

### 7.J.11 — Reproductive Cycles, Ovulation and Hormonal Feedback

**Student-facing description:** Explain menstrual and estrous cycles through follicular and luteal phases, estrogen feedback, the LH surge, ovulation, corpus luteum function and coordinated reproductive tract changes.

**Core scope:** Ovarian follicular and luteal phases, uterine menstrual, proliferative and secretory phases, cycle variability and comparative estrous cycles; FSH recruitment, dominant follicle selection, sustained estradiol positive feedback and LH surge, oocyte maturation, follicle rupture and ovulation, luteinisation, progesterone and feedback, luteolysis and menstruation in relevant species, pregnancy rescue as bridge to 7.J.12; induced versus spontaneous ovulation, anovulation, amenorrhoea and PCOS as representative physiological disturbances, menopause and reproductive ageing in appropriate context. Avoid assuming universal 28-day cycles or clinical treatment protocols.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret aligned hormone, follicle and endometrial graphs, predict consequences of altered feedback and ovulatory signals, compare estrous and menstrual regulation.

**Progression:** Build on 7.J.10 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.J.12.

### 7.J.12 — Fertilization, Pregnancy, Parturition and Lactation

**Student-facing description:** Connect fertilization and implantation with maternal–placental endocrine communication, pregnancy maintenance, parturition and prolactin- and oxytocin-mediated lactation.

**Core scope:** Sperm capacitation, acrosome reaction and fertilization as brief physiological links to Unit 5 detailed mechanisms; implantation, corpus luteum rescue by hCG in humans, placental endocrine transition, progesterone, estrogens, placental lactogen and maternal adaptations, fetal–maternal coordination with species-specific caveats; parturition, prostaglandins, oxytocin and positive feedback with multifactorial initiation, lactogenesis and galactopoiesis, prolactin, suckling feedback and oxytocin-mediated milk ejection, lactational suppression of reproductive axis as context. Avoid duplicating embryology or providing obstetric advice.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret pregnancy hormone trajectories, trace parturition and milk-ejection feedback, distinguish milk synthesis from ejection and explain endocrine changes after delivery.

**Progression:** Build on 7.J.11 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Bridge to 7.K host–microbe physiology and 7.L interorgan communication.
