## Six node contracts

### 7.K.01 — Foundations of Metaorganisms, Holobionts and Gut Microbiome Ecology

**Student-facing description:** Explore animals as hosts of microbial communities, distinguishing microbiota, microbiome, metaorganism, holobiont and hologenome, then connect colonisation, diversity and ecological interactions with the organisation of gut microbial communities.

**Core scope:** Microbiota versus microbiome (definitions vary by author), metaorganism and holobiont as host–associated microbial assemblages, hologenome as combined genetic complement; distinguish ecological association from an automatically unified evolutionary unit; mutualism, commensalism, competition and context-dependent pathogenicity; bacteria, archaea, fungi, protists and viruses/phages in gut ecosystems; microbial niches, oxygen and nutrient gradients, colonisation and succession across life stages, host species, diet, environment and medications, resilience and interindividual variation; representative animal symbioses and gut communities without a universal healthy taxonomic composition.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret community succession and environmental-gradient data, distinguish terms and relationship types, compare host-associated and free-living microbial communities and recognise limits of the hologenome concept.

**Progression:** Build on digestion, immunity and animal physiology. Prepare 7.K.02.

### 7.K.02 — Physiological Functions of the Gut Microbiome

**Student-facing description:** Trace microbial fermentation, short-chain fatty acid production, vitamin synthesis and bile acid transformation, explaining their contributions to nutrient utilisation, intestinal barriers, immune development and host homeostasis.

**Core scope:** Microbial degradation of otherwise poorly digestible substrates, fermentation and cross-feeding, acetate, propionate and butyrate, colonocyte energy and SCFA signalling via FFAR2/FFAR3 and related pathways, context-dependent histone deacetylase effects; microbial vitamin synthesis and limitations of host availability; bile salt deconjugation and secondary bile acids, FXR/TGR5 signalling at conceptual level; mucus, epithelial tight junctions, antimicrobial peptides and secretory IgA, immune maturation, regulatory responses and colonisation resistance through competition and microbial products; host–microbe bidirectionality, functional redundancy, species and diet differences. Detailed digestive transport remains in 7.I and immune pathways in Unit 4.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret metabolite and barrier-function data, trace substrate-to-metabolite-to-host-response relationships, predict consequences of changing fermentable substrate availability while distinguishing measured effects from proposed mechanisms.

**Progression:** Build on 7.K.01 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.K.03.

### 7.K.03 — Methods and Experimental Approaches to Study the Gut Microbiome

**Student-facing description:** Compare culture-based approaches, 16S rRNA gene profiling, shotgun metagenomics, metatranscriptomics and metabolomics, then interpret diversity, abundance and experimental controls to evaluate microbiome evidence.

**Core scope:** Representative sampling sites, stool versus mucosa limitations, sample handling and contamination/batch effects at conceptual level, microbial culture and culturomics versus sequence-based methods; 16S rRNA gene marker sequencing for bacteria and archaea with primer and resolution limitations, shotgun metagenomics for genes and taxonomic potential, metatranscriptomics for expression, metabolomics for measured chemical products; distinction between functional potential, transcription and actual physiological activity; alpha diversity (richness, evenness, Shannon index), beta diversity (between-community dissimilarity), relative versus absolute abundance and compositional constraints, qPCR and other absolute-load estimation concepts; controls, biological replicates, confounding by diet, age, medications and housing, longitudinal versus cross-sectional designs; no specialist wet-lab or computational pipelines.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate simple Shannon diversity examples, compare relative and absolute abundance, interpret community-distance plots and select appropriate measurement approaches and controls for a physiological question.

**Progression:** Build on 7.K.02 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.K.04.

### 7.K.04 — Germ-Free Animals and Gnotobiotic Models

**Student-facing description:** Distinguish germ-free, gnotobiotic, conventional and specific pathogen-free animals, then use controlled colonisation and microbiota-transfer experiments to evaluate causal contributions of microorganisms to host physiology.

**Core scope:** Axenic/germ-free animals operationally free of detectable microorganisms, gnotobiotic animals with known microbial status including defined-colonised animals, specific pathogen-free animals free of specified pathogens but carrying other microbes, conventional and antibiotic-treated models; isolator-based maintenance and verification as concepts only, no husbandry protocol; monoassociation, defined microbial consortia, conventionalisation and microbiota transplantation as experimental designs; developmental and physiological differences in germ-free models such as immune maturation, intestinal morphology and metabolism; litter, cage, diet, donor and recipient effects, sham/control groups, colonisation verification, rescue experiments, transferability versus necessity and sufficiency; limitations of animal-to-human extrapolation and germ-free developmental confounding.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare germ-free and colonised experimental groups, design conceptual controls for a causal hypothesis, interpret rescue and transfer evidence and identify alternative explanations without overstating human relevance.

**Progression:** Build on 7.K.03 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.K.05.

### 7.K.05 — Gut–Brain Axis and Neuroendocrine Communication

**Student-facing description:** Trace bidirectional signalling between the intestine and brain through vagal, enteric, endocrine, immune and microbial-metabolite pathways, linking gut microbial activity with neural and behavioural physiology.

**Core scope:** Gut–brain axis versus microbiota–gut–brain axis, vagal afferents and efferents, enteric and autonomic pathways, enteroendocrine signals, HPA-axis responses, cytokines and immune signalling, SCFAs and other microbial metabolites, tryptophan metabolism including indole and kynurenine pathways, peripheral serotonin and distinction from central serotonin (peripheral serotonin does not readily cross the blood–brain barrier), microbial neuroactive compounds and limits on direct brain access; brain-to-gut effects on motility, secretion and barrier function; representative germ-free, colonisation and vagal-pathway studies, behaviour assays and evidence limitations; avoid treating any microbial association as a demonstrated psychiatric cause. Cross-link 7.D, 7.H and 7.J rather than repeat neural or endocrine mechanisms.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace alternative signalling routes, interpret controlled behavioural and metabolite experiments, distinguish direct from indirect mechanisms and bidirectional feedback from one-way causation.

**Progression:** Build on 7.K.04 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.K.06.

### 7.K.06 — Gut Dysbiosis, Disease and Restoration of Microbial Homeostasis

**Student-facing description:** Examine disturbances in microbial community composition and function, relating dysbiosis to intestinal, metabolic and neurological disease evidence, then compare principles and limitations of probiotics, prebiotics and faecal microbiota transplantation.

**Core scope:** Dysbiosis as context-dependent alteration in composition or function, loss of colonisation resistance, antibiotic-associated changes and recovery, host inflammation and altered microbial ecology as potentially reciprocal processes; representative inflammatory bowel disease, recurrent Clostridioides difficile infection, obesity and metabolic disorder associations and selected neurological associations with explicit evidence hierarchy; distinguish association, longitudinal prediction, animal-model causation and demonstrated human clinical benefit; probiotics, prebiotics and synbiotics definitions, strain/substrate specificity, variability and evidence limits; faecal microbiota transplantation as a concept with established selected clinical indications and safety/regulatory constraints, no self-administration or procedural instructions; restoration, resilience and personalised variability; bridge to 7.L metabolic health without duplicating systemic mechanisms.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Evaluate study design and causal claims, compare microbial recovery and disease outcomes, distinguish established indication-specific evidence from hypotheses and explain why changes in diversity alone cannot establish health improvement.

**Progression:** Build on 7.K.05 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Bridge to 7.L interorgan communication and metabolic health.
