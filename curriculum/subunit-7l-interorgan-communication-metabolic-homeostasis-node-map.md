## Eight node contracts

### 7.L.01 — Foundations of Interorgan Communication and Metabolic Homeostasis

**Student-facing description:** Compare endocrine, neural, metabolite and immune signalling between organs, introducing organokines, extracellular vesicles, nutrient sensing and feedback networks that coordinate systemic metabolism.

**Core scope:** Distinguish local signalling and interorgan communication; circulating hormones, neural pathways, substrates and metabolites as both fuels and signals, cytokines and organokines; adipokines, myokines, hepatokines and batokines as source-based categories with overlapping tissue origins; extracellular vesicles as a potential communication route with evidence limitations; nutrient and energy sensing, feedback, feedforward and organ-specific responses, timescales of regulation, brain–gut–pancreas–liver–muscle–adipose–kidney network overview; distinguish energy balance, energy homeostasis and glucose homeostasis. Cross-reference receptor mechanisms in Unit 4 and 7.J.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace sender, signal, transport route, recipient and response in network diagrams; distinguish correlation from demonstrated interorgan signalling and compare feedback with anticipatory responses.

**Progression:** Build on Unit 1 metabolism and the preceding eleven animal physiology subunits. Prepare 7.L.02.

### 7.L.02 — Central Regulation of Appetite and Energy Homeostasis

**Student-facing description:** Explain how hypothalamic and brainstem circuits integrate leptin, ghrelin, insulin and gastrointestinal signals with melanocortin pathways, satiety, reward and autonomic control of energy balance.

**Core scope:** Arcuate AgRP/NPY and POMC/CART neuronal populations, melanocortin signalling and MC4R, paraventricular and lateral hypothalamic pathways, brainstem nucleus tractus solitarius and vagal input; leptin and insulin as energy-status signals, ghrelin and meal-related CCK, GLP-1 and PYY, distinctions between satiation and satiety; hedonic and homeostatic regulation, sensory/learned cues, autonomic output and energy expenditure, adaptive responses to energy deficit; avoid treating historical hunger/satiety centres as isolated switches or attributing behaviour to single hormones.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret food-intake and neuronal perturbation experiments, compare short- and long-term signals, predict responses to changes in leptin or melanocortin signalling with model limitations.

**Progression:** Build on 7.L.01 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.L.03.

### 7.L.03 — Gut–Pancreas–Liver Communication and Glucose Homeostasis

**Student-facing description:** Connect intestinal nutrient sensing and incretin signals with insulin–glucagon coordination, hepatic glucose production, nutrient storage and representative hepatokines such as FGF21.

**Core scope:** Enteroendocrine nutrient sensing, GLP-1 and GIP and glucose-dependent incretin effect, gut–brain–liver neural pathways as context; portal nutrient and hormonal delivery, pancreatic islet feedback, hepatic insulin and glucagon responses, regulation of glycogen turnover and gluconeogenesis at organ level; hepatic nutrient sensing and FGF21 as a context-dependent endocrine signal (including extrahepatic production), representative hepatokines, liver–adipose–brain communication, counterregulation during low glucose; distinguish portal and peripheral signals, cross-reference digestive absorption in 7.I and hormone secretion/action in 7.J.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare oral and intravenous glucose response data, interpret portal versus systemic glucose/hormone patterns and tracer estimates of hepatic glucose output, distinguish direct and indirect insulin effects.

**Progression:** Build on 7.L.02 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.L.04.

### 7.L.04 — Adipose Tissue–Skeletal Muscle Communication and Energy Expenditure

**Student-facing description:** Compare white, brown and beige adipose tissue and skeletal muscle as metabolic and signalling organs, connecting adipokines, myokines, batokines, fuel exchange and exercise-induced regulation of energy expenditure.

**Core scope:** White adipose storage and lipolysis, brown/beige thermogenesis as integration with 7.G; leptin, adiponectin and adipose-derived inflammatory mediators, skeletal muscle insulin-dependent and contraction-stimulated glucose uptake, GLUT4 trafficking and AMPK as context, lipid utilisation and substrate exchange, exercise-associated IL-6 and context-dependent effects distinct from chronic inflammation, irisin and batokines as representative signals with caveats on human evidence and physiological significance; muscle–adipose–liver communication, exercise adaptation and energy expenditure without repeating BMR methods.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret exercise time-course and tissue-specific perturbation data, compare contraction- and insulin-stimulated glucose uptake, evaluate evidence for candidate organokines and distinguish tissue origin from exclusive origin.

**Progression:** Build on 7.L.03 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.L.05.

### 7.L.05 — Integrated Metabolism During Feeding, Fasting and Exercise

**Student-facing description:** Trace organ-specific fuel use and substrate exchange during feeding, fasting and exercise, integrating glycogen turnover, gluconeogenesis, lipolysis, ketogenesis, the Cori cycle and glucose–alanine cycle.

**Core scope:** Fed, postabsorptive and prolonged fasting transitions, hepatic glycogen storage and mobilisation, adipose triglyceride turnover, muscle fuel selection, liver and kidney contributions to gluconeogenesis, ketone production and utilisation by appropriate tissues, glucose needs of erythrocytes and changing brain fuel use; exercise intensity/duration, muscle glycogen and circulating substrates, lactate exchange and Cori cycle, glucose–alanine cycle and nitrogen transfer; hormone and neural coordination, flux versus concentration, substrate competition and metabolic switching; pathways taught in Unit 1, nutritional states in 7.I, here focus on coordinated exchange and physiological context.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret stable-isotope tracer and arteriovenous substrate differences, reason through fed/fasted/exercise organ maps, compare flux with pool size and solve simple substrate exchange examples with units.

**Progression:** Build on 7.L.04 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.L.06.

### 7.L.06 — Metabolic Flexibility and Maintenance of Metabolic Health

**Student-facing description:** Relate metabolic flexibility, insulin sensitivity, mitochondrial function, AMPK–mTOR signalling and circadian coordination to the maintenance and experimental assessment of metabolic health.

**Core scope:** Metabolic flexibility as adjustment of substrate use to demand and availability, insulin sensitivity and tissue-specific differences, mitochondrial oxidative capacity and context-dependent dysfunction, AMPK and mTORC1 nutrient/energy sensing with cross-reference to Unit 4, circadian clocks and feeding/activity timing as interacting cues, metabolic adaptations to exercise and physiological challenges; glucose tolerance, fasting glucose and insulin, lipid measures, RER as whole-body proxy with steady-state limitations, hyperinsulinaemic–euglycaemic clamp principle and tracer-supported tissue-specific interpretation, limitations of surrogate indices and absence of a single universal metabolic-health definition; distinguish measurements, associations and causal mechanisms.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret glucose-tolerance and clamp curves, calculate or compare simple metabolic indicators with assumptions, assess substrate switching and distinguish RER from cellular RQ and association from causal evidence.

**Progression:** Build on 7.L.05 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.L.07.

### 7.L.07 — Obesity, Insulin Resistance and Diabetes Mellitus

**Student-facing description:** Explain how adipose dysfunction, ectopic lipid accumulation, altered inflammatory signalling and impaired insulin responses may contribute to obesity-associated insulin resistance, beta-cell dysfunction and type 2 diabetes.

**Core scope:** Energy balance and body composition, heterogeneity of obesity and metabolic risk, adipocyte hypertrophy and hyperplasia, adipose storage capacity and ectopic lipid, lipotoxic intermediates and inflammatory signalling, impaired insulin signalling in muscle, liver and adipose tissue, hepatic glucose production and defective glucose uptake, leptin resistance as a context-dependent concept, compensatory insulin secretion and progressive beta-cell dysfunction in susceptible individuals, type 2 diabetes pathophysiology and distinction from autoimmune type 1 diabetes; genetic, environmental and physiological heterogeneity, feedback and possible bidirectional causation. Avoid deterministic claims, stigma, diagnostic or treatment protocols; connect 7.J pancreatic mechanisms.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret insulin/glucose response patterns and tissue-specific experimental perturbations, distinguish insulin resistance from insulin deficiency, evaluate competing mechanistic models and explain why body mass alone does not establish metabolic dysfunction.

**Progression:** Build on 7.L.06 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Prepare 7.L.08.

### 7.L.08 — Dyslipidaemia, Fatty Liver and Integrated Metabolic Disorders

**Student-facing description:** Connect disrupted lipid transport and hepatic fat accumulation with dyslipidaemia, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, metabolic syndrome and cardiovascular consequences of altered interorgan coordination.

**Core scope:** Physiological lipoprotein transport (chylomicrons, VLDL, IDL, LDL, HDL), apolipoproteins and lipoprotein lipase at functional level, triglyceride delivery, hepatic lipid uptake and synthesis, fatty acid oxidation and VLDL export; mechanisms of dyslipidaemia and steatosis, MASLD terminology and relationship to older NAFLD terminology, MASH as inflammatory/injury context, metabolic syndrome as clustering of risk factors with definitions varying, links between insulin resistance, adipose dysfunction, liver metabolism, vascular endothelium and atherosclerotic risk; distinguish causal factors and biomarkers, heterogeneity and non-obligatory progression; integrated case-based physiological reasoning, no clinical diagnosis/treatment guidance.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace lipid and glucose flux in integrated network diagrams, interpret representative lipid profiles and organ-specific metabolic data at physiological level, distinguish association from causation and synthesise the major regulatory systems of Unit 7.

**Progression:** Build on 7.L.07 and relevant preceding mechanisms. Conclude Unit 7 by integrating organ-system regulation, physiological adaptation and metabolic dysfunction.
