## Subunit contract

Trace Mendelian transmission from segregation and independent assortment through allelic and non-allelic interactions, variable expression, imprinting, linkage and sex-dependent inheritance to organelle transmission and maternal effects.

**Legacy coverage:** Legacy A, C, E; multiple-allele applications support legacy B.

**Planned nodes:** 8. See [Unit 8 master map](unit-08-inheritance-biology-subunit-map.md) for complete traceability and cross-unit boundaries.

## Node contracts

### 8.A.01 — Foundations of Genetics and Mendelian Segregation

**Student-facing description:** Explain Mendel's experiments, chromosomal inheritance, genotype and phenotype, dominance, segregation, monohybrid crosses, test crosses and backcrosses.

**Core scope:** Pea experimental design, true-breeding lines, P/F1/F2, reciprocal crosses, locus and allele vocabulary, homozygotes and heterozygotes, meiotic segregation, 1:2:1 genotype and conditional 3:1 phenotype ratios; Sutton–Boveri theory. Distinguish dominance from fitness, frequency and molecular function.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Construct Punnett squares, infer unknown genotypes from testcrosses and interpret segregation counts.

**Progression:** Build on chromosome and meiosis foundations in Unit 2. Prepare 8.A.02.

### 8.A.02 — Independent Assortment and Probability in Genetics

**Student-facing description:** Apply independent assortment, probability rules, dihybrid and polyhybrid crosses, expected offspring ratios and chi-square tests to genetic predictions.

**Core scope:** Meiotic orientation, independent versus linked loci, product and sum rules, conditional probability, binomial outcomes, forked-line method, 9:3:3:1 and 1:1:1:1 under stated assumptions, expected counts, chi-square goodness of fit and degrees of freedom; small-sample limitations.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Solve multi-locus crosses and chi-square problems; distinguish failure to reject from proof of a model.

**Progression:** Build on 8.A.01. Prepare 8.A.03.

### 8.A.03 — Incomplete Dominance, Codominance and Allelic Relationships

**Student-facing description:** Compare complete dominance, incomplete dominance, codominance and multiple-allele inheritance using modified monohybrid ratios and blood-group examples.

**Core scope:** Allelic interactions at a single locus, snapdragon and ABO examples, IA/IB/i, dominance series and population-level multiple alleles versus diploid individuals, molecular explanations and environmental context; Bombay phenotype cross-link to epistasis, not a simple ABO allele.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Predict genotype and phenotype distributions, analyse ABO crosses without treating blood type as proof of parentage.

**Progression:** Build on 8.A.02. Prepare 8.A.04.

### 8.A.04 — Gene Interactions and Epistasis

**Student-facing description:** Explain non-allelic interactions, complementary, supplementary, duplicate and suppressor effects, relating modified dihybrid ratios to biochemical pathways.

**Core scope:** Epistasis versus dominance, recessive 9:3:4, dominant 12:3:1, complementary 9:7, duplicate dominant 15:1, additive interaction 9:6:1, inhibitory 13:3 under defined models; pathway order, suppressor and modifier genes, Bombay phenotype; distinguish ratio conventions across textbooks.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Derive ratios from genotype classes, infer pathway relationships from mutant phenotypes and test alternative models.

**Progression:** Build on 8.A.03. Prepare 8.A.05.

### 8.A.05 — Pleiotropy, Penetrance, Expressivity and Genomic Imprinting

**Student-facing description:** Distinguish pleiotropy, penetrance, expressivity, phenocopy and environmental effects, then explain parent-of-origin expression and genomic imprinting.

**Core scope:** One gene/multiple traits versus polygenic traits, incomplete and age-dependent penetrance, variable expressivity, phenocopy and gene–environment interaction, imprint establishment/maintenance/reset and methylation context, reciprocal transmission, Prader–Willi/Angelman as examples, distinguish imprinting from maternal effects and ordinary epigenetic regulation.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret pedigrees with incomplete penetrance, quantify penetrance, infer parent-of-origin effects and compare phenocopy with inherited variants.

**Progression:** Build on 8.A.04. Prepare 8.A.06.

### 8.A.06 — Chromosomal Linkage and Crossing Over

**Student-facing description:** Trace Morgan's linkage experiments, coupling and repulsion, complete and incomplete linkage and meiotic crossing over to explain parental and recombinant offspring.

**Core scope:** Bateson–Punnett coupling and repulsion, Morgan and Drosophila evidence, cis/trans haplotypes, homolog pairing, chiasmata and crossing over between non-sister chromatids, parental/recombinant classes, recombination frequency and its 50% ceiling, physical versus genetic distance overview; detailed mapping in 8.C.01.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Classify offspring, infer linkage phase and recombination fraction, explain why 50% recombination does not prove different chromosomes.

**Progression:** Build on 8.A.05. Prepare 8.A.07.

### 8.A.07 — Sex-Linked, Sex-Limited and Sex-Influenced Inheritance

**Student-facing description:** Compare X-linked, Y-linked and other sex-chromosome inheritance with sex-limited and sex-influenced expression, reciprocal crosses and dosage compensation.

**Core scope:** XX/XY, ZZ/ZW and XO as comparative contexts, hemizygosity, X-linked dominant/recessive, Y-linked and pseudoautosomal transmission, Drosophila reciprocal crosses, X-inactivation and mosaicism, comparative dosage compensation, autosomal sex-limited and sex-influenced characters and hormonal context; distinguish sex determination from sex linkage.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Predict reciprocal cross outcomes and recognise sex-dependent expression of autosomal traits versus chromosomal linkage.

**Progression:** Build on 8.A.06. Prepare 8.A.08.

### 8.A.08 — Extrachromosomal Inheritance and Maternal Effects

**Student-facing description:** Compare mitochondrial and chloroplast gene transmission, heteroplasmy, cytoplasmic inheritance and nuclear maternal effects through reciprocal crosses and organelle segregation.

**Core scope:** Organelle genomes and variable uniparental/biparental inheritance, Mirabilis plastid inheritance, yeast petite as mitochondrial example, mitochondrial heteroplasmy, replicative segregation, bottleneck and threshold effects, paternal leakage caveat, nuclear maternal-effect snail coiling, cytoplasmic symbionts as supporting examples; distinguish maternal inheritance, maternal effect and imprinting.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret reciprocal and multigeneration crosses, predict maternal-effect lag and explain variable heteroplasmy outcomes.

**Progression:** Build on 8.A.07. Bridge to subunit 8.B.
