## Subunit contract

Develop the gene and allele concepts, use complementation to identify functional units, then trace mutation mechanisms, mutant phenotypes, inheritance and experimental detection.

**Legacy coverage:** Legacy B and I; dominant-negative explicitly retained from revised scope.

**Planned nodes:** 5. See [Unit 8 master map](unit-08-inheritance-biology-subunit-map.md) for complete traceability and cross-unit boundaries.

## Node contracts

### 8.B.01 — Concept of Gene, Alleles and Pseudoalleles

**Student-facing description:** Explain classical and molecular gene concepts, loci, allelic series, multiple alleles, pseudoalleles and the historical cistron, recon and muton.

**Core scope:** Gene as functional and sequence unit with regulatory and transcript complexity, locus, alleles, wild type and variant, multiple alleles, allelic series, tightly linked pseudoalleles and historical fine-structure concepts; distinguish cistron, recon and muton as experimental concepts, not necessarily universal physical units; bridge to 8.C.08.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare allele series and infer genetic versus physical units from classical observations.

**Progression:** Build on preceding Unit 8 subunits and relevant molecular foundations. Prepare 8.B.02.

### 8.B.02 — Complementation Tests and Functional Analysis of Genes

**Student-facing description:** Use cis–trans comparisons and complementation groups to distinguish allelic and non-allelic mutations, including limitations and intragenic complementation.

**Core scope:** Recessive mutant testing in diploids or suitable partial diploids, cis and trans configurations, functional restoration, complementation groups, matrices, intragenic complementation of multimeric products, non-allelic non-complementation, dominant mutation limitations; distinguish complementation from recombination and suppressor rescue.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Analyse complementation matrices, infer minimum groups and explain ambiguous outcomes.

**Progression:** Build on 8.B.01. Prepare 8.B.03.

### 8.B.03 — Molecular Basis and Causes of Mutations

**Student-facing description:** Trace spontaneous and induced mutations from base substitutions, insertions and deletions to frameshifts and altered coding or regulatory sequences, relating them to mutagenic mechanisms.

**Core scope:** Transition/transversion, synonymous/missense/nonsense, frameshift versus in-frame indel, splice and regulatory mutations, repeat expansion, replication errors, tautomerism, deamination, depurination, oxidative lesions, UV and ionising radiation, base analogues, alkylation and intercalation at conceptual level; distinguish lesion from fixed mutation and rates from frequencies; repair mechanisms cross-link Unit 3.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Predict coding consequences from sequences, interpret mutation spectra and distinguish spontaneous from induced changes.

**Progression:** Build on 8.B.02. Prepare 8.B.04.

### 8.B.04 — Functional Classification and Inheritance of Mutations

**Student-facing description:** Classify lethal, conditional, biochemical, loss-of-function, gain-of-function, dominant-negative and other mutant phenotypes, distinguishing germline and somatic transmission.

**Core scope:** Null, hypomorphic, hypermorphic, neomorphic and antimorphic categories, haploinsufficiency, dominant-negative versus simple loss of function, lethal ratios, temperature-sensitive conditional mutants, auxotrophs, germinal/germline and somatic mutations, mosaicism, effects of genetic background; phenotype and dominance depend on context.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Infer mutant class from dosage, rescue and conditional phenotype data, predict lethal segregation ratios and distinguish germline from somatic consequences.

**Progression:** Build on 8.B.03. Prepare 8.B.05.

### 8.B.05 — Mutation Detection and Genetic Screening

**Student-facing description:** Compare forward and reverse genetics, mutant selection and screening, replica plating, fluctuation and Ames tests, insertional mutagenesis, reversion and suppression.

**Core scope:** Selection versus screening, positive/negative selection and replica-plating logic, Luria–Delbrück fluctuation evidence and Lederberg replica plating, Ames assay logic and metabolic activation concept, molecular detection by sequencing and marker methods, insertional mutagenesis including transposon/T-DNA as conceptual examples, true reversion and intra/intergenic suppressors; no operational mutagenesis protocol.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret fluctuation distributions and screening results, distinguish pre-existing mutation from selection, compare detection and functional validation.

**Progression:** Build on 8.B.04. Bridge to subunit 8.C.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Menu descriptions and curriculum contracts only, not completed lessons. Do not add unpublished URLs to menu or sitemap or change chapter-completion metrics.
- Use original accessible connected English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, sequential explanations, diagrams, historical experiments, solved crosses, quantitative reasoning with assumptions, misconceptions, Part B and Part C-style practice with explanations. Each core topic must be developed, not merely listed.
- Preserve official scope and conceptual balance, not forced identical word counts. Use examples to explain mechanisms rather than catalogue every disorder or marker.
- Unit 2 owns chromosome structure and meiosis; Unit 3 owns DNA replication/repair/recombination biochemistry; Unit 4 signalling and cancer; Unit 5 development; Unit 7 endocrine/reproductive physiology; Unit 9 detailed evolution; Unit 11 computational workflows; Units 13–14 specialist techniques. Unit 8 owns transmission, inference, mapping and genetic consequences. Use brief prerequisite previews where the official sequence introduces a later topic early (e.g. cytogenetics before detailed chromosome rearrangements).
- No personal genetic diagnosis, treatment, or operational mutagenesis/gene-transfer protocols. Comparative historical and experimental analysis is educational.
