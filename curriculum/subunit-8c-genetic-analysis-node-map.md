## Subunit contract

Progress from linkage and tetrad maps to marker-based mapping and plant populations, then compare microbial gene transfer and fine-structure analysis as complementary experimental approaches.

**Legacy coverage:** Legacy D and F, including somatic-cell hybrid mapping and interrupted mating.

**Planned nodes:** 8. See [Unit 8 master map](unit-08-inheritance-biology-subunit-map.md) for complete traceability and cross-unit boundaries.

## Node contracts

### 8.C.01 — Linkage Mapping and Recombination Analysis

**Student-facing description:** Calculate two-point and three-point linkage maps, gene order, double crossovers, coefficient of coincidence, interference and mapping functions.

**Core scope:** Testcross classes and phase, two- and three-point map distance, identifying double crossovers and middle gene, interval-specific recombination, observed versus expected double crossovers, coincidence/interference, centimorgans, Haldane and Kosambi assumptions and mapping functions, map additivity versus recombination saturation.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Solve multi-step mapping datasets, compare observed and expected crossovers, identify limitations of genetic versus physical distances.

**Progression:** Build on preceding Unit 8 subunits and relevant molecular foundations. Prepare 8.C.02.

### 8.C.02 — Tetrad Analysis and Fungal Genetics

**Student-facing description:** Interpret ordered and unordered tetrads, parental and non-parental ditypes, tetratypes, centromere mapping and gene conversion.

**Core scope:** Asci and haploid meiotic products, ordered Neurospora and unordered yeast tetrads, first/second division segregation, gene-centromere distance as half second-division frequency, PD/NPD/T and linkage inference, Perkins mapping assumptions, 6:2 and 5:3 segregation in octads and gene conversion context.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate centromere and gene distances, interpret tetrad distributions and distinguish ordinary segregation from conversion.

**Progression:** Build on 8.C.01. Prepare 8.C.03.

### 8.C.03 — Molecular Markers and Genetic Mapping Methods

**Student-facing description:** Compare RFLP, RAPD, AFLP, SSR and SNP markers, genetic and physical maps and marker-based mapping in plants, animals and bacteria, including somatic cell hybrids.

**Core scope:** Marker polymorphism, dominant versus codominant scoring, RFLP, RAPD, AFLP, SSR and SNP principles, marker informativeness, segregation and linkage analysis, physical coordinates and genetic map comparison, applications across plants/animals/bacteria with organism-specific assumptions, somatic cell hybrid chromosome retention and concordance, radiation hybrids as context; distinguish marker association from causal gene.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Analyse marker genotype tables and hybrid concordance, choose appropriate markers and compare physical and linkage distances.

**Progression:** Build on 8.C.02. Prepare 8.C.04.

### 8.C.04 — Development of Genetic Mapping Populations

**Student-facing description:** Compare F2, backcross, recombinant inbred, doubled haploid and near-isogenic plant populations, relating breeding design to segregation, recombination and mapping resolution.

**Core scope:** Parental polymorphism, F1/F2 and backcross designs, recombinant inbred lines, doubled haploids, near-isogenic lines, advanced intercross as context, expected genotype classes, fixation, recombination accumulation, population size and marker density, replication and environmental control, population suitability for linkage and QTL mapping; detailed QTL statistics in 8.E.05.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Predict segregation in each population, compare mapping power and choose a design for a stated trait or gene question.

**Progression:** Build on 8.C.03. Prepare 8.C.05.

### 8.C.05 — Bacterial Transformation and Genetic Mapping

**Student-facing description:** Explain natural competence and transformation evidence, relating DNA uptake, genetic integration and cotransformation frequency to bacterial gene mapping.

**Core scope:** Griffith and Avery–MacLeod–McCarty evidence, naked DNA uptake and competence, chromosomal integration versus plasmid establishment, selection and scoring concepts, linkage by cotransformation frequency, fragment length and marker distance, DNase sensitivity as experimental inference; avoid operational gene-transfer protocols.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret historical controls and cotransformation data, infer relative linkage and distinguish transformation from other transfer modes.

**Progression:** Build on 8.C.04. Prepare 8.C.06.

### 8.C.06 — Bacterial Conjugation, Sexduction and Interrupted Mating

**Student-facing description:** Compare F-positive, F-negative, Hfr and F-prime states, then explain conjugation, sexduction, partial diploidy and time-of-entry mapping.

**Core scope:** F factor and episome, plasmid transfer and chromosome-integrated Hfr, transfer origin and direction, incomplete transfer, F-prime excision and sexduction, merodiploids, interrupted mating and time-of-entry order, circular bacterial maps, selection as conceptual analysis; distinguish transfer time from physical base-pair distance.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Infer gene order and orientation from Hfr time-of-entry tables, compare F states and interpret partial diploid complementation.

**Progression:** Build on 8.C.05. Prepare 8.C.07.

### 8.C.07 — Bacteriophage-Mediated Transduction and Gene Mapping

**Student-facing description:** Compare generalized and specialized transduction, phage lysogeny and cotransduction, using genetic transfer evidence to infer bacterial linkage and gene order.

**Core scope:** Phage lytic/lysogenic context, generalized transfer by packaging errors and specialized transfer by aberrant prophage excision, transducing particles, restricted versus broad transferred loci, cotransduction frequency and packaging constraints, three-marker order and mapping assumptions, distinguish transformation and conjugation; no operational phage engineering.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret cotransduction frequencies and selected/unselected marker tables, infer linkage and compare transfer mechanisms.

**Progression:** Build on 8.C.06. Prepare 8.C.08.

### 8.C.08 — Fine-Structure Analysis of Genes

**Student-facing description:** Reconstruct Benzer's T4 rII genetic logic, relating complementation, intragenic recombination and fine-structure maps to functional gene units.

**Core scope:** Permissive/restrictive host logic, rIIA and rIIB complementation groups, cis–trans comparison, rare wild-type recombinants from intragenic crosses, deletion mapping and overlapping intervals, cistron/recon/muton historical context, distinguish functional rescue from sequence exchange, integrate eukaryotic and microbial mapping limits.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret conceptual rII cross and deletion-map data, calculate recombinant frequencies under stated recovery assumptions, distinguish mapping resolution from functional complementation.

**Progression:** Build on 8.C.07. Bridge to subunit 8.D.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Menu descriptions and curriculum contracts only, not completed lessons. Do not add unpublished URLs to menu or sitemap or change chapter-completion metrics.
- Use original accessible connected English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, sequential explanations, diagrams, historical experiments, solved crosses, quantitative reasoning with assumptions, misconceptions, Part B and Part C-style practice with explanations. Each core topic must be developed, not merely listed.
- Preserve official scope and conceptual balance, not forced identical word counts. Use examples to explain mechanisms rather than catalogue every disorder or marker.
- Unit 2 owns chromosome structure and meiosis; Unit 3 owns DNA replication/repair/recombination biochemistry; Unit 4 signalling and cancer; Unit 5 development; Unit 7 endocrine/reproductive physiology; Unit 9 detailed evolution; Unit 11 computational workflows; Units 13–14 specialist techniques. Unit 8 owns transmission, inference, mapping and genetic consequences. Use brief prerequisite previews where the official sequence introduces a later topic early (e.g. cytogenetics before detailed chromosome rearrangements).
- No personal genetic diagnosis, treatment, or operational mutagenesis/gene-transfer protocols. Comparative historical and experimental analysis is educational.
