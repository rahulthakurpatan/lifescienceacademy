## Subunit contract

Use family data, statistical linkage and cytogenetic evidence to understand human inheritance and representative genetic disorders without clinical diagnosis or individual counselling.

**Legacy coverage:** Legacy G.

## Node contracts

### 8.D.01 — Human Pedigree Analysis and Inheritance Patterns

**Student-facing description:** Interpret standard pedigrees for autosomal, sex-linked and mitochondrial transmission, calculating carrier probabilities and conditional recurrence risks.

**Core scope:** Pedigree conventions, autosomal dominant/recessive, X-linked dominant/recessive, Y-linked and mitochondrial patterns, consanguinity context, de novo variants, variable penetrance, phenocopy and small-family ambiguity, conditional probability and Bayes reasoning, distinguish theoretical recurrence from individual clinical risk.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Solve pedigree and carrier-probability exercises, compare plausible inheritance models and state assumptions.

**Progression:** Build on preceding Unit 8 subunits and relevant molecular foundations. Prepare 8.D.02.

### 8.D.02 — Human Linkage Analysis and LOD Scores

**Student-facing description:** Calculate and interpret LOD scores from human family data, relating likelihood ratios and recombination fractions to evidence for or against linkage.

**Core scope:** Likelihood under recombination fraction theta versus 0.5, log10 odds, informative meioses and phase, known-phase simplified counts, combined independent family LODs, conventional +3/-2 thresholds and their scope, maximum-likelihood theta, sex-specific and pedigree uncertainty context, linkage versus association.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate LOD values, combine independent family evidence, interpret score curves and distinguish odds from posterior probability.

**Progression:** Build on 8.D.01. Prepare 8.D.03.

### 8.D.03 — Human Karyotypes and Cytogenetic Analysis

**Student-facing description:** Identify human chromosome organisation, karyotype notation, banding and FISH evidence, linking cytogenetic observations with representative structural and numerical abnormalities.

**Core scope:** Human diploid complement, chromosome morphology and centromere position, karyogram versus karyotype, G-banding, representative notation (46,XX; 47,XY,+21), mosaicism, FISH and microarray comparison at conceptual level, balanced/unbalanced rearrangements and resolution limits; detailed meiotic mechanisms in 8.F.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Read schematic karyotypes and FISH patterns, compare test resolution and distinguish visible chromosome changes from sequence variants.

**Progression:** Build on 8.D.02. Prepare 8.D.04.

### 8.D.04 — Genetic Disorders and Human Disease Inheritance

**Student-facing description:** Compare monogenic, chromosomal, mitochondrial and multifactorial disorders, linking representative disease phenotypes with gene function, inheritance, repeat expansion and imprinting.

**Core scope:** Sickle cell disease, thalassaemia, haemophilia, cystic fibrosis, representative dominant and X-linked disorders, mitochondrial examples, trisomy 21, Turner and Klinefelter contexts, imprinting and repeat expansion (e.g. Huntington and fragile X), anticipation and locus/allelic heterogeneity, multifactorial risk and genetic versus environmental contribution; no clinical testing or treatment recommendations.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Connect molecular defects to inheritance patterns, compare mechanisms across disease examples and interpret hypothetical pedigrees without individual diagnosis.

**Progression:** Build on 8.D.03. Bridge to subunit 8.E.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Menu descriptions and curriculum contracts only, not completed lessons. Do not add unpublished URLs to menu or sitemap or change chapter-completion metrics.
- Use original accessible connected English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, sequential explanations, diagrams, historical experiments, solved crosses, quantitative reasoning with assumptions, misconceptions, Part B and Part C-style practice with explanations. Each core topic must be developed, not merely listed.
- Preserve official scope and conceptual balance, not forced identical word counts. Use examples to explain mechanisms rather than catalogue every disorder or marker.
- Unit 2 owns chromosome structure and meiosis; Unit 3 owns DNA replication/repair/recombination biochemistry; Unit 4 signalling and cancer; Unit 5 development; Unit 7 endocrine/reproductive physiology; Unit 9 detailed evolution; Unit 11 computational workflows; Units 13–14 specialist techniques. Unit 8 owns transmission, inference, mapping and genetic consequences. Use brief prerequisite previews where the official sequence introduces a later topic early (e.g. cytogenetics before detailed chromosome rearrangements).
- No personal genetic diagnosis, treatment, or operational mutagenesis/gene-transfer protocols. Comparative historical and experimental analysis is educational.
