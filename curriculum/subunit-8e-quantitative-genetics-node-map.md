## Subunit contract

Progress from population allele frequencies and Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium to quantitative traits, variance components, heritability, selection response and molecular mapping.

**Legacy coverage:** Legacy H plus revised population genetics and molecular mapping.

**Planned nodes:** 5. See [Unit 8 master map](unit-08-inheritance-biology-subunit-map.md) for complete traceability and cross-unit boundaries.

## Node contracts

### 8.E.01 — Population Genetics and Hardy–Weinberg Equilibrium

**Student-facing description:** Calculate allele and genotype frequencies, explain Hardy–Weinberg assumptions and apply equilibrium models to multiple alleles and carrier-frequency problems.

**Core scope:** Gene pool, allele counts and diploid genotype frequencies, p+q=1, p²+2pq+q²=1, random mating and assumptions for stable allele frequencies, equilibrium after random mating under ideal autosomal model, multiple alleles, X-linked context, observed versus expected counts and chi-square test, rare recessive carrier approximation and its limits.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate frequencies and expected counts, test equilibrium and compare exact versus approximate carrier estimates.

**Progression:** Build on preceding Unit 8 subunits and relevant molecular foundations. Prepare 8.E.02.

### 8.E.02 — Genetic Structure and Departure from Population Equilibrium

**Student-facing description:** Explain non-random mating, inbreeding, population subdivision and linkage disequilibrium, introducing selection, mutation, migration and drift as causes of changing genetic structure.

**Core scope:** Inbreeding coefficient F and genotype frequencies, assortative mating, Wahlund effect, subdivision and heterozygosity, linkage disequilibrium D and r² at introductory level, recombination decay and haplotype frequency, selection, mutation, migration and drift as departures from equilibrium assumptions; distinguish nonrandom mating effects on genotype versus allele frequencies, effective size as context; full evolution mechanisms in Unit 9.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate inbreeding-adjusted genotype frequencies and simple LD measures, interpret population mixture and distinguish demographic explanations.

**Progression:** Build on 8.E.01. Prepare 8.E.03.

### 8.E.03 — Polygenic Inheritance and Quantitative Trait Variation

**Student-facing description:** Connect multiple loci, additive and non-additive effects and environmental variation with continuous traits, threshold traits and quantitative phenotype distributions.

**Core scope:** Discrete versus continuous traits, additive polygenes and classical wheat kernel colour example, equal-effect model assumptions and 2n+1 phenotypic classes, distributions and environmental variance, gene–environment interaction, dominance and epistasis as non-additive effects, threshold and liability concepts, quantitative versus multifactorial traits.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Predict phenotype class counts and frequencies, interpret distributions and compare genetic and environmental explanations.

**Progression:** Build on 8.E.02. Prepare 8.E.04.

### 8.E.04 — Genetic Variance, Heritability and Response to Selection

**Student-facing description:** Partition phenotypic variation into genetic and environmental components, distinguishing broad- and narrow-sense heritability, regression-based estimates and predicted selection response.

**Core scope:** VP, VG, VE, additive VA, dominance VD and interaction VI, genotype–environment interaction and covariance caveats, H² and h², twin and family designs and parent–offspring regression assumptions, selection differential S, response R=h²S, realised heritability and limitations, repeatability context; heritability is population/environment-specific, not individual genetic destiny.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate heritability, regression and breeder's-equation predictions; critique design assumptions and interpret differences among environments.

**Progression:** Build on 8.E.03. Prepare 8.E.05.

### 8.E.05 — Quantitative Trait Loci and Molecular Mapping

**Student-facing description:** Use marker–trait associations, linkage-based QTL mapping, interval mapping, LOD profiles and introductory GWAS to connect quantitative phenotypes with genomic regions.

**Core scope:** QTL concepts, marker/phenotype data in mapping populations, single-marker analysis, interval mapping and LOD peaks, effect size and variance explained, confidence intervals, permutation-based threshold concept and multiple testing, linkage versus association mapping, LD and population structure in GWAS, polygenic architecture and validation, marker association versus causal variant; no advanced pipeline requirement.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret marker–trait tables and QTL plots, compare effect estimates and significance, identify confounding and explain validation strategies.

**Progression:** Build on 8.E.04. Bridge to subunit 8.F.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Menu descriptions and curriculum contracts only, not completed lessons. Do not add unpublished URLs to menu or sitemap or change chapter-completion metrics.
- Use original accessible connected English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, sequential explanations, diagrams, historical experiments, solved crosses, quantitative reasoning with assumptions, misconceptions, Part B and Part C-style practice with explanations. Each core topic must be developed, not merely listed.
- Preserve official scope and conceptual balance, not forced identical word counts. Use examples to explain mechanisms rather than catalogue every disorder or marker.
- Unit 2 owns chromosome structure and meiosis; Unit 3 owns DNA replication/repair/recombination biochemistry; Unit 4 signalling and cancer; Unit 5 development; Unit 7 endocrine/reproductive physiology; Unit 9 detailed evolution; Unit 11 computational workflows; Units 13–14 specialist techniques. Unit 8 owns transmission, inference, mapping and genetic consequences. Use brief prerequisite previews where the official sequence introduces a later topic early (e.g. cytogenetics before detailed chromosome rearrangements).
- No personal genetic diagnosis, treatment, or operational mutagenesis/gene-transfer protocols. Comparative historical and experimental analysis is educational.
