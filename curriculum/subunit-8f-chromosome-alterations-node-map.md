## Subunit contract

Explain recombination and transposition as sources of genome change, then trace deletions, duplications, inversions, translocations, aneuploidy and polyploidy through their meiotic and genetic consequences.

**Legacy coverage:** Legacy J and K, including non-homologous recombination and transposition.

**Planned nodes:** 6. See [Unit 8 master map](unit-08-inheritance-biology-subunit-map.md) for complete traceability and cross-unit boundaries.

## Node contracts

### 8.F.01 — Genetic Recombination and Transposition

**Student-facing description:** Compare homologous, non-homologous and site-specific recombination, unequal crossing over, gene conversion and transposition as sources of new genetic arrangements.

**Core scope:** Homologous recombination, reciprocal crossover and noncrossover outcomes, gene conversion, unequal crossing over, non-homologous end joining and rearrangement context, site-specific recombination, DNA transposons and retrotransposons, cut-and-paste versus copy-and-paste concepts, insertional effects and target-site duplication where applicable; distinguish mechanisms rather than equate all non-homologous events; molecular enzymology in Units 2–3.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace recombination outcomes and transposon-related genetic changes, distinguish gene conversion from reciprocal exchange and predict unequal crossover products.

**Progression:** Build on preceding Unit 8 subunits and relevant molecular foundations. Prepare 8.F.02.

### 8.F.02 — Chromosomal Deletions and Duplications

**Student-facing description:** Compare terminal and interstitial deletions and duplication arrangements, explaining gene dosage, pseudodominance, pairing behaviour and phenotypic effects.

**Core scope:** Chromosome segment loss and gain, terminal/interstitial deletions, tandem and displaced duplications, deficiency and duplication loops, pseudodominance, haploinsufficiency, dosage sensitivity, unequal crossing over, copy-number variation, deficiency mapping and viability context, distinction from whole-chromosome aneuploidy.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret pairing diagrams and deficiency mapping, predict dosage consequences and compare reciprocal unequal-crossover products.

**Progression:** Build on 8.F.01. Prepare 8.F.03.

### 8.F.03 — Chromosomal Inversions and Meiotic Consequences

**Student-facing description:** Distinguish paracentric and pericentric inversions, tracing inversion-loop pairing and crossover products to explain fertility effects and apparent recombination suppression.

**Core scope:** Inversions with or without centromere, balanced carriers and breakpoint effects, inversion heterozygote pairing, single crossover within paracentric inversion yielding dicentric/acentric products, pericentric duplication/deletion products, noncrossover and viable recombinant recovery, suppression of recovered recombination and evolutionary context, avoid assuming every inversion prevents crossing over.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Draw meiotic products, compare inversion types and predict recovery of balanced versus unbalanced gametes.

**Progression:** Build on 8.F.02. Prepare 8.F.04.

### 8.F.04 — Chromosomal Translocations and Genetic Rearrangements

**Student-facing description:** Trace reciprocal, non-reciprocal and Robertsonian translocations, relating meiotic pairing and segregation to fertility, position effects and genetic imbalance.

**Core scope:** Reciprocal exchanges and non-reciprocal rearrangements, Robertsonian fusion of acrocentric chromosomes, balanced versus unbalanced carriers, quadrivalent or trivalent pairing, alternate, adjacent-1/adjacent-2 and other segregation context, semisterility, pseudolinkage, gene disruption and position effects; human examples cross-link 8.D.03.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Predict segregation products from diagrams, distinguish balanced chromosome number from dosage balance, interpret fertility consequences without personal risk estimates.

**Progression:** Build on 8.F.03. Prepare 8.F.05.

### 8.F.05 — Aneuploidy and Chromosomal Nondisjunction

**Student-facing description:** Explain chromosome-specific gains and losses through meiotic and mitotic nondisjunction, relating monosomy, trisomy, nullisomy, tetrasomy and mosaicism to gene dosage.

**Core scope:** Euploid baseline versus aneuploid states, 2n±1 and 2n±2 distinctions, meiosis I versus II nondisjunction, anaphase lag and mitotic errors, gamete classes, postzygotic mosaicism, dosage imbalance, representative autosomal and sex-chromosome examples, chromosome rescue and uniparental disomy as context, viability differences in plants and animals.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Derive gamete and zygote classes for nondisjunction, distinguish nullisomy from double monosomy and interpret mosaic patterns.

**Progression:** Build on 8.F.04. Prepare 8.F.06.

### 8.F.06 — Euploidy, Polyploidy and Genome-Level Inheritance

**Student-facing description:** Compare haploidy, monoploidy, autopolyploidy and allopolyploidy, explaining chromosome doubling, meiotic pairing, fertility, genome evolution and plant breeding relevance.

**Core scope:** Basic chromosome number x versus gametic n and somatic 2n, haploids and monoploids, triploids and tetraploids, unreduced gametes and chromosome doubling, auto/allopolyploid origins, multivalent versus preferential bivalent pairing, polysomic versus disomic inheritance, double reduction as context, fertility and segregation, wheat and Brassica examples and conceptual plant breeding applications; distinguish polyploidy from aneuploidy.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate chromosome complements, compare pairing and inheritance models, explain restoration or loss of fertility and genome-level consequences.

**Progression:** Build on 8.F.05. Integrate Unit 8 and bridge to Unit 9 evolution.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Menu descriptions and curriculum contracts only, not completed lessons. Do not add unpublished URLs to menu or sitemap or change chapter-completion metrics.
- Use original accessible connected English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, sequential explanations, diagrams, historical experiments, solved crosses, quantitative reasoning with assumptions, misconceptions, Part B and Part C-style practice with explanations. Each core topic must be developed, not merely listed.
- Preserve official scope and conceptual balance, not forced identical word counts. Use examples to explain mechanisms rather than catalogue every disorder or marker.
- Unit 2 owns chromosome structure and meiosis; Unit 3 owns DNA replication/repair/recombination biochemistry; Unit 4 signalling and cancer; Unit 5 development; Unit 7 endocrine/reproductive physiology; Unit 9 detailed evolution; Unit 11 computational workflows; Units 13–14 specialist techniques. Unit 8 owns transmission, inference, mapping and genetic consequences. Use brief prerequisite previews where the official sequence introduces a later topic early (e.g. cytogenetics before detailed chromosome rearrangements).
