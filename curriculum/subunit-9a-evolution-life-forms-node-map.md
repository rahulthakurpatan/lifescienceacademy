## Subunit contract

Explore the origin and diversification of life, from prebiotic chemical evolution and early cellular systems to natural selection, molecular evolution, speciation, extinction and human evolution.

**Planned nodes:** 7. See [Unit 9 master map](unit-09-evolution-diversity-life-forms-subunit-map.md) for complete syllabus traceability and legacy crosswalk.

## Node contracts

### 9.A.01 — Foundations and Historical Development of Evolutionary Biology

**Student-facing description:** Trace Lamarckism, Darwin–Wallace theory, evidence of evolution, Mendelian inheritance, spontaneous mutation, reproductive fitness and the modern evolutionary synthesis.

**Core scope:** Historical context, variation, inheritance, struggle for existence, differential reproduction, adaptation and fitness; Lamarck's historical proposals versus modern evidence, Darwin and Wallace, fossils, biogeography, comparative anatomy, homologous and analogous structures, vestiges, embryology and molecular evidence; Mendelism, mutation spontaneity and modern synthesis; distinguish evolution of populations from individual acclimation, and fitness from strength.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare historical predictions, interpret evidence and Luria–Delbrück results in conjunction with 8.B.05; explain why adaptation is not goal-directed.

**Progression:** Build on cell biology, genetics and population genetics in earlier units. Prepare 9.A.02.

### 9.A.02 — Origin of Life and Evolution of Early Cellular Systems

**Student-facing description:** Connect early Earth and abiotic synthesis with the Oparin–Haldane hypothesis, Miller–Urey experiment, RNA world, protocells, LUCA, early prokaryotic evolution and the diversification of metabolism.

**Core scope:** Early Earth environments and uncertain atmospheric conditions; organic monomers and polymers, mineral surfaces and hydrothermal hypotheses, Miller's 1953 experimental conditions and limits, RNA catalysis and replication hypotheses, lipid vesicles, compartmentalisation and selection, competing origin-of-life models; distinguish LUCA from first life, early cellular evidence and uncertainty, anaerobic metabolism, chemolithotrophy, anoxygenic and oxygenic photosynthesis, Great Oxidation Event and aerobic respiration; avoid presenting hypotheses or dates of earliest life as settled.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret Miller–Urey controls and products, compare competing hypotheses, relate redox environments to metabolic possibilities and distinguish geochemical evidence from inference.

**Progression:** Build on 9.A.01. Prepare 9.A.03.

### 9.A.03 — Eukaryotic Evolution, Multicellularity and Geological History of Life

**Student-facing description:** Relate endosymbiosis, cellular compartmentalisation and multicellularity to the geological time scale, fossil evidence, major diversification events and mass extinctions.

**Core scope:** Mitochondrial and plastid ancestry, genome reduction and gene transfer, primary and secondary endosymbiosis, evolution of nucleus and cellular functions with unresolved hypotheses, unicellular and colonial eukaryotes, independent multicellular origins, adhesion, differentiation, tissues, organs and organ systems; eons, eras, periods, epochs, stratigraphy, fossilisation and radiometric dating, Ediacaran and Cambrian diversification, land colonisation, five major mass extinctions, biodiversity recovery, extinction selectivity and uncertainty; separate chronological evidence from causal hypotheses.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret fossil strata and half-life calculations, compare endosymbiotic evidence, reconstruct event timelines and analyse extinction/diversification graphs.

**Progression:** Build on 9.A.02. Prepare 9.A.04.

### 9.A.04 — Population Genetics and Mechanisms of Evolution

**Student-facing description:** Apply natural selection, relative fitness, mutation, genetic drift and gene flow to changing allele frequencies, including sexual selection, founder effects, bottlenecks and mutation–selection balance.

**Core scope:** Gene pool and Hardy–Weinberg baseline with 8.E cross-reference; relative fitness and selection coefficients, directional, stabilising, disruptive and balancing selection, heterozygote advantage, frequency dependence, sexual selection, mutation pressure and mutation–selection equilibrium under specified assumptions, migration, stochastic drift, fixation and loss, founder and bottleneck effects, effective population size and interaction among forces; distinguish adaptation from drift and genotype frequencies from allele-frequency changes.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate one-generation allele-frequency change under viability selection and migration, mutation–selection balance in simple models and drift probabilities; interpret selection and replicate population data.

**Progression:** Build on 9.A.03. Prepare 9.A.05.

### 9.A.05 — Speciation, Macroevolution and Coevolution

**Student-facing description:** Connect reproductive isolation and speciation with adaptive radiation, convergent and divergent evolution, evolutionary tempo, reciprocal selection and evolutionary arms races.

**Core scope:** Pre/postzygotic isolation, allopatric, peripatric, parapatric and sympatric models, gene flow, reinforcement, hybridisation, hybrid zones and polyploid speciation; adaptive radiation and ecological opportunity, convergence, parallelism, divergence, homologous and analogous features, adaptive modifications, gradualism and punctuated equilibrium, developmental constraints; coevolution of hosts and parasites, predators and prey, plants and pollinators, reciprocal selection, mutualistic coevolution and Red Queen hypothesis; distinguish co-occurrence from reciprocal evolutionary change.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret isolation and hybrid-zone data, compare speciation models, read radiation trees and assess evidence for reciprocal selection rather than assume every interaction is coevolution.

**Progression:** Build on 9.A.04. Prepare 9.A.06.

### 9.A.06 — Molecular Evolution and Evolution of Genomes

**Student-facing description:** Explain neutral and nearly neutral evolution, sequence divergence, molecular clocks, dN/dS, gene duplication and divergence, orthology, paralogy and the origin of new genes and proteins.

**Core scope:** Mutation versus substitution, neutral theory, nearly neutral selection and effective size, molecular divergence, protein and nucleotide sequence comparison, synonymous and nonsynonymous substitutions, dN/dS with assumptions and site/lineage caveats, clock calibration and rate variation, gene duplication, subfunctionalisation, neofunctionalisation, pseudogenisation, orthologues/paralogues, gene families, de novo genes and domain shuffling, horizontal transfer; molecular phylogeny applications cross-link 9.B.05 and computational methods Unit 11.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate simple clock and substitution ratios, interpret gene/species trees and duplication events, distinguish neutral expectations from evidence of selection.

**Progression:** Build on 9.A.05. Prepare 9.A.07.

### 9.A.07 — Primate and Human Evolution

**Student-facing description:** Trace primate and hominin diversification through fossil and molecular evidence, bipedalism, brain evolution, Homo lineages, human dispersal and archaic admixture.

**Core scope:** Primate diagnostic traits and branching relationships, strepsirrhines/haplorhines, apes and common ancestry, hominin fossils including Australopithecus, Paranthropus and representative Homo lineages, mosaic evolution of bipedality, dentition, brain and tool-related context, Homo sapiens origins and dispersal, Neanderthal and Denisovan admixture, fossil dating and genomic evidence; no linear progress ladder or biological hierarchy among modern human populations; unresolved phylogenetic placements identified.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret fossil character matrices and molecular trees, compare dating and admixture evidence, distinguish shared ancestry from descent from living apes.

**Progression:** Build on 9.A.06. Bridge to subunit 9.B.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only, not completed textbook chapters. No fabricated lesson links, sitemap modifications or chapter-completion counter changes.
- Accessible original connected academic English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, comparative tables, original diagrams and life cycles with ploidy/stages, phylogenies, geological timelines, evidence, quantitative examples and explained Part B/Part C-style questions.
- Retain all approved topic scopes, particularly merged 9.A and 9.G nodes. Organise dense chapters using internal sections; do not reduce breadth to equalise word counts. Compare traditional exam terminology with modern evidence and taxonomic uncertainty. Branching descent, not a ladder of modern organisms.
- Boundaries: Unit 1 biochemical pathways, Unit 2 cellular structure and microbial physiology, Unit 3 molecular mechanisms, Unit 4 pathogen/immune interactions, Unit 5 detailed development, Unit 6 plant physiology, Unit 7 animal physiology, Unit 8 inheritance and population-genetic prerequisites, Unit 10 detailed ecology/conservation/behaviour, Unit 11 computational algorithms, Unit 12 industrial biotechnology. Focus on evolutionary inference, diagnostic diversity, life cycles and comparative organisation.
- No clinical prescriptions or operational pathogen manipulation. Future lessons must distinguish established findings, models and hypotheses, and verify conservation status from authoritative sources.
