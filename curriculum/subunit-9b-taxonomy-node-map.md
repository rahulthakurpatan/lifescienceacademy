## Subunit contract

Understand how organisms are identified, named and classified using species concepts, taxonomic hierarchies, classical characters, quantitative approaches and molecular evidence of evolutionary relationships.

**Planned nodes:** 5. See [Unit 9 master map](unit-09-evolution-diversity-life-forms-subunit-map.md) for complete syllabus traceability and legacy crosswalk.

## Node contracts

### 9.B.01 — Foundations of Taxonomy, Systematics and Species Concepts

**Student-facing description:** Distinguish taxonomy, systematics, identification and classification, explaining hierarchical taxa and morphological, biological, ecological and phylogenetic species concepts.

**Core scope:** Taxon and rank, domain through species and infraspecific categories, classification versus identification, species delimitation, morphological/biological/ecological/phylogenetic concepts, reproductive isolation, cryptic species, fossils, asexual and microbial limitations, artificial/natural/phylogenetic classification, integrative taxonomy.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare species concepts against representative plant, animal and microbial cases; identify limitations and rank relationships.

**Progression:** Build on evolutionary principles and relevant preceding diversity/classification concepts. Prepare 9.B.02.

### 9.B.02 — Biological Nomenclature and Taxonomic Documentation

**Student-facing description:** Explain binomial nomenclature, typification, priority, synonyms, homonyms, author citation and the roles of taxonomic codes, herbaria, museums and reference collections.

**Core scope:** Scientific name formatting, type specimens and type strains, priority and exceptions, synonymy and homonymy, author citations and valid publication concepts; ICZN, ICN for algae/fungi/plants, ICNP for prokaryotes, ICTV virus taxonomy and nomenclature as distinct systems; vouchers, herbarium/museum records and reference databases, code-specific rules rather than assume identical rules.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret example names and type records, distinguish identification from nomenclature and resolve simplified priority scenarios under specified codes.

**Progression:** Build on 9.B.01. Prepare 9.B.03.

### 9.B.03 — Classical Taxonomy and Organismal Identification

**Student-facing description:** Compare morphological, anatomical, embryological, cytological, chemical and serological characters and microbial phenotypes, then use diagnostic characters and identification keys.

**Core scope:** Homology versus analogy in character selection, morphology and comparative anatomy, embryology, cytotaxonomy and chromosome counts, chemotaxonomy and secondary metabolites, serotaxonomy, bacterial staining/biochemical phenotypes, diagnostic traits across plants, animals and microbes, dichotomous keys and character variability, voucher-based documentation.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Construct and use simple keys, compare character matrices and explain convergence, plasticity and sampling limitations.

**Progression:** Build on 9.B.02. Prepare 9.B.04.

### 9.B.04 — Numerical Taxonomy and Quantitative Classification

**Student-facing description:** Apply phenetic character coding, operational taxonomic units, similarity coefficients, cluster analysis, dendrograms and ordination to quantitative classification.

**Core scope:** OTUs in numerical taxonomy, binary and continuous characters, scaling and weighting, missing data, simple matching and Jaccard coefficients, distance matrices, UPGMA and clustering principles, dendrogram interpretation, ordination as visualisation, reproducibility and sensitivity to character choice; phenograms versus phylogenetic trees.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Calculate similarity coefficients, perform small hand-worked clustering examples and interpret dendrograms without assuming evolutionary ancestry.

**Progression:** Build on 9.B.03. Prepare 9.B.05.

### 9.B.05 — Molecular Taxonomy, Cladistics and Phylogenetic Analysis

**Student-facing description:** Connect DNA barcoding, rRNA and ITS markers, genomic similarity and sequence comparison with cladistics, monophyly, tree construction and support assessment.

**Core scope:** COI, rbcL/matK, 16S/18S rRNA and ITS examples, marker selection and resolution, ANI for prokaryotes with contextual thresholds, nucleotide and protein sequence analysis, homology, ancestral/derived characters, synapomorphies, outgroups, mono-/para-/polyphyly, cladograms and branch lengths, parsimony/distance/likelihood principles and bootstrap support, gene-tree/species-tree discordance, integrative evidence; detailed algorithms Unit 11.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Read rotated and rooted trees, identify clades and shared derived traits, compare barcode/ANI results and interpret bootstrap support without confusing it with posterior probability.

**Progression:** Build on 9.B.04. Bridge to subunit 9.C.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only, not completed textbook chapters. No fabricated lesson links, sitemap modifications or chapter-completion counter changes.
- Accessible original connected academic English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, comparative tables, original diagrams and life cycles with ploidy/stages, phylogenies, geological timelines, evidence, quantitative examples and explained Part B/Part C-style questions.
- Retain all approved topic scopes, particularly merged 9.A and 9.G nodes. Organise dense chapters using internal sections; do not reduce breadth to equalise word counts. Compare traditional exam terminology with modern evidence and taxonomic uncertainty. Branching descent, not a ladder of modern organisms.
- Boundaries: Unit 1 biochemical pathways, Unit 2 cellular structure and microbial physiology, Unit 3 molecular mechanisms, Unit 4 pathogen/immune interactions, Unit 5 detailed development, Unit 6 plant physiology, Unit 7 animal physiology, Unit 8 inheritance and population-genetic prerequisites, Unit 10 detailed ecology/conservation/behaviour, Unit 11 computational algorithms, Unit 12 industrial biotechnology. Focus on evolutionary inference, diagnostic diversity, life cycles and comparative organisation.
- No clinical prescriptions or operational pathogen manipulation. Future lessons must distinguish established findings, models and hypotheses, and verify conservation status from authoritative sources.
