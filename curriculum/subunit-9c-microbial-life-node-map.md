## Subunit contract

Compare the structural, evolutionary and metabolic diversity of bacteria and archaea, explore viral organisation and replication, and explain the ecological, economic, agricultural and pathological significance of microorganisms.

**Planned nodes:** 6. See [Unit 9 master map](unit-09-evolution-diversity-life-forms-subunit-map.md) for complete syllabus traceability and legacy crosswalk.

## Node contracts

### 9.C.01 — Bacterial Diversity and Classification

**Student-facing description:** Compare bacterial morphology, cell-envelope organisation, major bacterial lineages and representative diagnostic criteria, relating traditional names to modern phylogenetic classification.

**Core scope:** Shapes, arrangements, Gram-positive/negative envelope patterns and exceptions, endospores as relevant diagnostic features, Pseudomonadota/Proteobacteria, Bacillota/Firmicutes, Actinomycetota/Actinobacteria, Bacteroidota, Cyanobacteria, Spirochaetota, Chlamydiota and representative bacteria, molecular versus phenotypic classification, naming changes and sources; detailed growth and cell structures in Unit 2.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare diagnostic traits and phylogenetic trees, identify why Gram reaction does not uniquely define an evolutionary clade.

**Progression:** Build on evolutionary principles and relevant preceding diversity/classification concepts. Prepare 9.C.02.

### 9.C.02 — Archaeal Diversity and Evolutionary Significance

**Student-facing description:** Explore archaeal cellular organisation, membrane chemistry and diversity, from methanogens and extremophiles to widely distributed archaea and their relationships with eukaryotes.

**Core scope:** Ether-linked isoprenoid membranes, cell walls and S-layers, absence of bacterial peptidoglycan, information-processing features, methanogens, extreme halophiles, thermophiles, ammonia oxidisers and non-extremophilic archaea; representative traditional and modern archaeal lineages including Asgard, evidence for archaeal ancestry of eukaryotic nuclear lineage, unresolved branches and nomenclature, comparative ecology.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare archaeal and bacterial features, relate membrane chemistry to adaptation without assuming all archaea are extremophiles, interpret simplified phylogenies.

**Progression:** Build on 9.C.01. Prepare 9.C.03.

### 9.C.03 — Microbial Metabolic Diversity and Ecological Roles

**Student-facing description:** Relate microbial nutritional and metabolic strategies to nitrogen and sulfur transformations, methanogenesis, decomposition, symbiosis and biogeochemical cycling.

**Core scope:** Energy/electron/carbon source classification, phototrophy and chemotrophy, autotrophy and heterotrophy, aerobic/anaerobic respiration and fermentation context, oxygenic/anoxygenic photosynthesis, nitrogen fixation, nitrification, denitrification, anammox, sulfur transformations, methanogenesis and methane oxidation, decomposition, syntrophy and symbiosis, aquatic and soil microbiomes; ecological functions rather than repeat Unit 1 pathways or Unit 10 ecosystem budgets.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Classify organisms from resource use, trace microbial transformations in cycles and infer metabolic niches from oxygen/redox gradients.

**Progression:** Build on 9.C.02. Prepare 9.C.04.

### 9.C.04 — Virus Structure, Diversity and Classification

**Student-facing description:** Compare viral genomes, capsid and envelope organisation, bacteriophages, Baltimore groups and ICTV taxonomy, distinguishing viruses from viroids, satellite agents and prions.

**Core scope:** Acellular viral organisation, genome types including segmentation and polarity, helical/icosahedral/complex capsids, envelopes and tropism context, DNA/RNA and reverse-transcribing viruses, seven Baltimore groups by route to mRNA, ICTV taxonomic ranks as distinct classification, representative plant/animal/bacterial viruses, viroids, satellite dependence and prions as different entities; avoid treating all infectious agents as viruses.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Classify representative genomes by mRNA-generation strategy, compare structural diagrams and distinguish taxonomy from replication grouping.

**Progression:** Build on 9.C.03. Prepare 9.C.05.

### 9.C.05 — Viral Replication and Evolutionary Impact

**Student-facing description:** Trace attachment, entry, genome expression, replication, assembly and release, comparing lytic and lysogenic cycles, retroviruses, viral variation and effects on host evolution and ecosystems.

**Core scope:** General infection-cycle stages with host and genome dependence, phage lysis and lysogeny, prophages, retroviral reverse transcription and integration, RNA and DNA replication strategies conceptually, reassortment versus recombination and mutation, host range, viral ecological effects and horizontal transfer, marine viral shunt and evolutionary interactions; no operational virus culture, engineering or enhancement methods; Unit 3 gene expression and Unit 4 host response own mechanistic depth.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare replication diagrams and genome-to-mRNA pathways, distinguish latency and lysogeny, interpret conceptual variation and ecological evidence.

**Progression:** Build on 9.C.04. Prepare 9.C.06.

### 9.C.06 — Economic, Agricultural and Medical Importance of Microorganisms

**Student-facing description:** Connect beneficial and harmful microbes with fermentation, biofertilisers, antibiotics, bioremediation, spoilage and representative pathogens of humans, domestic animals and crops.

**Core scope:** Food fermentation, industrial products and antibiotics as examples, nitrogen-fixing and other biofertiliser organisms, microbial bioremediation, food spoilage, pathogen versus commensal distinction, representative bacterial/viral diseases of humans, livestock and plants (tuberculosis, cholera, mastitis, foot-and-mouth disease, bacterial blights and plant mosaic viruses as examples), causative organism, host, transmission ecology and general prevention concepts; no clinical protocols or pathogen manipulation; fungal/protist pathogens in 9.D/E, Unit 12 production processes.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Match microbes to ecological/economic functions and diseases, compare transmission categories and evaluate beneficial-versus-harmful context.

**Progression:** Build on 9.C.05. Bridge to subunit 9.D.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only, not completed textbook chapters. No fabricated lesson links, sitemap modifications or chapter-completion counter changes.
- Accessible original connected academic English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, comparative tables, original diagrams and life cycles with ploidy/stages, phylogenies, geological timelines, evidence, quantitative examples and explained Part B/Part C-style questions.
- Retain all approved topic scopes, particularly merged 9.A and 9.G nodes. Organise dense chapters using internal sections; do not reduce breadth to equalise word counts. Compare traditional exam terminology with modern evidence and taxonomic uncertainty. Branching descent, not a ladder of modern organisms.
- Boundaries: Unit 1 biochemical pathways, Unit 2 cellular structure and microbial physiology, Unit 3 molecular mechanisms, Unit 4 pathogen/immune interactions, Unit 5 detailed development, Unit 6 plant physiology, Unit 7 animal physiology, Unit 8 inheritance and population-genetic prerequisites, Unit 10 detailed ecology/conservation/behaviour, Unit 11 computational algorithms, Unit 12 industrial biotechnology. Focus on evolutionary inference, diagnostic diversity, life cycles and comparative organisation.
- No clinical prescriptions or operational pathogen manipulation. Future lessons must distinguish established findings, models and hypotheses, and verify conservation status from authoritative sources.
