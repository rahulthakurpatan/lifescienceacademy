## Subunit contract

Explore the diverse eukaryotic lineages traditionally grouped as protists, comparing their cellular organisation, nutritional strategies, reproduction, evolutionary relationships and ecological significance.

**Planned nodes:** 5. See [Unit 9 master map](unit-09-evolution-diversity-life-forms-subunit-map.md) for complete syllabus traceability and legacy crosswalk.

## Node contracts

### 9.D.01 — Protist Diversity and Eukaryotic Classification

**Student-facing description:** Compare protist cellular organisation, locomotion, nutrition and reproduction, connecting traditional groupings with modern eukaryotic lineages and plastid origins.

**Core scope:** Protist as informal non-monophyletic grouping, unicellular/colonial/multicellular forms, cilia, flagella, pseudopodia, feeding and mixotrophy, sexual/asexual reproduction and life-cycle terminology, major eukaryotic supergroup frameworks including Archaeplastida, SAR, Amoebozoa, opisthokont protists and representative excavate-related lineages, primary/secondary plastids and endosymbiosis, classification uncertainty.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare cellular traits and trees, trace plastid origin scenarios and distinguish lifestyle categories from clades.

**Progression:** Build on evolutionary principles and relevant preceding diversity/classification concepts. Prepare 9.D.02.

### 9.D.02 — Green Algae, Red Algae and Primary Plastid Lineages

**Student-facing description:** Compare chlorophytes, streptophyte algae, red algae and glaucophytes through pigments, storage products, thallus organisation, reproduction and relationships with land plants.

**Core scope:** Primary plastid lineages, chlorophyll/pigment and storage differences, cell coverings, unicellular to multicellular organisation, Chlamydomonas, Volvox, Ulva, Spirogyra, Chara, representative red algae such as Polysiphonia and glaucophytes; haplontic, diplontic and haplodiplontic life cycles and red algal triphasic context, oogamy and alternation of generations; streptophyte affinity of land plants.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare pigment and storage tables, trace ploidy transitions and interpret relationships to embryophytes.

**Progression:** Build on 9.D.01. Prepare 9.D.03.

### 9.D.03 — Diatoms, Brown Algae and Other Photosynthetic Protists

**Student-facing description:** Explore stramenopile algae, diatoms, brown algae, dinoflagellates, euglenids, haptophytes and cryptophytes, relating plastid diversity and life cycles to aquatic productivity and blooms.

**Core scope:** Diatom silica frustules, size restoration and auxospores, brown algal thalli, pigments, laminarin and representative Ectocarpus/Laminaria/Fucus life cycles; dinoflagellate organisation, symbiosis and bloom diversity, euglenid mixotrophy, haptophytes and cryptophytes, plastid ancestry and caveats, plankton production and harmful blooms (not all blooms toxic).

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare photosynthetic lineages and ploidy cycles, interpret bloom/productivity data and distinguish shared pigments from close ancestry.

**Progression:** Build on 9.D.02. Prepare 9.D.04.

### 9.D.04 — Protozoan Diversity, Life Cycles and Parasitism

**Student-facing description:** Compare amoeboid, ciliate, kinetoplastid, apicomplexan and diplomonad diversity, examining locomotion, reproduction and representative free-living and parasitic life cycles.

**Core scope:** Amoeba, Paramecium and ciliate nuclear dimorphism/conjugation, amoeboid diversity, Entamoeba, Giardia, Plasmodium, Leishmania and Trypanosoma as representative examples; kinetoplast, apical complex and life-cycle stages at comparative level, host/vector relationships, parasitic and free-living adaptations, domestic animal and human disease examples; no pathogen culture or clinical treatment guidance.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare locomotion and reproduction, trace host and vector stages from life-cycle diagrams and identify differences between parasitic lineages.

**Progression:** Build on 9.D.03. Prepare 9.D.05.

### 9.D.05 — Slime Molds, Water Molds and Protist Ecology

**Student-facing description:** Compare cellular and plasmodial slime molds with oomycetes, explaining developmental life cycles, decomposition, aquatic food webs, symbiosis and agricultural significance.

**Core scope:** Dictyostelium aggregation and fruiting bodies, Physarum plasmodia and spores, amoebozoan affinities, oomycete stramenopile relationships, diploid vegetative context, cellulose-rich walls and zoospores, Phytophthora, Pythium and Saprolegnia as representative plant/aquatic pathogens, contrast fungi and fungus-like protists, grazers and decomposers, trophic interactions, symbiosis and nutrient cycling.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare life cycles, differentiate true fungi from oomycetes and explain the ecological roles of protists using food-web data.

**Progression:** Build on 9.D.04. Bridge to subunit 9.E.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only, not completed textbook chapters. No fabricated lesson links, sitemap modifications or chapter-completion counter changes.
- Accessible original connected academic English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, comparative tables, original diagrams and life cycles with ploidy/stages, phylogenies, geological timelines, evidence, quantitative examples and explained Part B/Part C-style questions.
- Retain all approved topic scopes, particularly merged 9.A and 9.G nodes. Organise dense chapters using internal sections; do not reduce breadth to equalise word counts. Compare traditional exam terminology with modern evidence and taxonomic uncertainty. Branching descent, not a ladder of modern organisms.
- Boundaries: Unit 1 biochemical pathways, Unit 2 cellular structure and microbial physiology, Unit 3 molecular mechanisms, Unit 4 pathogen/immune interactions, Unit 5 detailed development, Unit 6 plant physiology, Unit 7 animal physiology, Unit 8 inheritance and population-genetic prerequisites, Unit 10 detailed ecology/conservation/behaviour, Unit 11 computational algorithms, Unit 12 industrial biotechnology. Focus on evolutionary inference, diagnostic diversity, life cycles and comparative organisation.
- No clinical prescriptions or operational pathogen manipulation. Future lessons must distinguish established findings, models and hypotheses, and verify conservation status from authoritative sources.
