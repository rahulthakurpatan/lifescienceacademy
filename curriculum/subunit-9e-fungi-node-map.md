## Subunit contract

Understand fungal organisation, nutrition, reproduction and evolutionary relationships across major fungal lineages, connecting fungal diversity with ecological interactions, economic applications and diseases.

**Planned nodes:** 5. See [Unit 9 master map](unit-09-evolution-diversity-life-forms-subunit-map.md) for complete syllabus traceability and legacy crosswalk.

## Node contracts

### 9.E.01 — Foundations of Fungal Biology and Classification

**Student-facing description:** Explain fungal cell walls, hyphal and yeast organisation, absorptive nutrition, spores and sexual and asexual cycles, establishing a framework for fungal phylogeny.

**Core scope:** Chitin/glucan-rich walls, hyphae and mycelia, septate/coenocytic organisation, yeasts and dimorphism, absorptive nutrition and extracellular enzymes, spores and asexual propagation, plasmogamy, karyogamy, meiosis and dikaryotic stages, mating types, opisthokont affinity and fungal phylogeny, distinction from fungus-like protists.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Identify fungal structures and ploidy stages, compare nutritional strategies and reconstruct generic reproductive cycles.

**Progression:** Build on evolutionary principles and relevant preceding diversity/classification concepts. Prepare 9.E.02.

### 9.E.02 — Chytrids, Zygomycete Lineages and Glomeromycetes

**Student-facing description:** Compare chytrids, traditional zygomycetes and glomeromycetes using reproductive structures, life cycles, modern lineage relationships and mycorrhizal associations.

**Core scope:** Chytridiomycota and flagellated zoospores, representative aquatic/terrestrial forms and amphibian pathogen context, traditional zygomycetes as non-monophyletic grouping, Mucoromycota and Zoopagomycota frameworks with classification variation, Rhizopus/Mucor sporangia and zygospore formation, glomeromycetes and arbuscular mycorrhizae, arbuscules and nutrient exchange; modern classification alongside traditional exam terms.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare spores and hyphae, trace representative life cycles and interpret mycorrhizal nutrient-exchange evidence.

**Progression:** Build on 9.E.01. Prepare 9.E.03.

### 9.E.03 — Ascomycetes and Their Reproductive Diversity

**Student-facing description:** Trace sac-fungus reproduction, asci, ascospores, ascocarps and conidia through representative yeasts, filamentous fungi and model organisms.

**Core scope:** Ascomycota, ascus formation, dikaryotic ascogenous hyphae where applicable, croziers, ascospore counts and variation, ascocarps (apothecia, perithecia, cleistothecial/chasmothecial forms), conidia, Saccharomyces budding and life cycle, Neurospora ordered asci, Aspergillus and Penicillium, representative ecological and economic roles; tetrad mapping Unit 8.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Identify fruiting structures, trace ploidy and nuclear events and connect ascus organisation to genetic evidence.

**Progression:** Build on 9.E.02. Prepare 9.E.04.

### 9.E.04 — Basidiomycetes and Complex Fungal Life Cycles

**Student-facing description:** Compare basidia, basidiospores, dikaryotic mycelia, clamp connections and the reproductive cycles of mushrooms, rusts and smuts.

**Core scope:** Basidiomycota and representative lineages, primary and secondary mycelium, dikaryotic maintenance and clamps where present, basidiocarps and basidia, Agaricus life cycle, Puccinia heteroecious/macrocyclic rust example and spore stages, Ustilago smut cycle and host relationships, diversity and exceptions; distinguish dikaryotic from diploid stages.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace mushroom and rust life cycles, compare host alternation and spore functions, identify diagnostic reproductive structures.

**Progression:** Build on 9.E.03. Prepare 9.E.05.

### 9.E.05 — Fungal Ecology, Symbiosis and Pathological Importance

**Student-facing description:** Explain decomposition, lichens, mycorrhizae, endophytes, fungal applications and representative plant and human fungal diseases, connecting fungal diversity with ecological functions.

**Core scope:** Saprotrophy and decomposition, lichen fungal/photosynthetic partners and complex associations, ectomycorrhiza versus arbuscular mycorrhiza, endophytes, wood decay and nutrient cycling, fermentation and fungal-derived products, mycotoxins, crop pathogens including rusts, smuts, wilts and blast, Candida, Aspergillus and dermatophytes, distinction between infection, allergy and toxin exposure; no clinical or cultivation protocols.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare symbiotic structures and ecological roles, connect representative pathogens with host and disease category, interpret decomposition and nutrient-transfer studies.

**Progression:** Build on 9.E.04. Bridge to subunit 9.F.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only, not completed textbook chapters. No fabricated lesson links, sitemap modifications or chapter-completion counter changes.
- Accessible original connected academic English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, comparative tables, original diagrams and life cycles with ploidy/stages, phylogenies, geological timelines, evidence, quantitative examples and explained Part B/Part C-style questions.
- Retain all approved topic scopes, particularly merged 9.A and 9.G nodes. Organise dense chapters using internal sections; do not reduce breadth to equalise word counts. Compare traditional exam terminology with modern evidence and taxonomic uncertainty. Branching descent, not a ladder of modern organisms.
- Boundaries: Unit 1 biochemical pathways, Unit 2 cellular structure and microbial physiology, Unit 3 molecular mechanisms, Unit 4 pathogen/immune interactions, Unit 5 detailed development, Unit 6 plant physiology, Unit 7 animal physiology, Unit 8 inheritance and population-genetic prerequisites, Unit 10 detailed ecology/conservation/behaviour, Unit 11 computational algorithms, Unit 12 industrial biotechnology. Focus on evolutionary inference, diagnostic diversity, life cycles and comparative organisation.
- No clinical prescriptions or operational pathogen manipulation. Future lessons must distinguish established findings, models and hypotheses, and verify conservation status from authoritative sources.
