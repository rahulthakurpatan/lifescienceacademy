## Subunit contract

Trace plant evolution from aquatic ancestors to diverse land plants, comparing major plant lineages, morphological innovations, anatomical organisation, reproduction and adaptations to terrestrial environments.

**Planned nodes:** 8. See [Unit 9 master map](unit-09-evolution-diversity-life-forms-subunit-map.md) for complete syllabus traceability and legacy crosswalk.

## Node contracts

### 9.F.01 — Origin of Land Plants and Diversity of Bryophytes

**Student-facing description:** Relate streptophyte ancestry and terrestrial adaptations to alternation of generations and the diversity of liverworts, hornworts and mosses.

**Core scope:** Embryophyte ancestry and evidence, cuticle, protected embryos, sporopollenin and gametangia, stomatal distribution with exceptions, gametophyte-dominant life cycles, liverworts Marchantia/Riccia, hornworts Anthoceros, mosses Funaria/Sphagnum, sporophyte dependency, rhizoids, protonema and ecological functions, bryophyte phylogeny and caution against linear ladders.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace ploidy and water-dependent fertilisation, compare liverwort/hornwort/moss structures and identify adaptations from diagrams.

**Progression:** Build on evolutionary principles and relevant preceding diversity/classification concepts. Prepare 9.F.02.

### 9.F.02 — Seedless Vascular Plants and Evolution of Conducting Tissues

**Student-facing description:** Compare lycophytes, ferns and horsetails, connecting vascular tissues, stelar organisation, sporophyte dominance, heterospory and precursors of the seed habit.

**Core scope:** Tracheophyte innovations, lignification, roots and leaves, microphylls and megaphylls, protostele/siphonostele and representative stelar patterns, lycophytes Lycopodium/Selaginella, monilophytes including Equisetum, Pteris, Marsilea and representative fern allies, sporangia, sori, gametophytes, homo-/heterospory and seed-habit precursors, modern grouping versus traditional pteridophytes.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare steles and life cycles, trace spore and gametophyte ploidy, explain heterospory without implying modern seedless plants are direct ancestors.

**Progression:** Build on 9.F.01. Prepare 9.F.03.

### 9.F.03 — Gymnosperm Diversity and Evolution of Seeds

**Student-facing description:** Explain pollen, ovules and seed evolution through comparative study of cycads, Ginkgo, conifers and gnetophytes, connecting reproductive cycles with terrestrial adaptations.

**Core scope:** Seed habit, integumented megasporangium, pollen and reduced gametophytes, naked ovules relative to angiosperm carpels, cycads Cycas, Ginkgo, conifers Pinus and gnetophytes Ephedra/Gnetum/Welwitschia, cones and reproductive diversity, motile sperm in some lineages, pollination and fertilisation timing, seed development and storage tissue, ecological adaptations and evolutionary relationships.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare ovules, cones and life cycles, trace haploid/diploid tissues and distinguish seed from spore reproduction.

**Progression:** Build on 9.F.02. Prepare 9.F.04.

### 9.F.04 — Angiosperm Diversity and Evolutionary Classification

**Student-facing description:** Compare early-diverging angiosperms, magnoliids, monocots and eudicots, interpreting flowering-plant diversification, APG classification and diagnostic characters of representative families.

**Core scope:** Angiosperm innovations, flowers, enclosed ovules, double fertilisation preview, early-diverging lineages, magnoliids, monocots and eudicots, APG framework, traditional dicot terminology limitations, representative families including Poaceae, Fabaceae, Brassicaceae, Solanaceae, Asteraceae, Liliaceae and Orchidaceae as appropriate examples, diagnostic floral/vegetative characters, economic examples; family delimitations verified during full lesson research.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Use floral character matrices and simplified phylogenies, distinguish monocot/eudicot patterns from absolute rules and identify family-level characters.

**Progression:** Build on 9.F.03. Prepare 9.F.05.

### 9.F.05 — Plant Morphology and Adaptive Modifications

**Student-facing description:** Compare roots, stems, leaves, inflorescences, flowers, fruits and seeds, using floral diagrams and formulae to explain structural diversity and adaptive modifications.

**Core scope:** Root and shoot forms, adventitious structures, stem and leaf modifications, phyllotaxy and venation, inflorescence types, flower symmetry, merosity, aestivation, placentation, ovary position, floral formulae and diagrams, fruit and seed types, dispersal context, xerophytic/hydrophytic and other adaptive morphology; distinguish homologous structures from convergent functions.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret floral formulae and diagrams, identify organ modifications and relate morphology to pollination, dispersal and habitat.

**Progression:** Build on 9.F.04. Prepare 9.F.06.

### 9.F.06 — Plant Anatomy and Structural Organisation

**Student-facing description:** Trace meristems, tissues and vascular organisation in roots, stems and leaves, connecting primary and secondary growth, wood, periderm and anomalous growth with plant structure.

**Core scope:** Meristematic and permanent tissues, parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma, xylem and phloem, epidermis/stomata, root and stem anatomy of representative monocots and eudicots, leaf anatomy including dorsiventral/isobilateral examples, vascular cambium and cork cambium, secondary xylem/phloem, wood and annual rings with environmental caveats, anomalous secondary growth and adaptive anatomy; physiology in Unit 6.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Read transverse sections, compare vascular patterns, identify developmental origin of tissues and interpret wood-ring and adaptive-anatomy diagrams.

**Progression:** Build on 9.F.05. Prepare 9.F.07.

### 9.F.07 — Plant Reproduction and Comparative Life Cycles

**Student-facing description:** Integrate vegetative reproduction and alternation of generations with pollination, fertilisation, seed and fruit formation, apomixis and reproductive adaptations.

**Core scope:** Vegetative propagation, comparative gametophyte/sporophyte dominance across bryophytes, seedless vascular plants and seed plants, micro/megagametophytes, pollination modes and syndromes, double fertilisation, zygote/endosperm ploidy with exceptions, seed and fruit formation, apomixis and polyembryony as relevant context, reproductive diversity and adaptations; detailed developmental mechanisms Unit 5 and physiology Unit 6.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare complete life-cycle diagrams and ploidy, interpret pollination and reproductive adaptations, distinguish apomixis from ordinary sexual seed formation.

**Progression:** Build on 9.F.06. Prepare 9.F.08.

### 9.F.08 — Indian Plant Diversity, Phenology and Economic Importance

**Student-facing description:** Explore representative Indian flora and habitats, geographic origins, seasonal flowering and fruiting, monsoon-linked phenology, economically important plants and conservation concern.

**Core scope:** Himalayan and tropical forests, grasslands, deserts, wetlands and mangroves; representative native, endemic, introduced and cultivated flora, geographic distribution and dispersal history, seasonal phenology and monsoon effects, food, fibre, medicinal and timber plants as economic examples, rare and threatened taxa with status checked from current authoritative sources, conservation overview and cross-link Unit 10; avoid unverified distribution or threatened-status claims.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret phenology calendars and habitat occurrence data, distinguish native, endemic and invasive categories and compare representative economic plant groups.

**Progression:** Build on 9.F.07. Bridge to subunit 9.G.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only, not completed textbook chapters. No fabricated lesson links, sitemap modifications or chapter-completion counter changes.
- Accessible original connected academic English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, comparative tables, original diagrams and life cycles with ploidy/stages, phylogenies, geological timelines, evidence, quantitative examples and explained Part B/Part C-style questions.
- Retain all approved topic scopes, particularly merged 9.A and 9.G nodes. Organise dense chapters using internal sections; do not reduce breadth to equalise word counts. Compare traditional exam terminology with modern evidence and taxonomic uncertainty. Branching descent, not a ladder of modern organisms.
- Boundaries: Unit 1 biochemical pathways, Unit 2 cellular structure and microbial physiology, Unit 3 molecular mechanisms, Unit 4 pathogen/immune interactions, Unit 5 detailed development, Unit 6 plant physiology, Unit 7 animal physiology, Unit 8 inheritance and population-genetic prerequisites, Unit 10 detailed ecology/conservation/behaviour, Unit 11 computational algorithms, Unit 12 industrial biotechnology. Focus on evolutionary inference, diagnostic diversity, life cycles and comparative organisation.
- No clinical prescriptions or operational pathogen manipulation. Future lessons must distinguish established findings, models and hypotheses, and verify conservation status from authoritative sources.
