## Subunit contract

Explore animal evolutionary relationships through comparative body plans, embryological characteristics, anatomical organisation and the diversification of major invertebrate and vertebrate lineages, including their ecological and biological importance.

**Planned nodes:** 7. See [Unit 9 master map](unit-09-evolution-diversity-life-forms-subunit-map.md) for complete syllabus traceability and legacy crosswalk.

## Node contracts

### 9.G.01 — Origin of Animals, Fundamental Body Plans and Early Animal Diversity

**Student-facing description:** Connect animal origins and choanoflagellate relationships with symmetry, germ layers, body cavities, segmentation and the diversity of Porifera, Placozoa, Cnidaria and Ctenophora.

**Core scope:** Metazoan ancestry and phylogenetic uncertainty at animal root, cellular/tissue/organ/system organisation, symmetry, diploblastic/triploblastic concepts, coelom and pseudocoelom, segmentation and cephalisation, protostomes/deuterostomes with developmental exceptions; Porifera canal systems, choanocytes, spicules, major sponge groups, Placozoa, Cnidaria cnidocytes, polyp/medusa, metagenesis, coral symbiosis, Ctenophora comb plates and colloblasts; representative forms and reproduction; no ladder of complexity.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare body-plan and canal-system diagrams, interpret early animal trees and distinguish shared traits from debated ancestry.

**Progression:** Build on evolutionary principles and relevant preceding diversity/classification concepts. Prepare 9.G.02.

### 9.G.02 — Spiralian Diversity and Comparative Organisation

**Student-facing description:** Compare Platyhelminthes, Rotifera, Nemertea, Annelida, Mollusca and lophophorate lineages, relating body organisation, reproduction, larvae and evolutionary relationships.

**Core scope:** Spiralia/Lophotrochozoa framework and variation in cleavage, flatworm organisation, free-living/parasitic groups and protonephridia, rotifer corona and mastax, nemertean proboscis, annelid metamerism, coelom and parapodia, representative polychaete/clitellate groups, molluscan mantle/foot/radula and exceptions, gastropod torsion, bivalves and cephalopods, trochophore and veliger context, brachiopods, phoronids and bryozoans, representative lesser-known spiralians; comparative anatomy, larvae and reproduction. Parasite life cycles 9.G.07.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare diagnostic characters and body systems, trace representative life cycles and identify homologous versus independently evolved features.

**Progression:** Build on 9.G.01. Prepare 9.G.03.

### 9.G.03 — Ecdysozoan Diversity and Arthropod Evolution

**Student-facing description:** Relate moulting and cuticular organisation to Nematoda, Nematomorpha, Onychophora, Tardigrada and Arthropoda, comparing major arthropod lineages and insect metamorphosis.

**Core scope:** Ecdysozoa and ecdysis, nematode cuticle/pseudocoelom/longitudinal muscles, Caenorhabditis and parasitic forms, Nematomorpha, Onychophora, Tardigrada and representative other ecdysozoans; arthropod exoskeleton, jointed appendages, tagmosis, haemocoel and respiratory diversity, chelicerates, myriapods, crustaceans, hexapods and pancrustacean relationships, representative insect orders and ametabolous/hemi-/holometabolous development; adaptations and evolutionary success, detailed endocrine mechanisms cross-link Unit 7.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare appendages, segmentation and moulting, identify arthropod lineages from character sets and interpret metamorphosis and phylogenetic diagrams.

**Progression:** Build on 9.G.02. Prepare 9.G.04.

### 9.G.04 — Deuterostome Diversity, Chordate Origins and Evolution of Fishes

**Student-facing description:** Compare echinoderms, hemichordates, tunicates, cephalochordates, jawless and jawed fishes, connecting deuterostome relationships with vertebrate innovations.

**Core scope:** Echinoderm water vascular system, tube feet, pentaradial adults and bilateral larvae, major echinoderm classes; hemichordate body divisions, pharyngeal slits and stomochord distinction, ambulacrarian relationships; chordate notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal slits, endostyle/thyroid and postanal tail, tunicates/Urochordata and cephalochordates, vertebrate origins and neural crest context, cyclostomes, jaw evolution, Chondrichthyes, Actinopterygii and Sarcopterygii, comparative fins, gills, buoyancy and reproductive traits; tree rather than grade-based hierarchy.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Read comparative larval and chordate diagrams, compare diagnostic classes and reconstruct jaw/fin innovations on a phylogeny.

**Progression:** Build on 9.G.03. Prepare 9.G.05.

### 9.G.05 — Tetrapod Evolution and Diversity of Amphibians and Reptiles

**Student-facing description:** Trace the transition to land, amphibian diversification, metamorphosis and the amniotic egg, comparing turtles, lepidosaurs, crocodilians and extinct archosaurs.

**Core scope:** Lobe-finned ancestry and transitional fossil context, limbs and terrestrial challenges, amphibian orders Anura, Caudata and Gymnophiona, skin, respiration, reproductive diversity and metamorphosis; amniote membranes and egg, water conservation, synapsid/sauropsid split, turtles, lepidosaurs, crocodilians, extinct archosaur diversity, birds nested in reptiles, comparative anatomy and adaptations, avoid treating extant amphibians as ancestors of extant reptiles.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Interpret transitional fossil and amniote trees, compare reproductive/respiratory adaptations and identify diagnostic groups.

**Progression:** Build on 9.G.04. Prepare 9.G.06.

### 9.G.06 — Avian and Mammalian Diversity and Evolutionary Specialisations

**Student-facing description:** Compare dinosaur–bird relationships and avian flight adaptations with synapsid ancestry, mammalian characteristics and the diversification of monotremes, marsupials and placental mammals.

**Core scope:** Avian theropod ancestry, feather functions, wings, skeleton and flight, air sacs and unidirectional airflow context, palaeognaths/neognaths, representative avian groups, flightlessness and adaptive radiation; synapsid and therapsid ancestry, hair, mammary glands, dentition, jaw and middle-ear evolution, monotremes, marsupials and placentals, major mammalian orders including primates, rodents, carnivorans, bats and ungulates/cetaceans, aquatic, aerial and terrestrial adaptations; detailed organ physiology in Unit 7.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Compare bird and mammal character matrices and evolutionary trees, relate structural adaptations to function and distinguish analogous flight structures from shared skeletal homology.

**Progression:** Build on 9.G.05. Prepare 9.G.07.

### 9.G.07 — Animal Parasites, Biological Importance and Indian Faunal Diversity

**Student-facing description:** Connect animal parasite and vector life cycles with biological importance, then survey Indian fauna, habitats, migration, seasonality, phenology, endemism and conservation concern.

**Core scope:** Representative trematodes Fasciola, cestodes Taenia, nematodes Ascaris and Wuchereria, ectoparasites and arthropod vectors, definitive/intermediate hosts and transmission cycles, domestic-animal parasites and agricultural nematodes/pests, beneficial animals and pollinators; major habitats of Indian subcontinent, geographic origins and species migrations, common Indian mammals and birds, seasonal movements and phenology, representative endemic/rare/threatened fauna, distinction between rarity and formal threat categories and current-source verification at lesson stage; conservation strategies cross-link Unit 10, no clinical or vector manipulation procedures.

**Evidence and reasoning:** Trace parasite life cycles and host categories, compare habitat and migration data, identify representative fauna and evaluate conservation-status evidence.

**Progression:** Build on 9.G.06. Integrate diversity and bridge to Unit 10 ecology and behaviour.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only, not completed textbook chapters. No fabricated lesson links, sitemap modifications or chapter-completion counter changes.
- Accessible original connected academic English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, comparative tables, original diagrams and life cycles with ploidy/stages, phylogenies, geological timelines, evidence, quantitative examples and explained Part B/Part C-style questions.
- Retain all approved topic scopes, particularly merged 9.A and 9.G nodes. Organise dense chapters using internal sections; do not reduce breadth to equalise word counts. Compare traditional exam terminology with modern evidence and taxonomic uncertainty. Branching descent, not a ladder of modern organisms.
- Boundaries: Unit 1 biochemical pathways, Unit 2 cellular structure and microbial physiology, Unit 3 molecular mechanisms, Unit 4 pathogen/immune interactions, Unit 5 detailed development, Unit 6 plant physiology, Unit 7 animal physiology, Unit 8 inheritance and population-genetic prerequisites, Unit 10 detailed ecology/conservation/behaviour, Unit 11 computational algorithms, Unit 12 industrial biotechnology. Focus on evolutionary inference, diagnostic diversity, life cycles and comparative organisation.
- No clinical prescriptions or operational pathogen manipulation. Future lessons must distinguish established findings, models and hypotheses, and verify conservation status from authoritative sources.
