## Complete approved subunit descriptions and teaching boundaries

### 3.A — DNA Replication, Repair and Recombination

**Final website description:**

DNA replication units and origins, replication forks and enzymes, high-fidelity DNA copying, extrachromosomal replicons, DNA damage and repair pathways, homologous and site-specific recombination.

**Core learning question:** How does a cell copy DNA accurately, restore damaged DNA and exchange DNA at appropriate sites?

**Official scope to account for at later node-design stage:** Unit of replication; enzymes involved; origin and fork; fidelity; extrachromosomal replicons; DNA damage and repair; homologous and site-specific recombination.

**Complete syllabus-level coverage:** Replicons and origins; replication initiation/elongation/completion in bacterial and eukaryotic systems as appropriate; helicase, primase, polymerases, ligase, topoisomerases and other core helpers; semiconservative replication and leading/lagging strands; replication fidelity and proofreading; plasmid/other extrachromosomal replicons; common DNA damage types and repair principles such as direct reversal, excision/mismatch and double-strand break repair; homologous and site-specific recombination at standard NET/SET level.

**Avoid duplication and off-syllabus expansion:** Chromosome structure and transposon organization belong to 2.C; detailed genetic linkage/inheritance consequences belong to Unit 8; DNA sequencing and molecular cloning methodologies belong to methods-oriented syllabus areas.

### 3.B — RNA Synthesis, Processing and Regulation

**Final website description:**

Transcription machinery and RNA polymerases; initiation, elongation and termination; transcription factors, activators, repressors and inhibitors; RNA capping, splicing, editing, polyadenylation, transport and functional RNA types, including ribozymes, riboswitches and non-coding RNA.

**Core learning question:** How is DNA information transcribed into RNA, and how does a newly made RNA become a functional molecule?

**Official scope to account for at later node-design stage:** Mechanism/regulation of transcription; inhibitors; factors/machinery; activators/repressors; polymerases; capping; processing; editing; splicing; polyadenylation; structure/function of RNA types; transport; ribozymes; riboswitches; non-coding RNA.

**Complete syllabus-level coverage:** Prokaryotic and eukaryotic transcription initiation complexes, RNA polymerases and essential factors; promoter-related recognition and activators/repressors within the transcription mechanism; initiation, elongation and termination; transcriptional inhibitors as classic examples; pre-mRNA capping, splicing, RNA editing and polyadenylation; rRNA/tRNA maturation overview; RNA export and localization; types of coding and noncoding RNA, ribozymes and riboswitches with clear functions.

**Avoid duplication and off-syllabus expansion:** The structural layout of interrupted genes and operons was mapped under 2.C; system-wide gene regulation, phage regulation, chromatin silencing and epigenetic controls belong to 3.D; specialist RNA therapeutics and research-only RNA biology are excluded.

### 3.C — Protein Synthesis, Processing and Degradation

**Final website description:**

Ribosome structure, genetic code, tRNA charging and translation fidelity; initiation, elongation, termination and translational control; translation inhibitors, protein modifications, targeting and transport, and protein degradation.

**Core learning question:** How does an RNA message become a functional protein, and how are newly made proteins matured, delivered and removed?

**Official scope to account for at later node-design stage:** Ribosome; translation mechanism and regulation; translational inhibitors; post-translational modifications; protein trafficking/transport; protein degradation.

**Complete syllabus-level coverage:** Prokaryotic/eukaryotic ribosome organization; genetic code and codon recognition; aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases, tRNA identity and proofreading required in older CSIR/MP SET; translation initiation complex and factors, elongation, translocation and termination; basic translational regulation and standard inhibitors; protein folding and major post-translational modifications; targeting signals, trafficking/transport at a protein-level overview; proteasomal and lysosomal degradation concepts.

**Avoid duplication and off-syllabus expansion:** The membrane machinery for cargo sorting is structurally taught in 2.A.10 and organelle organization in 2.B; detailed signal-transduction pathways belong to Unit 4; advanced protein engineering and specialist proteomics excluded.

### 3.D — Control of Gene Expression at Transcription and Translation Levels

**Final website description:**

Integrated control of gene expression in bacteriophages, viruses, prokaryotes and eukaryotes, including transcriptional and translational regulation, chromatin-dependent control, gene silencing and epigenetic regulation.

**Core learning question:** How do cells and viruses control which genetic messages are produced, processed, translated or silenced?

**Official scope to account for at later node-design stage:** Control of gene expression at transcription/translation level; phages, viruses, prokaryotic and eukaryotic genes; role of chromatin; gene silencing; epigenetic regulation.

**Complete syllabus-level coverage:** Classical phage switch logic, transcriptional control in bacterial operons and other prokaryotic systems, broad viral gene-expression patterns; eukaryotic regulation via transcription factors and regulatory DNA elements in an exam-level framework; processing, RNA stability and translational regulation as integrated controls; histone/DNA modifications in chromatin accessibility, epigenetic principles; RNA-mediated gene silencing and gene expression feedback, using standard examples.

**Avoid duplication and off-syllabus expansion:** Basic transcription and translation mechanisms are taught thoroughly in 3.B and 3.C; structural gene maps and chromatin anatomy in 2.C; specialized cancer biology, developmental transcription networks, and PhD-level epigenomics excluded.

## Continuity and section boundaries

- **2.C → 3.A:** Genome structure is a prerequisite; do not rewrite the full structural-gene/chromosome chapter when teaching replication.
- **3.A → 3.B:** Once DNA integrity and copying are understood, teach how genetic information is transcribed into RNA.
- **3.B → 3.C:** RNA maturation and function lead to codon decoding, protein synthesis and protein lifetime.
- **3.B/C → 3.D:** Introduce the mechanics of individual steps first, then integrate how phages, viruses and different cells regulate those steps and how chromatin and noncoding RNA participate in expression control.
- **Unit 4/5/8 and methods:** Do not replace signaling, developmental gene programs, inheritance and experimental-method chapters with off-scope expansions in Unit 3.
