## Final student-facing descriptions and subunit teaching contracts

### 5.A — Basic Concepts of Development

**Final website menu description:**
Build the foundations of developmental biology through cell potency, commitment, specification, induction, competence, determination and differentiation. Understand morphogen gradients, cell fate and lineage tracing, stem cells, genomic equivalence, cytoplasmic determinants and developmental imprinting, then examine how mutants and transgenic models reveal developmental mechanisms.

**Full NET/SET topic assignment:** Potency and multipotency, differentiation, commitment, specification and determination as distinguishable stages; induction and developmental competence; positional information and morphogen gradients; cell fate maps and lineages; developmental stem-cell properties and genomic equivalence; nuclear-transfer evidence, cytoplasmic determinants, imprinting and mutants/transgenics as tools for investigating development.

**Cross-unit and advanced-depth limits:** Basic genomic epigenetic mechanisms are taught in Unit 3.D, general cell cycle in Unit 2.D and inheritance in Unit 8. Focus here on developmental fate and testable developmental mechanisms.

### 5.B — Gametogenesis, Fertilization and Early Development

**Final website menu description:**
Follow animal spermatogenesis and oogenesis, sperm–egg recognition, zygote formation, cleavage, blastula development, embryonic fields, gastrulation and germ-layer formation. Compare these events with plant pollen and male gametophyte development, embryo-sac formation, double fertilization, embryogenesis, body-axis symmetry, embryo–endosperm development, seed formation and germination.

**Full NET/SET topic assignment:** Animal gamete formation; sperm–egg recognition through cell-surface molecules and species specificity; fertilization, activation and zygote; animal cleavage patterns, blastula, embryonic fields, gastrulation, germ layers. Plant pollen and male gametophyte production, embryo sac/female gametophyte and double fertilization; plant zygote and early embryo symmetry; embryo and endosperm developmental dynamics, seed production and germination.

**Cross-unit and advanced-depth limits:** Full endocrinology and reproduction belong to Unit 7; plant hormone/seed physiology to Unit 6. Avoid confusing plant double fertilization with the animal fertilization mechanism or missing the 2026 male-gametophyte/embryo–endosperm scope.

### 5.C — Morphogenesis and Organogenesis in Animals

**Final website menu description:**
Study cell aggregation and differentiation in Dictyostelium; developmental axes and pattern formation in Drosophila, amphibians and chick embryos; and organogenesis through C. elegans vulva formation, eye-lens induction, vertebrate limb development and regeneration, and neuronal differentiation. Connect these examples to larval development, metamorphosis, environmental regulation and sex determination.

**Full NET/SET topic assignment:** Dictyostelium differentiation and aggregation; Drosophila maternal, segmentation and Hox patterning; amphibian organizers/axis specification and chick body axes; C. elegans vulval induction; eye lens induction, vertebrate limb patterning and regeneration; neuronal differentiation; larval stages/metamorphosis, environmental regulation of normal development and sex determination using standard model-organism comparisons.

**Cross-unit and advanced-depth limits:** 4.A signaling mechanisms, 4.B intercellular contacts and Unit 7 neural/endocrine physiology are prerequisites, not full duplicate chapters. Keep model organisms centered on developmental reasoning.

### 5.D — Morphogenesis and Organogenesis in Plants

**Final website menu description:**
Understand shoot and root apical meristem organization, stem-cell zones and progressive shoot, root and leaf development, including phyllotaxy. Follow the transition to flowering, floral-meristem identity, organ specification and floral development, using Arabidopsis and Antirrhinum as classic genetic models.

**Full NET/SET topic assignment:** SAM/RAM organization, maintenance and stem-cell activity; shoots and roots, polarity and tissue differentiation; leaf primordia and phyllotaxy; floral induction as developmental transition, floral-meristem identity, flower organ whorls and ABC/ABCE-model concepts at exam level; Arabidopsis and Antirrhinum floral mutants as classic case studies.

**Cross-unit and advanced-depth limits:** Plant hormone biosynthesis and light physiology are Unit 6. Plant organogenesis should emphasize patterning and identity, rather than a full hormone-metabolism or plant taxonomy course.

### 5.E — Programmed Cell Death, Aging and Senescence in Animals and Plants

**Final website menu description:**
Compare programmed cell death and developmental tissue remodeling in animals and plants, distinguishing regulated elimination from accidental injury. Explore cellular senescence, organismal aging and environmentally influenced decline through animal and plant examples such as developmental apoptosis, xylem differentiation, leaf senescence and nutrient remobilization.

**Full NET/SET topic assignment:** Normal developmental functions of programmed cell death in animals and plants; animal apoptosis only insofar as necessary for developmental context, plant PCD mechanisms with correct species-specific caveats; vascular development, organ abscission and tissue sculpting; cellular senescence vs organismal aging vs organ senescence; animal age-associated and plant leaf/flower aging examples, remobilization and normal life-cycle integration.

**Cross-unit and advanced-depth limits:** Unit 2.D owns complete core death pathways, 4.E cancer senescence escape and Unit 6 plant physiology. Do not equate plant PCD with canonical animal caspase systems or overextend into clinical aging research.

## Progressive teaching pathway

1. 5.A establishes developmental potential, induction and cell fate before students encounter particular organisms.
2. 5.B moves from gametes and fertilization to embryo/seed formation, explicitly separating animal and plant mechanisms.
3. 5.C explains how position and cell fate generate animal body axes, tissues and organs, then postembryonic developmental changes.
4. 5.D explains persistent meristem-driven plant growth, leaf and shoot/root organization, followed by floral transitions.
5. 5.E explains how regulated cell death and senescence reshape and eventually limit tissues and organs throughout development.

## Scientific accuracy and completeness checklist

- Explain commitment, specification and determination as distinguishable concepts, not loose synonyms.
- Keep cell lineages and classical induction experiments at syllabus level, with no independent doctoral experimental protocol.
- Include male gametophyte, embryo sac and double fertilization in plant development, not only animal gametes.
- Preserve 2026 embryo and endosperm developmental dynamics, not only seed germination.
- Animal case studies must include Dictyostelium, Drosophila, amphibia, chick, C. elegans vulva, lens, limb regeneration, neuronal differentiation, larval stages, metamorphosis, environmental regulation and sex determination.
- Plant organ development includes shoot and root apical meristems, leaves, phyllotaxy, floral transition, meristem identity and floral organogenesis, with SET's Arabidopsis and Antirrhinum examples.
- Developmental programmed cell death and senescence must distinguish plant and animal mechanisms; do not assume animal executioner-caspase pathways universally apply in plants.
- Individual full reading chapters will be planned later with authentic textbook-level scopes; avoid chapter-count quotas and thin pages.
- Preserve all Unit 01–04 menu regions and previously published notes while updating only the Unit 05 placeholder. Do not change site scripts, styles, workflows or sitemap for a menu-only release.
