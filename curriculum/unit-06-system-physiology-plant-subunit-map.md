## The eight official subunit descriptions and academic contracts

### 6.A — Photosynthesis

**Website menu description:**
Follow light capture by pigments and light-harvesting complexes through photosystems I and II, photosynthetic electron transport, proton gradients and photophosphorylation. Examine photoprotection against excess excitation, then compare Calvin-cycle CO₂ fixation with C3, C4 and CAM carbon-concentrating pathways and their environmental significance.

**Official revised 2026 topic wording (meaning preserved):**
Light-harvesting complexes; mechanisms of electron transport; photoprotective mechanisms; CO₂ fixation—C3, C4 and CAM pathways.

**Topic scope for future node planning:**
Chlorophylls/carotenoids and antenna organization; reaction centers PSII and PSI, water splitting, plastoquinone, cytochrome b6f, plastocyanin and ferredoxin/NADP+ reduction; linear and cyclic electron flow, thylakoid proton motive force, chloroplast ATP synthase; nonphotochemical quenching/xanthophyll cycle, photoinhibition and reactive oxygen management; Calvin–Benson cycle, Rubisco oxygenase versus carboxylase, RuBP regeneration, photoassimilate production; spatial C4 separation and temporal CAM separation, PEPC, Kranz anatomy where applicable, energetics and tradeoffs. Distinguish photosynthetic carbon fixation from photorespiration, which is developed as a pathway in 6.B.

**Driving scientific question:**
How do leaves convert absorbed light into ATP, NADPH and carbon compounds while avoiding excess-light damage?

**Cross-unit and anti-duplication boundary:**
Chloroplast ultrastructure is Unit 02.B; general redox/ATP biochemistry is Unit 01.E; photoreceptor-controlled development is 6.E; photorespiratory glycolate pathway 6.B. Do not retell complete glycolysis or atmospheric carbon cycle here.

### 6.B — Respiration and Photorespiration

**Website menu description:**
Connect plant carbon oxidation and the citric acid cycle with mitochondrial electron transport, proton-coupled ATP synthesis and alternative oxidase. Trace photorespiratory recycling of Rubisco oxygenation products through chloroplasts, peroxisomes and mitochondria, relating the pathway to energy balance and photosynthetic performance.

**Official revised 2026 topic wording (meaning preserved):**
Citric acid cycle; plant mitochondrial electron transport and ATP synthesis; alternative oxidase; photorespiratory pathway.

**Topic scope for future node planning:**
Respiratory inputs and acetyl-CoA/TCA as plant metabolic integration, mitochondrial NADH/FADH2 and complexes, ubiquinone, cytochrome chain and oxidative phosphorylation; plant-specific external/internal NAD(P)H dehydrogenases as exam-level context; alternative oxidase (AOX) bypass, cyanide-resistant respiration, energy conservation versus redox balance; distinctions between mitochondrial respiration and chloroplast electron transport. Rubisco oxygenation and 2-phosphoglycolate salvage; chloroplast→peroxisome→mitochondrion→peroxisome→chloroplast photorespiratory C2 cycle, glycine decarboxylase, CO₂/NH₃ costs, and C3 vs C4 implications; common light, CO₂, temperature and O₂ effects interpreted qualitatively.

**Driving scientific question:**
Why do plant cells respire even in light, and what metabolic cost follows from Rubisco's oxygenase reaction?

**Cross-unit and anti-duplication boundary:**
General glycolysis, chemiosmosis and detailed enzyme kinetics are taught in Unit 01; photosynthetic carbon fixation 6.A; leaf gas exchange and transport 6.F. Plant mitochondrial and photorespiratory mechanisms are primary here.

### 6.C — Nitrogen Metabolism

**Website menu description:**
Trace nitrogen acquisition from nitrate and ammonium through nitrate/nitrite reduction, the GS–GOGAT cycle and amino acid formation. Explain biological nitrogen fixation, including nitrogenase, oxygen sensitivity and plant–microbe symbiosis, and connect nitrogen use with plant growth and carbon metabolism.

**Official revised 2026 topic wording (meaning preserved):**
Nitrate and ammonium assimilation; amino acid biosynthesis; biological nitrogen fixation.

**Topic scope for future node planning:**
Soil nitrogen sources and root uptake context; cytosolic nitrate reductase and plastid nitrite reductase with reducing equivalents, compartmentation and regulation; ammonium sources, glutamine synthetase and glutamate synthase (GS/GOGAT) incorporating nitrogen, glutamate dehydrogenase as qualified alternative/role rather than universal primary assimilation; transamination to amino acids and carbon skeletons, asparagine/glutamine for nitrogen transport, biosynthetic families at NET level; free-living, associative and symbiotic nitrogen fixation, rhizobia and legume nodules, nodule development briefly as physiological interaction, nitrogenase ATP/electron demand, O₂ lability, leghemoglobin/microaerobiosis, N-source exchange; differentiate fixation, uptake, reduction and assimilation.

**Driving scientific question:**
How does inorganic or atmospheric nitrogen become amino acids without damaging plant nitrogen and energy balance?

**Cross-unit and anti-duplication boundary:**
Biological nitrogen fixation is explicit in the revised 2026 syllabus and must not be omitted even though the separate older CSIR and MP SET list assimilation without it. Complete microbial genetics in Unit 08, plant cell uptake mechanics in 6.F.

### 6.D — Plant Hormones

**Website menu description:**
Study the biosynthesis, conjugation/storage, breakdown and transport of major plant hormones, then connect hormone perception and signal transduction with growth, dormancy, germination, branching, flowering, ripening, senescence and stress responses. Compare auxin, gibberellins, cytokinins, abscisic acid, ethylene and other important regulators through clear physiological examples.

**Official revised 2026 topic wording (meaning preserved):**
Biosynthesis, storage, breakdown and transport; physiological effects and mechanisms of action.

**Topic scope for future node planning:**
Major classes: auxins, gibberellins, cytokinins, ABA, ethylene, brassinosteroids, jasmonates, salicylic acid and strigolactones; define biosynthetic origins, active/inactive conjugates, breakdown/inactivation and local versus long-distance transport appropriate to each hormone. Representative receptor/signaling mechanisms (TIR1/AFB–Aux/IAA, GID1–DELLA, cytokinin two-component response, PYR/PYL–PP2C–SnRK2, ethylene receptors/EIN2/EIN3, BRI1, COI1–JAZ, SA signaling, D14–MAX2) at NET/SET functional depth; concentration, sensitivity, response context and hormonal cross-talk. Link auxin to tropism/organ initiation, GA to stem growth/germination, cytokinin to division/meristems, ABA to dormancy/water stress and stomata, ethylene to ripening/senescence, BR to cell expansion, JA/SA to defense and strigolactones to branch control and mycorrhizal signaling. Avoid portraying one hormone as having a single universal effect.

**Driving scientific question:**
How do chemical regulators coordinate growth and stress responses across tissues and developmental stages?

**Cross-unit and anti-duplication boundary:**
General receptor pathways are Unit 4.A; meristem, floral organ, and embryo patterning is Unit 5.D/5.B. Photoperiod light perception is 6.E; stress-specific integrated responses 6.H. Teach plant hormone biosynthesis, transport and mechanism here, then cross-reference those applications.

### 6.E — Sensory Photobiology

**Website menu description:**
Explore how plants perceive light quality, intensity and duration through phytochromes, cryptochromes and phototropins. Connect photoreceptor structure, photoconversion and signaling with stomatal opening, photomorphogenesis, photoperiodic flowering and circadian biological clocks.

**Official revised 2026 topic wording (meaning preserved):**
Light perception; structure, function and mechanisms of action of phytochromes, cryptochromes and phototropins; stomatal movement; photoperiodism and biological clock.

**Topic scope for future node planning:**
Light as information as distinct from photosynthesis energy capture; red/far-red phytochrome photoconversion Pr/Pfr, chromophore and signaling effects, shade/seed responses as labeled examples; blue/UV-A perception by cryptochromes and phototropins, flavin chromophore and phototropin kinase basics, phototropism and chloroplast relocation as representative responses; guard-cell blue-light phototropin→H+-ATPase, K+ uptake, water potential, turgor and stomatal opening with ABA/drought closure as cross-link; flowering photoperiod, critical night length, long-day/short-day plant contexts, phytochrome and circadian coincidence logic, FT/florigen as developmental outcome; circadian entrainment, endogenous ~24 h rhythm, light input and rhythmic outputs, clock feedback as introductory framework. Do not claim phototropins alone govern every stomatal response.

**Driving scientific question:**
How can a leaf use light as a time, color and directional signal rather than merely a source of energy?

**Cross-unit and anti-duplication boundary:**
6.A photosynthetic light reactions generate ATP/NADPH; 4.A general light signaling and receptors are prerequisites; 5.D floral transition and photomorphogenesis form the developmental outcomes. Hormone synthesis is 6.D; transpiration 6.F.

### 6.F — Solute Transport and Photoassimilate Translocation

**Website menu description:**
Follow water, mineral ions, solutes and selected macromolecules from soil to roots, across membranes and through plant tissues. Explain water potential, membrane transporters, xylem ascent and transpiration, then trace phloem pressure flow, source–sink relations and the loading and unloading of photoassimilates.

**Official revised 2026 topic wording (meaning preserved):**
Uptake, transport and translocation of water, ions, solutes and macromolecules from soil, through cells, across membranes, through xylem and phloem; transpiration; mechanisms of loading and unloading of photoassimilates.

**Topic scope for future node planning:**
Water potential and component potentials, gradients and osmosis, passive and active transport; H+-ATPases, carriers, channels, transport selectivity and ion coupling at plant physiology level; soil–root interface, apoplastic, symplastic and transmembrane/cell-to-cell routes, endodermis/Casparian strip and selective uptake; aquaporins, plasmodesmata for symplastic transport and concept of macromolecular trafficking with context; xylem tracheary elements and cohesion–tension water ascent, root pressure limits, transpiration and guard cells, boundary-layer effects and water-use tradeoffs; phloem sieve elements/companion cells, source–sink relations, sucrose loading by apoplastic SUT/SUC transporter or symplastic mechanisms, unloading routes, pressure-flow Münch hypothesis and assimilate partitioning; contrast xylem water flow with phloem mass flow and interpret water potential/sink perturbations.

**Driving scientific question:**
How do plants draw water and solutes from soil and distribute photosynthetic products between distant organs?

**Cross-unit and anti-duplication boundary:**
2.A general membranes and transporters, 5.D vascular tissue development, 6.A photosynthate production, 6.E stomatal light regulation, 6.D/6.H hormonal and drought responses. Keep quantitative transport and whole-plant application in 6.F.

### 6.G — Secondary Metabolites

**Website menu description:**
Classify plant specialized metabolites and trace the principal routes producing terpenes, phenolic and phenylpropanoid compounds, alkaloids and other nitrogenous metabolites. Relate these chemicals to defense, signaling, pigmentation and ecological interactions, then introduce plant metabolic engineering principles.

**Official revised 2026 topic wording (meaning preserved):**
Biosynthesis of terpenes, phenolics, alkaloids, phenylpropanoids, nitrogenous compounds and their roles; metabolic engineering in plants.

**Topic scope for future node planning:**
Specialized ('secondary') versus primary metabolism and carbon/nitrogen precursors; terpenoid MVA/mevalonate and plastid MEP/DOXP pathways and isoprene units, representative monoterpenes, carotenoids and hormone precursors; phenolics/phenylpropanoids from shikimate/phenylalanine ammonia-lyase, flavonoids, lignin, anthocyanins as functional examples, distinguishing phenolics broad class from phenylpropanoid branch; alkaloid nitrogen incorporation from amino acid precursors and examples, other nitrogen-containing defenses such as cyanogenic glycosides and glucosinolates distinctly classified; storage/compartmentation, inducible defense, UV protection, pollinator attraction and allelopathy as grounded roles. Metabolic engineering concept: precursor supply, pathway genes, enzyme/branch regulation and flux/product changes, benefits and tradeoffs at exam concept level; exclude engineering procedures and assuming every nitrogenous compound is an alkaloid.

**Driving scientific question:**
How can basic plant carbon and nitrogen metabolism generate structurally diverse compounds with ecological functions?

**Cross-unit and anti-duplication boundary:**
Unit 01 owns generic amino-acid and carbohydrate biochemistry, 6.C nitrogen assimilation, 6.H pathogen/insect defense responses and Unit 13 biotech detailed engineering. Keep specialized biosynthesis and conceptual pathway engineering in this subunit.

### 6.H — Stress Physiology

**Website menu description:**
Explain how plants sense and respond physiologically and molecularly to drought, flooding, salinity, heat, cold, pathogens and herbivorous insects. Connect cellular protection, antioxidant defenses, osmotic adjustment, hormone signaling and plant innate immunity—including pattern-triggered and effector-triggered responses—with acclimation, resistance and survival.

**Official revised 2026 topic wording (meaning preserved):**
Physiological and molecular responses of plants to biotic and abiotic stresses; innate immunity.

**Topic scope for future node planning:**
Definitions of stress, resistance/tolerance/avoidance, acclimation versus adaptation, stress timing and dose; osmotic and dehydration stress, guard cell responses, aquaporin/ABA connections, osmolytes and water use; salinity osmotic versus ion toxicity, sodium homeostasis/ion compartmentation; cold/freezing and heat effects on proteins/membranes, acclimation and heat-shock proteins; flooding/hypoxia as additional representative abiotic case, ROS generation, detoxification/antioxidants and cross-talk. Biotic stress from pathogens and insects: physical/chemical barriers, receptor perception of PAMPs/MAMPs and damage signals, PTI, pathogen effectors/ETI, intracellular NLR/NB-LRR proteins as appropriate examples, local cell death/hypersensitive response and its caveats, defense phytohormones SA/JA/ET, systemic acquired resistance (SAR) and induced systemic resistance (ISR) with distinguishing contexts, herbivore attack and wound signals; contrast normal immune signaling with development and distinguish PTI/ETI from animal adaptive immunology.

**Driving scientific question:**
How do plants recognize environmental threats and coordinate rapid local protection with long-distance physiological adaptation?

**Cross-unit and anti-duplication boundary:**
6.D hormone biosynthesis and 6.F water transport are prerequisites; 4.D pathogen entry into hosts and 4.C animal innate/adaptive immunity are different subunits. Plant innate immunity is explicit in revised 2026 syllabus; do not misrepresent plants as having animal-like B/T-cell adaptive immunity.

## How the sections connect without reducing academic depth

1. **6.A Photosynthesis:** Light-derived redox energy and CO₂ fixation create chemical energy and carbon building blocks.
2. **6.B Respiration/photorespiration:** Respiration uses plant carbon to produce ATP and regulate metabolic redox states; Rubisco's oxygenase side reaction links photorespiratory recycling back to photosynthesis.
3. **6.C Nitrogen metabolism:** Inorganic or atmospheric nitrogen is reduced and incorporated into amino acids using energy and carbon skeletons generated by central metabolism.
4. **6.D Plant hormones:** Chemical regulators coordinate cellular and tissue growth responses, including functions that alter metabolism and transport.
5. **6.E Sensory photobiology:** Light information changes hormonal/developmental and guard-cell behavior; distinguish light acting as information from light as photosynthetic energy.
6. **6.F Solute and assimilate movement:** Water/minerals enter roots and xylem, while sucrose/solutes travel through phloem to sinks, linking the entire organism's metabolism and signal distribution.
7. **6.G Specialized metabolites:** Building blocks and redox resources produce plant-specific defenses, colors, signals and other ecological functions; metabolic engineering alters pathway flux conceptually.
8. **6.H Stress physiology:** Integrated hormone, light, transport, energy and specialized-metabolite responses protect plants from abiotic and biotic threats and coordinate innate immune defense.

## Official scope and scientific accuracy QA

- Photosystems/electron transport, photoprotection and C3/C4/CAM are distinct mechanisms; CAM and C4 both concentrate CO₂ but separate processes by time versus space in their classic forms.
- Alternative oxidase is a plant mitochondrial respiratory bypass, generally yielding less ATP from the same electron flux than the standard cytochrome route; it is not the same as alternative photosynthetic electron flow.
- Photorespiration is linked to Rubisco oxygenation and uses several compartments, not synonymous with mitochondrial respiration.
- Biological nitrogen fixation and nitrate/ammonium assimilation are separate routes into nitrogen metabolism. They must not be conflated.
- Major plant hormones differ in synthesis, transport, active forms, receptors and context-dependent effects; no single universal response can be inferred from a hormone name alone.
- Phytochromes respond mainly to red/far-red light; cryptochromes and phototropins are blue/UV-A light sensors with different functions. Circadian rhythms are endogenous but entrainable.
- Transpiration-driven xylem water movement and pressure-driven phloem photoassimilate transport differ in driving forces, transported materials and anatomical routes.
- Phenolics are a broad group, phenylpropanoids a biosynthetic class, and not all nitrogenous secondary metabolites are alkaloids.
- Plants have innate immune recognition and defense, including PTI/ETI; they do not mount animal-style antibody-mediated adaptive immunity.
- Do not duplicate general membrane physiology (Unit 2.A), basic metabolism (Unit 1), signaling mechanics (Unit 4.A), pathogen entry (Unit 4.D), developmental morphogenesis (Unit 5), animal immunity (Unit 4.C), or specialist engineering technology (Unit 13) in full.
- Preserve all already verified units and subunits; update only Unit 06 menu and this separate curriculum roadmap; no fake lesson hyperlinks, sitemap updates, global style/script changes or invented completed chapters.
