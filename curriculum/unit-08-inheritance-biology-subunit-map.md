## Subunit descriptions and complete learning sequences

### 8.A — Chromosomal and Extrachromosomal Inheritance

Trace Mendelian transmission from segregation and independent assortment through allelic and non-allelic interactions, variable expression, imprinting, linkage and sex-dependent inheritance to organelle transmission and maternal effects.

- 8.A.01 — **Foundations of Genetics and Mendelian Segregation:** Explain Mendel's experiments, chromosomal inheritance, genotype and phenotype, dominance, segregation, monohybrid crosses, test crosses and backcrosses.
- 8.A.02 — **Independent Assortment and Probability in Genetics:** Apply independent assortment, probability rules, dihybrid and polyhybrid crosses, expected offspring ratios and chi-square tests to genetic predictions.
- 8.A.03 — **Incomplete Dominance, Codominance and Allelic Relationships:** Compare complete dominance, incomplete dominance, codominance and multiple-allele inheritance using modified monohybrid ratios and blood-group examples.
- 8.A.04 — **Gene Interactions and Epistasis:** Explain non-allelic interactions, complementary, supplementary, duplicate and suppressor effects, relating modified dihybrid ratios to biochemical pathways.
- 8.A.05 — **Pleiotropy, Penetrance, Expressivity and Genomic Imprinting:** Distinguish pleiotropy, penetrance, expressivity, phenocopy and environmental effects, then explain parent-of-origin expression and genomic imprinting.
- 8.A.06 — **Chromosomal Linkage and Crossing Over:** Trace Morgan's linkage experiments, coupling and repulsion, complete and incomplete linkage and meiotic crossing over to explain parental and recombinant offspring.
- 8.A.07 — **Sex-Linked, Sex-Limited and Sex-Influenced Inheritance:** Compare X-linked, Y-linked and other sex-chromosome inheritance with sex-limited and sex-influenced expression, reciprocal crosses and dosage compensation.
- 8.A.08 — **Extrachromosomal Inheritance and Maternal Effects:** Compare mitochondrial and chloroplast gene transmission, heteroplasmy, cytoplasmic inheritance and nuclear maternal effects through reciprocal crosses and organelle segregation.

### 8.B — Genes and Mutations

Develop the gene and allele concepts, use complementation to identify functional units, then trace mutation mechanisms, mutant phenotypes, inheritance and experimental detection.

- 8.B.01 — **Concept of Gene, Alleles and Pseudoalleles:** Explain classical and molecular gene concepts, loci, allelic series, multiple alleles, pseudoalleles and the historical cistron, recon and muton.
- 8.B.02 — **Complementation Tests and Functional Analysis of Genes:** Use cis–trans comparisons and complementation groups to distinguish allelic and non-allelic mutations, including limitations and intragenic complementation.
- 8.B.03 — **Molecular Basis and Causes of Mutations:** Trace spontaneous and induced mutations from base substitutions, insertions and deletions to frameshifts and altered coding or regulatory sequences, relating them to mutagenic mechanisms.
- 8.B.04 — **Functional Classification and Inheritance of Mutations:** Classify lethal, conditional, biochemical, loss-of-function, gain-of-function, dominant-negative and other mutant phenotypes, distinguishing germline and somatic transmission.
- 8.B.05 — **Mutation Detection and Genetic Screening:** Compare forward and reverse genetics, mutant selection and screening, replica plating, fluctuation and Ames tests, insertional mutagenesis, reversion and suppression.

### 8.C — Genetic Analysis

Progress from linkage and tetrad maps to marker-based mapping and plant populations, then compare microbial gene transfer and fine-structure analysis as complementary experimental approaches.

- 8.C.01 — **Linkage Mapping and Recombination Analysis:** Calculate two-point and three-point linkage maps, gene order, double crossovers, coefficient of coincidence, interference and mapping functions.
- 8.C.02 — **Tetrad Analysis and Fungal Genetics:** Interpret ordered and unordered tetrads, parental and non-parental ditypes, tetratypes, centromere mapping and gene conversion.
- 8.C.03 — **Molecular Markers and Genetic Mapping Methods:** Compare RFLP, RAPD, AFLP, SSR and SNP markers, genetic and physical maps and marker-based mapping in plants, animals and bacteria, including somatic cell hybrids.
- 8.C.04 — **Development of Genetic Mapping Populations:** Compare F2, backcross, recombinant inbred, doubled haploid and near-isogenic plant populations, relating breeding design to segregation, recombination and mapping resolution.
- 8.C.05 — **Bacterial Transformation and Genetic Mapping:** Explain natural competence and transformation evidence, relating DNA uptake, genetic integration and cotransformation frequency to bacterial gene mapping.
- 8.C.06 — **Bacterial Conjugation, Sexduction and Interrupted Mating:** Compare F-positive, F-negative, Hfr and F-prime states, then explain conjugation, sexduction, partial diploidy and time-of-entry mapping.
- 8.C.07 — **Bacteriophage-Mediated Transduction and Gene Mapping:** Compare generalized and specialized transduction, phage lysogeny and cotransduction, using genetic transfer evidence to infer bacterial linkage and gene order.
- 8.C.08 — **Fine-Structure Analysis of Genes:** Reconstruct Benzer's T4 rII genetic logic, relating complementation, intragenic recombination and fine-structure maps to functional gene units.

### 8.D — Human Genetics

Use family data, statistical linkage and cytogenetic evidence to understand human inheritance and representative genetic disorders without clinical diagnosis or individual counselling.

- 8.D.01 — **Human Pedigree Analysis and Inheritance Patterns:** Interpret standard pedigrees for autosomal, sex-linked and mitochondrial transmission, calculating carrier probabilities and conditional recurrence risks.
- 8.D.02 — **Human Linkage Analysis and LOD Scores:** Calculate and interpret LOD scores from human family data, relating likelihood ratios and recombination fractions to evidence for or against linkage.
- 8.D.03 — **Human Karyotypes and Cytogenetic Analysis:** Identify human chromosome organisation, karyotype notation, banding and FISH evidence, linking cytogenetic observations with representative structural and numerical abnormalities.
- 8.D.04 — **Genetic Disorders and Human Disease Inheritance:** Compare monogenic, chromosomal, mitochondrial and multifactorial disorders, linking representative disease phenotypes with gene function, inheritance, repeat expansion and imprinting.

### 8.E — Quantitative Genetics

Progress from population allele frequencies and Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium to quantitative traits, variance components, heritability, selection response and molecular mapping.

- 8.E.01 — **Population Genetics and Hardy–Weinberg Equilibrium:** Calculate allele and genotype frequencies, explain Hardy–Weinberg assumptions and apply equilibrium models to multiple alleles and carrier-frequency problems.
- 8.E.02 — **Genetic Structure and Departure from Population Equilibrium:** Explain non-random mating, inbreeding, population subdivision and linkage disequilibrium, introducing selection, mutation, migration and drift as causes of changing genetic structure.
- 8.E.03 — **Polygenic Inheritance and Quantitative Trait Variation:** Connect multiple loci, additive and non-additive effects and environmental variation with continuous traits, threshold traits and quantitative phenotype distributions.
- 8.E.04 — **Genetic Variance, Heritability and Response to Selection:** Partition phenotypic variation into genetic and environmental components, distinguishing broad- and narrow-sense heritability, regression-based estimates and predicted selection response.
- 8.E.05 — **Quantitative Trait Loci and Molecular Mapping:** Use marker–trait associations, linkage-based QTL mapping, interval mapping, LOD profiles and introductory GWAS to connect quantitative phenotypes with genomic regions.

### 8.F — Structural and Numerical Alterations of Chromosomes

Explain recombination and transposition as sources of genome change, then trace deletions, duplications, inversions, translocations, aneuploidy and polyploidy through their meiotic and genetic consequences.

- 8.F.01 — **Genetic Recombination and Transposition:** Compare homologous, non-homologous and site-specific recombination, unequal crossing over, gene conversion and transposition as sources of new genetic arrangements.
- 8.F.02 — **Chromosomal Deletions and Duplications:** Compare terminal and interstitial deletions and duplication arrangements, explaining gene dosage, pseudodominance, pairing behaviour and phenotypic effects.
- 8.F.03 — **Chromosomal Inversions and Meiotic Consequences:** Distinguish paracentric and pericentric inversions, tracing inversion-loop pairing and crossover products to explain fertility effects and apparent recombination suppression.
- 8.F.04 — **Chromosomal Translocations and Genetic Rearrangements:** Trace reciprocal, non-reciprocal and Robertsonian translocations, relating meiotic pairing and segregation to fertility, position effects and genetic imbalance.
- 8.F.05 — **Aneuploidy and Chromosomal Nondisjunction:** Explain chromosome-specific gains and losses through meiotic and mitotic nondisjunction, relating monosomy, trisomy, nullisomy, tetrasomy and mosaicism to gene dosage.
- 8.F.06 — **Euploidy, Polyploidy and Genome-Level Inheritance:** Compare haploidy, monoploidy, autopolyploidy and allopolyploidy, explaining chromosome doubling, meiotic pairing, fertility, genome evolution and plant breeding relevance.

## Learning progression

Mendelian foundations → modified inheritance and linkage → gene function and mutation → experimental mapping → human genetics → population and quantitative traits → structural and numerical genome changes. The order follows official revised subunits; cross-links preview chromosome changes in human cytogenetics and reuse linkage foundations for quantitative mapping.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Menu descriptions and curriculum contracts only, not completed lessons. Do not add unpublished URLs to menu or sitemap or change chapter-completion metrics.
- Use original accessible connected English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, sequential explanations, diagrams, historical experiments, solved crosses, quantitative reasoning with assumptions, misconceptions, Part B and Part C-style practice with explanations. Each core topic must be developed, not merely listed.
- Preserve official scope and conceptual balance, not forced identical word counts. Use examples to explain mechanisms rather than catalogue every disorder or marker.
- Unit 2 owns chromosome structure and meiosis; Unit 3 owns DNA replication/repair/recombination biochemistry; Unit 4 signalling and cancer; Unit 5 development; Unit 7 endocrine/reproductive physiology; Unit 9 detailed evolution; Unit 11 computational workflows; Units 13–14 specialist techniques. Unit 8 owns transmission, inference, mapping and genetic consequences. Use brief prerequisite previews where the official sequence introduces a later topic early (e.g. cytogenetics before detailed chromosome rearrangements).
- No personal genetic diagnosis, treatment, or operational mutagenesis/gene-transfer protocols. Comparative historical and experimental analysis is educational.
