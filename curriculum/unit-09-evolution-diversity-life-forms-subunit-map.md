## Subunit descriptions and learning sequences

### 9.A — Evolution of Life and Life Forms

Explore the origin and diversification of life, from prebiotic chemical evolution and early cellular systems to natural selection, molecular evolution, speciation, extinction and human evolution.

- 9.A.01 — **Foundations and Historical Development of Evolutionary Biology:** Trace Lamarckism, Darwin–Wallace theory, evidence of evolution, Mendelian inheritance, spontaneous mutation, reproductive fitness and the modern evolutionary synthesis.
- 9.A.02 — **Origin of Life and Evolution of Early Cellular Systems:** Connect early Earth and abiotic synthesis with the Oparin–Haldane hypothesis, Miller–Urey experiment, RNA world, protocells, LUCA, early prokaryotic evolution and the diversification of metabolism.
- 9.A.03 — **Eukaryotic Evolution, Multicellularity and Geological History of Life:** Relate endosymbiosis, cellular compartmentalisation and multicellularity to the geological time scale, fossil evidence, major diversification events and mass extinctions.
- 9.A.04 — **Population Genetics and Mechanisms of Evolution:** Apply natural selection, relative fitness, mutation, genetic drift and gene flow to changing allele frequencies, including sexual selection, founder effects, bottlenecks and mutation–selection balance.
- 9.A.05 — **Speciation, Macroevolution and Coevolution:** Connect reproductive isolation and speciation with adaptive radiation, convergent and divergent evolution, evolutionary tempo, reciprocal selection and evolutionary arms races.
- 9.A.06 — **Molecular Evolution and Evolution of Genomes:** Explain neutral and nearly neutral evolution, sequence divergence, molecular clocks, dN/dS, gene duplication and divergence, orthology, paralogy and the origin of new genes and proteins.
- 9.A.07 — **Primate and Human Evolution:** Trace primate and hominin diversification through fossil and molecular evidence, bipedalism, brain evolution, Homo lineages, human dispersal and archaic admixture.

### 9.B — Principles & Methods of Taxonomy

Understand how organisms are identified, named and classified using species concepts, taxonomic hierarchies, classical characters, quantitative approaches and molecular evidence of evolutionary relationships.

- 9.B.01 — **Foundations of Taxonomy, Systematics and Species Concepts:** Distinguish taxonomy, systematics, identification and classification, explaining hierarchical taxa and morphological, biological, ecological and phylogenetic species concepts.
- 9.B.02 — **Biological Nomenclature and Taxonomic Documentation:** Explain binomial nomenclature, typification, priority, synonyms, homonyms, author citation and the roles of taxonomic codes, herbaria, museums and reference collections.
- 9.B.03 — **Classical Taxonomy and Organismal Identification:** Compare morphological, anatomical, embryological, cytological, chemical and serological characters and microbial phenotypes, then use diagnostic characters and identification keys.
- 9.B.04 — **Numerical Taxonomy and Quantitative Classification:** Apply phenetic character coding, operational taxonomic units, similarity coefficients, cluster analysis, dendrograms and ordination to quantitative classification.
- 9.B.05 — **Molecular Taxonomy, Cladistics and Phylogenetic Analysis:** Connect DNA barcoding, rRNA and ITS markers, genomic similarity and sequence comparison with cladistics, monophyly, tree construction and support assessment.

### 9.C — Microbial Life

Compare the structural, evolutionary and metabolic diversity of bacteria and archaea, explore viral organisation and replication, and explain the ecological, economic, agricultural and pathological significance of microorganisms.

- 9.C.01 — **Bacterial Diversity and Classification:** Compare bacterial morphology, cell-envelope organisation, major bacterial lineages and representative diagnostic criteria, relating traditional names to modern phylogenetic classification.
- 9.C.02 — **Archaeal Diversity and Evolutionary Significance:** Explore archaeal cellular organisation, membrane chemistry and diversity, from methanogens and extremophiles to widely distributed archaea and their relationships with eukaryotes.
- 9.C.03 — **Microbial Metabolic Diversity and Ecological Roles:** Relate microbial nutritional and metabolic strategies to nitrogen and sulfur transformations, methanogenesis, decomposition, symbiosis and biogeochemical cycling.
- 9.C.04 — **Virus Structure, Diversity and Classification:** Compare viral genomes, capsid and envelope organisation, bacteriophages, Baltimore groups and ICTV taxonomy, distinguishing viruses from viroids, satellite agents and prions.
- 9.C.05 — **Viral Replication and Evolutionary Impact:** Trace attachment, entry, genome expression, replication, assembly and release, comparing lytic and lysogenic cycles, retroviruses, viral variation and effects on host evolution and ecosystems.
- 9.C.06 — **Economic, Agricultural and Medical Importance of Microorganisms:** Connect beneficial and harmful microbes with fermentation, biofertilisers, antibiotics, bioremediation, spoilage and representative pathogens of humans, domestic animals and crops.

### 9.D — Protists

Explore the diverse eukaryotic lineages traditionally grouped as protists, comparing their cellular organisation, nutritional strategies, reproduction, evolutionary relationships and ecological significance.

- 9.D.01 — **Protist Diversity and Eukaryotic Classification:** Compare protist cellular organisation, locomotion, nutrition and reproduction, connecting traditional groupings with modern eukaryotic lineages and plastid origins.
- 9.D.02 — **Green Algae, Red Algae and Primary Plastid Lineages:** Compare chlorophytes, streptophyte algae, red algae and glaucophytes through pigments, storage products, thallus organisation, reproduction and relationships with land plants.
- 9.D.03 — **Diatoms, Brown Algae and Other Photosynthetic Protists:** Explore stramenopile algae, diatoms, brown algae, dinoflagellates, euglenids, haptophytes and cryptophytes, relating plastid diversity and life cycles to aquatic productivity and blooms.
- 9.D.04 — **Protozoan Diversity, Life Cycles and Parasitism:** Compare amoeboid, ciliate, kinetoplastid, apicomplexan and diplomonad diversity, examining locomotion, reproduction and representative free-living and parasitic life cycles.
- 9.D.05 — **Slime Molds, Water Molds and Protist Ecology:** Compare cellular and plasmodial slime molds with oomycetes, explaining developmental life cycles, decomposition, aquatic food webs, symbiosis and agricultural significance.

### 9.E — Fungi

Understand fungal organisation, nutrition, reproduction and evolutionary relationships across major fungal lineages, connecting fungal diversity with ecological interactions, economic applications and diseases.

- 9.E.01 — **Foundations of Fungal Biology and Classification:** Explain fungal cell walls, hyphal and yeast organisation, absorptive nutrition, spores and sexual and asexual cycles, establishing a framework for fungal phylogeny.
- 9.E.02 — **Chytrids, Zygomycete Lineages and Glomeromycetes:** Compare chytrids, traditional zygomycetes and glomeromycetes using reproductive structures, life cycles, modern lineage relationships and mycorrhizal associations.
- 9.E.03 — **Ascomycetes and Their Reproductive Diversity:** Trace sac-fungus reproduction, asci, ascospores, ascocarps and conidia through representative yeasts, filamentous fungi and model organisms.
- 9.E.04 — **Basidiomycetes and Complex Fungal Life Cycles:** Compare basidia, basidiospores, dikaryotic mycelia, clamp connections and the reproductive cycles of mushrooms, rusts and smuts.
- 9.E.05 — **Fungal Ecology, Symbiosis and Pathological Importance:** Explain decomposition, lichens, mycorrhizae, endophytes, fungal applications and representative plant and human fungal diseases, connecting fungal diversity with ecological functions.

### 9.F — Plant Life

Trace plant evolution from aquatic ancestors to diverse land plants, comparing major plant lineages, morphological innovations, anatomical organisation, reproduction and adaptations to terrestrial environments.

- 9.F.01 — **Origin of Land Plants and Diversity of Bryophytes:** Relate streptophyte ancestry and terrestrial adaptations to alternation of generations and the diversity of liverworts, hornworts and mosses.
- 9.F.02 — **Seedless Vascular Plants and Evolution of Conducting Tissues:** Compare lycophytes, ferns and horsetails, connecting vascular tissues, stelar organisation, sporophyte dominance, heterospory and precursors of the seed habit.
- 9.F.03 — **Gymnosperm Diversity and Evolution of Seeds:** Explain pollen, ovules and seed evolution through comparative study of cycads, Ginkgo, conifers and gnetophytes, connecting reproductive cycles with terrestrial adaptations.
- 9.F.04 — **Angiosperm Diversity and Evolutionary Classification:** Compare early-diverging angiosperms, magnoliids, monocots and eudicots, interpreting flowering-plant diversification, APG classification and diagnostic characters of representative families.
- 9.F.05 — **Plant Morphology and Adaptive Modifications:** Compare roots, stems, leaves, inflorescences, flowers, fruits and seeds, using floral diagrams and formulae to explain structural diversity and adaptive modifications.
- 9.F.06 — **Plant Anatomy and Structural Organisation:** Trace meristems, tissues and vascular organisation in roots, stems and leaves, connecting primary and secondary growth, wood, periderm and anomalous growth with plant structure.
- 9.F.07 — **Plant Reproduction and Comparative Life Cycles:** Integrate vegetative reproduction and alternation of generations with pollination, fertilisation, seed and fruit formation, apomixis and reproductive adaptations.
- 9.F.08 — **Indian Plant Diversity, Phenology and Economic Importance:** Explore representative Indian flora and habitats, geographic origins, seasonal flowering and fruiting, monsoon-linked phenology, economically important plants and conservation concern.

### 9.G — Animal Life

Explore animal evolutionary relationships through comparative body plans, embryological characteristics, anatomical organisation and the diversification of major invertebrate and vertebrate lineages, including their ecological and biological importance.

- 9.G.01 — **Origin of Animals, Fundamental Body Plans and Early Animal Diversity:** Connect animal origins and choanoflagellate relationships with symmetry, germ layers, body cavities, segmentation and the diversity of Porifera, Placozoa, Cnidaria and Ctenophora.
- 9.G.02 — **Spiralian Diversity and Comparative Organisation:** Compare Platyhelminthes, Rotifera, Nemertea, Annelida, Mollusca and lophophorate lineages, relating body organisation, reproduction, larvae and evolutionary relationships.
- 9.G.03 — **Ecdysozoan Diversity and Arthropod Evolution:** Relate moulting and cuticular organisation to Nematoda, Nematomorpha, Onychophora, Tardigrada and Arthropoda, comparing major arthropod lineages and insect metamorphosis.
- 9.G.04 — **Deuterostome Diversity, Chordate Origins and Evolution of Fishes:** Compare echinoderms, hemichordates, tunicates, cephalochordates, jawless and jawed fishes, connecting deuterostome relationships with vertebrate innovations.
- 9.G.05 — **Tetrapod Evolution and Diversity of Amphibians and Reptiles:** Trace the transition to land, amphibian diversification, metamorphosis and the amniotic egg, comparing turtles, lepidosaurs, crocodilians and extinct archosaurs.
- 9.G.06 — **Avian and Mammalian Diversity and Evolutionary Specialisations:** Compare dinosaur–bird relationships and avian flight adaptations with synapsid ancestry, mammalian characteristics and the diversification of monotremes, marsupials and placental mammals.
- 9.G.07 — **Animal Parasites, Biological Importance and Indian Faunal Diversity:** Connect animal parasite and vector life cycles with biological importance, then survey Indian fauna, habitats, migration, seasonality, phenology, endemism and conservation concern.

## Learning progression

Evolutionary history and mechanisms → taxonomy and phylogenetic inference → bacteria, archaea and viruses → protist diversity → fungi → plants → animals. This is a pedagogical sequence, not a linear evolutionary lineage. Apply the evolutionary and taxonomic framework to every organismal subunit.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only, not completed textbook chapters. No fabricated lesson links, sitemap modifications or chapter-completion counter changes.
- Accessible original connected academic English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, comparative tables, original diagrams and life cycles with ploidy/stages, phylogenies, geological timelines, evidence, quantitative examples and explained Part B/Part C-style questions.
- Retain all approved topic scopes, particularly merged 9.A and 9.G nodes. Organise dense chapters using internal sections; do not reduce breadth to equalise word counts. Compare traditional exam terminology with modern evidence and taxonomic uncertainty. Branching descent, not a ladder of modern organisms.
- Boundaries: Unit 1 biochemical pathways, Unit 2 cellular structure and microbial physiology, Unit 3 molecular mechanisms, Unit 4 pathogen/immune interactions, Unit 5 detailed development, Unit 6 plant physiology, Unit 7 animal physiology, Unit 8 inheritance and population-genetic prerequisites, Unit 10 detailed ecology/conservation/behaviour, Unit 11 computational algorithms, Unit 12 industrial biotechnology. Focus on evolutionary inference, diagnostic diversity, life cycles and comparative organisation.
- No clinical prescriptions or operational pathogen manipulation. Future lessons must distinguish established findings, models and hypotheses, and verify conservation status from authoritative sources.
