## Subunit descriptions and learning sequences

### 10.A — Introduction to Ecology

Explore the fundamental relationships between organisms and their environment, progressing from ecological organisation and environmental factors to habitat selection, ecological niches, adaptations and geographical patterns of life.

- 10.A.01 — **Foundations and Levels of Ecological Organisation:** Explore the history, scope and branches of ecology, connecting organisms, populations, communities, ecosystems, biomes and the biosphere through ecological investigation.
- 10.A.02 — **Abiotic and Biotic Environmental Factors:** Explain how light, temperature, water, climate, soil, salinity, nutrients and biological interactions limit organismal performance and distribution.
- 10.A.03 — **Habitat, Ecological Niche and Resource Utilisation:** Distinguish habitat from niche and compare Grinnellian, Eltonian and Hutchinsonian concepts, fundamental and realised niches, overlap, partitioning and character displacement.
- 10.A.04 — **Ecological Adaptations and Environmental Responses:** Compare morphological, physiological and behavioural adaptations and phenotypic plasticity, using plants and animals in contrasting environments to explain adaptive trade-offs.
- 10.A.05 — **Biogeography, Biomes and Species Distribution:** Relate major terrestrial biomes, diversity gradients, species–area relationships, island biogeography, dispersal and vicariance to geographical patterns of life and India's biogeographical zones.

### 10.B — Population Ecology

Understand how populations are organised, measured and regulated, using demographic information, mathematical growth models, life history strategies and spatial population dynamics to explain persistence and extinction.

- 10.B.01 — **Population Characteristics and Estimation Methods:** Measure population size, density, abundance, natality, mortality, migration and dispersion using quadrats, transects and mark–recapture approaches.
- 10.B.02 — **Population Growth Models and Carrying Capacity:** Compare geometric, exponential and logistic growth, intrinsic and finite rates of increase, doubling time, carrying capacity and mathematical population projections.
- 10.B.03 — **Population Regulation and Dynamic Fluctuations:** Explain density-dependent and density-independent effects, delayed feedback, environmental variability, population cycles, overshooting, crashes and Allee effects.
- 10.B.04 — **Life Tables, Survivorship and Reproductive Parameters:** Construct cohort and static life tables, comparing survival, mortality, fecundity, survivorship curves, life expectancy, net reproductive rate and generation time.
- 10.B.05 — **Age Structure, Sex Ratio and Population Projection:** Relate age pyramids, sex ratios and reproductive value to age-structured models, Leslie matrices and long-term demographic projections.
- 10.B.06 — **Life History Strategies and Evolutionary Trade-offs:** Compare classical r/K models with modern life history theory, reproductive timing, semelparity, iteroparity, offspring size–number trade-offs and allocation.
- 10.B.07 — **Metapopulation Dynamics and Spatial Population Ecology:** Connect demes and habitat patches with colonisation, local extinction, dispersal, Levins models, source–sink dynamics, rescue effects and landscape connectivity.

### 10.C — Community Ecology

Examine the structure and diversity of ecological communities, progressing from community attributes and trophic networks to species interactions, coexistence, succession and ecological stability.

- 10.C.01 — **Community Organisation, Structure and Attributes:** Describe species composition, abundance, dominance, stratification, zonation, guilds, functional groups, ecotones, edge effects and community assembly.
- 10.C.02 — **Species Diversity and Quantitative Community Analysis:** Measure richness, evenness, alpha, beta and gamma diversity using Shannon and Simpson indices, rank–abundance curves, species accumulation and similarity measures.
- 10.C.03 — **Food Webs, Trophic Structure and Keystone Species:** Connect grazing and detrital chains, trophic levels, food-web connectivity, omnivory, keystone and foundation species, ecosystem engineers and trophic cascades.
- 10.C.04 — **Competition, Coexistence and Resource Partitioning:** Explain intra- and interspecific competition, competitive exclusion, exploitation and interference, Gause's experiments and Lotka–Volterra competition models.
- 10.C.05 — **Predation, Herbivory and Consumer–Resource Dynamics:** Compare carnivory, herbivory and predator–prey dynamics using Lotka–Volterra models, Holling functional responses, numerical responses and prey defences.
- 10.C.06 — **Mutualism, Parasitism and Other Species Interactions:** Compare mutualism, commensalism, amensalism, facilitation and parasitism, using pollination, seed dispersal, symbiosis and interaction networks to explain context-dependent outcomes.
- 10.C.07 — **Ecological Succession and Community Stability:** Trace primary and secondary succession, autogenic and allogenic changes, facilitation, tolerance and inhibition, climax concepts, disturbance, resistance and resilience.

### 10.D — Ecosystem Ecology

Investigate ecosystem structure and functioning through biological productivity, energy transfer, decomposition, nutrient cycling, terrestrial and aquatic ecosystem processes, and the services ecosystems provide to society.

- 10.D.01 — **Ecosystem Structure, Function and Functional Organisation:** Distinguish ecosystem components, producers, consumers, decomposers, standing crop, standing state, biomass, compartments, boundaries and material exchange.
- 10.D.02 — **Ecological Productivity and Energy Flow:** Quantify primary and secondary production, ecosystem respiration, ecological efficiencies and Lindeman's trophic dynamics using productivity measurements and ecological pyramids.
- 10.D.03 — **Decomposition, Carbon Cycling and Ecosystem Metabolism:** Connect detritus processing, fragmentation, leaching, catabolism, humification and mineralisation with carbon reservoirs, fluxes, sequestration and ecosystem budgets.
- 10.D.04 — **Nitrogen, Phosphorus and Mineral Cycling:** Trace nitrogen fixation, ammonification, nitrification, denitrification, anammox and phosphorus cycling, relating nutrient limitation and stoichiometry to ecosystem productivity.
- 10.D.05 — **Water Cycling and Comparative Ecosystems of India:** Explain precipitation, evapotranspiration, infiltration, runoff and watershed processes, comparing forests, grasslands, lakes, rivers, wetlands, estuaries, marine ecosystems and mangroves.
- 10.D.06 — **Ecosystem Services and Integrated Ecosystem Functioning:** Link biodiversity and ecosystem functions to provisioning, regulating, cultural and supporting services, including pollination, soil, water, climate regulation and sustainability.

### 10.E — Human Impacts on Ecosystems

Analyse how human activities modify ecological systems through habitat transformation, climate change, pollution and biological invasions, and evaluate approaches to environmental monitoring, mitigation and ecological restoration.

- 10.E.01 — **Land Use Change, Habitat Loss and Ecosystem Fragmentation:** Compare deforestation, agriculture, urbanisation, mining and other land-use and land-cover changes, tracing habitat loss, degradation, fragmentation and desertification.
- 10.E.02 — **Climate Change and Global Ecological Responses:** Explain greenhouse forcing and anthropogenic warming, linking climate and ocean changes to range shifts, phenological mismatch, extreme events and ecosystem responses.
- 10.E.03 — **Environmental Pollution and Ecotoxicology:** Compare air, water and soil pollutants, acid rain, ozone depletion, eutrophication and ecotoxicological processes, using BOD, COD, dissolved oxygen and bioaccumulation evidence.
- 10.E.04 — **Biological Invasions and Invasive Species Ecology:** Trace introduction, establishment and spread of alien species, explaining propagule pressure, invasion hypotheses, ecological impacts and prevention through representative Indian examples.
- 10.E.05 — **Applied Ecology, Environmental Monitoring and Restoration:** Evaluate ecological indicators, biomonitoring, environmental impact assessment, remote sensing, GIS, pollution-control principles, bioremediation and ecological restoration.

### 10.F — Biodiversity and Conservation

Understand the value, distribution and vulnerability of biological diversity, using conservation genetics, population viability analysis, protected areas, ex-situ conservation and community-based approaches to evaluate biodiversity protection.

- 10.F.01 — **Levels, Patterns and Importance of Biodiversity:** Compare genetic, species, ecosystem, functional and phylogenetic diversity, biodiversity values, hotspots, endemism, inventories and global and Indian diversity patterns.
- 10.F.02 — **Biodiversity Threats and IUCN Extinction Risk Assessment:** Connect habitat loss, exploitation, invasions, pollution and climate change with extinction processes, coextinction, rarity and IUCN Red List categories and criteria A–E.
- 10.F.03 — **Conservation Genetics and Small-Population Biology:** Explain effective population size, genetic erosion, drift, inbreeding depression, loss of heterozygosity, genetic rescue and outbreeding risks in small populations.
- 10.F.04 — **Population Viability Analysis and Extinction Risk Modelling:** Integrate demographic and environmental stochasticity, catastrophes, extinction vortices and minimum viable population concepts with population viability models and conservation decisions.
- 10.F.05 — **In-situ Conservation and Protected Area Management:** Compare national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves, conservation and community reserves, corridors, reserve design and Indian conservation programmes.
- 10.F.06 — **Ex-situ Conservation and Species Recovery Strategies:** Connect botanical gardens, zoos, captive breeding, genetic resource banks, cryopreservation, reintroduction and translocation with integrated species recovery and monitoring.
- 10.F.07 — **Community-Based Conservation and Global Conservation Governance:** Integrate local and Indigenous knowledge, sacred groves, participatory conservation and human–wildlife coexistence with international conventions and Indian conservation policy.

### 10.G — Act and Policies

Examine agricultural biodiversity and plant genetic resources through germplasm conservation, climate-resilient strategies, international treaties, Indian biodiversity legislation and the protection of plant varieties and farmers' rights.

- 10.G.01 — **Agricultural Biodiversity and Plant Genetic Resources:** Relate crop diversity, Vavilov's centres of origin and diversity, landraces, crop wild relatives and germplasm to genetic erosion, sustainable agriculture and food security.
- 10.G.02 — **Seed Gene Banks and Climate-Resilient Germplasm Conservation:** Trace germplasm exploration, accessions, seed viability, base and active collections, regeneration, field and in-vitro banks, cryopreservation and climate-resilient conservation.
- 10.G.03 — **International Treaty on Plant Genetic Resources for Food and Agriculture:** Explain ITPGRFA conservation and sustainable-use objectives, the Multilateral System, Annex I crops, SMTA, access and benefit sharing, Farmers' Rights and germplasm exchange.
- 10.G.04 — **Biological Diversity Act 2002 and Biodiversity Governance in India:** Examine India's Biological Diversity Act, subsequent amendments and rules, NBA, SBBs, BMCs, People's Biodiversity Registers, access and benefit sharing and traditional knowledge.
- 10.G.05 — **Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights and Integrated Conservation Policies:** Compare breeders', farmers' and researchers' rights under the PPV&FR framework, plant variety registration, DUS criteria, benefit sharing and the National Gene Fund.

### 10.H — Behavioural Ecology

Understand animal behaviour through its developmental, neural, ecological and evolutionary foundations, progressing from individual decision-making and communication to navigation, social organisation, sexual selection and parental investment.

- 10.H.01 — **Foundations, Development and Methods of Studying Animal Behaviour:** Distinguish proximate and ultimate explanations using Tinbergen's four questions, innate and learned behaviour, fixed action patterns, sign stimuli, ethograms and sampling methods.
- 10.H.02 — **Neural Basis of Behaviour, Learning and Biological Rhythms:** Connect neural and endocrine regulation with habituation, sensitisation, conditioning, imprinting, cognition, memory, sleep, arousal, circadian and seasonal rhythms and domestication.
- 10.H.03 — **Foraging Behaviour and Habitat Selection:** Apply optimal foraging, prey choice, handling/search times, marginal value theorem, patch selection, risk-sensitive decisions and ideal free distribution to behavioural trade-offs.
- 10.H.04 — **Animal Communication, Conflict and Territorial Behaviour:** Compare visual, acoustic, chemical, tactile and electrical signals, honesty and deception, bee dances, dominance, aggression, territories and evolutionary game theory.
- 10.H.05 — **Migration, Dispersal, Orientation and Navigation:** Compare seasonal migration, natal and breeding dispersal, taxis, kinesis, compass orientation, landmark use, homing and biological timing.
- 10.H.06 — **Social Behaviour, Cooperation and Evolution of Altruism:** Explain group living, cooperation, kin selection, inclusive fitness, Hamilton's rule, reciprocal altruism, multilevel selection, eusociality and social conflict.
- 10.H.07 — **Sexual Selection, Mating Systems and Parental Investment:** Connect mate competition and choice, sexual dimorphism, mating systems, sperm competition, parental care, parental investment, parent–offspring conflict and reproductive success.

## Learning progression

Organism–environment relations → population dynamics → community interactions → ecosystem energy and material budgets → anthropogenic disturbance → conservation science → resource governance → behavioural ecology as an application of ecological and evolutionary principles. Behavioural mechanisms also draw on Units 7 and 9. Policy nodes build on biodiversity and conservation without duplicating their scientific explanations.
