## Complete menu with English descriptions

### 13.A — Recombinant DNA Technology

Connect molecular cloning, recombinant expression, functional genetic perturbation, sequencing and gene-expression analysis with the evidence used to evaluate biotechnology applications.

- 13.A.01 — **Foundations of Recombinant DNA and Molecular Cloning:** Restriction enzymes, DNA ligases, cloning concepts, plasmid and other vector features, insert–vector relationships, bacterial and eukaryotic cloning and isolation of specific nucleic acid sequences.
- 13.A.02 — **Genomic and cDNA Libraries and Gene Retrieval:** Genomic versus cDNA libraries, plasmid, phage, cosmid, BAC and YAC vector classes, representation, library screening principles, nucleic acid hybridisation and sequence-specific retrieval.
- 13.A.03 — **Recombinant Protein Expression and Product Characterisation:** Bacterial, animal and plant expression systems, regulatory elements, fusion tags, folding, post-translational modifications, product identity and expression versus biological activity.
- 13.A.04 — **In Vitro Mutagenesis, Deletion and Functional Gene Knockout:** Random and targeted mutation concepts, deletion analysis, bacterial and eukaryotic knockout, knockdown, conditional loss of function, complementation and rescue as evidence of gene function.
- 13.A.05 — **Genome Editing and Functional Genome Modification:** Conceptual comparison of ZFN, TALEN, CRISPR-associated editing, repair outcomes, base and prime editing, off-target uncertainty, mosaicism, verification and ethical constraints.
- 13.A.06 — **DNA and Protein Sequencing Technologies:** Sanger, historical chemical cleavage sequencing, next-generation and long-read sequencing, Edman degradation, mass-spectrometric peptide sequencing and post-translational modification detection.
- 13.A.07 — **Genome Sequencing Strategies and Gene Expression Analysis:** Whole-genome shotgun and hierarchical sequencing, coverage, assembly and annotation concepts, PCR/RT-qPCR, Southern and Northern blotting, microarrays, RNA-seq and protein-level expression analysis.

### 13.B — Medical Biotechnology

Apply immunology, molecular medicine, regenerative biology, neural research and biomedical devices to human health, distinguishing experimental promise from validated interventions.

- 13.B.01 — **Applied Immunology and Transplantation Biology:** Immunological applications in autoimmunity, transplantation, HLA compatibility, rejection, tolerance, tumour immunology and immune monitoring; mechanisms in Unit 4.
- 13.B.02 — **Vaccines, Adjuvants and Immunodiagnostics:** Live, killed, attenuated, subunit, recombinant protein, nucleic acid and cell-based vaccine concepts, immune memory, adjuvants, diagnostic sensitivity and specificity and validation.
- 13.B.03 — **Antibody Engineering and Immunotherapy:** Monoclonal antibodies, humanisation, antibody fragments, bispecific antibodies, phage display libraries, checkpoint immunotherapy, target specificity and therapeutic evidence.
- 13.B.04 — **Recombinant DNA-Based Therapies and Molecular Medicine:** Recombinant therapeutic proteins, gene addition and correction concepts, somatic gene therapy, delivery categories, RNA-based therapies, genomic medicine, evidence, safety and limitations.
- 13.B.05 — **Cell Therapy and Stem Cell Technology:** Cell-based therapies, adult and pluripotent stem cells, induced pluripotent stem cells, guided/directed differentiation, lineage identity, potency, quality and regenerative applications.
- 13.B.06 — **Tissue Engineering, Organoids and Disease Models:** Cells, scaffolds, extracellular matrix, biomaterials, self-organising stem-cell-derived 3D organoids, developmental and disease models, drug screening and model limitations.
- 13.B.07 — **Neurobiotechnology and Behavioural Assessment:** Brain electrophysiology, neural activity recording, behavioural tests, neurological disease models, experimental design and links between neural and behavioural evidence.
- 13.B.08 — **Medical Devices, Implants and Biosensors:** Biocompatibility, implant interfaces, medical devices, biosensor biorecognition and transduction, electrochemical and optical sensors, calibration, sensitivity, selectivity and clinical validation.

### 13.C — Animal Biotechnology

Connect animal cell systems and genetic technologies with breeding, germplasm preservation, genetic monitoring and veterinary applications.

- 13.C.01 — **Animal Cell and Tissue Culture Applications:** Primary and established cell lines, tissue explants, 2D/3D models, anchorage dependence, cell identity, viability, contamination awareness and applications in animal biotechnology.
- 13.C.02 — **Cell Cloning, Selection and Animal Reproductive Biotechnology:** Clonal populations, cell selection concepts, somatic cell nuclear transfer, embryo transfer, IVF concepts, cloning versus transgenesis, developmental limitations and animal welfare.
- 13.C.03 — **Transgenic and Genome-Modified Animals:** Transgenic, knockout, knock-in and conditional model concepts, animal models, recombinant product applications, phenotype validation and welfare and containment considerations.
- 13.C.04 — **Animal Breeding and Marker-Assisted Selection:** Selection, mating systems, crossbreeding, estimated breeding values, heritability, genomic selection, marker-assisted breeding and livestock trait trade-offs.
- 13.C.05 — **Animal Germplasm Conservation and Genetic Health Monitoring:** Germplasm banks, semen, oocytes and embryos, cryopreservation concepts, pedigree and genomic diversity, inherited disorders, genetic erosion and conservation breeding.
- 13.C.06 — **Veterinary Molecular Diagnosis, Medicine and Surgery:** Molecular diagnosis of animal pathogens, strain identification, genetic health screening, molecular medicine, biomaterials and molecularly informed surgery concepts, test accuracy and welfare.

### 13.D — Agriculture Biotechnology

Link plant culture, transformation, agricultural genomics, molecular diagnostics and marker-assisted crop improvement with environmental evaluation.

- 13.D.01 — **Plant Tissue and Cell Culture Biotechnology:** Totipotency, explants, callus, organogenesis, somatic embryogenesis, micropropagation, meristem culture, haploids and doubled haploids, protoplast and somatic hybridisation concepts.
- 13.D.02 — **Transgenic Plants and Plant Molecular Farming:** Genetic transformation and transgenic plant concepts, Agrobacterium-mediated and physical delivery categories, trait validation, representative GE crop traits, plantibodies and molecular farming.
- 13.D.03 — **Agricultural Genomics and Expressed Sequence Tags:** Plant genome organisation, sequencing resources, EST development concepts, transcript-derived markers, functional genomics, comparative genomics and crop trait associations.
- 13.D.04 — **Molecular Diagnosis, Strain Identification and Plant Genotyping:** Molecular approaches to crop disease diagnosis and strain identification, RFLP, RAPD, AFLP, SSR and SNP markers, non-gel techniques including SNP arrays, KASP and sequencing-based genotyping.
- 13.D.05 — **Germplasm Analysis and Marker-Assisted Breeding:** Genetic diversity, germplasm characterisation, linkage and QTL prerequisites, marker-assisted selection, marker–trait association, trait improvement and comparison with genomic selection.
- 13.D.06 — **Foreground and Background Selection, Introgression and Pyramiding:** Marker-assisted backcrossing concepts, foreground and background selection, recombinant selection, linkage drag, introgression, gene pyramiding and multiple-trait selection.
- 13.D.07 — **GE Crops, Biodiversity and Responsible Agricultural Innovation:** Environmental assessment of genetically engineered crops, gene flow concepts, non-target effects, biodiversity, agricultural sustainability, comparative risk assessment and regulatory context.

### 13.E — Marine Biotechnology

Connect oceanographic conditions and marine biological diversity with resource assessment, microbial functions, seafood quality, bioactive compounds and marine protection.

- 13.E.01 — **Oceanography and Marine Environmental Foundations:** Physical, chemical and biological oceanography, salinity, temperature, pressure, currents, tides, stratification, upwelling, dissolved oxygen and marine ecological zones.
- 13.E.02 — **Marine Organisms, Biology and Behaviour:** Plankton, nekton and benthos, algae, marine invertebrates, fishes and representative marine mammals, adaptation, reproduction, movement and behaviour.
- 13.E.03 — **Marine Resource Assessment and Population Protection:** Marine bioresources, biodiversity uses, fish-stock concepts, abundance and population assessment, sampling, sustainable harvest, habitat protection and marine conservation.
- 13.E.04 — **Marine Microbiology and Microbial Metabolites:** Marine microbial diversity, microbial loop, nutrient cycling, symbiosis, extremophile adaptations, microbial metabolites, enzymes and bioresource discovery.
- 13.E.05 — **Seafood Microbiology and Marine Pharmacology:** Seafood spoilage, foodborne hazards, quality indicators and safety principles, marine natural products, pharmacological discovery, bioactivity and evidence limitations.
- 13.E.06 — **Marine Biofilms, Biofouling and Biocorrosion:** Biofilm formation concepts, surface colonisation, macrofouling, microbially influenced corrosion, environmental and economic effects and sustainable prevention approaches.

### 13.F — Environmental Biotechnology

Apply biological principles to wastewater, pollution control, resource recovery, environmentally friendly products and remediation while evaluating ecological outcomes.

- 13.F.01 — **Wastewater Treatment and Biological Pollution Control:** Wastewater characteristics, BOD/COD, primary, secondary and tertiary treatment, activated sludge, trickling filters, anaerobic treatment concepts, nutrient removal and effluent assessment.
- 13.F.02 — **Bioremediation and Microbial Pollution Transformation:** Biodegradation, bioaugmentation and biostimulation concepts, in-situ/ex-situ remediation, organic contaminants, metals, biosorption, bioavailability, monitoring and limitations.
- 13.F.03 — **Phytoremediation and Plant-Based Environmental Recovery:** Phytoextraction, phytostabilisation, rhizofiltration, phytodegradation, rhizosphere interactions, contaminant fate and plant-based restoration limitations.
- 13.F.04 — **Biosurfactants, Biofertilisers and Biopesticides:** Biological surfactants, nutrient-mobilising biofertilisers, nitrogen-fixing associations, biological pest control concepts, ecological benefits, specificity and safety evaluation.
- 13.F.05 — **Integrated Waste Management, Biogas and Biofuels:** Waste hierarchy, segregation, composting, anaerobic digestion, biogas, biofuel from waste, circular economy, energy recovery and life-cycle assessment boundaries.
- 13.F.06 — **Microbially Enhanced Oil Recovery and Sustainable Technology Assessment:** Concepts of microbial effects on oil displacement, biosurfactant roles, environmental constraints, integrated pollution control, resource recovery and sustainability assessment.

## Learning progression

Molecular tools and evidence → medical applications → animal cell and breeding technologies → plant culture and crop genomics → marine resources → environmental biotechnology. Applications preview methods that are formally taught in Unit 14.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only; no unpublished lesson URLs, sitemap edits or lesson-completion counter changes.
- Original friendly academic English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, connected narratives, original diagrams, classical evidence, modern applications, quantitative examples with assumptions and units, misconception checks, Part B and analytical Part C practice with solutions.
- Unit 13 owns biotechnology application and conceptual comparison; Unit 14 owns measurement principles, experimental evidence, instrumentation, statistical interpretation and ethical governance. Unit 3 owns molecular mechanisms, Unit 4 immunology, Unit 5 development, Unit 8 inheritance, Unit 10 ecology, Unit 11 algorithms and Unit 12 industrial engineering. Preview later methods when needed without duplicating full chapters.
- Use benign educational examples, abstract models and published high-level evidence. No actionable pathogen culture or engineering, harmful genetic modification, production recipes, invasive experimentation, radioisotope handling or medical treatment instructions. Research involving biological hazards, animals, humans or radiation requires trained personnel and authorised oversight.
- Verify current laws, biosafety rules, regulatory bodies and journal AI policies at actual publication; distinguish prediction from experimental or clinical validation. Maintain provenance and research integrity.
