## Complete menu with English descriptions

### 14.A — Molecular Biology Techniques

Learn the principles and interpretation of macromolecule isolation, separation, purification and analytical methods, choosing appropriate techniques and quality controls.

- 14.A.01 — **Biological Sample Preparation and Macromolecule Analysis:** Sample integrity, fractionation concepts, recovery, purity, concentration, analytical controls, contamination and representative DNA, RNA, protein, carbohydrate and lipid measurements.
- 14.A.02 — **Isolation and Analysis of DNA and RNA:** Genomic and plasmid DNA, RNA isolation and purification principles, nucleic acid integrity, absorbance and fluorescence quantification, hybridisation, specific sequence detection and quality controls.
- 14.A.03 — **Protein, Carbohydrate and Lipid Isolation and Analysis:** Protein solubility and fractionation, carbohydrate and lipid extraction/separation principles, colorimetric assays, protein activity and standards, lipid and carbohydrate analytical approaches.
- 14.A.04 — **Centrifugation and Biological Fractionation:** Relative centrifugal force, sedimentation, differential and density-gradient centrifugation, rate-zonal versus isopycnic separation, analytical ultracentrifugation concepts.
- 14.A.05 — **Chromatographic Separation and Analytical Interpretation:** Partition, adsorption, ion exchange, affinity, size exclusion, TLC, GC and HPLC principles, retention, resolution, efficiency and calibration.
- 14.A.06 — **Electrophoresis and Molecular Profiling:** Agarose and PAGE, native and SDS-PAGE, one- and two-dimensional electrophoresis, isoelectric focusing, molecular markers and electropherogram interpretation.

### 14.B — Biophysical Methods

Progress from electromagnetic and optical measurements to structural determination, mass analysis and biomolecular interaction measurements.

- 14.B.01 — **Spectroscopy Foundations and UV–Visible Analysis:** Electromagnetic radiation, absorption, Beer–Lambert law, extinction coefficient, spectra, calibration, scattering interference, instrumentation and limitations.
- 14.B.02 — **Fluorescence Spectroscopy and Circular Dichroism:** Excitation/emission, Stokes shift, quantum yield, quenching, FRET, anisotropy, circular dichroism and protein conformational analysis.
- 14.B.03 — **NMR and ESR Spectroscopy:** Nuclear and electron spin, magnetic resonance, chemical shift, coupling, relaxation, multidimensional NMR concepts, ESR/EPR and paramagnetic species.
- 14.B.04 — **X-Ray Diffraction and Molecular Structure Determination:** Diffraction, Bragg's law, reciprocal space concepts, electron density, phase problem, model refinement, resolution, R factors and structural validation.
- 14.B.05 — **Cryo-Electron Microscopy and Integrated Structural Analysis:** Cryo-EM single-particle imaging, projection and reconstruction concepts, maps, resolution, heterogeneity, NMR structural restraints and comparison of X-ray, NMR and cryo-EM.
- 14.B.06 — **Mass Spectrometry and Molecular Identification:** Mass-to-charge ratio, ionisation concepts including ESI and MALDI, mass analysers, isotope patterns, tandem MS, peptide and metabolite identification, quantitative limitations.
- 14.B.07 — **Light Scattering and Surface Plasmon Resonance:** Static and dynamic light scattering, molecular size and aggregation, hydrodynamic radius, polydispersity, SPR sensorgrams, association/dissociation and affinity.

### 14.C — Genomics, Transcriptomics, Proteomics and Metabolomics

Connect genome-level measurements, molecular identity, expression, protein interactions, metabolic profiling and single-cell datasets with integrated experimental inference.

- 14.C.01 — **Genome Organisation and Comparative Genomics:** Prokaryotic and eukaryotic genome organisation, sequencing coverage, genome assembly and annotation evidence, comparative genomics, synteny and structural variation.
- 14.C.02 — **DNA Fingerprinting, Molecular Markers and DNA Barcoding:** RFLP, RAPD, AFLP, STR and SNP profiles, DNA fingerprinting applications, DNA barcoding, reference libraries and evidence limitations.
- 14.C.03 — **Global Gene Expression and Comparative Transcriptomics:** Microarray and RNA-seq principles, transcript quantification, differential gene expression, fold change, normalisation, biological replication, multiple testing and comparative transcriptomics.
- 14.C.04 — **Proteomics and Post-Translational Modification Analysis:** Protein separation and identification, 2D gels, LC-MS/MS, label-based and label-free quantification, proteome coverage and modification detection.
- 14.C.05 — **Protein Interaction Analysis and Mapping:** Yeast two-hybrid concepts, co-immunoprecipitation, pull-down assays, affinity purification–MS, proximity and interaction mapping, network evidence and orthogonal validation.
- 14.C.06 — **Targeted and Untargeted Metabolomics:** Metabolic profiling, targeted versus untargeted measurement, LC/GC-MS and NMR, internal standards, feature annotation, pathway interpretation and metabolic flux distinction.
- 14.C.07 — **Single-Cell Sequencing and Integrated Single-Cell Omics:** Single-cell DNA/RNA sequencing, single-cell chromatin accessibility and protein measurements, cell heterogeneity, barcodes, dropout, clustering, trajectory inference and spatial/multi-omics context.

### 14.D — Radiolabeling Techniques

Understand biological tracer measurements, isotope detection, interpretation, molecular imaging and radiation protection in authorised research settings.

- 14.D.01 — **Radioisotopes and Principles of Biological Tracing:** Isotopes, radioactive decay, alpha, beta and gamma emissions, half-life, activity units, specific activity and tracer versus stable isotope concepts.
- 14.D.02 — **Radiation Detection and Quantitative Measurement:** Geiger–Müller and scintillation detection principles, counting efficiency, background, quenching, count statistics and measurement uncertainty.
- 14.D.03 — **Radiolabel Incorporation and Biological Tracer Analysis:** Conceptual incorporation of labels in biological molecules, cells and tissues, pulse–chase logic, turnover, distribution and metabolic tracing.
- 14.D.04 — **Autoradiography and Molecular Imaging:** Autoradiographic localisation, spatial resolution, radiotracer imaging, PET and SPECT principles, signal interpretation and limitations.
- 14.D.05 — **Radiation Safety and Responsible Radioisotope Research:** Ionising radiation hazards, exposure versus contamination, ALARA, time/distance/shielding concepts, dosimetry, trained personnel, regulatory oversight and authorised waste management.

### 14.E — Histochemical and Immunotechniques

Connect antibody and staining principles with quantitative detection, protein identification, cell profiling and spatial localisation.

- 14.E.01 — **Histochemistry and Antibody Generation:** Histochemical stains, antigen recognition, polyclonal and monoclonal antibody generation concepts, hybridomas, antibody specificity, affinity and validation.
- 14.E.02 — **ELISA, Radioimmunoassay and Quantitative Immunodetection:** Direct, indirect, sandwich and competitive ELISA, RIA concepts, standard curves, assay range, cross-reactivity, sensitivity and specificity.
- 14.E.03 — **Western Blotting and Immunoprecipitation:** Protein transfer and antibody detection, loading controls, Western blot interpretation, immunoprecipitation and co-immunoprecipitation, specificity and limitations.
- 14.E.04 — **Flow Cytometry and Fluorescence-Activated Cell Analysis:** Light scatter, fluorescence channels, gating, compensation, cell populations, viability and fluorescence-activated sorting concepts.
- 14.E.05 — **Immunofluorescence and Detection in Living Cells:** Direct/indirect immunofluorescence, localisation, fluorescent proteins, live-cell reporters, colocalisation, photobleaching and control strategies.
- 14.E.06 — **In Situ Hybridisation, FISH and GISH:** Probe hybridisation concepts, fluorescence in situ hybridisation, genomic in situ hybridisation, chromosome and transcript localisation, specificity and spatial resolution.

### 14.F — Microscopic Techniques

Develop microscopy literacy from optical resolution and specimen contrast to advanced live imaging, electron microscopy, specimen preparation and quantitative image analysis.

- 14.F.01 — **Optical Foundations and Resolving Power of Microscopes:** Magnification, resolution, numerical aperture, diffraction, Abbe and Rayleigh criteria, working distance, contrast and sampling.
- 14.F.02 — **Light Microscopy and Contrast Methods:** Bright-field, dark-field, phase contrast, differential interference contrast, polarising microscopy, cell and organelle visualisation and method selection.
- 14.F.03 — **Fluorescence, Confocal and Advanced Live-Cell Microscopy:** Widefield fluorescence, confocal optical sectioning, multiphoton and light-sheet concepts, super-resolution overview, time-lapse microscopy, live-cell imaging and phototoxicity.
- 14.F.04 — **Transmission and Scanning Electron Microscopy:** Electron optics, TEM and SEM contrast, internal ultrastructure versus surface morphology, resolution and representative biological images.
- 14.F.05 — **Microscopy Sample Preparation and Structural Preservation:** Fixation and staining concepts, sectioning, dehydration and embedding, negative staining, freeze-fracture, freeze-etch, cryogenic preservation and preparation artefacts.
- 14.F.06 — **Digital Image Processing and Quantitative Microscopy:** Pixels and calibration, sampling, noise, background correction, segmentation, thresholding, colocalisation, morphometry, image integrity and reproducibility.

### 14.G — Electrophysiological Methods

Relate electrical recordings, neural perturbation evidence and brain imaging to physiological mechanisms and experimental interpretation.

- 14.G.01 — **Bioelectric Signals and Single-Neuron Recording:** Membrane potential, electrodes, intracellular and extracellular recording concepts, single-unit spikes, signal conditioning, noise and recording interpretation.
- 14.G.02 — **Patch-Clamp Recording and Ion Channel Analysis:** Voltage and current clamp, cell-attached, whole-cell, inside-out and outside-out configurations, current–voltage relations, conductance and reversal potential.
- 14.G.03 — **ECG and Integrated Physiological Recording:** ECG lead concepts, P–QRS–T waves, intervals, heart-rate calculation, EMG context, recording artefacts and physiological interpretation.
- 14.G.04 — **Brain Activity Recording and Functional Neurophysiology:** EEG, evoked potentials, neural population activity, MEG context, oscillatory bands, temporal/spatial resolution and artefacts.
- 14.G.05 — **Brain Lesion, Stimulation and Pharmacological Testing:** Conceptual lesion and stimulation studies, pharmacological perturbation, reversible versus permanent effects, controls, behavioural endpoints, causality and animal ethics.
- 14.G.06 — **PET, MRI, fMRI and Computed Tomography:** PET molecular imaging, MRI magnetic resonance contrast, fMRI BOLD response, CT/CAT X-ray attenuation, structural versus functional imaging and resolution limits.

### 14.H — Methods in Field Biology

Develop field sampling and observation literacy for plant and animal populations, movements, behaviour and habitat characterisation.

- 14.H.01 — **Field Study Design and Ecological Sampling:** Sampling frames, random, systematic and stratified sampling, replication, detection probability, observer bias, spatial/temporal scales and ethical field research.
- 14.H.02 — **Plant Population Density and Vegetation Assessment:** Quadrats, line and belt transects, density, frequency, cover, abundance, dominance, importance values and vegetation sampling limitations.
- 14.H.03 — **Animal Population Density and Abundance Estimation:** Direct counts, indirect signs, distance sampling, mark–recapture principles, camera-trap indices, occupancy and imperfect detection.
- 14.H.04 — **Ranging Patterns and Behavioural Observation:** Home range, territory, movement paths, direct and indirect observations, telemetry and GPS concepts, focal/scan sampling, ethograms and activity budgets.
- 14.H.05 — **Habitat Characterisation, Remote Sensing and GIS:** Ground surveys, environmental variables, satellite imagery, spectral signatures, NDVI, spatial resolution, land-cover classification, GIS and ground-truth validation.

### 14.I — Statistical Methods

Build statistical reasoning from measurement quality and probability to sampling, inference, association, comparative tests and multivariate analysis.

- 14.I.01 — **Measurement Quality, Experimental Error and Descriptive Statistics:** Accuracy, precision, calibration, systematic/random error, signal-to-noise ratio, mean, median, mode, range, variance, standard deviation, IQR and coefficient of variation.
- 14.I.02 — **Probability and Biological Probability Distributions:** Probability rules, conditional probability, random variables, binomial, Poisson and normal distributions, expectation, variance and biological applications.
- 14.I.03 — **Sampling Distributions, Estimation and Confidence Intervals:** Population versus sample, sampling distribution, central limit theorem, standard error, confidence intervals, sample size and estimation uncertainty.
- 14.I.04 — **Hypothesis Testing, Significance and Statistical Errors:** Null and alternative hypotheses, p-values, significance levels, type I/II errors, power, effect size, parametric and nonparametric assumptions, multiple testing and test selection.
- 14.I.05 — **Comparing Means and Distributions: t-Tests and Nonparametric Tests:** One-sample, paired and independent t-tests, Welch test, Mann–Whitney and Wilcoxon tests, assumption checks and confidence intervals.
- 14.I.06 — **ANOVA, Chi-Square and Categorical Data Analysis:** One- and two-way ANOVA, F ratios, interactions, post-hoc comparisons, Kruskal–Wallis context, chi-square goodness of fit and independence, expected counts and Fisher exact context.
- 14.I.07 — **Correlation, Regression and Introduction to Multivariate Statistics:** Pearson and Spearman correlation, linear regression, residuals, R-squared, prediction uncertainty, confounding, multiple regression, PCA, clustering and introductory multivariate interpretation.

### 14.J — IPR, Biosafety and Bioethics

Connect intellectual property, biological safety, animal and human research ethics, publication integrity and responsible AI use to scientific practice.

- 14.J.01 — **Intellectual Property Rights and Patent Information:** Patents, copyright, trademarks, designs, trade secrets and plant variety protection, novelty, inventive step, industrial applicability, patent databases including Indian Patent Advanced Search, WIPO PATENTSCOPE and Espacenet.
- 14.J.02 — **Biosafety, Containment and Regulatory Governance:** Biological risk assessment, biological safety measures, biosafety levels BSL-1–4, containment principles, institutional oversight, Indian DBT biosafety framework and relevant regulatory guidelines, dual-use awareness.
- 14.J.03 — **Animal Ethics and Responsible Biomedical Research:** 3Rs, animal welfare, ethical review, humane endpoints as governance concepts, CCSEA and IAEC context, informed consent, human research ethics, vulnerable participants and privacy.
- 14.J.04 — **Research Integrity, Publication Ethics and Plagiarism:** Study design integrity, falsification, fabrication, plagiarism, citation, authorship, conflicts, peer review, duplicate publication, corrections, retractions and data management.
- 14.J.05 — **Responsible Use of AI and Reproducible Scientific Practice:** AI in research and publication, hallucinations, disclosure, verification, authorship limitations, confidentiality, bias, reproducibility, data and code provenance and journal policies.

## Learning progression

Molecular sample preparation and separation → biophysical measurements → omics → tracers → immunodetection → microscopy → electrophysiology → field observations → statistical inference → intellectual property, safety and ethics. Statistical and ethical foundations apply throughout; ordering follows official headings, not the chronological order of every research project.

## Editorial and progression contract

- Curriculum architecture only; no unpublished lesson URLs, sitemap edits or lesson-completion counter changes.
- Original friendly academic English, BSc foundations to MSc/NET/SET depth, connected narratives, original diagrams, classical evidence, modern applications, quantitative examples with assumptions and units, misconception checks, Part B and analytical Part C practice with solutions.
- Unit 13 owns biotechnology application and conceptual comparison; Unit 14 owns measurement principles, experimental evidence, instrumentation, statistical interpretation and ethical governance. Unit 3 owns molecular mechanisms, Unit 4 immunology, Unit 5 development, Unit 8 inheritance, Unit 10 ecology, Unit 11 algorithms and Unit 12 industrial engineering. Preview later methods when needed without duplicating full chapters.
- Use benign educational examples, abstract models and published high-level evidence. No actionable pathogen culture or engineering, harmful genetic modification, production recipes, invasive experimentation, radioisotope handling or medical treatment instructions. Research involving biological hazards, animals, humans or radiation requires trained personnel and authorised oversight.
- Verify current laws, biosafety rules, regulatory bodies and journal AI policies at actual publication; distinguish prediction from experimental or clinical validation. Maintain provenance and research integrity.
