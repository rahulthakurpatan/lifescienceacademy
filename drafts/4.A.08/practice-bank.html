Original examination practice

These are original NET/SET-style practice questions, not official previous-year questions. All numerical datasets are hypothetical educational examples.

Part B conceptual questions

1. Which event directly terminates the active nucleotide state of a typical Gα subunit?

  1. GDP phosphorylation by receptor
  2. Hydrolysis of bound GTP
  3. Cleavage of receptor ligand
  4. Hydrolysis of cAMP
Answer: B

Gα hydrolyzes GTP to GDP and phosphate. Receptor exchange, ligand dissociation and PDE activity affect distinct steps.

2. A phosphatase removes an inhibitory phosphate from an enzyme. What is the immediate prediction?

  1. The enzyme is destroyed
  2. ATP is regenerated
  3. The receptor must internalize
  4. Enzyme activity can increase
Answer: D

The effect of dephosphorylation depends on the role of the site. Phosphatases hydrolyze phosphate ester bonds rather than regenerating ATP.

3. Which statement best describes a scaffold?

  1. It organizes compatible signaling partners
  2. It is necessarily a kinase
  3. It always amplifies all pathways
  4. It exclusively binds extracellular hormones
Answer: A

A scaffold assembles partners in space; it need not catalyze a reaction, and its effects can be selective.

4. Which observation most directly demonstrates adaptation?

  1. Response declines after ligand removal
  2. Receptor abundance is low before stimulation
  3. Response declines while ligand remains constant
  4. Response increases with ligand concentration
Answer: C

Adaptation is a decline under continuing input. Decline after removal is termination, not necessarily adaptation.

5. Which enzyme converts cAMP to 5′-AMP?

  1. Adenylyl cyclase
  2. A cyclic nucleotide phosphodiesterase
  3. Protein kinase A
  4. A GTPase-activating protein
Answer: B

A PDE hydrolyzes the cyclic phosphodiester linkage. Cyclase produces cAMP; PKA and RGS act elsewhere.

6. Which process replenishes the ER calcium store after release?

  1. PMCA-mediated extrusion
  2. Rhodopsin phosphorylation
  3. Calcium binding to extracellular proteins
  4. SERCA-mediated uptake
Answer: D

SERCA uses ATP to move cytosolic Ca²⁺ into the ER. PMCA instead exports calcium from the cell.

7. Which event can reduce GPCR coupling without reducing receptor number?

  1. GRK-dependent phosphorylation and arrestin recruitment
  2. Receptor gene deletion
  3. Lysosomal receptor destruction
  4. Reduced receptor synthesis
Answer: A

Arrestin binding to appropriate phosphorylated receptors can uncouple G proteins before receptor abundance changes.

8. A downstream kinase induces a phosphatase that inactivates that same kinase. This is:

  1. Simple convergence
  2. Positive feedback
  3. Negative feedback
  4. Passive ligand binding
Answer: C

The kinase promotes production of its own inhibitor; the overall loop opposes the original change.

9. Which process distinguishes downregulation from simple internalization?

  1. Binding of an agonist
  2. Reduction of total receptor abundance
  3. Movement into a recycling vesicle only
  4. GTP binding to Gα
Answer: B

Downregulation involves a decrease in receptor abundance, often through degradation; internalization alone need not decrease total receptor.

10. Which variable is primarily reported by a conventional calibrated cytosolic Ca²⁺ indicator?

  1. All intracellular calcium including ER stores
  2. Rate of ATP hydrolysis
  3. Number of open calcium channels
  4. Free cytosolic Ca²⁺ concentration
Answer: D

A free-ion signal does not independently quantify bound calcium, total cellular calcium or channel activity.

11. Which interaction illustrates pathway convergence?

  1. Two receptors regulate one shared downstream target
  2. One receptor returns to the surface
  3. A PDE hydrolyzes cAMP
  4. An inactive receptor binds ligand
Answer: A

Convergence means distinct inputs reach a common target; it does not by itself prove direct cross-talk between their proximal pathways.

12. What is the role of RGS proteins in many heterotrimeric G-protein pathways?

  1. They synthesize GDP
  2. They catalyze receptor endocytosis
  3. They accelerate Gα GTP hydrolysis
  4. They directly synthesize cAMP
Answer: C

RGS proteins act as GTPase-activating proteins for compatible Gα proteins. They do not substitute for cyclases or trafficking proteins.

13. Why can a sustained stimulus produce an early peak followed by partial decline?

  1. Receptor activation must be irreversible
  2. Delayed inhibitory feedback can accumulate
  3. Ligand concentration necessarily becomes zero
  4. All active proteins become permanently inactive
Answer: B

Delayed inhibition can reduce an ongoing response without eliminating it. Ligand depletion is an alternative that should be controlled experimentally.

14. Which enzyme directly opposes class I PI3K-dependent PIP3 accumulation by removing its 3-phosphate?

  1. PKA
  2. SERCA
  3. GRK2
  4. PTEN
Answer: D

PTEN converts PIP3 to PIP2. The other proteins have unrelated catalytic roles.

15. Which feature distinguishes a feed-forward inhibitor from a feedback inhibitor in the simplified network discussed?

  1. Its induction can originate from input independently of the inhibited output
  2. It is necessarily extracellular
  3. It cannot produce a transient response
  4. It always activates the output
Answer: A

The input can separately induce an inhibitor in an incoherent feed-forward motif. Feedback requires a causal influence of downstream activity on the regulator.

16. Which conclusion about positive feedback is justified?

  1. It always causes irreversible differentiation
  2. It necessarily eliminates thresholds
  3. It can reinforce activation but need not create bistability
  4. It always lowers signal gain
Answer: C

Positive feedback reinforces its own cause; bistability or memory requires appropriate strengths and nonlinearities.

17. Which statement about receptor internalization is correct?

  1. All internalized receptors are immediately destroyed
  2. Some internalized receptors recycle or signal from endosomes
  3. Internalization is the same as ligand binding
  4. All internalized receptors increase surface abundance
Answer: B

Receptor fates differ. Internalization changes location; recycling, degradation and endosomal signaling are possible.

18. What is the strongest interpretation of the classic PC12 EGF/NGF comparison?

  1. All ERK signals cause identical responses
  2. ERK is unique to differentiation
  3. ERK duration is unimportant
  4. Different ERK temporal profiles can contribute to different cell fates
Answer: D

The PC12 model supports the importance of temporal context, not a universal mapping from signal duration to cell fate.

19. Which reaction directly consumes ATP in a calcium clearance mechanism?

  1. SERCA transport of calcium into the ER
  2. Calcium binding to a buffer
  3. Passive calcium flow through a channel
  4. Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchange itself
Answer: A

SERCA directly couples ATP hydrolysis to transport. Exchange uses electrochemical gradients; buffering and passive flow do not directly hydrolyze ATP.

20. Why can phosphatase inhibition increase basal phosphorylation while lowering fold-change after a stimulus?

  1. Phosphorylation cannot occur in the mutant
  2. The receptor necessarily disappears
  3. The baseline denominator can increase
  4. The final absolute activity is always smaller
Answer: C

A higher baseline can reduce the ratio of peak to baseline even when absolute peak activity is greater.

Part C analytical and numerical questions

21. A cell has normal Gα-GTP decay after washout, but cAMP decays slowly. Which experiment most directly distinguishes impaired PDE activity from persistent cyclase activity?

  1. Count nuclei
  2. Stop cyclase production and measure cAMP decay
  3. Measure receptor ligand affinity only
  4. Measure total Gα at one time
Answer: B

With formation suppressed, decay more directly reflects clearance. Normal Gα decay alone does not exclude G-protein-independent cyclase activity.

22. Control kinase activity is 1, 9, 4, 2 at 0, 2, 10, 30 min; mutant activity is 1, 9, 8, 7. Ligand is maintained. What is the best inference?

  1. Mutant has no kinase
  2. Initial peak proves feedback is absent
  3. Mutant receptor is definitely absent
  4. Regulation of sustained activity differs; the specific cause remains uncertain
Answer: D

The initial peak is equal, later activity differs. Increased sustained input or reduced inactivation are both plausible.

23. A receptor mutant has normal ligand binding and surface abundance but weak G protein activation after repeated stimulation. Which explanation is favored?

  1. Functional uncoupling without obligatory receptor loss
  2. Complete destruction of all surface receptors
  3. Lack of extracellular ligand binding
  4. Increased synthesis of receptor
Answer: A

Functional uncoupling fits the preserved binding and abundance; it is not by itself proof of a particular kinase or arrestin mechanism.

24. With first-order clearance, production is 8 µM/min and k = 0.4 min⁻¹. What is the steady-state messenger concentration?

  1. 3.2 µM
  2. 0.05 µM
  3. 20 µM
  4. 8.4 µM
Answer: C

At steady state production equals kM, so M = 8/0.4 = 20 µM. Multiplying v by k gives incorrect units.

25. After production stops, messenger falls from 32 to 4 µM in 9 min. Under first-order decay, what is the half-life?

  1. 9 min
  2. 3 min
  3. 4.5 min
  4. 1 min
Answer: B

32→16→8→4 represents three halvings in nine minutes, or three minutes per half-life.

26. In a two-state phosphorylation model, a=0.3 min⁻¹ and b=0.7 min⁻¹. Doubling both constants changes the steady fraction to:

  1. 0.6
  2. 0.15
  3. 0.5
  4. 0.3
Answer: D

f=a/(a+b)=0.3 in both cases. Relaxation becomes faster; the steady fraction is unchanged.

27. Ligand A gives reporter value 8, B gives 10, baseline is 3, and both give 15. Relative to baseline-subtracted addition, what is the result?

  1. Consistent with addition
  2. Strong inhibition
  3. Superadditive by 3
  4. Impossible to compare
Answer: A

A adds 5, B adds 7, expected combined raw value=3+5+7=15. Adding raw signals double-counts baseline.

28. Two pulses have equal area: 4 units for 2 min and 2 units for 4 min. Which target distinguishes them by peak alone?

  1. A linear nondecaying integrator
  2. A target independent of signal
  3. A rapidly responding target activated above 3 units
  4. A reporter responding solely to integrated area
Answer: C

Only the first pulse crosses the 3-unit threshold. Equal integrated area does not imply equal response for a threshold decoder.

29. A scaffold mutant binds its kinase normally but cannot bind the downstream substrate. Purified kinase activity is normal. Which result is plausible?

  1. No change in any cellular output is possible
  2. Reduced substrate phosphorylation in intact cells
  3. Kinase catalytic site must be destroyed
  4. All receptors must be downregulated
Answer: B

The mutation can reduce effective encounters in cells without altering intrinsic catalysis in a purified assay.

30. In a SERCA-inhibited cell, first cytosolic calcium response is prolonged but the next release in calcium-free medium is smaller. Why?

  1. The first calcium response destroys every receptor
  2. Calcium is chemically degraded
  3. PMCA generates calcium ions
  4. Store refilling is impaired despite prolonged cytosolic elevation
Answer: D

SERCA loss reduces uptake into the ER, potentially increasing the first cytosolic signal while reducing calcium available for the next release.

31. A receptor activates a target and independently induces an inhibitor. Which result favors feed-forward over feedback from the target?

  1. Inhibitor induction persists when target activation is selectively blocked
  2. Inhibitor is detectable only after stimulation
  3. Target declines during continuous ligand
  4. Target activity is higher before inhibition
Answer: A

Persistence of inhibitor induction despite target blockade supports an input-driven inhibitory branch. Timing alone does not distinguish the motifs.

32. For Gα exchange e=0.6 s⁻¹ and hydrolysis h=0.4 s⁻¹, h rises to 0.8 s⁻¹. What is the new active steady fraction?

  1. 0.8
  2. 0.6
  3. Approximately 0.429
  4. Approximately 0.167
Answer: C

g=e/(e+h)=0.6/1.4≈0.429; the regulator increases hydrolysis rather than changing total Gα.

33. A mutant retains normal receptor phosphorylation but has deficient Gα hydrolysis. What should happen to Gα-GTP after exchange is stopped?

  1. It immediately becomes GDP regardless of mutation
  2. It decays more slowly
  3. It cannot have been active initially
  4. It is converted into cAMP
Answer: B

Hydrolysis directly governs the decay in the stated model. Normal receptor phosphorylation does not repair a Gα hydrolysis defect.

34. A phosphatase knockout increases pERK. An upstream kinase knockout abolishes phosphorylation at the assayed site, including in the double mutant. What can be inferred?

  1. The phosphatase is itself a ligand
  2. The two enzymes are the same protein
  3. The phosphatase definitely directly binds ERK
  4. The elevated readout requires the activating kinase
Answer: D

The activating kinase is required for the observed phosphorylation. Direct physical substrate assignment needs additional evidence.

35. A control has activity 2→10 and a mutant 5→15. Which comparison is correct?

  1. Mutant peak and absolute increment are greater, fold-change lower
  2. Mutant fold-change is greater
  3. Both absolute increments equal
  4. Mutant peak is smaller
Answer: A

Control increment 8, fold 5; mutant increment 10, fold 3. Report baseline, absolute and relative measures separately.

36. A cyclic AMP analogue restores a reporter response in a receptor-defective cell. Which claim is strongest?

  1. The receptor is conclusively the only possible affected molecule
  2. Endogenous PDE activity is proved normal
  3. The downstream reporter machinery can respond to the bypass input
  4. All cyclic nucleotide effectors are functional
Answer: C

Rescue supports functional downstream responsiveness under the test conditions; it does not uniquely locate every defect.

37. A ligand increases kinase phosphorylation but a second ligand suppresses it. What additional result supports cross-talk upstream of a common reporter?

  1. The reporter has a limited dynamic range
  2. The second ligand changes a proximal first-pathway marker
  3. Both ligands alone activate the reporter
  4. The combined reporter value equals baseline
Answer: B

A proximal change supports functional interaction prior to the reporter; reporter saturation can otherwise mimic negative interaction.

38. A phosphodiesterase mutant retains basal hydrolysis but lacks activation by PKA phosphorylation. Which prediction fits a PKA–PDE negative loop?

  1. Basal cAMP must be zero
  2. PKA cannot bind cAMP
  3. The enzyme now produces ATP
  4. Stimulus-induced cAMP clearance can be weakened without complete loss of basal hydrolysis
Answer: D

The specific feedback defect can differ from deletion of the enzyme because basal catalytic activity remains.

39. In a hypothetical rod cell, rhodopsin is rapidly quenched but transducin hydrolysis is slow. What is expected?

  1. PDE activity may persist and cGMP recovery may be delayed
  2. cGMP must rise immediately above baseline
  3. No active transducin remains
  4. Rhodopsin must remain active
Answer: A

Downstream G protein activity can persist after receptor quenching. Activated transducin promotes cGMP hydrolysis.

40. One cell has normal bulk cAMP, but a reporter near an AKAP complex shows an enlarged local signal. Which interpretation is most plausible?

  1. The measurements are logically incompatible
  2. The local reporter necessarily measures DNA synthesis
  3. Local production, buffering or clearance may differ from the bulk average
  4. cAMP cannot diffuse
Answer: C

Spatially heterogeneous production, buffering and degradation can give distinct local responses.

Assertion–reason

41. Assertion: Desensitization can occur without receptor degradation. Reason: A phosphorylated GPCR can recruit arrestin that interferes with productive G-protein coupling.

  1. Both true; reason explains assertion
  2. Both true; reason does not explain
  3. Assertion true; reason false
  4. Assertion false; reason true
Answer: A

Both statements are true and the reason supplies a molecular mechanism for reduced coupling with preserved receptor number.

42. Assertion: A phosphatase always decreases the activity of its substrate. Reason: Some phosphorylation sites are inhibitory.

  1. Both true; reason explains assertion
  2. Both true; reason does not explain
  3. Assertion true; reason false
  4. Assertion false; reason true
Answer: D

An inhibitory phosphate can be removed to activate a substrate. Hence the universal assertion is false.

43. Assertion: A constant messenger concentration can coexist with nonzero synthesis. Reason: Equal formation and removal rates give zero net concentration change.

  1. Both true; reason explains assertion
  2. Both true; reason does not explain
  3. Assertion true; reason false
  4. Assertion false; reason true
Answer: A

This is a dynamic steady state, not necessarily the absence of biochemical flux.

44. Assertion: Two equal-area pulses need not produce equal downstream responses. Reason: A threshold-sensitive effector may respond to only the higher peak.

  1. Both true; reason explains assertion
  2. Both true; reason does not explain
  3. Assertion true; reason false
  4. Assertion false; reason true
Answer: A

A threshold decoder is nonlinear, so equal areas do not force identical activity.

45. Assertion: Increasing RGS activity can accelerate signal decay after exchange stops. Reason: RGS proteins catalyze cAMP synthesis.

  1. Both true; reason explains assertion
  2. Both true; reason does not explain
  3. Assertion true; reason false
  4. Assertion false; reason true
Answer: C

The assertion is correct for compatible G proteins; RGS accelerates GTP hydrolysis, not messenger synthesis.

46. Assertion: Internalized receptors can sometimes signal. Reason: PDEs hydrolyze cyclic nucleotides.

  1. Both true; reason explains assertion
  2. Both true; reason does not explain
  3. Assertion true; reason false
  4. Assertion false; reason true
Answer: B

Both are true, but PDE hydrolysis does not explain why receptors may signal from endosomes.

Multiple-statement and matching

47. Select the correct statements: I. Feedback requires a causal loop. II. Every inhibitor is feedback. III. A shared output alone proves direct cross-talk. IV. Delayed inhibition may produce a transient response.

  1. I and IV
  2. II and III
  3. I and III
  4. II and IV
Answer: A

I and IV are correct. Constitutive inhibition need not be feedback, and independent convergence can produce a shared output.

48. Select the correct statements: I. SERCA replenishes ER Ca²⁺. II. PMCA exports Ca²⁺. III. Buffers chemically destroy Ca²⁺. IV. Exchange direction depends on gradients.

  1. I and III
  2. II and III
  3. I, II and IV
  4. All four
Answer: C

SERCA and PMCA move calcium to different destinations; exchange depends on gradients. Buffers bind but do not destroy ions.

49. Select the correct statements: I. Gα hydrolysis produces GDP and phosphate. II. GRK directly hydrolyzes Gα-bound GTP. III. RGS can accelerate hydrolysis. IV. GPCRs generally promote nucleotide exchange.

  1. I and II
  2. I, III and IV
  3. II and IV
  4. III only
Answer: B

I, III and IV correctly distinguish G protein hydrolysis and exchange; GRK phosphorylates receptor substrates rather than hydrolyzing Gα-bound GTP.

50. Match 1. PDE, 2. DUSP, 3. GRK, 4. SERCA with a. ER uptake, b. GPCR phosphorylation, c. cyclic nucleotide hydrolysis, d. MAPK dephosphorylation (appropriate DUSP).

  1. 1-b,2-c,3-d,4-a
  2. 1-a,2-d,3-c,4-b
  3. 1-d,2-b,3-a,4-c
  4. 1-c,2-d,3-b,4-a
Answer: D

The assignments distinguish messenger hydrolysis, MAPK dephosphorylation, receptor phosphorylation and calcium transport.

51. Match 1. Ste5, 2. PTEN, 3. arrestin, 4. RGS with a. Gα hydrolysis acceleration, b. PIP3 dephosphorylation, c. yeast mating scaffold, d. GPCR uncoupling.

  1. 1-c,2-b,3-d,4-a
  2. 1-a,2-c,3-b,4-d
  3. 1-d,2-a,3-c,4-b
  4. 1-b,2-d,3-a,4-c
Answer: A

Ste5 assembles mating MAPK components, PTEN opposes PIP3 accumulation, arrestin can uncouple GPCRs and RGS accelerates hydrolysis.

52. Select the correct statements: I. Equal peak activity ensures equal output. II. Different pulse durations can be decoded. III. A population mean can hide single-cell pulses. IV. Equal integrated area always predicts equal output.

  1. I and IV
  2. I and III
  3. II and III
  4. II and IV
Answer: C

Only II and III hold generally. Thresholds, decay and adaptation can cause different outcomes despite equal peaks or areas.
