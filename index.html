    02Cellular Organization⌄

    Study the cell in a connected sequence: cell boundaries and membrane transport → intracellular organelles → genetic organization → cell division and life cycle → microbial physiology. The five subunits below follow the revised official syllabus.

    2.ACell Wall and Cell Membrane: Structure and Function⌄

    Cell wall; physical structure of model membranes in prokaryotes and eukaryotes; lipid bilayer and membrane proteins; diffusion, osmosis, ion channels, active transport and membrane pumps; mechanisms of sorting and regulation of intracellular transport; and electrical properties of membranes.

    Cell walls and membranes · Learning sequence

    1. 2.A.01Cell Walls: Structure, Diversity and Biological Functions — Compare the architecture and physiological roles of plant, bacterial, fungal and archaeal cell walls.
    2. 2.A.02Membrane Lipids and Formation of the Lipid Bilayer — Explore amphipathic lipids, bilayer self-assembly, lipid composition and membrane stability.
    3. 2.A.03Membrane Models, Architecture and Dynamics — Follow the experimental evidence for membrane models, fluidity, asymmetry and dynamic organization.
    4. 2.A.04Membrane Proteins: Structure, Organization and Functions — Relate membrane protein topology, lipid interactions and surface organization to biological function.
    5. 2.A.05Diffusion, Membrane Permeability and Facilitated Transport — Explain passive solute movement using concentration gradients, permeability and carrier transport.
    6. 2.A.06Osmosis, Water Transport and Osmotic Balance — Apply osmotic pressure, tonicity and water potential to water movement in plant and animal cells.
    7. 2.A.07Ion Channels: Selectivity, Gating and Ion Movement — Examine channel selectivity, gating, conductance and ion movement along electrochemical gradients.
    8. 2.A.08Active Transport, Cotransporters and Membrane Pumps — Understand ATP-driven pumps, coupled transport, transport stoichiometry and energy requirements.
    9. 2.A.09Electrical Properties of Membranes and Membrane Potential — Connect membrane capacitance and ion gradients to equilibrium and resting membrane potentials.
    10. 2.A.10Intracellular Membrane Sorting, Vesicular Transport and Regulation — Trace cargo sorting, vesicle formation, targeting, fusion, membrane recycling and transport control.
    2.BStructural Organization and Function of Intracellular Organelles⌄

    Nucleus and its organization and dynamics; mitochondria; Golgi bodies; lysosomes; endoplasmic reticulum; peroxisomes; plastids; vacuoles; chloroplasts; and the structure and function of the cytoskeleton, including its role in cell motility.

    Intracellular organelles · Learning sequence

    1. 2.B.01Nucleus: Structure, Organization and Dynamics — Understand the nuclear envelope, nuclear pores, lamina and nucleolus, and how nuclear organization changes with cellular activity.
    2. 2.B.02Endoplasmic Reticulum: Structure and Cellular Functions — Follow the continuous ER membrane system through rough and smooth regions, protein handling, lipid synthesis and calcium storage.
    3. 2.B.03Golgi Apparatus: Organization, Modification and Sorting — Trace materials through the cis, medial and trans Golgi regions as the cell modifies and directs secretory products.
    4. 2.B.04Lysosomes: Structure, Digestion and Cellular Recycling — Explain how acidic lysosomal compartments digest cellular material while protecting the surrounding cytoplasm.
    5. 2.B.05Vacuoles: Storage, Osmoregulation and Cell Homeostasis — Compare plant, fungal and protist vacuoles in storage, pH regulation, cell volume control and intracellular digestion.
    6. 2.B.06Peroxisomes: Oxidation, Detoxification and Specialized Roles — Study single-membrane oxidative compartments, hydrogen peroxide handling and the specialized peroxisomes found in plants.
    7. 2.B.07Mitochondria: Structure, Energy Functions and Dynamics — Connect mitochondrial membranes, cristae, matrix, organelle DNA and division with the organelle's role in cellular energy supply.
    8. 2.B.08Plastids and Chloroplasts: Diversity, Structure and Functions — Discover plastid types and connect the chloroplast envelope, thylakoids, grana and stroma with photosynthetic function.
    9. 2.B.09Cytoskeleton: Actin, Microtubules and Intermediate Filaments — Compare the three major cytoskeletal systems in cell shape, mechanical support, polarity and internal organization.
    10. 2.B.10Cytoskeletal Motility: Molecular Motors, Cilia and Flagella — Explain myosin, kinesin and dynein movement, ciliary beating, cell migration and intracellular transport.
    2.COrganization of Genes and Chromosomes⌄

    Operons; unique and repetitive DNA; interrupted genes; gene families; structure of chromatin and chromosomes; heterochromatin; euchromatin; and transposons.

    Genes and chromosomes · Learning sequence

    1. 2.C.01Genome and Gene Organization in Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells — Build a clear picture of genes, genomes, DNA organization, bacterial nucleoids and eukaryotic chromosomes.
    2. 2.C.02Operons and the Organization of Prokaryotic Genes — Learn how functionally related bacterial genes are arranged in operons, using the lac and trp systems as familiar examples.
    3. 2.C.03Interrupted Genes and Eukaryotic Gene Architecture — Distinguish exons, introns and other gene regions to understand why many eukaryotic genes have interrupted coding information.
    4. 2.C.04Unique, Moderately Repetitive and Highly Repetitive DNA — Classify single-copy and repeated DNA, including tandem repeats, satellites, minisatellites and microsatellites.
    5. 2.C.05Gene Families, Gene Duplications and Pseudogenes — Connect related gene sequences with duplication, functional specialization and representative families such as globins.
    6. 2.C.06Histones, Nucleosomes and the Fundamental Structure of Chromatin — Follow how DNA wraps around histones to form nucleosomes and the basic chromatin fiber.
    7. 2.C.07Chromatin Packing and Higher-Order Chromosomal Organization — Connect nucleosomes to chromatin loops, chromosome scaffolding and the organization of long DNA molecules.
    8. 2.C.08Chromosome Architecture: Centromeres, Telomeres and Chromatids — Identify chromosome arms and key structural landmarks that help organize and maintain genetic material.
    9. 2.C.09Euchromatin and Heterochromatin: Organization and Differences — Compare loosely and densely packed chromatin, including constitutive and facultative heterochromatin.
    10. 2.C.10Transposons and Retrotransposons: Mobile Genetic Elements — Explain how DNA transposons and retrotransposons move, and why mobile sequences shape genome organization.
    2.DCell Division and Cell Cycle⌄

    Mitosis and meiosis and their regulation; steps of the cell cycle; regulation and control of the cell cycle; apoptosis; necrosis; and autophagy.

    Cell division and cell cycle · Learning sequence

    1. 2.D.01Cell Cycle: Phases, Organization and Biological Significance — G₁, S, G₂, M and G₀ phases, interphase, cell-cycle progression, DNA content and chromosome-number interpretation.
    2. 2.D.02Molecular Regulation of the Cell Cycle: Cyclins and CDKs — Cyclins, cyclin-dependent kinases, CDK inhibitors and the mechanisms coordinating transitions between cell-cycle phases.
    3. 2.D.03Cell-Cycle Checkpoints and Control Mechanisms — G₁/S, intra-S, G₂/M and spindle-assembly checkpoints, DNA damage responses and cell-cycle arrest.
    4. 2.D.04Mitosis: Chromosome Behavior and Sequential Stages — Prophase, prometaphase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase, with the orderly distribution of chromosomes.
    5. 2.D.05Mitotic Regulation, Spindle Function and Cytokinesis — Spindle attachment, chromosome segregation, cohesin regulation, animal cleavage furrows and plant cell-plate formation.
    6. 2.D.06Meiosis I: Homologous Chromosomes and Reduction Division — Homolog pairing, synapsis, five stages of prophase I, crossing over, chiasmata and homolog segregation.
    7. 2.D.07Meiosis II: Chromatid Separation and Meiotic Regulation — Meiosis II stages, sister chromatid separation, control of meiotic divisions, chromosome counts and nondisjunction.
    8. 2.D.08Apoptosis: Programmed Cell Death and Its Regulation — Characteristic cellular changes, intrinsic and extrinsic pathways, caspases, regulation and biological significance.
    9. 2.D.09Necrosis: Cellular Injury and Mechanisms of Cell Death — Causes of necrotic injury, cell swelling, membrane disruption, inflammatory consequences and comparison with apoptosis.
    10. 2.D.10Autophagy: Cellular Recycling, Survival and Homeostasis — Autophagosome formation, lysosomal degradation, major forms of autophagy and its relationship with cellular stress and survival.
    2.EMicrobial Physiology⌄

    Growth kinetics; strategies of cell division; stress responses; and antimicrobial resistance (AMR).

    Microbial physiology · Learning sequence

    1. 2.E.01Microbial Growth: Population Characteristics and Measurement — Microbial growth, population increase, viable and total cell counts, colony-forming units, turbidity and biomass measurement.
    2. 2.E.02Batch Culture and the Microbial Growth Curve — Lag, exponential, stationary and death phases, with their biological significance and changes in microbial populations.
    3. 2.E.03Microbial Growth Kinetics: Growth Rate and Generation Time — Exponential growth equations, specific growth rate, doubling time, generation number and quantitative growth problems.
    4. 2.E.04Growth Yield, Nutrient Limitation and Environmental Factors — Biomass yield, substrate utilization, growth efficiency and the effects of nutrients, temperature, pH and oxygen.
    5. 2.E.05Binary Fission: Mechanism and Regulation of Bacterial Cell Division — Cell enlargement, chromosome segregation, FtsZ-ring formation, divisome assembly, septation and daughter-cell separation.
    6. 2.E.06Alternative Microbial Cell-Division and Reproductive Strategies — Budding, asymmetric division, fragmentation and multiple fission, with comparisons across representative microorganisms.
    7. 2.E.07Microbial Responses to Temperature, Osmotic and pH Stress — Heat and cold adaptation, chaperones, osmotic adjustment, compatible solutes and cellular pH homeostasis.
    8. 2.E.08Oxidative Stress, Nutrient Starvation and Microbial Survival — Reactive oxygen species, antioxidant defenses, starvation responses, stress adaptation and microbial dormancy.
    9. 2.E.09Antimicrobial Action, Susceptibility and Resistance Fundamentals — Major antimicrobial targets, susceptibility testing principles, MIC, MBC, intrinsic resistance and acquired resistance.
    10. 2.E.10Antimicrobial Resistance: Mechanisms, Spread and Selection — Drug-inactivating enzymes, target modification, efflux pumps, permeability changes and selection or transfer of resistance.

    03Fundamental Processes⌄

    Follow the flow of genetic information through DNA replication and repair, RNA synthesis and maturation, protein production and turnover, and the mechanisms that regulate gene expression.

    3.ADNA Replication, Repair and Recombination⌄

    DNA replication units and origins, replication forks and enzymes, high-fidelity DNA copying, extrachromosomal replicons, DNA damage and repair pathways, homologous and site-specific recombination.

    DNA replication, repair and recombination · Learning sequence

    1. 3.A.01Evidence for DNA Replication and the Replicon Concept — Semiconservative DNA replication, its experimental evidence, replication units, replication bubbles and the meaning of a replicon.
    2. 3.A.02DNA Replication Origins and Initiation — Origin recognition, local DNA unwinding, initiator proteins and controlled initiation in bacterial and eukaryotic cells.
    3. 3.A.03Replication Fork Architecture and Core Replication Enzymes — DNA synthesis directionality, helicase, primase, polymerases, ligase, topoisomerases and leading- and lagging-strand coordination.
    4. 3.A.04Bacterial DNA Replication: Elongation, Okazaki Fragments and Termination — The bacterial replication sequence using DNA polymerase III, polymerase I, DNA ligase, fork convergence and chromosome completion.
    5. 3.A.05Eukaryotic DNA Replication: Fork Progression and Chromosome Ends — Eukaryotic replication polymerases, multi-origin fork progression, chromatin replication and telomere completion.
    6. 3.A.06Extrachromosomal Replicons and Their Replication Strategies — Plasmids, organelle DNA and representative viral DNA replication, including theta and rolling-circle strategies.
    7. 3.A.07Replication Fidelity, Polymerase Proofreading and Mismatch Repair — Accurate nucleotide selection, 3′→5′ proofreading, post-replicative mismatch recognition and correction.
    8. 3.A.08DNA Damage: Sources, Lesions and Consequences — Spontaneous and induced DNA lesions, UV photoproducts, oxidative and chemical damage, and DNA strand breaks.
    9. 3.A.09DNA Repair I: Direct Reversal, Base Excision and Nucleotide Excision — Photoreactivation and chemical reversal where present, base-excision repair and nucleotide-excision repair.
    10. 3.A.10DNA Repair II: Strand Break Repair and DNA Damage Tolerance — Single- and double-strand break repair, end joining, template-directed restoration and lesion bypass.
    11. 3.A.11Homologous Recombination: Strand Exchange and Holliday Junctions — RecA/Rad51-mediated homology recognition, strand invasion, Holliday junctions, branch migration and recombinant products.
    12. 3.A.12Site-Specific Recombination: Integration, Excision and Inversion — Recognition of short DNA sites by recombinases, phage integration and orientation-dependent DNA rearrangements.
    3.BRNA Synthesis, Processing and Regulation⌄

    Transcription machinery and RNA polymerases; initiation, elongation and termination; transcription factors, activators, repressors and inhibitors; RNA capping, splicing, editing, polyadenylation, transport and functional RNA types, including ribozymes, riboswitches and non-coding RNA.

    RNA synthesis, processing and regulation · Learning sequence

    1. 3.B.01Fundamentals of Transcription: From DNA Template to RNA — Understand transcription units, template and coding strands, RNA synthesis direction, promoters and the basic transcription cycle.
    2. 3.B.02Prokaryotic RNA Polymerase, Promoters and Transcription Initiation — Explore bacterial RNA polymerase, sigma factors, promoter recognition, open-complex formation and initiation.
    3. 3.B.03Prokaryotic Transcription Elongation, Termination and Inhibition — Follow RNA chain extension, transcription pausing and rho-dependent or intrinsic termination, with standard inhibitor examples.
    4. 3.B.04Eukaryotic RNA Polymerases and General Transcription Machinery — Compare RNA polymerases I, II and III, the transcripts they produce and the general factors needed for transcription.
    5. 3.B.05Eukaryotic Transcription Initiation, Activators and Repressors — Build the pre-initiation complex and explain how transcription factors, activators and repressors affect RNA polymerase recruitment.
    6. 3.B.06Eukaryotic Transcription Elongation, Termination and Inhibitors — Trace RNA polymerase II through promoter escape, elongation and transcription termination, and compare major transcription inhibitors.
    7. 3.B.07Eukaryotic mRNA End Processing: 5′ Capping and 3′ Polyadenylation — Explain how newly synthesized pre-mRNA receives a 5′ cap and a cleaved, polyadenylated 3′ end.
    8. 3.B.08Pre-mRNA Splicing: Introns, Spliceosome and Alternative Splicing — Follow splice-site recognition, lariat formation, exon ligation and the basic outcomes of alternative splicing.
    9. 3.B.09RNA Editing and Maturation of rRNA and tRNA — Distinguish RNA editing from splicing and examine major processing steps that form functional ribosomal and transfer RNAs.
    10. 3.B.10RNA Export, Intracellular Transport and Quality Control — Explain how mature RNAs reach their working locations and how cells retain or remove defective RNA transcripts.
    11. 3.B.11Major RNA Types: Structure, Function and Cellular Roles — Compare messenger, ribosomal, transfer and small nuclear/nucleolar RNAs in their structural and biological roles.
    12. 3.B.12Non-coding RNAs: Classes and Biological Functions — Understand microRNAs, siRNAs, piRNAs and long non-coding RNAs through their formation and core roles.
    13. 3.B.13Catalytic and Regulatory RNAs: Ribozymes and Riboswitches — Discover catalytic RNAs and ligand-responsive riboswitches through classic examples and clear molecular mechanisms.
    3.CProtein Synthesis, Processing and Degradation⌄

    Ribosome structure, genetic code, tRNA charging and translation fidelity; initiation, elongation, termination and translational control; translation inhibitors, protein modifications, targeting and transport, and protein degradation.

    Protein synthesis, processing and degradation · Learning sequence

    1. 3.C.01Genetic Code and Principles of Translational Decoding — Understand triplet codons, reading frames, start and stop signals, degeneracy, wobble and the basic properties of the genetic code.
    2. 3.C.02tRNA Identity, Aminoacylation and Translational Proofreading — Follow tRNA recognition, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases, ATP-dependent charging and editing that helps place the correct amino acid on each tRNA.
    3. 3.C.03Ribosome Architecture and the Molecular Basis of Translation — Compare 70S and 80S ribosomes, their subunits, functional A/P/E sites and catalytic rRNA roles in protein synthesis.
    4. 3.C.04Prokaryotic Translation Initiation and Initiation Factors — Build a bacterial translation-initiation complex using Shine–Dalgarno pairing, initiator fMet-tRNA and IF1, IF2 and IF3.
    5. 3.C.05Eukaryotic Translation Initiation and Its Key Factors — Explain cap-dependent initiation, the 40S scanning complex, AUG selection, eIFs and 60S subunit joining.
    6. 3.C.06Translation Elongation, Translocation and Decoding Accuracy — Track the repeating cycle of aminoacyl-tRNA entry, codon recognition, peptide-bond formation and ribosome movement.
    7. 3.C.07Translation Termination, Ribosome Recycling and Polysomes — Follow stop-codon recognition, release factors, polypeptide release, ribosome recycling and multiple ribosomes translating one mRNA.
    8. 3.C.08Regulation of Translation in Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells — Understand how cells adjust protein production through initiation-factor activity, mRNA features and nutrient or stress conditions.
    9. 3.C.09Translational Inhibitors and Their Sites of Action — Compare standard translation inhibitors by their ribosomal targets and the stage of protein synthesis they interrupt.
    10. 3.C.10Nascent Protein Folding, Chaperones and Maturation — Follow newly synthesized polypeptides through folding, chaperone assistance, assembly and basic protein quality control.
    11. 3.C.11Post-translational Modifications: Types and Biological Functions — Compare proteolytic cleavage, phosphorylation, glycosylation, acetylation, methylation and ubiquitin attachment as protein-processing events.
    12. 3.C.12Protein Targeting to the ER and Secretory Pathway — Explain signal peptides, signal recognition particle, co-translational translocation and basic sorting of secreted or membrane proteins.
    13. 3.C.13Protein Import into Nucleus, Mitochondria, Plastids and Peroxisomes — Compare organelle-specific targeting signals and transport mechanisms that deliver proteins to their correct cellular compartments.
    14. 3.C.14Protein Degradation: Ubiquitin–Proteasome and Lysosomal Pathways — Follow protein turnover, ubiquitin tagging, proteasomal breakdown and lysosome-associated degradation in cellular homeostasis.
    3.DControl of Gene Expression at Transcription and Translation Level⌄

    Integrated control of gene expression in bacteriophages, viruses, prokaryotes and eukaryotes, including transcriptional and translational regulation, chromatin-dependent control, gene silencing and epigenetic regulation.

    Gene expression control · Learning sequence

    1. 3.D.01Fundamental Principles of Gene Expression Regulation — Why cells regulate gene expression; constitutive and regulated genes, positive and negative control, inducible and repressible systems, and transcriptional versus translational regulation.
    2. 3.D.02Regulation of the Lac Operon: An Inducible Gene System — Understand lactose-dependent regulation through the lac repressor, operator, inducer and transcriptional switching, with mutation-based reasoning.
    3. 3.D.03Regulation of the Trp Operon: Repression and Attenuation — Follow tryptophan-dependent repression, leader-region regulation, attenuation and the relationship between transcription and translation in bacteria.
    4. 3.D.04Positive Control and Translational Regulation in Prokaryotes — Explain cAMP–CRP regulation, regulatory sigma factors, small regulatory RNAs and control of bacterial translation initiation.
    5. 3.D.05Bacteriophage Gene Regulation: The Lambda Genetic Switch — Explore how bacteriophage λ regulates lytic and lysogenic gene-expression programs through CI, Cro and associated regulatory decisions.
    6. 3.D.06Regulation of Gene Expression in Animal and Plant Viruses — Understand representative viral early and late gene-expression programs, dependence on host machinery and basic viral transcriptional and translational controls.
    7. 3.D.07Eukaryotic Transcriptional Regulation: Enhancers, Silencers and Transcription Factors — Connect promoters, enhancers, silencers, regulatory DNA-binding proteins and co-regulators to selective expression of eukaryotic genes.
    8. 3.D.08Post-transcriptional and Translational Regulation in Eukaryotes — Explain how RNA stability, untranslated regions, RNA-binding proteins and translation-initiation control determine protein output from an mRNA.
    9. 3.D.09Chromatin Remodeling and Histone-Based Gene Regulation — Relate nucleosome positioning, chromatin accessibility, ATP-dependent remodeling and histone modifications to activation and repression of genes.
    10. 3.D.10DNA Methylation and Epigenetic Regulation — Understand DNA methylation, its maintenance, interactions with chromatin and its role in stable patterns of gene expression.
    11. 3.D.11RNA-Mediated Gene Silencing: RNA Interference — Follow miRNA and siRNA processing, Argonaute/RISC action, target recognition and post-transcriptional gene silencing.
    12. 3.D.12Epigenetic Memory, Genomic Imprinting and X-Chromosome Inactivation — Connect stable gene-expression states, parent-of-origin imprinting and X-inactivation with the biological significance of epigenetic regulation.

    04Cell Communication and Cell Signaling⌄

    Explore how cells receive signals, communicate with one another, coordinate immunity, interact with pathogens and control cell growth.

    4.ACell Signaling⌄

    Learn how cells respond to hormones and other cues through hormone receptors, cell-surface receptors, G-protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs), second messengers and signal-transduction pathways. Examine how signaling is regulated, including bacterial and plant two-component systems, light signaling in plants, bacterial chemotaxis and quorum sensing.

    Cell signaling · Learning sequence

    1. 4.A.01Principles of Cell Signaling: Signals, Receptors and Responses — Understand signal molecules, receptor specificity, signal reception, transduction, amplification and the cellular response.
    2. 4.A.02Hormones and Receptor Classes: Cell-Surface and Intracellular Signaling — Compare peptide, amine and steroid or thyroid hormone signaling through GPCRs, enzyme-linked and ion-channel receptors or intracellular receptors.
    3. 4.A.03G-Protein-Coupled Receptors and Heterotrimeric G-Protein Activation — Follow GPCR ligand binding, seven-transmembrane receptor activation, GDP–GTP exchange, Gαβγ effectors and the G-protein cycle.
    4. 4.A.04Cyclic Nucleotide Second Messengers: cAMP, cGMP and Their Effectors — Trace cAMP and cGMP formation, protein kinase activation and messenger removal in representative signaling pathways.
    5. 4.A.05Phosphoinositide, IP₃, DAG and Calcium Signaling — Explain phospholipase C activation, PIP₂ cleavage, IP₃-triggered calcium release, DAG–PKC signaling and calcium-dependent proteins.
    6. 4.A.06Receptor Tyrosine Kinases and the Ras–MAPK Signaling Cascade — Follow growth-factor receptor activation, tyrosine phosphorylation, adaptor recruitment, Ras and MAPK cascades, and a basic PI3K–Akt branch.
    7. 4.A.07Cytokine and Serine–Threonine Kinase Receptor Pathways — Compare JAK–STAT signaling with TGF-β-family receptor–SMAD signaling as additional enzyme-associated transduction mechanisms.
    8. 4.A.08Regulation and Integration of Cell-Signaling Pathways — Understand signal amplification, feedback, receptor desensitization, phosphatases, messenger removal and pathway cross-talk.
    9. 4.A.09Bacterial Two-Component Systems: Histidine Kinases and Response Regulators — Explore stimulus detection, histidine-to-aspartate phosphotransfer and bacterial transcriptional or behavioral responses.
    10. 4.A.10Plant Two-Component Signaling and Cytokinin Phosphorelays — Follow cytokinin perception through plant histidine-kinase receptors, histidine phosphotransfer proteins and response regulators.
    11. 4.A.11Light Signaling in Plants: Phytochromes and Blue-Light Receptors — Explain light perception by phytochromes, cryptochromes and phototropins and their control of basic light-responsive signaling.
    12. 4.A.12Bacterial Chemotaxis: Sensory Receptors, Che Proteins and Adaptation — Trace detection of attractants or repellents to CheA–CheY signaling, flagellar behavior and sensory adaptation.

    1. 7.K.01Foundations of Metaorganisms, Holobionts and Gut Microbiome Ecology — Explore animals as hosts of microbial communities, distinguishing microbiota, microbiome, metaorganism, holobiont and hologenome, then connect colonisation, diversity and ecological interactions with the organisation of gut microbial communities.
    2. 7.K.02Physiological Functions of the Gut Microbiome — Trace microbial fermentation, short-chain fatty acid production, vitamin synthesis and bile acid transformation, explaining their contributions to nutrient utilisation, intestinal barriers, immune development and host homeostasis.
    3. 7.K.03Methods and Experimental Approaches to Study the Gut Microbiome — Compare culture-based approaches, 16S rRNA gene profiling, shotgun metagenomics, metatranscriptomics and metabolomics, then interpret diversity, abundance and experimental controls to evaluate microbiome evidence.
    4. 7.K.04Germ-Free Animals and Gnotobiotic Models — Distinguish germ-free, gnotobiotic, conventional and specific pathogen-free animals, then use controlled colonisation and microbiota-transfer experiments to evaluate causal contributions of microorganisms to host physiology.
    5. 7.K.05Gut–Brain Axis and Neuroendocrine Communication — Trace bidirectional signalling between the intestine and brain through vagal, enteric, endocrine, immune and microbial-metabolite pathways, linking gut microbial activity with neural and behavioural physiology.
    6. 7.K.06Gut Dysbiosis, Disease and Restoration of Microbial Homeostasis — Examine disturbances in microbial community composition and function, relating dysbiosis to intestinal, metabolic and neurological disease evidence, then compare principles and limitations of probiotics, prebiotics and faecal microbiota transplantation.
    7.LInterorgan Communication and Energy Homeostasis; Metabolic Health and Disorders⌄

    Integrate endocrine, neural and metabolite signals between the brain, gut, liver, skeletal muscle, adipose tissue and pancreas. Explain fed and fasting metabolism, appetite and energy expenditure, then relate disrupted interorgan signaling to insulin resistance, obesity and other metabolic disorders.
