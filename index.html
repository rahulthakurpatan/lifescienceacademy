    14 Units

    Molecular & Cellular Foundations

    01Structure and Function of Biomolecules⌄

    Study biomolecules in six connected learning sections, from chemical foundations and molecular structure to catalysis, bioenergetics and integrated metabolism.

    1.AChemical and Biophysical Foundations⌄

    Establish the chemical and physicochemical foundations of living systems, connecting atomic structure, molecular interactions, biological solutions, reaction kinetics and thermodynamics.

    Chemical and Biophysical Foundations · Learning sequence

    1. 1.A.01Atomic Structure and Chemical Bonding — Atoms, isotopes, ions, electronic configuration, orbitals, periodic properties, chemical bonds, hybridisation, resonance and molecular geometry.
    2. 1.A.02Water and Molecular Stabilising Interactions — Water properties, polarity, hydration, electrostatic interactions, hydrogen bonds, van der Waals forces and hydrophobic effects.
    3. 1.A.03Biological Solutions, pH and Buffer Systems — Concentration units, colligative properties, osmotic pressure, acids, bases, pKa, titration curves, Henderson–Hasselbalch equation and biological buffers.
    4. 1.A.04Chemical Kinetics and Reaction Dynamics — Reaction rates, rate laws, reaction order, molecularity, half-life, activation energy and Arrhenius equation.
    5. 1.A.05Thermodynamics, Chemical Equilibrium and Redox Principles — Thermodynamic laws, enthalpy, entropy, Gibbs free energy, equilibrium constants, redox potentials and Nernst equation.
    1.BCarbohydrates, Lipids and Nucleic Acids⌄

    Explore the composition, chemical properties, structural organisation, stability and biological functions of carbohydrates, lipids and nucleic acids.

    Carbohydrates, Lipids and Nucleic Acids · Learning sequence

    1. 1.B.01Carbohydrate Chemistry and Structural Organisation — Monosaccharides, stereochemistry, anomers, epimers, mutarotation, reducing sugars, disaccharides, polysaccharides and complex carbohydrates.
    2. 1.B.02Lipid Chemistry and Biological Organisation — Fatty acids, neutral lipids, phospholipids, glycolipids, sphingolipids, cholesterol, steroids, eicosanoids, micelles and lipid bilayers.
    3. 1.B.03Nucleotide Chemistry and DNA Conformation — Bases, nucleosides, nucleotides, phosphodiester bonds, double helix, base pairing and A-, B- and Z-DNA.
    4. 1.B.04RNA Structure and Functional Conformations — RNA folding, secondary and tertiary structures, hairpins, pseudoknots, mRNA, tRNA, rRNA, regulatory RNAs and ribozymes.
    5. 1.B.05Nucleic Acid Topology and Molecular Stability — Linking number, twist, writhe, supercoiling, base stacking, melting temperature, denaturation, renaturation and hybridisation.
    1.CAmino Acids and Protein Structure⌄

    Trace protein organisation from amino acid chemistry and peptide formation to higher-order conformations, folding, stability and biological functions.

    Amino Acids and Protein Structure · Learning sequence

    1. 1.C.01Amino Acid Chemistry and Peptide Formation — Amino acid classification, stereochemistry, zwitterions, pKa, isoelectric point, titration and peptide bonds.
    2. 1.C.02Protein Backbone Conformation and Secondary Structure — Primary structure, peptide planarity, phi and psi angles, Ramachandran plots, alpha helices, beta sheets, turns and loops.
    3. 1.C.03Protein Tertiary and Quaternary Organisation — Motifs, domains, folds, structural classification, subunit interactions, protein assemblies and stabilising forces.
    4. 1.C.04Protein Folding, Stability and Denaturation — Folding energetics, Anfinsen's experiment, chaperones, folding landscapes, denaturation, renaturation and aggregation.
    5. 1.C.05Protein Structure–Function Relationships — Fibrous and globular proteins, collagen, keratin, myoglobin, haemoglobin, ligand binding, cooperativity and allostery.
    1.DEnzymes and Catalysis⌄

    Understand biological catalysis through enzyme properties, catalytic mechanisms, quantitative kinetics, inhibition and regulation of enzyme activity.

    Enzymes and Catalysis · Learning sequence

    1. 1.D.01Enzyme Fundamentals and Catalytic Mechanisms — Classification, specificity, active sites, cofactors, coenzymes, transition states and major catalytic mechanisms.
    2. 1.D.02Michaelis–Menten Kinetics and Quantitative Analysis — Derivation, steady-state assumptions, Km, Vmax, kcat, catalytic efficiency and kinetic plots.
    3. 1.D.03Enzyme Inhibition and Activity Modulation — Competitive, noncompetitive, uncompetitive, mixed and irreversible inhibition, inhibition constants and graphical interpretation.
    4. 1.D.04Multisubstrate and Allosteric Enzyme Kinetics — Sequential and ping-pong mechanisms, cooperative binding, Hill equation, MWC and KNF models.
    5. 1.D.05Enzyme Regulation and Isozymes — Feedback inhibition, covalent modification, phosphorylation, zymogens, enzyme abundance, isozymes and metabolic regulation.
    1.EBioenergetics and Carbohydrate Metabolism⌄

    Explain cellular energy conversion and carbohydrate metabolism through interconnected biochemical pathways, respiratory mechanisms and metabolic regulation.

    Bioenergetics and Carbohydrate Metabolism · Learning sequence

    1. 1.E.01Bioenergetics and Biological Energy Transduction — ATP, coupled reactions, group-transfer potential, high-energy compounds, biological electron carriers and energy conversion.
    2. 1.E.02Glycolysis, Pyruvate Metabolism and Fermentation — Glycolytic reactions, energetics, regulation, aerobic and anaerobic metabolism, pyruvate dehydrogenase and acetyl-CoA formation.
    3. 1.E.03Citric Acid Cycle and Metabolic Integration — TCA cycle reactions, regulation, energetic yield, amphibolic functions and anaplerotic reactions.
    4. 1.E.04Electron Transport and Oxidative Phosphorylation — Respiratory complexes, chemiosmosis, proton motive force, ATP synthase, respiratory control, inhibitors, uncouplers and mitochondrial shuttles.
    5. 1.E.05Glucose Homeostasis and Alternative Carbohydrate Pathways — Gluconeogenesis, glycogenesis, glycogenolysis, Cori cycle, pentose phosphate pathway, fructose and galactose metabolism.
    1.FLipid, Amino Acid, Nucleotide and Vitamin Metabolism⌄

    Integrate the biosynthesis, degradation and regulation of lipids, amino acids, nucleotides and vitamins within the broader framework of cellular metabolic homeostasis.

    Lipid, Amino Acid, Nucleotide and Vitamin Metabolism · Learning sequence

    1. 1.F.01Fatty Acid and Ketone Body Metabolism — Fatty acid activation, carnitine shuttle, beta oxidation, fatty acid biosynthesis, ketogenesis, ketolysis and regulation.
    2. 1.F.02Complex Lipid and Cholesterol Metabolism — Triacylglycerols, phospholipids, sphingolipids, cholesterol, lipoproteins, bile acids and eicosanoid metabolism.
    3. 1.F.03Amino Acid Metabolism and Nitrogen Homeostasis — Transamination, deamination, ammonia transport, urea cycle, amino acid biosynthesis, degradation and carbon skeleton metabolism.
    4. 1.F.04Purine and Pyrimidine Nucleotide Metabolism — De novo synthesis, salvage pathways, nucleotide degradation, deoxyribonucleotide synthesis and metabolic regulation.
    5. 1.F.05Vitamins, Coenzymes and Integrated Metabolic Regulation — Vitamin structures, biological functions, active forms, coenzyme chemistry, vitamin metabolism, deficiency concepts and integrated metabolic pathways.

    02Cellular Organization⌄

    Study the cell in a connected sequence: cell boundaries and membrane transport → intracellular organelles → genetic organization → cell division and life cycle → microbial physiology. The five subunits below follow the revised official syllabus.

    2.ACell Wall and Cell Membrane: Structure and Function⌄

    Cell wall; physical structure of model membranes in prokaryotes and eukaryotes; lipid bilayer and membrane proteins; diffusion, osmosis, ion channels, active transport and membrane pumps; mechanisms of sorting and regulation of intracellular transport; and electrical properties of membranes.

    Cell walls and membranes · Learning sequence

    1. 2.A.01Cell Walls: Structure, Diversity and Biological Functions — Compare the architecture and physiological roles of plant, bacterial, fungal and archaeal cell walls.
    2. 2.A.02Membrane Lipids and Formation of the Lipid Bilayer — Explore amphipathic lipids, bilayer self-assembly, lipid composition and membrane stability.
    3. 2.A.03Membrane Models, Architecture and Dynamics — Follow the experimental evidence for membrane models, fluidity, asymmetry and dynamic organization.
    4. 2.A.04Membrane Proteins: Structure, Organization and Functions — Relate membrane protein topology, lipid interactions and surface organization to biological function.
    5. 2.A.05Diffusion, Membrane Permeability and Facilitated Transport — Explain passive solute movement using concentration gradients, permeability and carrier transport.
    6. 2.A.06Osmosis, Water Transport and Osmotic Balance — Apply osmotic pressure, tonicity and water potential to water movement in plant and animal cells.
    7. 2.A.07Ion Channels: Selectivity, Gating and Ion Movement — Examine channel selectivity, gating, conductance and ion movement along electrochemical gradients.
    8. 2.A.08Active Transport, Cotransporters and Membrane Pumps — Understand ATP-driven pumps, coupled transport, transport stoichiometry and energy requirements.
    9. 2.A.09Electrical Properties of Membranes and Membrane Potential — Connect membrane capacitance and ion gradients to equilibrium and resting membrane potentials.
    10. 2.A.10Intracellular Membrane Sorting, Vesicular Transport and Regulation — Trace cargo sorting, vesicle formation, targeting, fusion, membrane recycling and transport control.
    2.BStructural Organization and Function of Intracellular Organelles⌄

    Nucleus and its organization and dynamics; mitochondria; Golgi bodies; lysosomes; endoplasmic reticulum; peroxisomes; plastids; vacuoles; chloroplasts; and the structure and function of the cytoskeleton, including its role in cell motility.

    Intracellular organelles · Learning sequence

    1. 2.B.01Nucleus: Structure, Organization and Dynamics — Understand the nuclear envelope, nuclear pores, lamina and nucleolus, and how nuclear organization changes with cellular activity.
    2. 2.B.02Endoplasmic Reticulum: Structure and Cellular Functions — Follow the continuous ER membrane system through rough and smooth regions, protein handling, lipid synthesis and calcium storage.
    3. 2.B.03Golgi Apparatus: Organization, Modification and Sorting — Trace materials through the cis, medial and trans Golgi regions as the cell modifies and directs secretory products.
    4. 2.B.04Lysosomes: Structure, Digestion and Cellular Recycling — Explain how acidic lysosomal compartments digest cellular material while protecting the surrounding cytoplasm.
    5. 2.B.05Vacuoles: Storage, Osmoregulation and Cell Homeostasis — Compare plant, fungal and protist vacuoles in storage, pH regulation, cell volume control and intracellular digestion.
    6. 2.B.06Peroxisomes: Oxidation, Detoxification and Specialized Roles — Study single-membrane oxidative compartments, hydrogen peroxide handling and the specialized peroxisomes found in plants.
    7. 2.B.07Mitochondria: Structure, Energy Functions and Dynamics — Connect mitochondrial membranes, cristae, matrix, organelle DNA and division with the organelle's role in cellular energy supply.
    8. 2.B.08Plastids and Chloroplasts: Diversity, Structure and Functions — Discover plastid types and connect the chloroplast envelope, thylakoids, grana and stroma with photosynthetic function.
    9. 2.B.09Cytoskeleton: Actin, Microtubules and Intermediate Filaments — Compare the three major cytoskeletal systems in cell shape, mechanical support, polarity and internal organization.
    10. 2.B.10Cytoskeletal Motility: Molecular Motors, Cilia and Flagella — Explain myosin, kinesin and dynein movement, ciliary beating, cell migration and intracellular transport.
    2.COrganization of Genes and Chromosomes⌄

    Operons; unique and repetitive DNA; interrupted genes; gene families; structure of chromatin and chromosomes; heterochromatin; euchromatin; and transposons.

    Genes and chromosomes · Learning sequence

    1. 2.C.01Genome and Gene Organization in Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells — Build a clear picture of genes, genomes, DNA organization, bacterial nucleoids and eukaryotic chromosomes.
    2. 2.C.02Operons and the Organization of Prokaryotic Genes — Learn how functionally related bacterial genes are arranged in operons, using the lac and trp systems as familiar examples.
    3. 2.C.03Interrupted Genes and Eukaryotic Gene Architecture — Distinguish exons, introns and other gene regions to understand why many eukaryotic genes have interrupted coding information.
    4. 2.C.04Unique, Moderately Repetitive and Highly Repetitive DNA — Classify single-copy and repeated DNA, including tandem repeats, satellites, minisatellites and microsatellites.
    5. 2.C.05Gene Families, Gene Duplications and Pseudogenes — Connect related gene sequences with duplication, functional specialization and representative families such as globins.
    6. 2.C.06Histones, Nucleosomes and the Fundamental Structure of Chromatin — Follow how DNA wraps around histones to form nucleosomes and the basic chromatin fiber.
    7. 2.C.07Chromatin Packing and Higher-Order Chromosomal Organization — Connect nucleosomes to chromatin loops, chromosome scaffolding and the organization of long DNA molecules.
    8. 2.C.08Chromosome Architecture: Centromeres, Telomeres and Chromatids — Identify chromosome arms and key structural landmarks that help organize and maintain genetic material.
    9. 2.C.09Euchromatin and Heterochromatin: Organization and Differences — Compare loosely and densely packed chromatin, including constitutive and facultative heterochromatin.
    10. 2.C.10Transposons and Retrotransposons: Mobile Genetic Elements — Explain how DNA transposons and retrotransposons move, and why mobile sequences shape genome organization.
    2.DCell Division and Cell Cycle⌄

    Mitosis and meiosis and their regulation; steps of the cell cycle; regulation and control of the cell cycle; apoptosis; necrosis; and autophagy.

    Cell division and cell cycle · Learning sequence

    1. 2.D.01Cell Cycle: Phases, Organization and Biological Significance — G₁, S, G₂, M and G₀ phases, interphase, cell-cycle progression, DNA content and chromosome-number interpretation.
    2. 2.D.02Molecular Regulation of the Cell Cycle: Cyclins and CDKs — Cyclins, cyclin-dependent kinases, CDK inhibitors and the mechanisms coordinating transitions between cell-cycle phases.
    3. 2.D.03Cell-Cycle Checkpoints and Control Mechanisms — G₁/S, intra-S, G₂/M and spindle-assembly checkpoints, DNA damage responses and cell-cycle arrest.
    4. 2.D.04Mitosis: Chromosome Behavior and Sequential Stages — Prophase, prometaphase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase, with the orderly distribution of chromosomes.
    5. 2.D.05Mitotic Regulation, Spindle Function and Cytokinesis — Spindle attachment, chromosome segregation, cohesin regulation, animal cleavage furrows and plant cell-plate formation.
    6. 2.D.06Meiosis I: Homologous Chromosomes and Reduction Division — Homolog pairing, synapsis, five stages of prophase I, crossing over, chiasmata and homolog segregation.
    7. 2.D.07Meiosis II: Chromatid Separation and Meiotic Regulation — Meiosis II stages, sister chromatid separation, control of meiotic divisions, chromosome counts and nondisjunction.
    8. 2.D.08Apoptosis: Programmed Cell Death and Its Regulation — Characteristic cellular changes, intrinsic and extrinsic pathways, caspases, regulation and biological significance.
    9. 2.D.09Necrosis: Cellular Injury and Mechanisms of Cell Death — Causes of necrotic injury, cell swelling, membrane disruption, inflammatory consequences and comparison with apoptosis.
    10. 2.D.10Autophagy: Cellular Recycling, Survival and Homeostasis — Autophagosome formation, lysosomal degradation, major forms of autophagy and its relationship with cellular stress and survival.
    2.EMicrobial Physiology⌄

    Growth kinetics; strategies of cell division; stress responses; and antimicrobial resistance (AMR).

    Microbial physiology · Learning sequence

    1. 2.E.01Microbial Growth: Population Characteristics and Measurement — Microbial growth, population increase, viable and total cell counts, colony-forming units, turbidity and biomass measurement.
    2. 2.E.02Batch Culture and the Microbial Growth Curve — Lag, exponential, stationary and death phases, with their biological significance and changes in microbial populations.
    3. 2.E.03Microbial Growth Kinetics: Growth Rate and Generation Time — Exponential growth equations, specific growth rate, doubling time, generation number and quantitative growth problems.
    4. 2.E.04Growth Yield, Nutrient Limitation and Environmental Factors — Biomass yield, substrate utilization, growth efficiency and the effects of nutrients, temperature, pH and oxygen.
    5. 2.E.05Binary Fission: Mechanism and Regulation of Bacterial Cell Division — Cell enlargement, chromosome segregation, FtsZ-ring formation, divisome assembly, septation and daughter-cell separation.
    6. 2.E.06Alternative Microbial Cell-Division and Reproductive Strategies — Budding, asymmetric division, fragmentation and multiple fission, with comparisons across representative microorganisms.
    7. 2.E.07Microbial Responses to Temperature, Osmotic and pH Stress — Heat and cold adaptation, chaperones, osmotic adjustment, compatible solutes and cellular pH homeostasis.
    8. 2.E.08Oxidative Stress, Nutrient Starvation and Microbial Survival — Reactive oxygen species, antioxidant defenses, starvation responses, stress adaptation and microbial dormancy.
    9. 2.E.09Antimicrobial Action, Susceptibility and Resistance Fundamentals — Major antimicrobial targets, susceptibility testing principles, MIC, MBC, intrinsic resistance and acquired resistance.
    10. 2.E.10Antimicrobial Resistance: Mechanisms, Spread and Selection — Drug-inactivating enzymes, target modification, efflux pumps, permeability changes and selection or transfer of resistance.

    03Fundamental Processes⌄

    Follow the flow of genetic information through DNA replication and repair, RNA synthesis and maturation, protein production and turnover, and the mechanisms that regulate gene expression.

    3.ADNA Replication, Repair and Recombination⌄

    DNA replication units and origins, replication forks and enzymes, high-fidelity DNA copying, extrachromosomal replicons, DNA damage and repair pathways, homologous and site-specific recombination.

    DNA replication, repair and recombination · Learning sequence

    1. 3.A.01Evidence for DNA Replication and the Replicon Concept — Semiconservative DNA replication, its experimental evidence, replication units, replication bubbles and the meaning of a replicon.
    2. 3.A.02DNA Replication Origins and Initiation — Origin recognition, local DNA unwinding, initiator proteins and controlled initiation in bacterial and eukaryotic cells.
    3. 3.A.03Replication Fork Architecture and Core Replication Enzymes — DNA synthesis directionality, helicase, primase, polymerases, ligase, topoisomerases and leading- and lagging-strand coordination.
    4. 3.A.04Bacterial DNA Replication: Elongation, Okazaki Fragments and Termination — The bacterial replication sequence using DNA polymerase III, polymerase I, DNA ligase, fork convergence and chromosome completion.
    5. 3.A.05Eukaryotic DNA Replication: Fork Progression and Chromosome Ends — Eukaryotic replication polymerases, multi-origin fork progression, chromatin replication and telomere completion.
    6. 3.A.06Extrachromosomal Replicons and Their Replication Strategies — Plasmids, organelle DNA and representative viral DNA replication, including theta and rolling-circle strategies.
    7. 3.A.07Replication Fidelity, Polymerase Proofreading and Mismatch Repair — Accurate nucleotide selection, 3′→5′ proofreading, post-replicative mismatch recognition and correction.
    8. 3.A.08DNA Damage: Sources, Lesions and Consequences — Spontaneous and induced DNA lesions, UV photoproducts, oxidative and chemical damage, and DNA strand breaks.
    9. 3.A.09DNA Repair I: Direct Reversal, Base Excision and Nucleotide Excision — Photoreactivation and chemical reversal where present, base-excision repair and nucleotide-excision repair.
    10. 3.A.10DNA Repair II: Strand Break Repair and DNA Damage Tolerance — Single- and double-strand break repair, end joining, template-directed restoration and lesion bypass.
    11. 3.A.11Homologous Recombination: Strand Exchange and Holliday Junctions — RecA/Rad51-mediated homology recognition, strand invasion, Holliday junctions, branch migration and recombinant products.
    12. 3.A.12Site-Specific Recombination: Integration, Excision and Inversion — Recognition of short DNA sites by recombinases, phage integration and orientation-dependent DNA rearrangements.
    3.BRNA Synthesis, Processing and Regulation⌄

    Transcription machinery and RNA polymerases; initiation, elongation and termination; transcription factors, activators, repressors and inhibitors; RNA capping, splicing, editing, polyadenylation, transport and functional RNA types, including ribozymes, riboswitches and non-coding RNA.

    RNA synthesis, processing and regulation · Learning sequence

    1. 3.B.01Fundamentals of Transcription: From DNA Template to RNA — Understand transcription units, template and coding strands, RNA synthesis direction, promoters and the basic transcription cycle.
    2. 3.B.02Prokaryotic RNA Polymerase, Promoters and Transcription Initiation — Explore bacterial RNA polymerase, sigma factors, promoter recognition, open-complex formation and initiation.
    3. 3.B.03Prokaryotic Transcription Elongation, Termination and Inhibition — Follow RNA chain extension, transcription pausing and rho-dependent or intrinsic termination, with standard inhibitor examples.
    4. 3.B.04Eukaryotic RNA Polymerases and General Transcription Machinery — Compare RNA polymerases I, II and III, the transcripts they produce and the general factors needed for transcription.
    5. 3.B.05Eukaryotic Transcription Initiation, Activators and Repressors — Build the pre-initiation complex and explain how transcription factors, activators and repressors affect RNA polymerase recruitment.
    6. 3.B.06Eukaryotic Transcription Elongation, Termination and Inhibitors — Trace RNA polymerase II through promoter escape, elongation and transcription termination, and compare major transcription inhibitors.
    7. 3.B.07Eukaryotic mRNA End Processing: 5′ Capping and 3′ Polyadenylation — Explain how newly synthesized pre-mRNA receives a 5′ cap and a cleaved, polyadenylated 3′ end.
    8. 3.B.08Pre-mRNA Splicing: Introns, Spliceosome and Alternative Splicing — Follow splice-site recognition, lariat formation, exon ligation and the basic outcomes of alternative splicing.
    9. 3.B.09RNA Editing and Maturation of rRNA and tRNA — Distinguish RNA editing from splicing and examine major processing steps that form functional ribosomal and transfer RNAs.
    10. 3.B.10RNA Export, Intracellular Transport and Quality Control — Explain how mature RNAs reach their working locations and how cells retain or remove defective RNA transcripts.
    11. 3.B.11Major RNA Types: Structure, Function and Cellular Roles — Compare messenger, ribosomal, transfer and small nuclear/nucleolar RNAs in their structural and biological roles.
    12. 3.B.12Non-coding RNAs: Classes and Biological Functions — Understand microRNAs, siRNAs, piRNAs and long non-coding RNAs through their formation and core roles.
    13. 3.B.13Catalytic and Regulatory RNAs: Ribozymes and Riboswitches — Discover catalytic RNAs and ligand-responsive riboswitches through classic examples and clear molecular mechanisms.
    3.CProtein Synthesis, Processing and Degradation⌄

    Ribosome structure, genetic code, tRNA charging and translation fidelity; initiation, elongation, termination and translational control; translation inhibitors, protein modifications, targeting and transport, and protein degradation.

    Protein synthesis, processing and degradation · Learning sequence

    1. 3.C.01Genetic Code and Principles of Translational Decoding — Understand triplet codons, reading frames, start and stop signals, degeneracy, wobble and the basic properties of the genetic code.
    2. 3.C.02tRNA Identity, Aminoacylation and Translational Proofreading — Follow tRNA recognition, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases, ATP-dependent charging and editing that helps place the correct amino acid on each tRNA.
    3. 3.C.03Ribosome Architecture and the Molecular Basis of Translation — Compare 70S and 80S ribosomes, their subunits, functional A/P/E sites and catalytic rRNA roles in protein synthesis.
    4. 3.C.04Prokaryotic Translation Initiation and Initiation Factors — Build a bacterial translation-initiation complex using Shine–Dalgarno pairing, initiator fMet-tRNA and IF1, IF2 and IF3.
    5. 3.C.05Eukaryotic Translation Initiation and Its Key Factors — Explain cap-dependent initiation, the 40S scanning complex, AUG selection, eIFs and 60S subunit joining.
    6. 3.C.06Translation Elongation, Translocation and Decoding Accuracy — Track the repeating cycle of aminoacyl-tRNA entry, codon recognition, peptide-bond formation and ribosome movement.
    7. 3.C.07Translation Termination, Ribosome Recycling and Polysomes — Follow stop-codon recognition, release factors, polypeptide release, ribosome recycling and multiple ribosomes translating one mRNA.
    8. 3.C.08Regulation of Translation in Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic Cells — Understand how cells adjust protein production through initiation-factor activity, mRNA features and nutrient or stress conditions.
    9. 3.C.09Translational Inhibitors and Their Sites of Action — Compare standard translation inhibitors by their ribosomal targets and the stage of protein synthesis they interrupt.
    10. 3.C.10Nascent Protein Folding, Chaperones and Maturation — Follow newly synthesized polypeptides through folding, chaperone assistance, assembly and basic protein quality control.
    11. 3.C.11Post-translational Modifications: Types and Biological Functions — Compare proteolytic cleavage, phosphorylation, glycosylation, acetylation, methylation and ubiquitin attachment as protein-processing events.
    12. 3.C.12Protein Targeting to the ER and Secretory Pathway — Explain signal peptides, signal recognition particle, co-translational translocation and basic sorting of secreted or membrane proteins.
    13. 3.C.13Protein Import into Nucleus, Mitochondria, Plastids and Peroxisomes — Compare organelle-specific targeting signals and transport mechanisms that deliver proteins to their correct cellular compartments.
    14. 3.C.14Protein Degradation: Ubiquitin–Proteasome and Lysosomal Pathways — Follow protein turnover, ubiquitin tagging, proteasomal breakdown and lysosome-associated degradation in cellular homeostasis.
    3.DControl of Gene Expression at Transcription and Translation Level⌄

    Integrated control of gene expression in bacteriophages, viruses, prokaryotes and eukaryotes, including transcriptional and translational regulation, chromatin-dependent control, gene silencing and epigenetic regulation.

    Gene expression control · Learning sequence

    1. 3.D.01Fundamental Principles of Gene Expression Regulation — Why cells regulate gene expression; constitutive and regulated genes, positive and negative control, inducible and repressible systems, and transcriptional versus translational regulation.
    2. 3.D.02Regulation of the Lac Operon: An Inducible Gene System — Understand lactose-dependent regulation through the lac repressor, operator, inducer and transcriptional switching, with mutation-based reasoning.
    3. 3.D.03Regulation of the Trp Operon: Repression and Attenuation — Follow tryptophan-dependent repression, leader-region regulation, attenuation and the relationship between transcription and translation in bacteria.
    4. 3.D.04Positive Control and Translational Regulation in Prokaryotes — Explain cAMP–CRP regulation, regulatory sigma factors, small regulatory RNAs and control of bacterial translation initiation.
    5. 3.D.05Bacteriophage Gene Regulation: The Lambda Genetic Switch — Explore how bacteriophage λ regulates lytic and lysogenic gene-expression programs through CI, Cro and associated regulatory decisions.
    6. 3.D.06Regulation of Gene Expression in Animal and Plant Viruses — Understand representative viral early and late gene-expression programs, dependence on host machinery and basic viral transcriptional and translational controls.
    7. 3.D.07Eukaryotic Transcriptional Regulation: Enhancers, Silencers and Transcription Factors — Connect promoters, enhancers, silencers, regulatory DNA-binding proteins and co-regulators to selective expression of eukaryotic genes.
    8. 3.D.08Post-transcriptional and Translational Regulation in Eukaryotes — Explain how RNA stability, untranslated regions, RNA-binding proteins and translation-initiation control determine protein output from an mRNA.
    9. 3.D.09Chromatin Remodeling and Histone-Based Gene Regulation — Relate nucleosome positioning, chromatin accessibility, ATP-dependent remodeling and histone modifications to activation and repression of genes.
    10. 3.D.10DNA Methylation and Epigenetic Regulation — Understand DNA methylation, its maintenance, interactions with chromatin and its role in stable patterns of gene expression.
    11. 3.D.11RNA-Mediated Gene Silencing: RNA Interference — Follow miRNA and siRNA processing, Argonaute/RISC action, target recognition and post-transcriptional gene silencing.
    12. 3.D.12Epigenetic Memory, Genomic Imprinting and X-Chromosome Inactivation — Connect stable gene-expression states, parent-of-origin imprinting and X-inactivation with the biological significance of epigenetic regulation.

    04Cell Communication and Cell Signaling⌄

    Explore how cells receive signals, communicate with one another, coordinate immunity, interact with pathogens and control cell growth.

    4.ACell Signaling⌄

    Learn how cells respond to hormones and other cues through hormone receptors, cell-surface receptors, G-protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs), second messengers and signal-transduction pathways. Examine how signaling is regulated, including bacterial and plant two-component systems, light signaling in plants, bacterial chemotaxis and quorum sensing.

    Cell signaling · Learning sequence

    1. 4.A.01Principles of Cell Signaling: Signals, Receptors and Responses — Understand signal molecules, receptor specificity, signal reception, transduction, amplification and the cellular response.
    2. 4.A.02Hormones and Receptor Classes: Cell-Surface and Intracellular Signaling — Compare peptide, amine and steroid or thyroid hormone signaling through GPCRs, enzyme-linked and ion-channel receptors or intracellular receptors.
    3. 4.A.03G-Protein-Coupled Receptors and Heterotrimeric G-Protein Activation — Follow GPCR ligand binding, seven-transmembrane receptor activation, GDP–GTP exchange, Gαβγ effectors and the G-protein cycle.
    4. 4.A.04Cyclic Nucleotide Second Messengers: cAMP, cGMP and Their Effectors — Trace cAMP and cGMP formation, protein kinase activation and messenger removal in representative signaling pathways.
    5. 4.A.05Phosphoinositide, IP₃, DAG and Calcium Signaling — Explain phospholipase C activation, PIP₂ cleavage, IP₃-triggered calcium release, DAG–PKC signaling and calcium-dependent proteins.
    6. 4.A.06Receptor Tyrosine Kinases and the Ras–MAPK Signaling Cascade — Follow growth-factor receptor activation, tyrosine phosphorylation, adaptor recruitment, Ras and MAPK cascades, and a basic PI3K–Akt branch.
    7. 4.A.07Cytokine and Serine–Threonine Kinase Receptor Pathways — Compare JAK–STAT signaling with TGF-β-family receptor–SMAD signaling as additional enzyme-associated transduction mechanisms.
    8. 4.A.08Regulation and Integration of Cell-Signaling Pathways — Understand signal amplification, feedback, receptor desensitization, phosphatases, messenger removal and pathway cross-talk.
    9. 4.A.09Bacterial Two-Component Systems: Histidine Kinases and Response Regulators — Explore stimulus detection, histidine-to-aspartate phosphotransfer and bacterial transcriptional or behavioral responses.
    10. 4.A.10Plant Two-Component Signaling and Cytokinin Phosphorelays — Follow cytokinin perception through plant histidine-kinase receptors, histidine phosphotransfer proteins and response regulators.
    11. 4.A.11Light Signaling in Plants: Phytochromes and Blue-Light Receptors — Explain light perception by phytochromes, cryptochromes and phototropins and their control of basic light-responsive signaling.
    12. 4.A.12Bacterial Chemotaxis: Sensory Receptors, Che Proteins and Adaptation — Trace detection of attractants or repellents to CheA–CheY signaling, flagellar behavior and sensory adaptation.
    13. 4.A.13Quorum Sensing: Autoinducers and Population-Dependent Responses — Explain bacterial autoinducer production, sensing and feedback through classic LuxI–LuxR and peptide-sensing examples.
    4.BCellular Communication⌄

    5. 13.C.05Animal Germplasm Conservation and Genetic Health Monitoring — Germplasm banks, semen, oocytes and embryos, cryopreservation concepts, pedigree and genomic diversity, inherited disorders, genetic erosion and conservation breeding.
    6. 13.C.06Veterinary Molecular Diagnosis, Medicine and Surgery — Molecular diagnosis of animal pathogens, strain identification, genetic health screening, molecular medicine, biomaterials and molecularly informed surgery concepts, test accuracy and welfare.
    13.DAgriculture Biotechnology⌄

    Link plant culture, transformation, agricultural genomics, molecular diagnostics and marker-assisted crop improvement with environmental evaluation.

    Agriculture Biotechnology · Learning sequence

    1. 13.D.01Plant Tissue and Cell Culture Biotechnology — Totipotency, explants, callus, organogenesis, somatic embryogenesis, micropropagation, meristem culture, haploids and doubled haploids, protoplast and somatic hybridisation concepts.
    2. 13.D.02Transgenic Plants and Plant Molecular Farming — Genetic transformation and transgenic plant concepts, Agrobacterium-mediated and physical delivery categories, trait validation, representative GE crop traits, plantibodies and molecular farming.
    3. 13.D.03Agricultural Genomics and Expressed Sequence Tags — Plant genome organisation, sequencing resources, EST development concepts, transcript-derived markers, functional genomics, comparative genomics and crop trait associations.
    4. 13.D.04Molecular Diagnosis, Strain Identification and Plant Genotyping — Molecular approaches to crop disease diagnosis and strain identification, RFLP, RAPD, AFLP, SSR and SNP markers, non-gel techniques including SNP arrays, KASP and sequencing-based genotyping.
    5. 13.D.05Germplasm Analysis and Marker-Assisted Breeding — Genetic diversity, germplasm characterisation, linkage and QTL prerequisites, marker-assisted selection, marker–trait association, trait improvement and comparison with genomic selection.
    6. 13.D.06Foreground and Background Selection, Introgression and Pyramiding — Marker-assisted backcrossing concepts, foreground and background selection, recombinant selection, linkage drag, introgression, gene pyramiding and multiple-trait selection.
    7. 13.D.07GE Crops, Biodiversity and Responsible Agricultural Innovation — Environmental assessment of genetically engineered crops, gene flow concepts, non-target effects, biodiversity, agricultural sustainability, comparative risk assessment and regulatory context.
    13.EMarine Biotechnology⌄

    Connect oceanographic conditions and marine biological diversity with resource assessment, microbial functions, seafood quality, bioactive compounds and marine protection.

    Marine Biotechnology · Learning sequence

    1. 13.E.01Oceanography and Marine Environmental Foundations — Physical, chemical and biological oceanography, salinity, temperature, pressure, currents, tides, stratification, upwelling, dissolved oxygen and marine ecological zones.
    2. 13.E.02Marine Organisms, Biology and Behaviour — Plankton, nekton and benthos, algae, marine invertebrates, fishes and representative marine mammals, adaptation, reproduction, movement and behaviour.
    3. 13.E.03Marine Resource Assessment and Population Protection — Marine bioresources, biodiversity uses, fish-stock concepts, abundance and population assessment, sampling, sustainable harvest, habitat protection and marine conservation.
    4. 13.E.04Marine Microbiology and Microbial Metabolites — Marine microbial diversity, microbial loop, nutrient cycling, symbiosis, extremophile adaptations, microbial metabolites, enzymes and bioresource discovery.
    5. 13.E.05Seafood Microbiology and Marine Pharmacology — Seafood spoilage, foodborne hazards, quality indicators and safety principles, marine natural products, pharmacological discovery, bioactivity and evidence limitations.
    6. 13.E.06Marine Biofilms, Biofouling and Biocorrosion — Biofilm formation concepts, surface colonisation, macrofouling, microbially influenced corrosion, environmental and economic effects and sustainable prevention approaches.
    13.FEnvironmental Biotechnology⌄

    Apply biological principles to wastewater, pollution control, resource recovery, environmentally friendly products and remediation while evaluating ecological outcomes.

    Environmental Biotechnology · Learning sequence

    1. 13.F.01Wastewater Treatment and Biological Pollution Control — Wastewater characteristics, BOD/COD, primary, secondary and tertiary treatment, activated sludge, trickling filters, anaerobic treatment concepts, nutrient removal and effluent assessment.
    2. 13.F.02Bioremediation and Microbial Pollution Transformation — Biodegradation, bioaugmentation and biostimulation concepts, in-situ/ex-situ remediation, organic contaminants, metals, biosorption, bioavailability, monitoring and limitations.
    3. 13.F.03Phytoremediation and Plant-Based Environmental Recovery — Phytoextraction, phytostabilisation, rhizofiltration, phytodegradation, rhizosphere interactions, contaminant fate and plant-based restoration limitations.
    4. 13.F.04Biosurfactants, Biofertilisers and Biopesticides — Biological surfactants, nutrient-mobilising biofertilisers, nitrogen-fixing associations, biological pest control concepts, ecological benefits, specificity and safety evaluation.
    5. 13.F.05Integrated Waste Management, Biogas and Biofuels — Waste hierarchy, segregation, composting, anaerobic digestion, biogas, biofuel from waste, circular economy, energy recovery and life-cycle assessment boundaries.
    6. 13.F.06Microbially Enhanced Oil Recovery and Sustainable Technology Assessment — Concepts of microbial effects on oil displacement, biosurfactant roles, environmental constraints, integrated pollution control, resource recovery and sustainability assessment.

    14Methods in Biology⌄
    14.AMolecular Biology Techniques⌄

    Learn the principles and interpretation of macromolecule isolation, separation, purification and analytical methods, choosing appropriate techniques and quality controls.

    Molecular Biology Techniques · Learning sequence

    1. 14.A.01Biological Sample Preparation and Macromolecule Analysis — Sample integrity, fractionation concepts, recovery, purity, concentration, analytical controls, contamination and representative DNA, RNA, protein, carbohydrate and lipid measurements.
    2. 14.A.02Isolation and Analysis of DNA and RNA — Genomic and plasmid DNA, RNA isolation and purification principles, nucleic acid integrity, absorbance and fluorescence quantification, hybridisation, specific sequence detection and quality controls.
    3. 14.A.03Protein, Carbohydrate and Lipid Isolation and Analysis — Protein solubility and fractionation, carbohydrate and lipid extraction/separation principles, colorimetric assays, protein activity and standards, lipid and carbohydrate analytical approaches.
    4. 14.A.04Centrifugation and Biological Fractionation — Relative centrifugal force, sedimentation, differential and density-gradient centrifugation, rate-zonal versus isopycnic separation, analytical ultracentrifugation concepts.
    5. 14.A.05Chromatographic Separation and Analytical Interpretation — Partition, adsorption, ion exchange, affinity, size exclusion, TLC, GC and HPLC principles, retention, resolution, efficiency and calibration.
    6. 14.A.06Electrophoresis and Molecular Profiling — Agarose and PAGE, native and SDS-PAGE, one- and two-dimensional electrophoresis, isoelectric focusing, molecular markers and electropherogram interpretation.
    14.BBiophysical Methods⌄

    Progress from electromagnetic and optical measurements to structural determination, mass analysis and biomolecular interaction measurements.

    Biophysical Methods · Learning sequence

    1. 14.B.01Spectroscopy Foundations and UV–Visible Analysis — Electromagnetic radiation, absorption, Beer–Lambert law, extinction coefficient, spectra, calibration, scattering interference, instrumentation and limitations.
    2. 14.B.02Fluorescence Spectroscopy and Circular Dichroism — Excitation/emission, Stokes shift, quantum yield, quenching, FRET, anisotropy, circular dichroism and protein conformational analysis.
    3. 14.B.03NMR and ESR Spectroscopy — Nuclear and electron spin, magnetic resonance, chemical shift, coupling, relaxation, multidimensional NMR concepts, ESR/EPR and paramagnetic species.
    4. 14.B.04X-Ray Diffraction and Molecular Structure Determination — Diffraction, Bragg's law, reciprocal space concepts, electron density, phase problem, model refinement, resolution, R factors and structural validation.
    5. 14.B.05Cryo-Electron Microscopy and Integrated Structural Analysis — Cryo-EM single-particle imaging, projection and reconstruction concepts, maps, resolution, heterogeneity, NMR structural restraints and comparison of X-ray, NMR and cryo-EM.
    6. 14.B.06Mass Spectrometry and Molecular Identification — Mass-to-charge ratio, ionisation concepts including ESI and MALDI, mass analysers, isotope patterns, tandem MS, peptide and metabolite identification, quantitative limitations.
    7. 14.B.07Light Scattering and Surface Plasmon Resonance — Static and dynamic light scattering, molecular size and aggregation, hydrodynamic radius, polydispersity, SPR sensorgrams, association/dissociation and affinity.
    14.CGenomics, Transcriptomics, Proteomics and Metabolomics⌄

    Connect genome-level measurements, molecular identity, expression, protein interactions, metabolic profiling and single-cell datasets with integrated experimental inference.

    Genomics, Transcriptomics, Proteomics and Metabolomics · Learning sequence

    1. 14.C.01Genome Organisation and Comparative Genomics — Prokaryotic and eukaryotic genome organisation, sequencing coverage, genome assembly and annotation evidence, comparative genomics, synteny and structural variation.
    2. 14.C.02DNA Fingerprinting, Molecular Markers and DNA Barcoding — RFLP, RAPD, AFLP, STR and SNP profiles, DNA fingerprinting applications, DNA barcoding, reference libraries and evidence limitations.
    3. 14.C.03Global Gene Expression and Comparative Transcriptomics — Microarray and RNA-seq principles, transcript quantification, differential gene expression, fold change, normalisation, biological replication, multiple testing and comparative transcriptomics.
    4. 14.C.04Proteomics and Post-Translational Modification Analysis — Protein separation and identification, 2D gels, LC-MS/MS, label-based and label-free quantification, proteome coverage and modification detection.
    5. 14.C.05Protein Interaction Analysis and Mapping — Yeast two-hybrid concepts, co-immunoprecipitation, pull-down assays, affinity purification–MS, proximity and interaction mapping, network evidence and orthogonal validation.
    6. 14.C.06Targeted and Untargeted Metabolomics — Metabolic profiling, targeted versus untargeted measurement, LC/GC-MS and NMR, internal standards, feature annotation, pathway interpretation and metabolic flux distinction.
    7. 14.C.07Single-Cell Sequencing and Integrated Single-Cell Omics — Single-cell DNA/RNA sequencing, single-cell chromatin accessibility and protein measurements, cell heterogeneity, barcodes, dropout, clustering, trajectory inference and spatial/multi-omics context.
    14.DRadiolabeling Techniques⌄

    Understand biological tracer measurements, isotope detection, interpretation, molecular imaging and radiation protection in authorised research settings.

    Radiolabeling Techniques · Learning sequence

    1. 14.D.01Radioisotopes and Principles of Biological Tracing — Isotopes, radioactive decay, alpha, beta and gamma emissions, half-life, activity units, specific activity and tracer versus stable isotope concepts.
    2. 14.D.02Radiation Detection and Quantitative Measurement — Geiger–Müller and scintillation detection principles, counting efficiency, background, quenching, count statistics and measurement uncertainty.
    3. 14.D.03Radiolabel Incorporation and Biological Tracer Analysis — Conceptual incorporation of labels in biological molecules, cells and tissues, pulse–chase logic, turnover, distribution and metabolic tracing.
    4. 14.D.04Autoradiography and Molecular Imaging — Autoradiographic localisation, spatial resolution, radiotracer imaging, PET and SPECT principles, signal interpretation and limitations.
    5. 14.D.05Radiation Safety and Responsible Radioisotope Research — Ionising radiation hazards, exposure versus contamination, ALARA, time/distance/shielding concepts, dosimetry, trained personnel, regulatory oversight and authorised waste management.
    14.EHistochemical and Immunotechniques⌄

    Connect antibody and staining principles with quantitative detection, protein identification, cell profiling and spatial localisation.

    Histochemical and Immunotechniques · Learning sequence

    1. 14.E.01Histochemistry and Antibody Generation — Histochemical stains, antigen recognition, polyclonal and monoclonal antibody generation concepts, hybridomas, antibody specificity, affinity and validation.
    2. 14.E.02ELISA, Radioimmunoassay and Quantitative Immunodetection — Direct, indirect, sandwich and competitive ELISA, RIA concepts, standard curves, assay range, cross-reactivity, sensitivity and specificity.
    3. 14.E.03Western Blotting and Immunoprecipitation — Protein transfer and antibody detection, loading controls, Western blot interpretation, immunoprecipitation and co-immunoprecipitation, specificity and limitations.
    4. 14.E.04Flow Cytometry and Fluorescence-Activated Cell Analysis — Light scatter, fluorescence channels, gating, compensation, cell populations, viability and fluorescence-activated sorting concepts.
    5. 14.E.05Immunofluorescence and Detection in Living Cells — Direct/indirect immunofluorescence, localisation, fluorescent proteins, live-cell reporters, colocalisation, photobleaching and control strategies.
    6. 14.E.06In Situ Hybridisation, FISH and GISH — Probe hybridisation concepts, fluorescence in situ hybridisation, genomic in situ hybridisation, chromosome and transcript localisation, specificity and spatial resolution.
    14.FMicroscopic Techniques⌄

    Develop microscopy literacy from optical resolution and specimen contrast to advanced live imaging, electron microscopy, specimen preparation and quantitative image analysis.

    Microscopic Techniques · Learning sequence

    1. 14.F.01Optical Foundations and Resolving Power of Microscopes — Magnification, resolution, numerical aperture, diffraction, Abbe and Rayleigh criteria, working distance, contrast and sampling.
    2. 14.F.02Light Microscopy and Contrast Methods — Bright-field, dark-field, phase contrast, differential interference contrast, polarising microscopy, cell and organelle visualisation and method selection.
    3. 14.F.03Fluorescence, Confocal and Advanced Live-Cell Microscopy — Widefield fluorescence, confocal optical sectioning, multiphoton and light-sheet concepts, super-resolution overview, time-lapse microscopy, live-cell imaging and phototoxicity.
    4. 14.F.04Transmission and Scanning Electron Microscopy — Electron optics, TEM and SEM contrast, internal ultrastructure versus surface morphology, resolution and representative biological images.
    5. 14.F.05Microscopy Sample Preparation and Structural Preservation — Fixation and staining concepts, sectioning, dehydration and embedding, negative staining, freeze-fracture, freeze-etch, cryogenic preservation and preparation artefacts.
    6. 14.F.06Digital Image Processing and Quantitative Microscopy — Pixels and calibration, sampling, noise, background correction, segmentation, thresholding, colocalisation, morphometry, image integrity and reproducibility.
    14.GElectrophysiological Methods⌄

    Relate electrical recordings, neural perturbation evidence and brain imaging to physiological mechanisms and experimental interpretation.

    Electrophysiological Methods · Learning sequence

    1. 14.G.01Bioelectric Signals and Single-Neuron Recording — Membrane potential, electrodes, intracellular and extracellular recording concepts, single-unit spikes, signal conditioning, noise and recording interpretation.
    2. 14.G.02Patch-Clamp Recording and Ion Channel Analysis — Voltage and current clamp, cell-attached, whole-cell, inside-out and outside-out configurations, current–voltage relations, conductance and reversal potential.
    3. 14.G.03ECG and Integrated Physiological Recording — ECG lead concepts, P–QRS–T waves, intervals, heart-rate calculation, EMG context, recording artefacts and physiological interpretation.
    4. 14.G.04Brain Activity Recording and Functional Neurophysiology — EEG, evoked potentials, neural population activity, MEG context, oscillatory bands, temporal/spatial resolution and artefacts.
    5. 14.G.05Brain Lesion, Stimulation and Pharmacological Testing — Conceptual lesion and stimulation studies, pharmacological perturbation, reversible versus permanent effects, controls, behavioural endpoints, causality and animal ethics.
    6. 14.G.06PET, MRI, fMRI and Computed Tomography — PET molecular imaging, MRI magnetic resonance contrast, fMRI BOLD response, CT/CAT X-ray attenuation, structural versus functional imaging and resolution limits.
    14.HMethods in Field Biology⌄

    Develop field sampling and observation literacy for plant and animal populations, movements, behaviour and habitat characterisation.

    Methods in Field Biology · Learning sequence

    1. 14.H.01Field Study Design and Ecological Sampling — Sampling frames, random, systematic and stratified sampling, replication, detection probability, observer bias, spatial/temporal scales and ethical field research.
    2. 14.H.02Plant Population Density and Vegetation Assessment — Quadrats, line and belt transects, density, frequency, cover, abundance, dominance, importance values and vegetation sampling limitations.
    3. 14.H.03Animal Population Density and Abundance Estimation — Direct counts, indirect signs, distance sampling, mark–recapture principles, camera-trap indices, occupancy and imperfect detection.
    4. 14.H.04Ranging Patterns and Behavioural Observation — Home range, territory, movement paths, direct and indirect observations, telemetry and GPS concepts, focal/scan sampling, ethograms and activity budgets.
    5. 14.H.05Habitat Characterisation, Remote Sensing and GIS — Ground surveys, environmental variables, satellite imagery, spectral signatures, NDVI, spatial resolution, land-cover classification, GIS and ground-truth validation.
    14.IStatistical Methods⌄

    Build statistical reasoning from measurement quality and probability to sampling, inference, association, comparative tests and multivariate analysis.

    Statistical Methods · Learning sequence

    1. 14.I.01Measurement Quality, Experimental Error and Descriptive Statistics — Accuracy, precision, calibration, systematic/random error, signal-to-noise ratio, mean, median, mode, range, variance, standard deviation, IQR and coefficient of variation.
    2. 14.I.02Probability and Biological Probability Distributions — Probability rules, conditional probability, random variables, binomial, Poisson and normal distributions, expectation, variance and biological applications.
    3. 14.I.03Sampling Distributions, Estimation and Confidence Intervals — Population versus sample, sampling distribution, central limit theorem, standard error, confidence intervals, sample size and estimation uncertainty.
    4. 14.I.04Hypothesis Testing, Significance and Statistical Errors — Null and alternative hypotheses, p-values, significance levels, type I/II errors, power, effect size, parametric and nonparametric assumptions, multiple testing and test selection.
    5. 14.I.05Comparing Means and Distributions: t-Tests and Nonparametric Tests — One-sample, paired and independent t-tests, Welch test, Mann–Whitney and Wilcoxon tests, assumption checks and confidence intervals.
    6. 14.I.06ANOVA, Chi-Square and Categorical Data Analysis — One- and two-way ANOVA, F ratios, interactions, post-hoc comparisons, Kruskal–Wallis context, chi-square goodness of fit and independence, expected counts and Fisher exact context.
    7. 14.I.07Correlation, Regression and Introduction to Multivariate Statistics — Pearson and Spearman correlation, linear regression, residuals, R-squared, prediction uncertainty, confounding, multiple regression, PCA, clustering and introductory multivariate interpretation.
    14.JIPR, Biosafety and Bioethics⌄

    Connect intellectual property, biological safety, animal and human research ethics, publication integrity and responsible AI use to scientific practice.

    IPR, Biosafety and Bioethics · Learning sequence

    1. 14.J.01Intellectual Property Rights and Patent Information — Patents, copyright, trademarks, designs, trade secrets and plant variety protection, novelty, inventive step, industrial applicability, patent databases including Indian Patent Advanced Search, WIPO PATENTSCOPE and Espacenet.
    2. 14.J.02Biosafety, Containment and Regulatory Governance — Biological risk assessment, biological safety measures, biosafety levels BSL-1–4, containment principles, institutional oversight, Indian DBT biosafety framework and relevant regulatory guidelines, dual-use awareness.
    3. 14.J.03Animal Ethics and Responsible Biomedical Research — 3Rs, animal welfare, ethical review, humane endpoints as governance concepts, CCSEA and IAEC context, informed consent, human research ethics, vulnerable participants and privacy.
    4. 14.J.04Research Integrity, Publication Ethics and Plagiarism — Study design integrity, falsification, fabrication, plagiarism, citation, authorship, conflicts, peer review, duplicate publication, corrections, retractions and data management.
    5. 14.J.05Responsible Use of AI and Reproducible Scientific Practice — AI in research and publication, hallucinations, disclosure, verification, authorship limitations, confidentiality, bias, reproducibility, data and code provenance and journal policies.
