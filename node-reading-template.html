1. Introduction

An atom is the smallest unit of a chemical element that retains the chemical identity of that element. Everything around us, including living organisms, is made of matter composed of atoms.

Atoms are extremely small. Their structure helps explain why different elements behave differently and how they combine to form molecules.

Key concept

Atoms of different elements have different numbers of protons. The number of protons is called the atomic number.

2. Basic Structure of an Atom

An atom has a small, dense nucleus containing protons and (in most atoms) neutrons. Electrons occupy regions around the nucleus called orbitals.

3. Biological Relevance

Living systems depend on atoms such as carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, phosphorus, and sulfur. Their chemical properties help determine the structure and function of biological molecules.

Example: Water

A water molecule (H₂O) contains two hydrogen atoms bonded to one oxygen atom. The unequal sharing of electrons makes the molecule polar, which contributes to water's important properties in living systems.

4. Quick Revision

  • An element is identified by its atomic number.
  • The nucleus contains protons and usually neutrons.
  • Electrons are described using quantum-mechanical orbitals.
  • Atomic structure provides a foundation for understanding chemical bonding and biological molecules.
