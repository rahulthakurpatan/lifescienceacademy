1.A.1.24.1 The chlorine puzzle: why a periodic table says 35.45

One element, several atomic masses

A student reads 35.45 beside chlorine and wonders where the atom with 35.45 nucleons is found. There is none in the ordinary two-isotope model. A chlorine atom's nucleus has a whole-number mass number, but a chemical sample contains chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 in unequal proportions.

Counting atoms gives the clue

Imagine three hypothetical atoms of mass 35u and one of mass 37u. Their total mass is 142u and mean mass per atom is 142/4=35.5u. None has mass 35.5u; the number is an average of a population.

Measured masses refine the classroom story

Neutral chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 have masses around 34.968853u and 36.965903u. NIST gives representative fractions 0.7576 and 0.2424. Their weighted mean is near 35.453u, not exactly the mean of their integer mass numbers.

Three different questions

The isotope mass describes one specified atom, the weighted mean describes a specified sample, and the CIAAW standard atomic weight describes evaluated normal materials. One printed periodic-table number cannot automatically represent every enriched or environmental specimen.

A study route

We will derive the weighted mean from counting, verify formulas and units, recover abundance fractions, solve multi-isotope and spectroscopy problems, then apply the mathematics to molecular biology, environmental labeling and uncertainty.

Learning outcomes: derive mass-average formulas; normalize isotope abundances; use measured isotope masses rather than mass numbers for precision; solve inverse and multi-isotope systems; connect isotope distributions to bioanalytical evidence without overclaiming rates.

1.A.1.24.2 Chronological science: Dalton to isotope-resolved mass spectrometry

Chemistry first measured combination ratios

Early nineteenth-century chemists, including John Dalton, developed relative atomic masses by analyzing the amount of material combining in reactions. Before modern instruments, single-atom masses were far too small to weigh independently.

Fractional values needed an explanation

More accurate chemical formulas and measurements improved early tables. Later, scientists recognized that one element can include atoms with different nuclear neutron numbers. The fractional average was no contradiction: different nuclides contribute different masses.

Soddy's isotope concept and Aston's experiments

The study of radioactive series contributed to Frederick Soddy's isotope concept. Francis Aston's mass spectrometry resolved isotope species, showing that ordinary samples can be mixtures of atomic masses.

Why carbon-12 became a reference

Older oxygen-based reference scales were not completely unified across scientific communities. The agreed carbon-12 standard in the 1960–1961 period established a common denominator for atomic masses.

Modern measurement requires provenance

NIST supplies evaluated isotope mass data and representative fractions; CIAAW evaluates standard atomic weights, including intervals where normal isotope composition varies. A good calculation cites compatible masses, fractions and sample context.

1.A.1.24.3 What exactly is weighted: isotope atom count, not sample mass?

The weights count atoms of one element

For a chlorine sample, the atom fraction of chlorine-37 is its number of chlorine-37 atoms divided by all chlorine atoms. It is not the fraction of total sample mass composed of chlorine-37 unless separately converted.

The physical mass is a different column

A table should have isotope identity, measured neutral isotope mass in u, and isotope amount fraction. The fraction acts as the weight; the isotope mass is the value being averaged.

The proportions must describe the same sample

A carbon-13 fraction measured in one leaf and a carbon-12 fraction measured in another specimen should not be combined as if they were one normalized sample. The closure condition Σfᵢ=1 applies to a defined element pool.

When counts replace percentages

If isotope A has 600 atoms and isotope B has 400, fractions are 0.6 and 0.4. This is the statistical foundation of abundance-weighted averaging.

Why a mass fraction needs special treatment

A heavy isotope contributes slightly more mass per atom. A weight percentage by grams differs from an atom percentage. If a question provides only weight fractions, a conversion involving isotope masses is needed before using atom-fraction weighting.

1.A.1.24.4 Mass number, atomic mass, relative atomic mass and molar mass

Mass number A

A=Z+N is a dimensionless integer count of nuclear protons and neutrons. For ³⁵Cl, A=35; this is not the measured mass 34.968852682u.

Neutral isotope mass m

The mass of one specified ground-state neutral atom is a physical quantity, commonly expressed in unified atomic mass units u or daltons Da. Carbon-12 has mass exactly 12u by the unit definition; carbon-13 is around 13.003355u.

Relative isotope mass and relative atomic mass

Dividing an isotope's atom mass or a sample mean atomic mass by the unified atomic mass unit produces a dimensionless ratio. Thus Aᵣ is written without u even though its numerical value closely matches the mass measured in u.

Molar mass

M is mass per mole, often g/mol. In ordinary chemistry, its numerical value in g/mol is extremely close to relative atomic mass, but the quantities have different dimensions. After the 2019 SI reform the Avogadro constant is exactly defined.

Why the distinction protects numericals

A question asking for Aᵣ=35.45 should not receive 35.45u. A question asking for mean mass per chlorine atom may receive about 35.45u, with the sample and precision clearly stated.

1.A.1.24.5 Derive the weighted-mean formula, rather than memorizing it

Begin with total isotope mass

A population contains N₁ atoms of mass m₁, N₂ of mass m₂, and possibly more isotope species. The combined mass is M_total=N₁m₁+N₂m₂+...

Divide by total atoms

The element's total atom count is N_total=ΣNᵢ. Mean mass per atom is M_total/N_total. Substitute fᵢ=Nᵢ/N_total to obtain m̄=Σfᵢmᵢ.

The closure rule follows automatically

Because the counted populations sum to N_total, Σfᵢ=1. Each fᵢ lies between zero and one. With nonnegative fractions, the mean must lie between the lightest and heaviest isotope masses.

A dimensionless version

Divide by mᵤ=1u to obtain relative atomic mass Aᵣ(sample)=ΣfᵢAᵣ(isotope i). The same arithmetic can produce a mass in u or a ratio without units depending on the target quantity.

Meaning of the answer

A fractional result represents the mean of a material's atom population. It does not imply a fractional neutron, an electron fraction or the existence of an atom with exactly the mean mass.

Mean mass m̄=(ΣNᵢmᵢ)/(ΣNᵢ)=Σfᵢmᵢ, where fᵢ=Nᵢ/ΣNᵢ and Σfᵢ=1.

1.A.1.24.6 The complete data pipeline: atom counts, percentages and ratios

From raw frequency to atom fraction

For isotope counts 300, 150 and 50, total atoms=500 and fractions are 0.6, 0.3 and 0.1. Use normalized amounts, not each unscaled count as a weight.

From percentage to fraction

A 75% abundance is 0.75 as a multiplier. Using 75 rather than 0.75 makes an answer one hundred times too large unless the whole sum is divided by 100.

From heavy-to-light ratio

If there are exactly two isotopes and R=N_H/N_L, then heavy fraction f_H=R/(1+R). For R=1/3 this is 0.25, not one third.

From delta notation

A δ measurement compares an isotope ratio with a reference. To calculate abundance, first reconstruct R_sample=R_reference(1+δ/1000); then convert R to fractions, with any additional isotopes accounted for.

From mass spectrum peak heights

Raw detector intensities may not be equal to isotope number fractions because of isotope-dependent response, molecular adducts, interference and background. Unless the problem explicitly assumes ideal corrected atomic signals, peak fractions should not be blindly treated as atom fractions.

counts→fᵢ=Nᵢ/ΣN; percentages→fᵢ=pᵢ/100; two-isotope ratio→f_H=R/(1+R).

1.A.1.24.7 The number line: an average must move toward the majority isotope

Build the visual

Put light isotope mass m_L on the left and heavy isotope mass m_H on the right. A mean of a mixture is inside the interval between the masses.

Rewrite the formula

If x is the heavy isotope fraction, m̄=(1−x)m_L+xm_H=m_L+x(m_H−m_L). The mean begins at m_L when x=0 and reaches m_H when x=1.

A quick mental problem

With approximate isotope masses 35 and 37, x=0.25 gives 35+0.25×2=35.5. x=0.75 gives 36.5. When x=0.5 the result is exactly the midpoint, 36.

Sensitivity

One percentage-point increase in heavy atom fraction raises mean mass by 0.01(m_H−m_L). For a two-unit gap that is approximately 0.02u per percentage point.

Check before trusting numbers

If a mixture of 35u and 37u is reported as 38u, either data, normalization, or the chosen model is inconsistent. This is a mathematical impossibility for nonnegative normalized fractions.

m̄=m_L+x(m_H−m_L), with 0≤x≤1.

1.A.1.24.8 Chlorine: calculate with NIST exact masses and representative fractions

The precise data

NIST lists neutral ³⁵Cl atomic mass 34.968852682u with representative atom fraction 0.7576, and ³⁷Cl atomic mass about 36.965902602u at fraction 0.2424. The tabulated composition has uncertainty and is not the exact proportion in every normal specimen.

Set up the formula with dimensions

m̄(Cl)=0.7576×34.968852682u+0.2424×36.965902602u≈35.45294u. Aᵣ for this specific representative mixture has numerical value 35.45294 without units.

Compare integer mass-number approximation

Replacing actual isotope masses by nominal 35u and 37u gives approximately 35.4848u for those same fractions. The difference is caused by actual isotope masses not being their whole-number nucleon counts.

Check the official interval

CIAAW's standard chlorine atomic-weight interval for normal materials is [35.446,35.457]. The representative calculation lies within it. Specific processed or enriched samples may be outside the range.

Do not make one measurement claim universal

A correct worked answer states the isotope masses, fractions and source, then reports precision consistent with those fractions. Many textbooks round chlorine's atomic weight to 35.45 for routine chemistry.

m̄(Cl)≈0.7576(34.968852682)+0.2424(36.965902602)≈35.45294 u.

1.A.1.24.9 The carbon-12 reference and a sample containing carbon-13

Exactly twelve has a narrow meaning

The free neutral ¹²C atom in the ground state has mass 12u exactly by definition. That does not require the mean mass of every naturally occurring carbon sample to equal twelve.

A natural-like numerical example

Take fractions 0.9893 for ¹²C and 0.0107 for ¹³C, with isotope masses 12u and 13.003354835u. The weighted mean is approximately 12.010736u; corresponding relative atomic mass is about 12.010736 without unit.

Why rare radiocarbon usually does not matter numerically

Carbon-14 may be present in traces in ordinary natural material but generally contributes negligibly to conventional atomic-weight calculations due to its extremely small abundance. It can still matter greatly to radiocarbon science.

A stable-isotope enriched sample

If 90% of carbon atoms are ¹³C, mean≈0.10×12u+0.90×13.003355u=12.9030195u. The isotope-enriched reagent is still carbon, but its sample mean differs from CIAAW's normal-material carbon interval.

Life sciences connection

Stable ¹³C-labelled glucose can be tracked as its carbon enters metabolites. Detecting isotope substitutions can support atom movement, but a calculated average isotope mass does not by itself yield metabolic flux.

m̄(C)=f₁₂(12u)+f₁₃(13.003354835u) under a two-isotope model.

1.A.1.24.10 Bromine: when the two fractions are almost equal

Measured data

NIST gives bromine-79 mass around 78.9183376u with representative fraction 0.5069, and bromine-81 mass around 80.9162897u with fraction 0.4931.

Predict the answer first

With fractions near 50% each, mean should be close to the midpoint around 79.917u, and slightly closer to lighter bromine-79 because it is more abundant.

Compute precisely enough

m̄≈0.5069×78.9183376+0.4931×80.9162897≈79.9035u. For ordinary reference materials this is consistent with CIAAW's bromine standard atomic weight range [79.901,79.907].

Spectroscopy insight

The near-equal isotope fractions can give two similar-intensity molecular isotope peaks around nominal M and M+2 for a one-bromine ion under suitable conditions. The peak positions and intensities also depend on chemical composition and instrument response.

An exam warning

Bromine's average near 79.9 does not mean that every atom has mass number 80. The mean of two isotopes need not be the mass of an existing isotope.

1.A.1.24.11 Copper: use real masses for a two-isotope metal

The reference isotope population

NIST representative copper has ⁶³Cu fraction 0.6915 and ⁶⁵Cu fraction 0.3085. Their neutral isotope masses are about 62.92959772u and 64.92778970u.

Calculate the mean

Aᵣ≈0.6915×62.92959772+0.3085×64.92778970≈63.546. The mass per atom has the same numerical value in u. The result lies between the two measured masses.

Interpret the balance

The mean is closer to copper-63 because almost seven out of ten copper atoms in this representative sample are copper-63.

A biological context and its limits

Copper participates in metalloenzymes and electron transport chemistry. Its average isotope mass does not tell us copper concentration, whether an organism is deficient, or how active its enzymes are; those require independent measurements.

1.A.1.24.12 Magnesium: three isotopes demand three terms

Three stable magnesium nuclides

NIST representative magnesium isotope fractions are ²⁴Mg 0.7899, ²⁵Mg 0.1000 and ²⁶Mg 0.1101. Their masses are approximately 23.985041697u, 24.985836976u and 25.982592968u.

One complete expression

m̄=0.7899×23.985041697+0.1000×24.985836976+0.1101×25.982592968≈24.305u. All three contributions are necessary.

Check closure and bounds

Fractions total exactly one at the given precision. The result is between 23.985 and 25.983 and nearer the lightest mass because magnesium-24 dominates.

Why 25Mg matters

Ignoring the 10% middle isotope and renormalizing only the endpoints would describe a different hypothetical sample. It would not be an acceptable calculation for the stated data.

Biological interpretation

Magnesium is crucial in enzyme systems, ATP-associated chemistry and nucleic acid structure. A magnesium isotope mean describes physical isotope composition, not the enzyme's catalytic rate or cellular Mg²⁺ concentration.

m̄(Mg)=0.7899m₂₄+0.1000m₂₅+0.1101m₂₆.

1.A.1.24.13 Three isotopes and the generalization to any number of species

Start with a toy exercise

Suppose masses 10u, 11u and 12u have normalized fractions 0.1, 0.2 and 0.7. The mean is 0.1×10+0.2×11+0.7×12=11.6u.

Why the result is nearer the heaviest

Seventy percent of atoms are mass-12, so the average is closer to twelve than to ten. This is an intuitive check separate from arithmetic.

N isotope species

For any number of isotopes with known fractions, sum fᵢmᵢ. The number of isotopes does not alter the rule, but all physically relevant isotope fractions must be included or explicitly assumed negligible.

If a fraction is missing

When exactly three species are known and two fractions are 0.1 and 0.2, the third is 0.7 by closure. If the number of species is unknown, the same trick may not be justified.

Actual versus nominal masses

Using isotope mass numbers as if they were measured isotope masses is only an approximation. High-resolution questions may need many more significant figures and evaluated NIST data.

m̄=Σᵢ fᵢmᵢ, where fᵢ≥0 and Σfᵢ=1.

1.A.1.24.14 The six-step solving method for paper and spreadsheet

Step one: state the target

Identify whether the question asks for mass number, atomic mass in u, relative atomic mass without units, molecular mass, molar mass or ion m/z. The correct numerical operation depends on the quantity.

Step two: audit the isotope list

Record names, actual isotope masses and isotope atom fractions in separate columns. Determine whether the list represents a complete element sample.

Step three: normalize

If raw atom counts are 200, 300 and 500, convert to 0.2, 0.3 and 0.5. If a row contains 20%, use 0.20; do not multiply a percentage directly without dividing by 100.

Step four: compute contributions

Multiply each isotope mass by its matching fraction and add once. A spreadsheet formula such as SUMPRODUCT(mass_range,fraction_range) is conceptually equivalent when the data are verified.

Step five: check arithmetic

All fractions must sum near one within rounding; the mean must lie between minimum and maximum masses and be pulled toward common species.

Step six: present a scientific answer

Report the correct unit or its absence, input precision and sample context. A specific enriched tracer sample need not match CIAAW normal-material interval values.

Correct computation = validated isotope masses × normalized atom fractions → sum → unit, bounds and precision checks.

1.A.1.24.15 Derive inverse abundance: solve for the hidden heavy fraction

Begin with two unknown proportions linked by closure

For exactly two isotopes, write heavy fraction x and light fraction 1−x. The measured average is m̄=(1−x)m_L+xm_H.

Rearrange explicitly

Expand m̄=m_L−xm_L+xm_H=m_L+x(m_H−m_L). Subtract m_L and divide by the isotope mass gap to obtain x=(m̄−m_L)/(m_H−m_L).

A toy numerical

Masses 10u and 11u with mean 10.4u give x=(10.4−10)/(11−10)=0.4, so 40% heavy and 60% light. Back-substitution gives 10.4u as required.

Check impossible values

If the mean lies above m_H or below m_L, this formula yields x outside [0,1]. Rather than forcing a percentage into range, recognize that the assumed data or two-isotope model is inconsistent.

Why a measured mass needs adequate precision

When two isotope masses are close together, dividing by a small gap amplifies small errors in the measured mean. An inferred fraction may need an uncertainty interval.

x_H=(m̄−m_L)/(m_H−m_L); x_L=1−x_H.

1.A.1.24.16 Reference intervals and real natural material: standard is not sample exactness

CIAAW's intervals matter

For certain elements natural isotope composition varies enough that CIAAW reports standard atomic weights as intervals. Current normal-material ranges include C [12.0096,12.0116], O [15.99903,15.99977], Mg [24.304,24.307], Cl [35.446,35.457].

Why an interval is not simply poor instrument accuracy

The material itself can have a different isotope composition depending on source. Variation among real normal samples cannot be eliminated by reporting more digits from an instrument.

Representative fractions versus a particular specimen

NIST representative compositions support illustrative means but may not describe the exact abundance in an isotope-enriched laboratory reagent or a mineral source. If a problem specifies actual sample fractions, use those.

Theoretical bounds and practical ranges are different

A two-isotope mixture's mathematical mean can lie anywhere between pure isotope masses for any fraction in [0,1]. A normal-material CIAAW interval is much narrower because naturally observed fraction variation is limited.

Conclude with scientific care

A result of 35.45294u for a representative chlorine mixture is not a universal exact constant, even when arithmetic uses precise isotope masses. Precision of the fractions and sample context limit how strongly it can be stated.
